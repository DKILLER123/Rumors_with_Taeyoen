Chapter One Hundred Three

Seven Days to Ignition

Take after take after take. Sometimes for the breath-weight of a single word; sometimes for the balance point between hesitation and hunger across one melodic rise.

Kim Si-won spent most of it with his arms folded, listening, offering the occasional technical nudge — this pass built more naturally; pull the breath sound in a touch — because he could see the obvious: the harshest judge of Ji-ho was already in the room, wearing Ji-ho’s headphones.

IU watched him with open professional admiration. Passes she’d have signed off as perfect, he kept finding fault with — and infuriatingly, after the fix, they WERE better. In front of him, a sunbae with years of catalog felt oddly like a rookie again.

Adjust. Re-sing. Adjust. Re-sing.

And finally: the heart of the song — the duet exchanges. “Some” lived or died on the call-and-response, that push-pull dialogue of two people circling the same unspoken word.

Performance

He sings the aching question — what ARE we, exactly? — confusion pressed into every syllable.

She answers on cue — the confession of her own confusion, barbed with a small reproach: stop being so dense.

From the console:

“This exchange can’t just be SUNG — it has to be an actual conversation. Ji-ho — after your question lands, in that hanging beat, imagine you’re looking at her, waiting on the answer. Ji-eun — when you come back at his slowness, I want a grain of ‘you KNOW exactly what we are’ in it. Eyes included.”

So they tried it facing each other — locking eyes across the booth, pushing the lyric’s ambiguity and testing and impatience down the sightline. Stiff for a take or two. Then it caught.

The final line — say you love me — was his. Longing and hope compressed into one held phrase, the emotional double-bar at the end of the song. The control room stayed silent for seconds after it decayed.

Then Kim Si-won was on his feet, applauding, excitement undisguised.

“Good! VERY, very good! The frame and the emotional density are both fully there. What’s left is fine polish — details only.”

Ji-ho came out of the booth with a fine sweat on his forehead. IU followed, blew out a long breath, and grinned at him.

“Wah. Now THAT is what a deep-dive session feels like.”

“Ji-ho — you’re not just the writer and a singer. You’re a genuinely good DIRECTOR of feeling.”

“Working with you was a pleasure.”

“That’s my line, nuna. You handed me more inspiration than I brought.”

High-intensity, high-standard co-creation had done what it always does: forged a fast, professional mutual trust. The first session ended in that particular blend of exhaustion and satisfaction that studio people chase for a living.

Tracking done, edit and mix went to the specialists. Ji-ho and IU moved to the MV: a simple treatment — two people orbiting each other through ordinary daily scenes, all almost-reaches and hesitations, ending open-ended and sweet on an offered earbud and a sent invitation. With two functional actors on set, shooting wrapped in under two days.

Shin Min-hyuk hadn’t budgeted for this velocity. The company recalibrated on the fly, and LOEN’s whole machine spun up to match.

In the back row of a moving van, Taeyeon chewed her soft pink lip and scrolled the news, one headline at a time.

IU collab. “SOME.” LOEN rookie. Every keyword landed on a nerve.

Her boyfriend was debuting. A good company. A launch beside IU that rookies would sell organs for. She was happy for him — genuinely, bouncingly, proudly happy.

And underneath, unbudging, the sediment of loss: that song was MINE.

If it were me, I’d sing it better than IU. She had that much confidence. But a person can do some things in this life and not others. SM had made her — and SM caged her. Fine.

The song got taken. The MAN is mine.

Which led, inevitably, to the tent. The heat of it. Heart and body wholly overrun, entirely at his mercy — the face, the frankly weaponized physique, those deep eyes, and that devastating—

“What is WRONG with Taeyeon eonni?” Yoon-a’s doe eyes were locked on from across the van, chin in hand. “Her whole face is doing spring things. She’s red as a monkey’s backside.”

“Strange. Deeply strange.” Sunny’s eyes narrowed to blades. “She came back from Jeonju WRONG.”

“Right?? RIGHT??” Yuri scooted in, wearing the face of a trauma survivor. “Day one back, she INSISTS on cooking us ramyeon.”

“I even got my hopes up. And then…” The memory alone triggered a small dry heave. “WHO puts an entire BLOCK of cheese in ramyeon?!”

“Half the pot was tteok,” Yoon-a testified, face collapsing. “All of it half-cooked~”

“And the scallion.” Sunny’s hand went to her forehead. “One scallion. Cut into TWO pieces. Thrown in whole.”

“Ahh~”

A three-part synchronized sigh.

“Taeyeon would NEVER volunteer to cook for us. Someone put her up to it.” Sunny’s eyes glinted with terrible intelligence, and swiveled to Yoon-a. “You. Find out who. Whoever it is — they’re already dead.”

Yoon-a glanced at the seat next to Taeyeon, currently occupied by a sleeping Jessica, and radiated reluctance.

“Why ME?”

Yu-ri gazed at her with tranquil finality.

“You tell us.”

Eye contact confirmed it: the maknae-line has no human rights.

Yoon-a rose and gently patted the sleeping Jessica — a maneuver every member knew was performed at one’s own risk. In the exact instant Jessica’s eyes opened, Yoon-a deployed aegyo at lethal concentration:

“Eonni~ your seat looks SO uncomfortable. Omo, and the sun’s right on you! Trade with me? Mine’s shady AND you can lean on the window. Heavenly, honestly.”

Jessica blinked. Rose on autopilot. Shuffled toward Yoon-a’s seat — and even turned back to say, sincerely:

“Yoon-a-yah… thank you.”

Little Jessica: acquired. Yoon-a preened, dropped into the seat beside Taeyeon, and craned over.

“Eonni~ whatcha reading?”

“Oh? ‘SOME’? Isn’t that the song Ji-ho wrote?”

“A duet with IU — wait, Ji-ho’s DEBUTING?”

Two rows up, Tiffany’s phone was out of her bag before Yoon-a finished the sentence. He went to LOEN? She scrolled — then locked the screen with a snap, sulking at the window. Who CARES where he went? So annoying.

Yoon-a sat blinking in the wreckage of her charm offensive. Does eonni not love the cutest Yoon-a anymore??
