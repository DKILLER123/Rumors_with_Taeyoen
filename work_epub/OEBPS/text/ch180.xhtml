Chapter One Hundred Eighty

Girls’ Night — Ji-ho in Peril!

That evening Hara’s villa soju, chicken feet, one sleeping girl on his chest — and a doorbell

He could picture her on the other end with perfect accuracy: lower lip caught in her teeth, wanting him to come and terrified of being a bother, in exactly equal parts. Something in his chest went soft. Refusal was never on the table.

“Ha-ra nuna’s having a rough time?” Concern first, then the smile crept in. “And our Sulli’s inviting me — where would I find the energy to be tired? Send the address, I’m on my way. Although—” the voice dropped, teasing, “—you’re sure it’s only ‘a tiny bit’ of drinking? Because LAST time, a certain someone—”

“YAH! Oppa!” The squawk came through at full indignation — the memory of being carried to bed in a state of total soju defeat clearly still tender. “That was an accident! It won’t happen again! I PROMISE!”

“Okay, okay. Our Sulli is the best-behaved.” A grin she could hear. “Wait for me — I’ll pick up something good on the way. Soju wrecks the stomach; it needs something hot next to it.”

“Ne~!” Instant recovery to full sparkle. “Oppa’s the BEST! Drive safe, I’m waiting!”

She tossed the phone onto the cushion and rolled twice along the sofa out of sheer contentment.

So this is what the sweet kind of dating feels like.

Fine — the secret kind of sweet. A hidden-track girlfriend is still on the album. Exactly like Oppa said: she was simply dating a very busy man who loved her very much.

And next on the agenda: approach Taeyeon eonni. Nestle in close. Become the caring, harmless little sister-in-law — the one nobody guards against. Then, someday, when the OTHERS had clawed each other to exhaustion and worn out their welcome one by one—

—Oppa would be hers alone. Heh. Heh heh.

✦   ✦   ✦

The call ended, and the smile stayed on his face longer than he noticed. He started the car, detoured past a well-reviewed spicy chicken-feet place and a fish-cake soup stall — clean flavors that drank well, and hot broth to buffer the alcohol — added fresh fruit, and followed the pin she’d dropped.

Hara lived in a villa complex with good security. Good location. Building showing its age. And — he clocked it as he parked — a very short walk from her ex-boyfriend’s agency.

This house. Every window of it must be full of him.

He rang the bell, bags in both hands.

The door opened on Sulli — visibly assembled for the occasion: a lotus-pink cotton house dress, hair brushed glossy over her shoulders, a smile pre-installed and brightening on contact.

“Oppa!” she half-sang, relieving him of bags with practiced domestic efficiency.

“Waited long?”

He followed her in, gaze snagging a half-beat on the home-clothes version of her — less stage lighting, more soft focus, disproportionately effective.

“No~ eonni and I were just talking.”

She shook her head, then leaned in close and delivered, in a breath meant for an audience of exactly one:

“Missed you.”

A quick rise to her toes; a kiss pressed to the corner of his mouth. Then she went scarlet at her own audacity and fled toward the living room, leaving him a fragrant retreating figure and a laugh he had to swallow.

The living room lights were dialed low — confession lighting. Hara sat on a floor cushion by the window, a small table in front of her already set with soju bottles, glasses, and modest anju. She looked drawn, eyes swollen at the rims, but she pulled a smile up when he entered.

“Ji-ho-ssi. Chukhahae — the film’s going to be huge.” Her voice had sand in it.

“Thank you, nuna. I brought food — eat something warm first. Drinking on an empty stomach is the real enemy.”

“Wah, Oppa bought EVERYTHING!”

Sulli was already unpacking with quick hands, and the chicken-feet aroma and fish-cake steam went up like a flare, physically brightening the room.

“You’re too generous, jinjja. Sit, sit. Sulli — a glass for him.”

“Ne~”

She fetched a clean glass and settled onto the cushion beside his, close enough that their knees touched, entirely as if that were the only available floor space in the room. He made no move to correct the seating chart. He filled Ha-ra’s glass first, then gave Sulli a strictly ornamental pour, then his own.

“Ha-ra sunbae. One glass from me. Whatever happened — here’s to getting through it soon.”

A tug at the corner of her mouth; glass met glass. She drank deep and winced at the burn.

Sulli took her ceremonial sip, then immediately went to work — one soft-simmered chicken foot into Ha-ra’s bowl. “Eonni, eat this. Oppa picked the place, it’s amazing.” Then, with the exact same naturalness, she lifted a piece to HIS mouth. “Oppa too. You haven’t had dinner, right?”

He blinked once — met the bright, faintly ingratiating, wholly affectionate eyes above the chopsticks — and ate.

Ha-ra watched the little exchange in silence. Something dimmed in her face, then resolved into a complicated species of wistfulness. She looked down, ate her chicken foot — it WAS good — and found her chest aching harder for it.

✦   ✦   ✦

A few glasses in, the words loosened.

Ha-ra talked in fits and lulls — the early sweetness, then the drift: schedules eating the calendar, messages answered later and later, small collisions turning cold. At the worst parts the tears came back and she let them.

Sulli’s own eyes rimmed red. She pressed close against him, one hand unconsciously twisting his shirt hem, drawing comfort straight through the fabric. He kept the listener’s post — a tissue at the right moment, a quiet top-up of Ha-ra’s glass, no speeches.

“—he said I was never around. Said I’d CHANGED.” She dragged a wrist across her eyes and laughed at nothing. “But he got busy first. HE went cold first. So how does it end up being my fault?”

“Eonni, don’t think of it that way—” Sulli had her hand in both of hers.

He let a moment pass, then spoke, slow and level.

“Nuna. With feelings, assigning fault is usually the least useful math. But — maybe the parting isn’t purely a loss. When a relationship is producing more pain than joy, when two people are grinding each other down — stopping, giving each other air, can be its own kind of mercy.”

A beat.

“And — don’t begin the next one lightly. You, me, Sulli — the childhoods we carry make us hold on with everything once we finally hold on. People like us, when we meet someone with bad intentions — we’re the ones who get hurt worst, precisely because we mean it most. So be careful. Twice careful. That’s all.”

He knew what he was looking at. Hara was terrified of being alone — the textbook all-in heart. It was why most of her savings were parked in a house whose main feature was proximity to a man who was now gone.

Sulli, listening, thought of Choi Jae — the case still grinding through the prosecutor’s office, no trial date yet, and the fragments her lawyer let slip already dark enough to freeze the blood. Her grip tightened on his hand of its own accord.

Thank god. Thank god I met him first.

He patted the back of her hand, twice, light. “Sulli. I’m here.”

She looked up at him, made a small sound of assent, and tucked herself into his side.

Ha-ra stared at the two of them for a long moment — then let out a breath that had a whole year in it. “…Maybe. Thank you, Ji-ho-ssi.”

✦   ✦   ✦

The mood climbed slowly out of the pit. One soju bottle died; a second was opened.

Sulli’s tolerance remained decorative. Two pretty flushes had taken up residence on her cheeks; her eyes had gone glossy; her word count doubled. She consoled Ha-ra with escalating warmth, then pivoted mid-sentence into shooting-day stories, then began a slow structural lean that ended with most of her weight on his shoulder. He fielded her tipsy narration with a listener’s patience, kept her upright with small course corrections, and periodically administered a spoonful of hot fish-cake broth. His fingers, in passing, brushed a cheek running several degrees hot — and received, in acknowledgment, a small cat-like nuzzle against his shoulder.

Ha-ra watched them through the soft blur of the alcohol — the unhidden, ungoverned closeness of it — and felt envy and déjà vu arrive together.

Once — not even long ago — she’d had this exact thing.

Heartbroken people drink the way rivers run downhill. She kept pace with herself until the room tilted pleasantly, then folded against the cushions, eyelids losing altitude.

Sulli, herself two blinks from shutdown, rallied heroically. “Oppa — let’s clean up — and help eonni to bed—?”

“Mm.”

They worked quietly: table cleared, trash bagged. He got Ha-ra up with careful hands, Sulli steadying the other side, and they walked her to the bedroom and got the blanket over her.

The door clicked shut. The living room held just the two of them and one warm floor lamp.

And with the caretaker duty discharged, Sulli’s remaining systems stood down all at once. The alcohol and the sleepiness arrived in a single wave; she swayed on her feet.

His arm was around her waist before the sway finished. “Careful.”

“Oppa~” She poured herself into his chest, arms looping his neck, and tipped her flushed face up — eyes swimming, voice all nose and honey. “Head’s spinny… hold me.”

No known defense exists.

His heart liquefied on schedule. He swept her up in both arms; she gave one small startled sound, then burrowed into the crook of his neck, inhaled deeply, and mumbled, “Oppa’s smell… I really like it.”

“I’ll drive you back to the dorm?”

“Mmm… don’t wanna move.” The voice was shrinking. “Stay with me a little… just a little…”

He looked down at the girl in his arms — lips faintly pouted, guard entirely gone — and sighed the sigh of a man losing a negotiation he had no interest in winning.

“Okay. A little.”

He carried her to the sofa, arranged her curled against him at the angle she immediately optimized further, and pulled the throw blanket over her. A contented hum; a small burrowing adjustment; and inside a minute her breathing had gone long and even.

He leaned back into the cushions, a warm, sweet-smelling weight rising and falling against him, and pressed one very light kiss to her forehead.

The room was quiet. The lamp was warm. Somewhere in it, he slept too.

✦   ✦   ✦

Nine o’clock, give or take.

Ha-ra hadn’t actually drunk that much — she’d just drunk it fast, and her bladder had opinions. She navigated the dark on autopilot, resolved the emergency, and padded back toward bed — via the living room.

Where Sulli lay sprawled across Ji-ho’s chest, sleeping with the total abandon of a small pig in a sunbeam.

These two.

She’d smelled it on them the first time she saw them in a room together. So it HAD happened — somewhere between “Action” and “Cut,” the way it always does on a long shoot.

Ding-dong. Ding-dong.

The doorbell went off like a fire alarm in the quiet.

He woke instantly; Sulli made a protesting noise into his shirt. He glanced toward Ha-ra — awake, upright, mid-living-room — and gently jostled the weight on his chest. “Sulli. Wake up. Someone’s here.”

She surfaced in confused installments, blinking, and pushed slowly vertical.

Ha-ra stood frozen a beat longer. This hour. Who? And then the second thought, the one with the hook in it: …Him?

She crossed to the door with her heart doing something complicated, and put her eye to the peephole.

Ji-eun.

The drop of disappointment landed first; the relief followed it down. She let out a breath, fixed her face, and opened the door smiling.

“Eonni~ SURPRISE~!”

Jiyeon sprang out from behind Ji-eun and captured Ha-ra in a full-contact hug.

“Ji-eun! Ji-yeon! What are you two doing here?!”

The two had seen the breakup confirmed on the wires and mobilized the same evening — comfort duty, jointly executed. They’d have brought Peaches too, except Peaches inexplicably wasn’t answering her phone.

“Missing you, obviously. Eonni — ta-da, LOOK.” Ji-eun hoisted her bags. “We brought booze, dried squid, AND kimchi-tofu~”

“Perfect timing — Sulli and Ji-ho are here too. Come in, come in!”

Sulli. And Ji-ho.

Two faces changed in perfect synchronization on the doorstep.

Jiyeon’s brain served up, unrequested and in high fidelity, the memory of one scorching kiss.

Ji-eun’s brain served up the entire unresolved file: a wager, a perfume audit, a boundary neither of them had ever agreed on where to draw.

And inside, on the sofa, the man in question kept his face in perfect order while his pulse quietly redlined.

Choi Sulli. Ji-eun. Jiyeon. Under one roof. With alcohol.

This was what he got for having a documented weakness for best-friend clusters.

Caught at last.
