Chapter One Hundred Fifty-Six

Craving Joo-hyun’s Samgyetang

January 2013 SM Entertainment — a trainee practice room four girls, one secret in the room

The practice-room door banged open, and Wendy bounced in like a small excited bird, eyes shining, locked directly onto Irene mid-stretch.

“Eonni! Is it true Ji-ho came to the company today? Is it REALLY true?”

Everyone else in the room stopped what they were doing. Seulgi, flat on the floor mid-stretch, sat up. Joy set down her water bottle, round cheeks still pink from practice.

The corner of Joo-hyun’s mouth lifted, just barely.

“Mm. I heard he came to discuss a project.”

“Wah!” Seung-wan’s hands flew to her own cheeks, adoration undisguised. “He just keeps getting more incredible.” She puffed her chest out with tremendous pride. “As expected of MY friend.”

“I genuinely envy you, Seung-wan.” Soo-young sighed, and it was the honest kind. “Having someone like Ji-ho as a close friend…”

She couldn’t help thinking back — she and Ji-ho had been trainees in the same intake, but she’d kept her head down and practiced, and the window for getting close had simply… closed. A small regret, still warm to the touch.

Seung-wan, on the strength of exactly that friendship, had starred in one of his album MVs — and the camera had loved her bright eyes and unguarded energy. A line on the résumé most trainees would trade a year for.

“I listened to the whole album,” Seul-gi jumped in, nodding hard. “Every track. Especially the intro on ‘Eyes, Nose, Lips’ — how does a person even WRITE a melody like that?”

“The scary part is the SALES.” Soo-young already had her phone out, thumb flying. “Six hundred thousand! As a soloist — a ROOKIE soloist — outselling the Super Junior sunbaes? That number shouldn’t exist.”

Seung-wan’s smile curved her whole face; praising him made her happier than being praised herself.

“And I don’t know if it’s the styling or the makeup, but he’s gotten SO much more handsome. His whole aura changed.”

“Right?? Even how he talks now.” Seul-gi struck a pose in imitation — shoulders square, chin steady. “Calm. Confident. Compared to his trainee days it’s a different human being.”

Seung-wan’s eyes went sly. She dropped her voice to conspiracy volume.

“What do you think the sajang-nim’s face looked like when the album passed the Super Junior sunbaes?”

“Like he swallowed a fly~” Soo-young clapped a hand over her own giggle.

“Pfft—” The room broke up. “I would PAY to have seen it.”

The girls chattered on — and not one of them noticed that Joo-hyun’s ears had gone the color of ripe cherries. She wiped nonexistent sweat with great composure while the sweetness churned in her chest, tugging her mouth upward without permission.

The dazzling Ji-ho they’re all talking about spent fifteen minutes on the phone with me last night, saying things I couldn’t repeat out loud if my life depended on it.

“Hey—” Seul-gi’s eyes lit with a new and dangerous idea. “Do you think Ji-ho has a girlfriend?”

The room went silent for exactly one second.

“He… shouldn’t? Right?” Seung-wan recovered first, a thread of fluster in her voice she didn’t hear herself. “There’s been no scandal. And he’s in the middle of promotions — where would he find the TIME to date—”

“But someone that impressive has to have a line of girls after him.” Soo-young was counting on her fingers, merciless with facts. “The face. The talent. The numbers. The personality seems good too— ah, whoever gets to be his girlfriend someday is going to be SO happy!”

Seul-gi clutched her heart with theatrical anguish.

“I don’t even need to be the girlfriend. Just ONE collaboration song~” A beat, then a grin. “Besides, Seung-wan AND eonni both got MVs. I wasn’t exactly on bad terms with him either, hehe.”

Joo-hyun finally allowed herself a small cough, voice arranged into perfect neutrality.

“Practice time.”

But Soo-young was still deep in the topic.

“Eonni — you used to teach him dance, right? Was he like this back then too?”

Every eye in the room swung to Irene.

She smoothed her long hair with total composure, voice steady through sheer discipline.

“He… always worked hard.” A deliberate sideways glance at Seung-wan. “Unlike a certain someone who trained with us and spent half of it plotting snack breaks.”

Seung-wan ducked her head, guilty as charged.

“JUST hard work?” Soo-young’s eyebrow went up, unconvinced. “Lots of people work hard. Not everyone writes ‘Eyes, Nose, Lips.’”

Images flickered through Joo-hyun’s memory — the midnight practice room and a boy grinding through fundamentals without complaint; the monthly evaluation he detonated with one song; the day he looked at the company’s blood-contract and simply walked; and now, this.

“He sees things from angles other people don’t.” She let that stand as the whole answer, then pivoted hard. “NOW we practice. Evaluations are coming.”

The girls scattered reluctantly back to their marks — the commentary continuing at reduced volume.

“But seriously,” Seul-gi murmured to Soo-young, “Ji-ho is a complete ideal type now. The writing, the results, the face— and the BUILD, whew. Perfect-boyfriend spec.”

“Right?? Whichever woman ends up with him—” Soo-young nodded like a woodpecker, stars in her eyes. “I hope she knows how lucky she is.”

Joo-hyun’s ears stood at full attention, and the smile would not leave her face. Every compliment landed like a drop of honey, hers to swallow in secret.

The “future girlfriend” they’re all envying is standing in this room, listening to them praise her boyfriend. This secret is mine. Only mine.

“Eonni, what do you think?” Soo-young rounded on her suddenly. “What would Ji-ho’s ideal type even BE?”

Joo-hyun nearly choked on her own breath. She turned away, face wrestled into stillness.

“How— how would I know that?”

“Guess! Eonni’s so pretty — maybe YOU’RE his type!” Soo-young was only half-joking.

Seul-gi gave Joo-hyun a long, appraising look — and shook her head sadly.

“A shame. A genuine shame.”

Joo-hyun’s eyes narrowed a dangerous half-degree.

“A shame HOW?”

“Eonni’s beautiful, obviously — skin like milk, takes care of everybody.” Seul-gi sighed like a judge delivering a hard verdict. “But Ji-ho’s TALL now. Height-wise… Soo-young’s the better match.”

“Really?” Soo-young lit up instantly and struck a pose, deploying the legs with great ceremony. “So if Ji-ho pursued me… do I say yes? Or do I say YES?”

Seung-wan rolled her eyes so hard it was audible.

“Are you INSANE? Why would Ji-ho ever chase YOU?”

“Ani, why NOT? These legs? This proportion—”

“Okay. Practicing now.” Joo-hyun stood abruptly and marched to the sound system. “Music is going on.”

The music went on. The girls finally practiced.

But Joo-hyun’s mind had drifted somewhere else entirely.

Seul-gi didn’t know — of course she didn’t; between close friends this was ordinary teasing. But he was HER boyfriend, and the height joke had gone in like a splinter and stayed.

I really am too short. He keeps rising and rising… will he start to mind? Will he leave?

Because she loved him, the fear had somewhere to live.

Knock, knock.

She looked up — and the person in the doorway was the one she’d been thinking about for days. The smile that broke across her face was spring arriving early.

Before she could speak, Seung-wan shot past like a startled deer.

“WAH, the big star is here!”

She moved in, arms already reaching to loop his — and he dodged on pure reflex, reversing into a two-finger flick square on her forehead.

“Teasing me now, are we.”

“AH!” She clapped a hand over her brow and deployed maximum wounded-puppy. “Appa~”

“Stop acting.” He pressed her head gently aside and steered around her. “I barely touched you.”

Honestly — my girlfriend is standing right there and you’re grabbing at my arm. Have some decorum. If you must, do it where she can’t see.

“Hehe.” Seung-wan stuck her tongue out, entirely unrepentant, and fell into step behind him like a small bodyguard. “So what brings you to the company today?”

He looked at Joo-hyun as he answered.

“Film casting. I recommended Sulli for the lead.”

“Mwo? Sulli sunbae-nim?” Seung-wan’s mouth fell open.

“Mm.” The words were pointed at one listener in particular. “A big part of how fast I rose came from the attention around Sulli’s incident. So—”

He didn’t finish it. He didn’t need to; the whole room translated it at once — gratitude, repaid — and the collective filter over Ji-ho thickened by another admiring layer. Soo-young, sixty seconds removed from war-gaming how he might pursue her, went quiet as a quail and studied the floor.

Seul-gi, loose and unbothered as ever, stepped up and thumped him on the shoulder.

“Ya. The MVs. You cast eonni, you cast Seung-wan — why not ME? When eonni was teaching you choreography, who was standing next to her doing the demonstrations, huh? That was LABOR.”

He arranged his face into wounded shock.

“You have the nerve to ask? Nuna and Seung-wan don’t greet me with their FISTS.”

“Mwo?” She looked down at her own small fists, genuinely baffled. “THAT’S why I didn’t get an MV?”

“Joking.” He broke first, laughing. “Next time there’s a chance — we’ll work together.”

“You SAID it.” Her eyes went serious and locked on. “I’m remembering this.”

“Of course.”

All four of them, eventually. The whole future lineup — packed up and carried out the door.

“Right — I have somewhere to be. Heading out.”

“Mm, bye-bye~”

He made it two steps, stopped, and turned back.

“Joo-hyun nuna — I need to see Kim Mi-yeon seonsaeng-nim about something. Do you know where she is right now?”

“Seonsaeng-nim? The vocal studio, probably.”

“Just came from there. She’s not in.”

“Ah—” She tapped her own forehead. “She has evaluations today. I’ll take you.”

“Mm. Thank you, nuna.”

“Let’s go.”

They walked the corridor side by side, trainees bowing to him in passing with unconcealed envy. Then the elevator doors closed, and the small box held exactly two people.

“Nuna.” Soft as a hand on a shoulder.

“Mm~” Her voice had gone soft without asking her.

“You look happy today.”

“What do YOU think?” The smile curved her eyes into crescents. “I got to stand there listening to my members praise my boyfriend — and debate his ideal type.”

He leaned close to her ear.

“And did nuna tell them his ideal type is a long-haired girl with a beautiful smile who dances like a spirit — one Irene?”

Her face went up in flame, heart hammering at the base of her throat.

“Nuna, your face is all red. Are you feeling unwell?” The concern was flawlessly acted.

“N-no. It’s warm in here.” She fanned herself in disarray — and stole a look up at him.

Flats today. Eye level: his collarbone. Crown of her head: his chin.

Next to him, everything about me comes up… small.

She hesitated, then asked it quietly.

“Ji-ho. When… when do you rest?”

Something moved in his chest; he understood her completely.

“The company’s in film pre-production. Honestly, I’m barely busy at all.”

“I… I’m off this weekend.” Her head dipped as she said it.

“Perfect. I’ve been wanting to try your samgyetang forever. And — I moved. You can inspect the new place and tell me everything that’s wrong with how I’ve arranged it.”

She nodded, cheeks warm, and the “mm” that escaped her was barely audible over the elevator hum.

The doors opened. She delivered him to Kim Mi-yeon and slipped away.

He greeted the vocal teacher with deep, genuine courtesy. Back at the beginning — before any of it — she had helped him more than she knew: extra coaching, patience, even a part-time job referral. He’d lasted exactly one day at that job. She’d never once held it against him.

Some debts you carry on purpose, because carrying them feels right.
