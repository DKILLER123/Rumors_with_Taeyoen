Chapter Fifty-Two

The Month-End Evaluation

Outside the conference room, Kim Young-min issued his marching orders without breaking stride.

“The moment the SBS piece runs — release the announcement that Ji-ho appears in TaeTiSeo’s ‘Twinkle’ MV. Start the TTS teasers on the same beat. Full promotional push.”

“Understood, sajang-nim.”

An hour later, the interview wrapped. Ji-ho emerged kneading his temples — a first interview meant sixty minutes at maximum vigilance, terrified of every possible misstep, and it had drained him to the socks. Sulli, beside him, was fresh as morning — a veteran swimming in home waters. She fell in at his shoulder, all light steps and concern.

“Oppa, is it evaluation time now?”

“Company slotted me for the afternoon. Right now the plan is cafeteria, then collapse somewhere quiet.”

“I haven’t eaten at the company cafeteria in FOREVER…” A genuine pout assembled itself. “And I’ve got a schedule this afternoon, so I have to leave now. Otherwise I’d keep you company.”

“What is there to miss about that cafeteria? The food is genuinely tragic. Given any choice at all, I would not be eating there.”

She shot him a magnificent eye-roll, cheeks puffing. The POINT was never the food, you enormous blockhead. The point was the company. MY company. Me.

He watched the sulk build — thoroughly enjoying it — and relented before detonation.

“Since the cafeteria’s inedible anyway — and since I’m freshly rich — I clearly owe you a proper meal. A good one.”

Storm to sunshine in a single frame.

“Deal! I’ll be waiting for that feast, oppa~” Then, suddenly, a light tug at his sleeve. He stopped, eyebrows up. Her eyes had gone bright with a fresh scheme. “Also — I’m buying a car next month. But cars are, you know… I don’t know anything about cars. Come with me?”

“Sure.”

“Thank you, oppa!” She released the sleeve, luminous. “Good luck this afternoon — go get a great score. I’m off!”

“Bye.”

“Bye~”

13:30 · April 30 SM Entertainment — Performance Hall the sorting of futures

The month-end evaluation was the beating heart of the Big Three’s trainee machinery — the mechanism that decided who moved toward debut and who moved toward the exit. Two days of it: the lower-priority trainees processed on the 29th, and the ones the company actually watched reserved for the final day. Trainees prepared assessed material around the month’s training focus — assigned vocal pieces, group or solo dance, and, for those who could, original work.

Ji-ho signed in, delivered the “A Boy” arrangement files to the music team, and took his place in the waiting area — men’s side left, women’s right. The room hummed at low, tense voltage; almost nobody chatted. Every spare second went into last adjustments.

Performance order ran by lottery; having missed the draw, he defaulted to the final slot.

Then the judges filed in — and the waiting area’s tension jumped a full octave. The legendary producer Yoo Young-jin. Music education head The One. Veteran vocalist Kangta. And the architect of the entire SM artist system, founder Lee Soo-man himself — flanked by the vocal, dance, and performance faculty.

Lee Soo-man settled into his seat and gave the emcee a small nod.

“Number 15 to the stage. Number 16, stand by.”

Four trainees mounted the stage together from the men’s side, bowed deep, and introduced themselves in turn:

“Hello, judges — I’m Mark Lee.”

“Seo Young-ho.”

“Nakamoto Yuta.”

“Lee Tae-yong.”

“Today we’re performing Super Junior sunbae-nims’ ‘Mr. Simple.’”

“Mr. Simple” — Yoo Young-jin’s own production, a full-throttle dance track that punished anything less than complete command. Choosing it announced ambition; they’d clearly drilled it for weeks. The execution, though, landed at merely adequate. The judges dissected the gaps with professional thoroughness and held all four at their current ranks.

Sixteen. Seventeen. Eighteen. The line moved.

“Number 19 to the stage. Number 20, stand by.”

“Hello, judges — I’m Irene.”

“Seulgi.”

“Today we’re performing f(x) sunbae-nims’ ‘LA chA TA.’”

Familiar names — Ji-ho’s attention snapped fully to the stage.

“LA chA TA,” f(x)’s debut single: hip-hop drums fused to pure pop momentum, wild and free-swinging, merciless on body control. It demanded clean edges, real power, real speed — and these two delivered. Irene had built her legend on exactly this: crisp, controlled, near-perfect marks at every internal evaluation across five trainee years, with peer reviews that hovered around full points. And Seulgi took first place in SM’s monthlies often enough that the trainee floors had nicknamed her “little Taeyeon.”

A-rank trainees rarely performed in pairs — but Seul-gi was fighting through vocal nodules just now, mid-pivot toward dance, her singing rationed to protect the cords. The duet was the workaround. And even in her hardest season, two trainees of this caliber together lit the stage end to end. The judges’ verdict came collective and unhesitating: straight A’s, both.

Joo-hyun came off the stage glowing — and the instant her eyes found Ji-ho’s, her brows arched into open, delighted provocation.

Hmph. It took ME two years of bleeding to touch A-rank the first time. You’ve trained one month and you think you’re jumping to A? Delusional. Utterly delusional. Now — what SHOULD I make him do when he loses…
