Chapter Two Hundred Sixty-Four

Kill Them

Late That Night Bangbae-dong · IU’s Villa one drunk chairman, one empty bed

Seocho-gu. Bangbae-dong.

Song Ji-ho stepped out of the car and the night wind took him directly in the face, multiplying the spin behind his eyes by a factor he could not quite count.

He swayed. Ryu Dong-hwan caught him.

“Chairman — are you all right?”

“Fine.” A wave of the hand. “Head back. Send someone in the morning.”

“Yes, Chairman.”

Dong-hwan walked him to the door and held position — watching him enter the code, watching him find his balance on the threshold — before he turned and left.

The villa was dark. No lights on anywhere.

“Ji-eun?”

“Lee Ji-eun?”

Nothing.

He changed into slippers, hit the kitchen light, poured a glass of ice water and drank it standing. The cold ran from throat to stomach like a reset button, and some fraction of himself came back online.

Not home yet.

He killed the light, went through the bathroom motions, shed his clothes where they fell, and slid naked into the bed.

The duvet smelled like her — that faint, sweet, particular warmth Lee Ji-eun carried on her skin. He burrowed into it and went down fast.

◆ ◆ ◆

“Ya! We said tonight, we keep drinking!”

“We absolutely keep drinking!”

“Celebrating our Peach becoming a million-viewer movie lead!”

“Wuhuuu!”

Four girls, arms linked, the whole cocktail of them pouring through the villa’s front door.

Park Ji-yeon. Goo Ha-ra. Choi Jin-ri. Lee Ji-eun.

Ji-eun, in the lead, pushed through the door first.

She paused.

Her eyes landed on a pair of men’s leather shoes in the shoe cabinet. Song Ji-ho’s shoes. Hadn’t she tucked those inside the cabinet already?

…Whatever.

She shook her swimming head, kicked the shoes further into the cabinet with one foot, and wobbled onward.

“Keep drinking!”

The living room came alive fast. Four girls arranged themselves across the sofa like a selection of exotic birds, soju bottles lined up on the coffee table beside a scattered archipelago of snacks.

“Cheers!”

“Peach-ah, you’re a big deal now.”

“What big deal. I’m still me.”

“Not the same, not the same — million-viewer movie lead. Say it out loud, how good does that sound?”

“That’s oppa writing well, the director shooting well, I just got lucky—”

“Luck IS talent!”

Goo Ha-ra leaned back, cheeks carrying the warm flag of the drink, her eyes bent into crescents. She’d worn a pale slip top tonight, collarbones clean and exposed, dark hair loose over her shoulders, a few strands pasted to her neck by the summer heat.

Choi Jin-ri was nested at the other end, laughing at nothing in particular, teeth on full display — her skin so pale it seemed to generate its own light in the dim lamp.

Lee Ji-eun sat cross-legged on the rug, head dipping, dipping, one more nod from unconscious.

Park Ji-yeon held her glass high, face thoroughly pink, eyes glittering — drink or joy, impossible to tell which, probably both.

“Another round!”

“Drink!”

◆ ◆ ◆

Time passed, or stopped pretending to.

Goo Ha-ra’s stomach went first.

She clapped a hand over her mouth and bolted. The bathroom door slammed. The sounds that followed were not subtle.

“Huuurgh—”

When it was done she hauled herself up at the sink, ran cold water, splashed her face, rinsed. The mirror returned a flushed, unfocused version of herself — hair wet at the temples, the particular carnage of a night that had gone slightly sideways.

She shook her head, still swimming, and walked out.

In the living room, the other three were still going. She didn’t have the fuel.

Lie down. Five minutes.

She knew where Ji-eun’s bedroom was. She’d been here plenty of times. Up the stairs, down the hall, the master bedroom door.

She pushed it open. Dark inside.

Fumbled her way to the bed, pulled back the duvet, and climbed in.

Soft. So soft.

She curled into a ball. Just as sleep was taking her, she noticed — a warm heat source, right there, next to her.

Warm. Good.

She shifted toward it, pressed against it.

Still a little cold. She pressed closer.

Mm. Warm now. A little too warm.

In the automatic, unreasoning way of the very drunk, she squirmed out of her clothes — kicked them off the side of the bed — and in her underwear, wrapped herself around the heat source, and went under.

◆ ◆ ◆

Downstairs, the remaining three soldiered on.

“Where’s Ha-ra-unnie?”

“Bathroom, probably?”

“That was a while ago—”

“Forget her. Come on, drink!”

More time. More bottles.

Lee Ji-eun went down first — face-planted into the sofa and stopped moving.

Park Ji-yeon keeled sideways against a cushion and began the even breathing of the fully departed.

Choi Jin-ri curled up on the rug with a throw pillow, sleeping the clean sleep of someone whose conscience has never once been late.

The living room went quiet. Only breathing.

Small Hours The Master Bedroom one heat source, one wrong person

Song Ji-ho woke because his body had an opinion about his bladder and the hour.

He fumbled the bedside lamp on, squinted his way to the bathroom, handled it. Coming back, he glanced at the bed: a small shape curled under the duvet, and the air carried a faint edge of alcohol.

Ji-eun was drinking tonight?

He didn’t think further. Killed the lamp. Slid back in.

The movement woke the person beside him.

Goo Ha-ra, half-asleep and wholly unmoored, registered only that the heat source had returned. She pressed toward it. Her hand groped, encountered — smooth, warm skin. Taut. Flat. Ridged.

Mm… is this abs?

It felt real. Too real for a dream. She stroked it, drowsily, once or twice.

Her wrist was caught.

And then her mouth was covered.

“Mm—”

It hit her like a current — the whole length of her body went liquid. She’d been single for over half a year. The kiss was real, and it was hot, and she had no defenses armed for either.

A few seconds passed.

Then: wait.

Goo Ha-ra’s eyes flew open.

In the low lamplight, the face above hers resolved into focus.

Song Ji-ho.

He was seeing her at the same moment.

Goo Ha-ra.

Both of them went rigid simultaneously.

It was already too late for one of those recencies to matter.

“YA!”

Her eyes went enormous with fury. She shoved at his chest with both hands.

“You—!! YOU—!!”

“Ha-ra?” He was still rebooting. “How are you—what are you doing here?”

“THAT’S what I’m asking YOU!” She dropped her voice, caught between fury and panic. “Why are you in Ji-eun’s bed?!”

“Ji-eun is my woman.” He looked at her steadily. “Keep your voice down. Where is she?”

A tremor ran through Goo Ha-ra.

Ji-eun… Peach… Ji-yeon… they’re all in the living room.

“They’re— they’re out there—” Her voice shook. “You— get off me first!”

He tried to lever himself up.

She went completely soft, all at once.

“Don’t— don’t move!”

Song Ji-ho: …

Several seconds of mutual paralysis.

“How did you end up in here?”

“I was drunk… I’ve slept in Ji-eun’s bed before, I always—” She was near tears. “Just— think of something!”

“Think of what?”

“How should I know?!”

They stared at each other.

“Ha-ra.” He drew a breath. “It’s done. Let’s be calm.”

“How am I supposed to be calm!” The rims of her eyes went red. “You expect me to be calm like this—”

“Then scream.” He looked at her. “Wake them all. Let them come in and see.”

She froze.

She couldn’t. How could she?

“You… you bastard…” The words shook. Tears sat in her eyes, refusing to fall.

He looked at her, and something in his chest gave a small sigh.

Goo Ha-ra. Lee Ji-eun’s best friend — part of the four-girl inner circle that also included Choi Jin-ri and Park Ji-yeon.

Peach had told him once: Ha-ra’s parents divorced when she was small. Her mother walked out and never came back. Her grandmother raised her and her brother alone.

The grandmother was her world.

“Don’t cry.” He lowered his voice. “Let me ask you something. Has Ji-eun ever told you about us — about our relationship?”

She sniffed. Said nothing.

“What about Jin-ri? Ji-yeon?”

She went still.

Jin-ri and Kim Tae-yeon — she already knew Song Ji-ho was… not a one-woman man.

“They… all know?” Voice shaking.

“All of them know.” He held her gaze. “And all of them chose to stay.”

Goo Ha-ra’s brain stalled.

This was not the structure she’d imagined. She’d thought Song Ji-ho was Kim Tae-yeon’s boyfriend. Choi Jin-ri’s boyfriend. A philanderer, yes — but their philanderer, in the ordinary, explicable way.

And the closeness between him and Peach — she’d envied that. Genuinely envied it.

And now Lee Ji-eun was one of them. And Peach and Jin-ri knew, and they stayed.

“What… what kind of arrangement is this—”

“Messy.” He didn’t flinch from it. “They all know. They all accept. I’ve never lied to any of them.”

She was quiet.

She thought about the kiss. The way the current had gone through her whole body. She hadn’t felt that in a long time. She’d thought she’d forgotten the feeling. But for one full second, she’d gone completely soft.

“Ha-ra.” He spoke again. “You’ve always wanted a family, haven’t you?”

Her whole body jolted.

“Ji-eun told me. Your grandmother raised you. What you want most in the world is people who stay.” He held her gaze. “I can give you that.”

“What are you— what are you talking about…” She turned her face away. “This was an accident—”

“An accident is still a kind of fate.” He said it without irony. “And I don’t dislike you. And I don’t think you dislike me.”

She said nothing.

She didn’t dislike him. Song Ji-ho. Who would? The face, the talent, the way he treated people—

“I’m confident I can make you fall for me.” Quietly. “Feelings can grow.”

She bit her lip.

“I’ll go with you to see your grandmother.”

Her head snapped toward him.

“What?”

“I’ll go with you to meet your grandmother.” He repeated it. “The person who matters most to you. I’ll go.”

She stared.

Grandmother. The center of her world. The person who’d raised her from a child, who she wanted, more than anything, to be proud of her.

Grandmother always said, “You just need to be well. I don’t need anything.”

If she brought Song Ji-ho home—

Last time she’d watched his stage performance with her grandmother, the old woman had said: “That boy is so handsome. Whoever marries him is lucky.”

The face her grandmother would make, seeing him at the door—

It would split open with joy.

“You’re… serious?” Her voice trembled.

“Today. Right now, if you want.” He said it levelly. “It was an accident, but I’ll take responsibility. That much I have.”

She looked at him. In the low light, his eyes were bright, and serious.

Her heart dropped a beat.

This is wrong. This is all wrong.

But it’s already happened. And Ji-eun, Jin-ri, Ji-yeon — they all know, they all stay…

One more. What’s one more?

And the kiss. The way her whole body had dissolved.

She hadn’t felt that in such a long, long time.

“Should I… continue?” Low.

She didn’t answer. She turned her face away and closed her eyes. Her lashes trembled. The flush in her cheeks hadn’t faded — alcohol, or something else, impossible to separate.

He bent and kissed her.

This time, she didn’t resist.

Morning IU’s Villa · Living Room the discovery

Sunlight found the gap in the curtains and fell across the floor like a thin gold blade.

Lee Ji-eun surfaced in stages. Face-down on the sofa, neck declaring formal war on the rest of her body. She rubbed it, sat up, and took inventory.

Park Ji-yeon: tilted against the far armrest, asleep, breathing steady.

Choi Jin-ri: on the rug, wrapped around a throw pillow, a small line of drool at the corner of her mouth.

Ji-eun almost laughed.

She stood, stretched the stiffness out, and then — a thought. The shoes in the cabinet last night. Song Ji-ho’s shoes.

He’d come back?

She went up the stairs on quiet feet and pushed open the master bedroom door.

And stopped.

The bed: Song Ji-ho, center, duvet at his waist, torso bare. To his right, curled tight: Goo Ha-ra.

Both of them, deeply asleep.

Lee Ji-eun: …

She drew one long breath through her nose. Closed the door — gently, without a sound.

Went downstairs. Poured a glass of ice water. Drained it. Sat on the sofa. Stared at the ceiling.

Choi Jin-ri woke, rubbing her eyes.

“What’s wrong?”

Ji-eun pointed at the ceiling.

Jin-ri blinked. Went upstairs. Came back. Her expression was… complicated.

They looked at each other.

“So…” Jin-ri, barely audible. “Ha-ra-unnie too?”

“Appears so.”

“When did he get here?”

“Before we came home, I think—”

“Then how did she end up up there?”

“How should I know?”

“She probably mistook the bed for yours.”

“Aish!”

Two seconds of silence.

“Are you angry?” Jin-ri asked.

Ji-eun considered.

She shook her head.

“Not angry?”

“A little… blank.” Honestly. “I don’t know if I should be angry.”

Jin-ri nodded, then tilted her head.

“So what now?”

Ji-eun looked at her.

“What do you think we should do?”

Choi Jin-ri’s face went hard.

“Kill them.”
