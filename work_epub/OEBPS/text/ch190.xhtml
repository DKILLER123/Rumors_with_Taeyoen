Chapter One Hundred Ninety

The Cat’s Burning Curiosity

May 19, 2013 · afternoon → night LOEN studio → a hanwoo house in Gangnam the LOEN endgame takes shape — and one ice princess catches fire

On the system’s screen, a line of half-transparent text only he could see unfolded quietly:

DREAM LIFE — STATUS · CHARM
CHARM69

Face 9 · Physique 8 · Voice 9 · Presence 6 · Experience 6 · Communication 6 · Confidence 9 · Learning 7 · Wealth 2 (UNDISCLOSED — currently hidden; +8 modifier applies only to informed parties) · Fame 7

UNDISCLOSED.

He stared at the word. So the system priced charm not merely on what a man had — but on who KNEW he had it. The 461.5 billion won carried a wealth modifier of +8… payable only in the eyes of people holding the secret. To Taeyeon, to Tiffany, to everyone still outside the vault door, his wealth read as a 2 — and the composite sat pinned at 69.

One point from 70. One point from whatever the system had promised at the threshold. A new skill? Higher clearance? Or—

He pulled his thoughts back to the money itself and confirmed the number one more time. ₩461.5 billion. At the current rate, comfortably north of $400 million — and in the 2013 Korean entertainment industry, a sum capable of moving tectonic plates.

Buying LOEN? More than enough.

The target had never been sentimental. LOEN’s crown asset wasn’t Ji-eun, and it wasn’t the production division — it was Melon: the peninsula’s largest music platform, holding better than half the market, functionally the tollgate of digital music itself. SM’s top idols, bedroom independents — everyone released through Melon, and Melon took its cut of every download and every stream. A cash cow with no off-season. The industry’s water main.

Founding another entertainment company meant nothing. Channels meant everything. Hold Melon, and half the Korean music industry routed its bloodstream through your hands.

He’d spent weeks mapping the terrain, and the door stood exactly where his first life remembered it.

STRATEGIC FILE

compiled from public filings and prior-life memory · May 2013

RULINGFair Trade Commission ultimatum under the Monopoly Regulation and Fair Trade Act: acquire LOEN to 100% — or cut holdings below 20%. Roughly two years’ grace, expiring 2013.

BUDGETHis entry may inflate the price. ₩300 billion should close it — comfortably inside the war chest.

And after LOEN — the company of his own. Channel in hand: artist development, production, distribution, promotion, one unbroken chain. Whether LOEN was eventually resold or run forever, every road from there ran uphill in his favor.

And then there was Ji-eun. The girl tangled into everything.

The smile that crossed his face at that point had something distinctly entertained in it. Little miss. Do try to stay on my good side. Ji-eun-ah — you wouldn’t want to end up shelved by your own company, would you? Heh—

“Ji-ho-ya. Ji-ho-ya.” An elbow arrived in his ribs, and a deeply suspicious face with it. “What are you thinking about? Your expression just now was — genuinely deviant.”

“Ahem. Nothing.” He reassembled himself into a producer. “Back to the guide track. Tiffany nuna — the chorus can carry more. Fill it with that contradiction: desperate to be loved, terrified of being hurt.”

The direction landed somewhere it shouldn’t have. I DO want to love, properly, all the way through, Tiffany thought at the mic. And I’m afraid of exactly everything after that — hurting me, hurting Taeyeon, losing him AND her in one catastrophic move.

Taeyeon found her boyfriend faintly odd today and decided not to pursue it. In front of IU, he’d put his arm around her shoulders — publicly, deliberately. The stone that had been sitting on her chest since the corridor had dissolved on the spot.

Only Jessica remained in orbit, unable to land. She knew exactly what he’d been grinning about. 461.5 billion! Enough to buy GIRLS’ GENERATION outright!

Whether it was the aftershock of all those zeros or something less numerical, she spent most of the afternoon looking at him and forgetting to stop. The old complaints — acid-tongued, arrogant, professionally meddlesome — had been packed up and shipped to some offshore archive. What remained wore a thick, warm filter.

He really is handsome, the filter reported. Not idol-pretty — something with more grain to it. The brows; the clean jaw; the small roll of the throat when he spoke; the two-millimeter frown of total focus. All of it, suddenly, watchable.

And the temperament — so steady. So patient. Taeyeon’s English vowels kept drifting; he corrected the same syllable eleven times with zero visible wear. Tiffany lost the thread of a phrase’s feeling; he walked her through the exact scene in the film until it clicked. He taught them diaphragm support, long-note stability, seamless register turns — working tools, real ones, that a decade in the industry had somehow never handed them.

And the songs. She’d replayed both demos more times than she’d admit. One a year would make a producer’s career. He seemed to shake them out of his sleeves.

How did I never notice any of this before?

She sat with her chin propped, following him around the console with her eyes. The window light put a full drama-lead rim glow on him as he leaned to adjust a fader, shirt sleeves rolled, forearms in evidence.

Her heart missed a step.

So this is what they mean by — attraction at first billion? Love at first fund?

✦   ✦   ✦

The day’s work closed at five.

Tiffany had “Love Me Like You Do” nearly in hand — one more day of practice and she’d record for real. Native English did its work: pronunciation flawless, feeling arriving without translation, adjustments minimal. Taeyeon, meanwhile, remained locked in single combat with the English of “Shallow” — certain syllables simply refused Korean-born mouths — but her notebook had filled edge to edge with phonetic marks and battle plans.

Clap. Clap. Clap.

“Okay, okay — nunas, that’s today.” His voice had gone pleasantly hoarse from an afternoon of talking. “Genuinely hard work. Thank you.”

“Ne — you worked hard too.”

Three voices, one chord.

“Come on. Dinner’s on me.” He reached for his jacket. “What are we eating?”

“BEEF!” Taeyeon’s hand was up before the sentence ended. “Can we? Tour’s coming — I have to manage my body — beef is PERFECT for that!”

“Beef it must be, then. I know a hanwoo house — the quality’s serious.”

“Wah, DAEBAK!” Tiffany’s eyes lit. “If YOU’RE paying, I’m not holding back tonight!”

“Today did burn a lot,” Jessica said, smiling. “Proper replenishment is medically indicated.”

The four of them left together, laughing down the elevator — and made no particular effort to dodge the fans at the doors. Ji-ho at dinner with three of Girls’ Generation was, itself, the cheapest and best film marketing on the market.

Sure enough: phones came up on the sidewalk.

“It’s SONG JI-HO!”

“And Taeyeon! Tiffany! JESSICA!”

“What IS this lineup, oh my god—”

Flashes stuttered. He drifted, naturally, to the outside of the three women, angling his body between them and the most enthusiastic lenses. Taeyeon and Tiffany waved and smiled; Jessica held her trademark cool — with, tonight, a soft curve at the mouth that the fan photos would argue about for days.

✦   ✦   ✦

The hanwoo house hid in a Gangnam side alley, traditional and quiet. A private room; the top-grade set menu. They’d barely sat down when Tiffany yelped at her phone.

“Wah — we’re TRENDING!”

She turned the screen: a photo of the four of them leaving the LOEN building, angle immaculate — him walking shield-side, Taeyeon on Tiffany’s arm mid-laugh, Jessica half a step back, cool-eyed and warm-mouthed.

SNS · spotted at LOEN

#1 What IS this dream crossover?!

#2 Ji-ho writing for Girls’ Generation again?? “I Got a Boy 2.0” WHEN?

#3 Is nobody going to mention how he positions himself between them and the cameras? Because I noticed. I noticed a lot.

#4 Jessica is GLOWING today, what happened

#5 It’s the film OST, people — The Girl in the Sun is about to drop. He wrote the theme AND the OST for Tiffany and Taeyeon. Our Taeyeon’s beloved hoobae does it again!!

#6 Anticipating-ing. Hard.

Taeyeon leaned in, read the thread, and laughed until her eyes vanished. “Well — there’s the marketing budget saved.”

“Mm.” He filled their cups with barley tea. “Here — drink first. The meat’s coming.”

On cue, the cart arrived: marbled hanwoo laid out on gleaming brass like geology made edible, fat threading the red in fine even veins. Banchan crowded in after — kimchi in three tempers, salads, mushrooms, tteok, and a stone pot of doenjang-guk breathing steam.

“WAH!” — three voices, involuntary, in unison.

He took up the tongs and worked the grill. Fat hit charcoal; the hiss and the smell arrived together, and the room’s collective blood sugar reorganized around it.

“First piece — for the hardworking Taeyeon nuna.”

“Hmph. SOME conscience, at least.” The smirk sat at maximum altitude.

“Second — for the suffering Tiffany nuna.”

“Thank you~” An eye-smile at full wattage.

“Third—” He looked over at Jessica.

Her heartbeat, for no reason she was prepared to certify, picked up.

“—for Sica nuna, who kept us company all afternoon and never once complained.” The piece landed on her plate. “Thank you for that.”

“…Thanks.”

She said it quietly, looking down at the cut — charred outside, blush-pink at the heart, timed to the second. She dipped it in salt and ate. Tender; the fat unfolding across the tongue; the specific deep sweetness that only top-grade hanwoo delivered.

Delicious. And it tasted, she noticed, of something more than beef — of being looked after in small, precise ways. And of — she was honest with herself, she’d always been honest with herself — four hundred sixty-one point five billion won of gravitational pull.

✦   ✦   ✦

The dinner ran nearly two hours — set stories, tour prep, industry gossip, futures.

“Ji-ho-ya.” Tiffany had a little soju color in her cheeks now. “What’s your long-term plan? Staying here to build?”

Taeyeon’s chest clenched; her head came up fast.

His tongs paused mid-turn — then resumed. “That’s the plan. I want to open my own entertainment company.”

Taeyeon exhaled.

“Like a one-man studio?” Tiffany tilted her head. “Wouldn’t it be safer to stay under a big roof?”

“Small companies can grow.” He kept it light. “But that’s all later. First — the work in front of us.”

Jessica said nothing, listening. Small company. With 461.5 billion. Right. She watched him deflect and understood something else, too: an ordinary person handed that number would have been vibrating with it — announcing it, wearing it. He carried it like pocket change. As if the money were merely a tool that had arrived on schedule.

Maybe that’s what a real genius looks like when you finally see one at the right angle.

And she — SHE — was the one holding the secret. Even Taeyeon didn’t know. The feeling was strange and specific: like being handed the master code to something enormous, with no instructions about whether, or how, to use it.

“It’s getting late.” He checked the time. “Practice tomorrow. Shall I drive you back?”

“No no—” Tiffany waved. “Manager eonni’s picking us up. Taeyeon?”

“Ji-ho is taking me.” Her arm was already threaded through his, ownership casually absolute.

Jessica watched the two of them — the ease, the fit — and something layered moved through her chest. Envy? A little. Something less comfortable than envy? Also a little. But more than either, and stranger than both: momentum.

I want to understand this man. I want to see what 461.5 billion becomes. I want to know how he means to build — and learn it. Talk to him, properly, founder to founder. And if he saw merit in my ideas — if he chose to invest, or even to BUILD it with—

“Sica?” Tiffany tapped her shoulder. “You’ve drifted off again.”

“Ah.” She rose, gathering her bag, cool restored in one motion. “It’s nothing. — Ji-ho. Taeyeon. See you tomorrow.”

“Tomorrow.” He nodded.

✦   ✦   ✦

Outside, Seoul burned its usual ten million lights. The van collected them; LOEN’s garage swallowed the couple’s car; waves were exchanged through windows.

“Sica, Fany — safe ride home.”

“Mm. You two as well.”

Jessica watched him and Taeyeon walk off toward the garage, arm in arm, until her phone buzzed against her palm.

Messages · Hyo-yeon

“Sica-yah, I’ve set it with Jun-hyung’s friend — this Friday evening. Good?”

“Sorry, Hyo-yeon. This Friday I don’t have time.”

“Also — that friend. No need to introduce him after all. Thank you, though.”

Send.

She raised her head and drew one long breath of the spring night.

Her mind held exactly one thought, in two parts.

461.5 billion won. And — Ji-ho.

How on earth did he DO it?

Somewhere behind her sternum, a cat’s curiosity — the famous kind, the fatal kind — had caught fire, and was burning brighter by the minute.
