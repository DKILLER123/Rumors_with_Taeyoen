Ripples

July 19, 2012 SM Entertainment — Director’s Office the one that got away

Lee Soo-man watched “Some” complete its All-Kill across every board, and his feelings arrived thoroughly mixed.

My eye wasn’t wrong. The boy manufactured a miracle on schedule.

He rose, walked to the window, looked out over the towers, and sighed.

Ji-ho’s departure, he understood now, had been structural. Inevitable.

Field Notes

The founder’s postmortem, at the window: it was the Park Jin-young problem all over again — you could joke forever about having turned him away for his face, but could SM ever have KEPT a Park Jin-young? Impossible. People with both firepower and ambition do not stay beneath someone else’s hand. Where a company succeeds is precisely where it will fail.

SM had won by industrializing stardom itself — TVXQ, Super Junior, Girls’ Generation, and now EXO, a repeatable production line for cultural product. It had made INNOVATION itself a process. But a process cannot metabolize genuine, disruptive artistic personality. The Park Jin-youngs, the G-Dragons — those are founders and artists, not executors. They need platforms, resources, and freedom, not instructions. When the system can’t flex, leaving becomes arithmetic. And the logic would keep collecting names for years — the singers with real engines would keep walking.

Add the second law of the house: THE GROUP ABOVE ALL. It kept the brands immortal and the revenue maximal — and it guaranteed collision with any member whose center of gravity drifted toward the individual. That was the whole grammar of Jessica’s eventual fate, or anyone’s: tilt your career personal, cost the team its schedule, bring nothing to trade — and the company amputates, however painful the limb.

He had lost Ji-ho at the exact junction where the machine always loses that kind of man. A pity, he thought. Not a regret. The machine, after all, was the point.

Bae Soo-bin’s knee had healed completely; classes and stage-workshop rehearsals had reclaimed her days. Ji-ho was busy now — sightings of her neighbor had grown rare.

Music, then.

She put the chart on shuffle-by-rank, and the #1 song opened on a low, magnetic male voice — singing, with wry tenderness, about the restlessness that comes and goes while the feeling underneath refuses to move.

…That voice. That’s — Ji-ho?

Track: Some. Written and composed by: Ji-ho. Performed by: Ji-ho, IU.

The song’s beautiful. He blew up. That’s wonderful. Congratulations, Ji-ho. …So why does something hurt?

He was like a dream that wandered into MY dream — and wandered right back out.

Soo-bin-ah. From here on, you and Ji-ho live in two different worlds.

She let the crush fold itself quietly away into the wind, and turned her eyes back to her own road.

…Although. If I became an actress — a real one — there’d be sets, someday. Maybe even one with him on it.

Somehow, the acting dream had just acquired horsepower.

Joo-hyun removed her earphones. She’d played “Some” more times than she was prepared to disclose.

That rich, warm tone, narrating instead of performing. Breath rock-steady, runs like silk, vibrato fine-grained, dynamics fully in hand — the vocal is simply UNFAIR. Hooks that don’t let go; lyrics that install themselves in your own memories. Then IU’s storytelling instrument and her fanbase on top. And the man himself — twice a nationwide story before ever releasing a note: once beloved, once burned at the stake. A nobody who swept the entire internet twice in three months. No. His success was never in question.

She looked at the vibrating Seung-wan, and said, out loud and against her own peace of mind:

“Seung-wan-ah. When Ji-ho said he’d open a company before you debut… I don’t think that was a joke anymore.”

“So you’d better go practice. Unless you’d like him to be a CEO while you’re still a trainee. Imagine the embarrassment.”

Seung-wan’s breath snagged. A very small mutter escaped.

“…Eonni’s in the same boat, though.”

“WHAT was that?” The elegant brows inverted. “You and I are NOT the same. My evaluations are straight A’s. You’ve had three monthlies and no A yet.”

“I am DEFINITELY debuting before Ji-ho opens any company!”

Seung-wan produced a sheepish grin and a tiny pinch of air between two fingers.

“Eonni, last monthly I was THIS close~”

Joo-hyun’s finger arrived on her forehead with a tap.

“Always ‘this close.’ WHY always ‘this close’? Because practice gets ‘close enough’ — so the real thing lands ‘just short.’”

“Move. Tonight we practice one extra hour.”

“Aaah~ noooo, eonni~”
