Chapter Two Hundred Six

Sulli — Forever Free, Forever Burning, Forever Bright as the Morning Sun

June 5, 2013 · dawn to morning The villa → a mountain road with the top down a midnight migration, two witnesses at the sofa — and one red convertible in the morning light

Taeyeon had gone several glasses past her limit last night; the mattress had claimed her on contact.

Deep in the night, an insistent bodily summons hauled her up out of the murk. She surfaced blinking, head thick and pounding, and lurched upright on legs that reported the floor as cotton. The bathroom was located by sonar; the errand completed; and at the sink, the splash of cold water on her hands bought back a little clarity — though the small hammer inside her skull kept its rhythm.

It was on the walk back that she noticed: the shape beside her bed wasn’t him. It was Tiffany.

She stood swaying a moment — and thought, with the flawless logic of the drunk and aching, of how it felt to sleep inside his arms. Warmth. Slack. Zero hammers.

No further deliberation occurred. She killed the light, padded out on soundless feet, and found him in the living room, cocooned in a blanket on the sofa, deeply asleep.

She lifted the blanket’s edge and inserted herself into his arms with a burglar’s care.

“…What is it?” — thick, automatic, eyes still shut, arms already closing around her.

“Head hurts. Want to be with you.”

“Okay. Sleep.”

A hand found her hair in the dark and smoothed it once. Inside the warmth of him, the knot between her brows loosened by degrees, and she went under again.

The Boyfriend Halo hummed on, quietly, dissolving the ache.

✦   ✦   ✦

Morning light came through the curtain seams and lay gently across the living room.

Yoon-a woke first — she always did. Washed, dressed, in search of water, she rounded the corner into the living room and stopped.

On the sofa: two people folded into one blanket. Taeyeon curled entirely inside his arms, sleeping like a fed cat; his arm around her, chin resting lightly on her hair; the whole composition so quiet and so complete it made intrusion feel like vandalism.

She leaned against the wall and watched, and something unexpected rose in her chest.

Envy.

Taeyeon had gone to sleep in the master bedroom last night. By morning she’d migrated into his arms — couldn’t stand even one night’s distance. Eonni… you love him THAT much? She had seen so much performance, so much arithmetic dressed as affection — and this simple picture, two people breathing in sync under one blanket, struck her as suddenly, absurdly precious.

She was still staring when soft footsteps arrived behind her.

Tiffany had woken to a cold, empty half of the bed. She came out rubbing her eyes — and found Yoon-a planted at the living-room threshold.

“Yoon-a?” She padded closer, curious. “What are you looking a—”

The sentence died. She saw the sofa too.

And went completely rigid.

Last night’s memory arrived first — heat climbing her face without permission. And then the stronger thing rolled over it: guilt, and grief, and a bitterness that netted around her ribs and pulled tight. She watched Taeyeon sleep — utterly safe, utterly certain, in the exact place Tiffany had been the night before — and her nails found her palm again without being asked.

Yoon-a caught the change in her peripheral vision, and her stomach dipped.

So there IS something. Fany eonni and Ji-ho — there’s something.

She wet her lips and pitched her voice as light as it would go.

“Taeyeon eonni sleeps so soundly, doesn’t she… I guess it’s true — nowhere’s as safe as your own boyfriend’s arms.”

The sentence went in like a pin. Something contracted at the bottom of Tiffany’s pupils.

She dropped her eyes fast, buried whatever had surfaced, and came back up wearing a smile that cost more than it showed, voice a little dry.

“Yeah… she’s always been that way. When she doesn’t feel well, she gets clingy.” A beat — and then, as if to prove something to the room, briskly: “We should wake her. We need to get back early — the whole team’s walking the venue today.”

She crossed to the sofa and rocked Taeyeon’s shoulder gently. “Taeyeon. Time to get up.”

Taeyeon stirred in his arms, eyes opening slow and fogged. She registered Tiffany and Yoon-a, blanked for a second — then remembered her midnight migration and produced a small, sheepish smile.

She extracted herself with exaggerated care, one limb at a time, and didn’t wake him.

Upright, she paused — and blinked. Clear-headed. No hammer. No fog. Better than she’d felt in weeks.

“I knew it. You’re my fate.”

She bent and pressed a kiss to his sleeping forehead.

The house woke in stages after that — Yuri and Sunny extracted from their room, faces reassembled, bags gathered. On her way through the living room, Sunny’s gaze crossed the sleeping figure on the sofa once — complicated, quickly withdrawn — and she packed in silence.

A line of quiet young women let themselves out of the villa into the morning.

✦   ✦   ✦

When he woke, sunlight owned half the living room. The space beside him was empty; the blanket still held a faint ghost of her scent. The house was so quiet the whole night might have been a dream.

He sat up, rubbed his temple. Outside, the morning was bright and new.

While he washed up, Ryu Dong-hwan arrived with the season’s Gucci. He changed into comfortable casuals — the salon first, then the afternoon’s press conference for The Girl in the Sun.

The two of them had barely cleared the front door when a low engine note rolled up the hill and a bright-red BMW convertible braked crisply at the gate.

The door swung up; Sulli hopped down light as a cat, smile at full wattage.

Morning light was being kind to no one else this generously: precise makeup over that glass-clear skin; natural black hair, long and straight under airy see-through bangs — cool and sweet at once. A loose, big red baby-doll dress against snow-pale skin, the roomy cut trading curves for a girl’s playful languor. One hundred seventy centimeters of tall, elegant, current-season sunlight, parked in front of his house.

“Oppa!”

She skipped to his side and took his arm as if the arm had been issued to her, then — manners intact — bobbed a bow at the manager, smile sweet.

“Annyeonghaseyo, Dong-hwan Oppa.”

“Sulli-ssi, good morning.” A glance slid to his artist, loaded with mild inquiry.

“Dong-hwan Oppa, you don’t need to wait for him~” she preempted, breezy. “Oppa’s riding with me!”

Another glance. He surrendered with a small shrug at the koala attached to his arm. “Sulli-yah. I have to go get styled first.”

She tilted her head and audited him carefully, eyes bright. “Oppa needs NO makeup. In my eyes you’re already the most handsome man in the world, effective immediately.”

“Hahaha!” He laughed outright, shook his head, and waved at his manager. “Hyung — go ahead to the salon. I’ll ride with Sulli.”

“Got it.” Dong-hwan, a man fluent in when not to exist, was already walking. “Sulli-ssi. See you.”

“Drive safe, Dong-hwan Oppa~”

She waved until the car disappeared down the hill — and did not release the arm.

“You plan to hold on like that forever? Not worried someone photographs it and posts it?”

“Really?? That would be AMAZING!” Far from letting go, she perked up and began actively scanning the treeline for lenses. “My fans WANT me dating Oppa!”

“Well, I’M scared.”

“Aigoo. Oppa is USELESS.” She curled her lip and administered a disdainful glare — while the arm-grip tightened. “Then I’m still not letting go. I’m the little sister right now. A sister may hold her brother’s arm. Hmph!”

Star Counter-Surveillance: silent. The hillside compound was private; residents only. And the girl currently pouting up at him with her whole righteous chin was, objectively, very kissable.

He dipped his head and stole one quick, soft peck off her lips.

Peach sugar and clean morning girl.

She froze for exactly one frame — then her eyes bent into crescents, and she was up on her toes with both arms around his neck, answering with the appetite of a small greedy animal, all offense, no fatigue.

A minute in, he pinched the soft of her waist — the stop signal. She bit his lower lip once, punitively, before releasing him, and licked her own lips with unhidden regret.

She LOVED kissing him. The smell of him up close; the way her pulse tripped; the way her knees filed complaints.

“Let’s go.” He smoothed her wind-ruffled hair.

She swatted his hand with a glare that contradicted the smile underneath it, finger-combed the long hair back into order, and bounced into the driver’s seat.

He’d just settled into the passenger side and reached for the belt when she stopped him.

“I’ll do it! I’LL do it!”

She leaned across him in full drama-heroine choreography, drawing the belt out with theatrical slowness, eyes shining up at him from very close range, breath on his jaw.

Thock.

He rapped one knuckle gently off her forehead. “Too many dramas. Move — we’ll be late.”

“AH! Oppa! APPA!” She clutched her forehead with operatic anguish, retreated to her seat, and crossed her arms hard. “Not driving! I’m angry now! The un-coaxable kind!”

Something glinted at the bottom of his eyes. He leaned across HER, unhurried, arm reaching past her shoulder, and drew her seat belt out — slowly. Deliberately. Pausing. Watching her from a handspan away the entire time.

Her heart missed once, then broke into a full sprint. His face at this range was frankly unconstitutional — the brows, the nose line, the fine down on his cheek visible in the morning light. Skin that should be actionable. Warm breath crossing her cheek, clean and infuriating. She could hear her own pulse. Her face began broadcasting heat.

Click.

The buckle seated. He ruffled her hair and withdrew to his seat, voice full of suppressed laughter.

“Drive.”

The pressure and the proximity vanished all at once. She swallowed. One hand pressed itself flat over her still-galloping heart. HATEFUL. Ambushed AGAIN. Bad man. Criminally handsome AND weaponizing it. Someone should handcuff him to her permanently. For public safety.

“What are you spacing out for?” The smile in his voice thickened. “Forgot how to drive?”

“Huh? — OH!”

She snapped back, redder, and attacked the ignition with fumbling hands — key, gear, mirror — every practiced motion suddenly two sizes too big for her fingers.

The car finally rolled out smooth.

The downhill road ran empty and green, trees closing overhead. She hit the button and dropped the top; morning air poured in, all grass and leaves, and threw her long hair into the wind. One hand on the wheel, the other tucking hair behind an ear, she laughed — cloudless, unguarded, entirely free — eyes curved shut, burning bright.

He turned his head and watched her.

Flying hair. Lit eyes. That reckless, weatherless smile.

His Sulli — exactly like this. Forever free. Forever burning. Forever bright as the morning sun.

He smiled too, leaned back into the seat, and let the wind have his face all the way down the mountain.
