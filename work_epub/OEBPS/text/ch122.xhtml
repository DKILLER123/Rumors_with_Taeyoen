Chapter One Hundred Twenty-Two

Morning Glories, Love, and Autumn

The balcony wind carried early autumn’s chill. It cooled nothing on Jiyeon’s face.

The unsealed memories kept surging: the bedroom’s dim warmth, the air thick with breath, his clean scorching scent, the electric shiver where his fingers had crossed her waist — every detail now in ruthless focus, and her heart in complete disarray.

“What do I DO—”

She gnawed a thumbnail, pulse hammering — flutter, mortification, and something deeper and more frightened, all braided. And underneath the memory: the present, pressing down like weather.

Half a month of T-ara as a small boat in a hurricane. The “bullying” storm gripping the whole team by the throat. “Sexy Love” — momentum guillotined. The first solo concert — shelved indefinitely. The eonnis’ opportunities bleeding out one by one: Eun-jung eonni forced off her drama, So-yeon eonni’s project boycotted. Endorsements terminating in a blizzard of penalty clauses. And the company — silent. Silent in a way that froze the blood. Every day another meter into a tunnel with no visible end.

Which was why she’d bolted for IU’s invitation — a few hours of oxygen. She had not budgeted for meeting Ji-ho here. The man who had left a burn mark on the chaos-chapter of her life.

Blame him? SHE had kissed HIM — riding soju and some smash-the-jar recklessness. Once is an accident. Twice is… a permission she didn’t dare examine. She had no standing to accuse anyone. And besides — the Ji-ho standing in that hallway now was no longer a trainee squinting up at sunbaes with an unwritten future. He stood there clean-lined and certain, market-certified, star-bright. Brighter than the memory. And — further away.

“Either don’t forget — or don’t REMEMBER!”

She thumped her forehead against the cool railing. This half-state was the worst of all: remembered too poorly to explain, felt too deeply to dismiss. Pretend nothing happened, and something aggrieved and unwilling sat lodged in her chest. Take it seriously — with her world currently on fire? What kind of timing was THAT for feelings?

“Ji-yeon-ah — what are you doing out here?”

IU’s voice, soft and soju-loosened, from behind her.

Ji-yeon rebuilt her face in half a second and turned with a manufactured smile.

“Nothing. Getting air.”

She let herself be towed back inside. The grill smell and the noise resumed; Sulli was clinking glasses with Ji-ho, who listened with his head tilted, profile clean and warm — behaving, in every observable particular, as if that chaotic night had never existed. As if she were just another friend at his table.

Something sour and unreasonable spiked through her.

By what RIGHT? Why am I the only one going insane over here? You remember EVERYTHING — and you get to sit there weightless, no probing glances, not even one lousy text message?

If he’d reached out first — even one vague greeting — looking the way he looks now, maybe she’d already have—

She poured another shot down her throat. The burn did not reach the cold place where a small, weak hope had just been slapped flat by reality. Women, she mocked herself. Always drafting “what ifs” after the fact. Back then he was an anonymous foreign trainee, and she was a member of the girl group challenging Girls’ Generation. THAT “what if” was never getting funded. The answer was cruel and clean.

Ji-ho’s attention sat, to all appearances, entirely inside Sulli’s joke. Peripherally, it was cataloguing every one of Jiyeon’s micro-struggles: the rigid spine, the drifting eyes, the too-frequent glass, the complicated glances that kept sliding his way and bouncing off.

He was not unmoved. That night lived in his memory with, if anything, better resolution than hers.

But that was exactly why the discipline mattered.

Field Notes

The cold arithmetic, from his side of the grill: now was not the time. HE was one hit song old — visible, but without real weight. And T-ara, for all the battering, had not yet reached the floor: residual popularity, the company’s final flailing, the members’ own refusal to fold — the supports were cracked, not collapsed. Approach now, and what could he actually offer? Pale comfort? His pocket-sized fame thrown against a national tide? An empty “I can help you,” nothing more?

And he wanted more than a moment’s warmth, more than an opportunist’s borrowed evening. He was — he could admit it in the privacy of his own skull — greedy: for the girl across the table holding herself together with both hands; for the girl in a Banpo apartment who had given him the word “home”; for others besides — the smiling eyes, the practice-room comrade, the rooftop birthday girl. Sustaining THAT much greed on pure feeling was architecture built on air. It needed bedrock: capital sufficient to turn tides. Standing that compelled respect. And — the irreplaceable gratitude of a hand extended at the exactly correct hour.

So he was waiting. For the storm to grind out the last of their hope; for the “everyone has abandoned us” hour to arrive; or — more realistic and more brutal — for her, Jiyeon, out of roads, to reach out first. THEN the extended hand would not be a hand. It would be a rope. And people who catch ropes rarely let go. It was a calculation cold to the point of cruelty — and it was also, in this industry, simply how survival and acquisition worked. Love might germinate in pure attraction; to make it grow in soil like this took ambition, patience, and a measured dose of ruthlessness, mixed by hand.

He smiled, raised his glass to Sulli’s toast, eyes clear as rinsed glass. Only he knew what root system was spreading, patient and directional, beneath that particular soil.

Nothing else happened that night. The alcohol ran out; everyone got sleepy. It wasn’t his first time in IU’s guest room, nor his first night in that bed.

And he had a quiet suspicion it would not be too many more nights before the room changed. She had ambition, that one. But his was bigger.

Next Morning LOEN Entertainment — Gym, Then a Conference Room twelve zeroes and one condition

Ji-ho was toweling wet hair off a post-workout shower when Ryu Dong-hwan arrived at above-standard velocity, folder thick, grin thicker. He dropped onto the sofa and patted the cushion.

“Sit down first. This news is a sitting-down news.”

Ji-ho sat, obliging and curious.

“ELITE.” Dong-hwan inhaled hugely and produced a thumbs-up. “THE Elite. The school-uniform brand of this entire country. They came to US — and they named YOU and Ji-eun. Joint endorsement, this year’s autumn line!”

Ji-ho’s towel paused. He knew the name, of course — practically a synonym for “school uniform” on the peninsula, the badge of youth, tradition, quality. Its models had always been artists of maximal national reach and maximally clean image.

“Me? And Ji-eun nuna?”

“Correct! Dual endorsement! And HERE’S the point—” the folder slid across, one finger pounding the figures line, “—one year, exclusive, THIS number.”

Elite Uniform · Endorsement Offer — Key Terms
Fee

One-year exclusive dual endorsement: ₩1.2 billion.

Core Condition

Not an existing song: a brand-new commercial theme song matching brand identity, to the brand’s satisfaction — created and performed by Song Ji-ho, featuring IU.

Incentive

Should the song drive a significant increase in autumn uniform sales: additional bonus of ₩200 million.

— they liked the “Some” chemistry. Now they want lightning to order.

For a rookie months out of debut: not generous — dreamlike. But the money was the fruit. The COMMISSION was the exam: not a test of his popularity as a singer, but of his core value and repeatability as a WRITER. Pass, and “singer-songwriter” hardened from press-release adjective into market position — and the song-commission requests would start arriving on their own, ready to convert his catalog into money, name, and network at speed.

“Pressure’s heavy, right?” Dong-hwan softened. “But it’s also the door. Elite campaigns are TALK-OF-THE-COUNTRY campaigns. A good CM song walks you into ears that never open a chart app.”

Ji-ho set down the towel, fingers drumming his knee once.

Pressure? What pressure? The only question on the table is WHICH song.

“Does Ji-eun nuna know yet?”

“Not yet. The question is whether YOU have the confidence to catch this.”

“Hyung. Relax.” He turned, and the certainty in his face was total. “The melody’s already forming. Set up the meetings — brand side, and Ji-eun nuna.”

Dong-hwan’s eyes lit; the shoulder-slap landed hard.

“THAT’S the energy!”

The song he’d already chosen: a small sweet gem from two years up the timeline — one IU herself would someday write, a springtime perennial that recharted every year. Move the season, swap the flower: cherry blossoms out, MORNING GLORIES in — the flower whose language meant love everlasting, the flower that bloomed hardest in autumn. Sweet acoustic guitar, a jazz-bright swing in the hook, harmonies built for singing along in a school hallway. And the lyrics — those he intended to hand to IU. Raising a song together, after all, was a little like raising a child together. It left two people with memories nobody else could hold.

✦   ✦   ✦

Elite’s conference room: floor-to-ceiling city, grey-white minimalism, diffuser and coffee in the air. Brand side on one flank — marketing director, promotion director, staff. On the other: Ji-ho, Dong-hwan, and — arriving minutes later — IU and her manager.

Pleasantries were brief. The promotion director, a crisp woman in fine glasses, cut to it.

“We loved the delicate, lovely atmosphere you two built in ‘Some.’ But this is a UNIFORM campaign — we need something brighter, more direct. ‘Youth,’ at first listen: school, autumn, and that pure, heart-fluttering-possibility kind of feeling.”

IU listened intently, nodding — cream knit, soft hair, light makeup, her focus giving her a stiller gravity than usual. She was already running melodies against the brief herself.

“Ji-ho-ssi — we hear you’ve got initial concepts?” The marketing director’s smile carried real curiosity.

Every eye converged. Under the table, Dong-hwan’s fist quietly clenched.

Ji-ho didn’t lead with melody. He led with an image.

“I’ve been wondering — what if the core image were the morning glory?”

“Morning glory?” The brand side traded glances — not a standard youth symbol.

IU’s eyes ticked brighter. She looked at him.

“Morning glories bloom fullest in autumn,” he continued. “The vines are soft but tenacious — they climb toward light. In the language of flowers, they stand for love that holds. But to me they also look like YOUTH itself: blooming all-out in its one season, taking its knocks, and growing anyway — stubborn, alive, carrying hope up the wall.”

A pause.

“Build the song around that, and you get the autumn timeliness — and something bigger than a simple love song. The shape of youth itself.”

The promotion director considered — then nodded slowly, warming as she spoke.

“That angle is… genuinely distinctive. It escapes surface-level sweetness — there’s aftertaste. And the morning glory’s profile fits our ‘elegant growth’ brand language remarkably well.”

IU stared at him with open astonishment shading into admiration. Skipping the cherry blossoms and maple leaves every writer reached for — choosing the morning glory — took observation, poetic association, and the nerve to be different. His excavation of a theme clearly didn’t stop at melody depth.

“And the musical direction?” the director pressed.

Only now did he produce the tablet, connect it to the room’s speakers, and cue a file.

“A rough demo fragment — melody and basic chords only. Just to feel it.”

Performance

Play.

Bright, clean acoustic guitar arpeggios — morning sunlight in early autumn. A supple piano line joins, carrying a light jazz swing, cheerful without sugar-shock. The melodic line is absurdly graceful, hooks planted deep; by the second pass of the chorus fragment, half the table is suppressing the urge to hum along. Even as a skeleton, the whole thing radiates campus, autumn air, and a tenderness of expectation tucked under the bounce.

Ninety seconds. Then several seconds of silence in the conference room.

“Wah—” The marketing director broke first. “That melody — that’s IT. That’s exactly the feeling! Fresh, lovely, energetic — and it still has CLASS!”

“Yes! Pop reach AND musicality! And that mid-register chorus line is built for duets — it’s SO campus! Ji-ho-ssi, the reputation is entirely deserved!”

Dong-hwan exhaled and glowed. IU sat very still, heart executing several unscheduled beats.

She considered her own musical education broad. But THIS — the speed of seizing a concept’s center, the quality and precision of the response, delivered like a man handing over a minor sketch instead of the key deliverable of a twelve-figure contract — this wasn’t just gift. It was a terrifying kind of accuracy.

And then he turned to her, tone gone earnest.

“About the lyrics — I have a proposal.”

The room turned with him.

“I’d like Ji-eun nuna to lead the lyric writing.”

“Me?” A blink; a reflexive finger at her own sternum.

She’d published her own lyrics — on B-sides, on album deep cuts. For major commercial work, the company and she herself had always defaulted to name-brand professional lyricists. The safe math.

“Mm.” He nodded. “I’ve always admired nuna’s sensitivity with words. This song’s image is ‘morning glories and autumn’ — it needs those fine-grained, youth’s-eye-view feelings: dew on a petal caught in morning light. The feel of a vine creeping over an old wall. That wider, more open kind of girl’s heart that knows autumn holds more than just love. Nuna interpreting that — I can’t think of anyone better.”

A beat, then:

“And — this isn’t only Elite’s commercial. It’s OUR second song. If the words carry nuna’s real feelings, it stops being an assignment and becomes ours. It’ll be more alive that way.”

It went straight through her. He was endorsing her music — trusting her as an EXPRESSER, not just a voice. In an industry that priced lyricists by name recognition and insurance value, this explicit, public confidence was rare to the point of preciousness: respect grounded in professional judgment, and a real chance to push her own creative boundary. A hot current surged up from somewhere deep and washed the last of the rivalry-and-probing games clean off the table. She searched his face for the angle, the hedge, the calculation. Nothing. He believed she could write it. He wanted to share the weight of making with her.

“I—” Her throat had tightened; she nodded hard, eyes gone extraordinarily bright. “I’ll give it everything. Thank you for the trust, Ji-ho.”

And in exactly that moment, the blurry competitive itch — the “beat Taeyeon” of it all — quietly changed state. What replaced it was simpler and hotter: being genuinely overwhelmed by his talent; being genuinely moved by his respect. Wanting to know him more — to stand beside him — not as a scoreboard against another woman, but because the man himself burned this bright.

Through the remaining details she was uncharacteristically immersed — pen flying, cataloguing the brand’s requirements, his section notes, and the lyric fragments already sprouting: morning glories, autumn, dew, old walls.

The brand side left smiling. IU packed up, crossed to him, and put out her hand, face luminous.

“Ji-ho. To a good collaboration. You’ll have my first draft soon.”

“To a good collaboration, nuna. I’m looking forward to your words.”

Palms met, warm; fingers parted lightly. She walked out with her notebook hugged to her chest, steps quick, carrying one heavy, sweet homework assignment — and one heart accelerating, quietly, for a particular person.

This IU — ambitious, fluent in cost and benefit — was also barely past twenty, and still kept a corner of herself for romance. Add the iron rule she lived by — if I like it, I get it — and the competitive fire Taeyeon had personally lit… and every additional degree of feeling was going to convert, directly and inevitably, into ACTION.
