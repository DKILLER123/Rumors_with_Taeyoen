Chapter Two Hundred Thirty

The Diligent Student, IU

August 2–3, 2013 The T-ara dorm · a parked car in the dark · Ji-eun’s villa a promise weighed in the night — and a chart queen caught mid-homework

Jiyeon floated back into the dorm wearing the unmistakable expression of a girl in the first chapter of something.

She stepped into the living room — and found Ham Eun-jung slumped on the sofa, staring at nothing, the lines of her whole body written in defeat.

“Eonni. What’s wrong?”

The good mood made the concern come easy.

Eun-jung tipped her head back against the cushions and let out a long, tired breath.

“The audition passed on me again. ‘Image doesn’t fit.’”

Ji-yeon had no answer for that. There wasn’t one.

In this industry’s weather, today’s T-ara was a small boat in open storm — endorsements frozen, resources zeroed, every member absorbing the internet’s fists daily and calling it Tuesday. Eun-jung eonni had been acting since she was a child. Now she couldn’t book a three-line cameo.

Ji-yeon knew her own luck for what it was. She had found — stumbled into — a man who checked every box she’d ever secretly drawn. And Ji-ho wasn’t some warped chaebol third-generation or a grease-slicked fifty-year-old with a fund; if it had been one of THOSE, she’d have starved first. He’d come up from nothing. He didn’t treat women as furniture. And what he felt for her — she believed it was real.

“Eonni. It’s all going to get better. Really.”

It was the only thing she could offer — but she said it from the floor of her heart, because she believed HIM. A man who had built all of that by twenty didn’t break promises made across a table at midnight.

“Let’s hope.”

Eun-jung shook her head slowly, eyes full of fog and fatigue.

✦ ✦ ✦

After dropping Ji-yeon at the dorm, Ji-ho sat alone in the parked car, engine off, thinking in the dark.

What had been done to T-ara was, inside the industry, an open secret. Half the stylists and makeup artists in Seoul had stories about the troublemaker’s tantrums — and her sister’s threatening messages to the girl who’d quit the group early were spoken of in at least three greenrooms he’d personally sat in. Everyone knew. And no one — NO ONE — would stand up and say it on the record.

Credit where due: the SM second-generation teams had been the only ones with spine, standing next to T-ara in public when the whole peninsula was throwing stones.

As for the girl’s family — the rumors ran the whole spectrum: an assemblyman, a prosecutor, a general, nothing at all. No one knew. What Ji-ho DID know was Kim Kwang-soo — a man out of the rough end of the business, whose methods were legend: the story of a top-of-his-fame singer getting his ribs broken and blacklisted until he knelt was not a metaphor. And THAT man, faced with one problem trainee, had gone strangely, uncharacteristically soft. Which suggested the father was real, senior, and powerful — and, if the later pattern held, close to retirement. Rumors of the troublemaker’s temper would eventually leak, once the protection aged out — and even then her career would keep landing softly. Residual influence did that.

So: step one, find out who the father actually was. THEN design the campaign. He might be generous with his affection, but a promise made to one of his women was a debt he paid in full, on schedule, every time.

As for Ji-yeon’s own career — the plan sketched itself: point the new album at the Western market, where her look and sound fit the current like they’d been cut for it. A collaboration track, or the lead single’s music video. Let her build heat OUTSIDE the peninsula’s blockade, then come home over the top of it with the truth about the bullying — and watch the public apologize its way back to her. By then he’d be moving into directing anyway; there’d be a role with her name on it. Multiple fronts, one campaign.

✦ ✦ ✦

Ji-eun’s villa, later that night.

The luxurious living room was fully lit and dead silent — except for the occasional tinny exclamation from a tablet, and, at intervals, a small, deeply unrespectable giggle.

Ji-eun lay sprawled on the sofa on her stomach, bare feet swinging in lazy alternation, holding the tablet at documentary-viewing distance, wearing the expression of a scholar deep in the archives.

She was, to be clear, STUDYING. Ji-eun had always been a woman with a plan. Having accepted the position, she intended to be the best in it — the most doted-on, the least replaceable. Taeyeon, she reasoned, was probably a homebody bore off the clock; that left an enormous competitive lane wide open. Last night’s reviews had been — she permitted herself the memory — excellent. To consolidate the gains, she had requisitioned certain specialized educational materials from Yoo In-na, and was now conducting a rigorous curriculum review. Men, as everyone knew, wanted elegance in the parlor and enthusiasm behind closed doors; she intended to overdeliver on both.

“Daebak… so THAT’S possible…”

“Wah. Truly daebak.”

Ji-ho let himself in, hung his coat on the rack, and followed the strange commentary into the living room — where the top student was so deep in her studies she hadn’t registered the door at all.

“Ji-eun. What are we watching?”

“—SONG JI-HO?!”

She detonated off the sofa like a startled quail — paused the video with both thumbs, crammed the tablet under a cushion, sat ON the cushion, and smoothed her hair with the composure of a woman who had definitely been reading poetry.

“You— when did you— why are you here?!”

He took in the scarlet cheeks and the eyes doing evasive maneuvers, filed it under ‘investigate later,’ and kept his face perfectly bland.

“What, I’m not allowed to come?”

“Ani!” Recovery was instant and total: she bounced up, wrapped both arms around his arm, and beamed. “Of course I’m happy you came! You just didn’t SAY anything first — I thought you had other plans tonight.”

And then, a small tactical masterpiece: the head dipped, the lashes lowered, the voice went soft at the edges.

“After all… I’m only ONE of your women. All I can do is wait quietly for my turn…”

Oho. So the little minx had begun studying the arts of favor-competition too. The technique was raw — the seams showed — but measured against her old porcupine settings, this was progress worth encouraging.

He pulled her in. She came with a soft collision of warmth and that clean sweet scent, and the youth and give of her made letting go seem like poor policy.

“Mianhae. I’ll call ahead from now on.”

She hadn’t expected the apology to arrive that easily, and logged the data point with satisfaction: so men really WERE soft against soft. Then her small nose twitched once against his shirt — and found a familiar perfume that was not hers. Ji-yeon’s favorite. Heavy florals. Recent. CLOSE-contact recent.

Her eyes gleamed. Opportunity. A woman should show exactly enough jealousy to prove the man is worth fighting over — and not one gram more, lest it curdle into nagging. Deployed correctly, guilt converts directly into doting. She pushed back from his chest, face clouding on cue.

“You were just with Ji-yeon, weren’t you.”

He swore internally at his own slip. He was normally meticulous about exactly this — but the night had run long, and he’d walked another woman’s perfume straight into this house. Genuinely graceless.

“I was with Ji-yeon just now, yes. Mianhae — I should have changed first.”

She sniffed, cold as a January window.

“And yet here you are at MY house tonight. What happened — did the great Casanova finally miss a shot? Well, of course. Ji-yeon’s standards are HIGH. Not like me — captured with a few pretty sentences.”

You’ve overestimated Ji-yeon and underestimated yourself, he thought, and declined to litigate it while she was enjoying her grievance. He rose instead.

“I’ll go shower and change.”

“Running away when you’re losing. Typical.” But her hand caught his sleeve anyway. “Wait. I’ll run the bath for you.”

The sequence genuinely surprised him. She was angry — legitimately, by her own accounting — and mid-anger, the caretaking still came first, automatic as breathing. That particular species of consideration did something to a man’s chest.

He caught her from behind, arms folding around her, and said it quietly into her hair.

“You’re good to me, Ji-eun.”

Her mouth curved where he couldn’t see it. Effective. The jealousy gambit: effective. But she knew the deeper rule too — the tantrum is never the point; the POINT is that even angry, she puts him first. THAT’S the version of a woman that lives rent-free in a man’s chest.

“Who told you to be my man.” A small sigh, deliberately worldly. “All right, let go. Water first, then I’ll bring your clothes — your sleepwear’s already in the second drawer. Prepared ages ago.”

Instead of releasing her, he turned her gently by the shoulders, something surfacing.

“Ji-eun. Your third full album — song selection starts soon, right?”

Her heart did a small hopeful jump. Was he offering—?

“Maja. The drama wraps, then album prep starts in August. The direction’s set — I want to try something more grown-up this time.”

“Grown-up.” He turned it over a moment. “Then let’s make the title track together. Same split as before — my composition, your lyrics.”

“Really?!” The light that went on in her face was instant and unguarded — and then, visibly, she caught it, folded it, and put on the sensible face. “…No. Better not.”

“Why not?”

The refusal genuinely puzzled him. She was clearly thrilled; his involvement was upside on every axis — quality, publicity, charts. There was no rational lane in which she says no.

“You don’t want my songs anymore?”

“Ani! Of course I want them!” Both hands waved the idea away, and the look she gave him was unfiltered admiration. “Everyone on the peninsula knows a Ji-ho song is a guaranteed hit. That’s not it.”

“Then what is it?”

Her face gentled into something warm and reasonable and quietly devastating.

“You have your OWN album to make. And now the whole company sits on your shoulders too. How could I take your focus for MY record?”

“My album is a small thing. You’re my man — Ji-eun’s man — and your work comes first. That’s all.”

And she rose on her toes and kissed him, soft and unhurried, then broke into the bright crescent smile.

“Saranghae, Ji-ho.”

And that — that landed. All the way through. He examined it for artifice and found he didn’t want to look very hard. Fair enough: he was, by now, extraordinary enough that a woman falling genuinely was the LIKELY outcome, not the long shot. He was her first man; he’d carried her career once already and conquered the rest of her the night before. The love made sense. And if he was honest about his own ledger — with Ji-eun he had invested mostly calculation and collected mostly trophies. Little heart, considerable technique. Tonight, listening to her hand him first place in her own life without being asked — the account quietly changed categories. He began, actually, to let her in.

“Ji-eun. I love you too.”

He bent and kissed her properly, and she answered with her whole warm weight, and for a while the room was very quiet in the best way.

Then she patted his chest, glowing.

“Wait here. Bathwater, then clothes.”

“Mm. Thank you.”

She walked off wearing a small, victorious smile. She DID love him — that part was true all the way down; living beside him had only sharpened it. But love and ambition had never once been enemies in Ji-eun’s economy. LOEN’s chairman’s WIFE — the words alone made her knees weak. Taeyeon, do you know how to do any of this? Being first is your great advantage — and your fatal flaw. First position breeds a wife’s entitlement; when the wandering surfaces, you’ll rage, or you’ll swallow it and go cold and quarrelsome. Either way: openings. Openings for the one woman with no standing to defend and every incentive to be perfect. As long as he stays unmarried, the seat is open. And the one who serves best, helps most, and never once makes him tired — SHE sits in it.

✦ ✦ ✦

While the water ran, Ji-ho dropped back onto the sofa, thoroughly pleased with the universe — and his hand landed on something hard under the cushion.

He excavated. The tablet. The very tablet she had been so fascinated by — and then hidden with her entire body. He looked at the dark screen. He looked at the bathroom door. He pressed power.

An office set. Melodramatic lighting. A young woman with an aggrieved expression addressing someone as ‘Kaichou’— wait. Japanese? …Oh. OH. The genre announced itself within four seconds, exactly as he’d begun to suspect. Well, well, Ji-eun. The famously 19-rated artist, living up to the certification. He assumed a rigorously critical, scholarly expression and continued the screening. For research. One had to admit — the production values were…

“Ji-ho, the water’s read— YA!!!”

She came around the corner with his robe over one arm, saw the tablet in his hands, and achieved liftoff — a scarlet-faced pounce worthy of a nature documentary.

“DON’T LOOK! Give it BACK!”

“Eyy—” He raised it overhead, entirely out of reach, grinning. “Ji-eun. I had NO idea this was your genre.”

“SHUT UP! Give it! GIVE IT!”

She bounced. She climbed. The height difference declined to cooperate; sweat actually beaded at her hairline from the effort, which improved his mood further.

Force having failed, she executed an instantaneous doctrine change: the whole small body went soft, the eyes went liquid, the voice dropped into pure spun sugar.

“Hoejang-nim~ sweetheart~ give it back, hm? Please~? Hm? Hmm~ give it baaack~”

The peninsula’s women were armed with aegyo the way other nations were armed with artillery — and this particular specialist had a clean sweet face and enormous glittering eyes and no shame whatsoever. His resolve had the structural integrity of warm butter.

“Fine, fine—” He lowered the tablet, laughing. “Returned.”

She reached for it, delighted— and he snatched it back up at the apex.

“ONE condition. Tell me first — what exactly were you WATCHING this for?”

She bit her lip. The blush went all the way to her ears. The answer arrived at the volume of a falling leaf.

“…Studying.”

“Ah. STUDYING.” His interest achieved new altitudes. “And what, exactly, has the diligent student learned?”

“I’ll… tell you at bedtime.”

The head went down; the ears went redder.

“Why wait for bedtime? Now works.”

He tossed the tablet onto the cushions and scooped her clean off the floor.

“YA!” — arms locking around his neck by reflex, laughter spilling out of her — “ahahaha— wait, WAIT, you haven’t even bathed yet—”

“The bath and the seminar can run concurrently.”

He carried her toward the steam with the stride of a man late for something important, and the last thing the living room heard was her shriek dissolving into giggles.

The door clicked shut. The lesson, by all accounts, was thorough.

Ji-eun. Truly — a woman beyond resisting.
