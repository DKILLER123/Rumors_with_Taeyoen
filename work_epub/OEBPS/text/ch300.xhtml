Having done her worrying in the approved format, she had moved on to the news, and it was there, mid-scroll, that the twenty-thousand-recommend comment found her. She read it twice, with rising delight. She read it a third time, mouth moving.

Say what you want about this country’s netizens. The eyes are SHARP. Taeyeon sunbaenim: correct. Ji-eun: correct. Peach: correct. Full marks, all around.

“Hateful!” The slap landed on his arm with the weight of an offended pigeon, and he caught the hand that did it, laughing, and pulled her in by the waist, and the teasing drained out of his voice between one word and the next.

“Enough, enough. No more free jealousy. You’re spending it on comment sections. I’ve been thinking. In October, you, and the rest of T-ara: you’re all coming to America with me.”

She looked up at him, lost.

“America?”

“The pre-release single goes up on the third. Two weeks ahead of the album, one week of promotion here, one week there.” A beat, and then, plainly, as if reporting the weather: “For the America week: you five stand on stage with me. As my dancers.”

Dance backup for him. And America. The two facts hit the desk of her mind at the same moment and knocked everything else off it. She had his arm in both hands.

“Really? Truly? You’d really let us dance for you?”

“I just said so.”

“Then it’s the best news of the—” The delight snagged on arithmetic. “But there are only ten days. Can choreography even be built that fast?”

“By then you’ll have overseas recognition behind you. That is momentum, and momentum is wind. The vindication is the match. Wind carries fire, and fire in a wind like that does not go out until it has burned the verdict off you.”

Momentum is the wind. Vindication is the match. Wind carries fire, and a verdict, given enough of both, will turn.

He said it with the calm of a man reading tomorrow’s paper, and she believed him with her whole chest, because he said it, and because she had watched him be right in increasingly expensive ways for two years.

Though let’s be honest: coming home “with momentum” is the single hardest trick in Asian entertainment. The West has eaten a hundred of our best and called it a niche market. And this man announces America the way other people announce lunch. If it were anyone else, I’d file it under fantasy. It’s him, so I file it under schedule.

A year. It had been a year, and she had never once let herself look down the whole length of it.

Memory · the year the lights went out, one at a time

It began with the endorsements: contracts that were not renewed, renewed ones cancelled, penalty clauses arriving by registered mail like subpoenas. Then the schedules, thinning from rivers to creeks to a dry bed. Then the comment sections, which she stopped reading on a doctor-adjacent basis, because there is only so many weeks a person can spend being informed of what a stranger hopes happens to her.

There had been no single day it became unbearable. It became unbearable in installments: a passport not needed, a stage not offered, a group chat that learned to talk about the weather. Her own name, in her own country, turning into a thing people typed AT each other.

And now the file is open, and the year gets an ending after all.

And the ending had a face she intended to visit upon it.

Ryu Hwa-young. You’d better enjoy the shade while it lasts. The truth is already in a folder, the folder is already on a desk, and the desk is already his. Everything you watched happen to me (the chasing, the jeering, the boycott), you are about to experience as a season, with no commercial breaks. And I’m not stopping at clean hands, oh no. I’m going to be MORE popular than the peak of us. On purpose. Where you can watch.

The anticipation ran up her neck and set her face glowing before she’d said a word. The feeling needed a receipt, and the only counter open was the man at the window.

Yoga. Yes. Historically, he was in favor of yoga.

But the CCM merger plan was sitting on his desk with the afternoon still on it, and duty, regrettably and wretchedly, has a body like his. He set one finger against her forehead and pushed the temptation back to arm’s length.

He delivered the threat over his shoulder and went back to the desk and the merger documents. She licked her lips, filed tonight where it belonged, and addressed the remaining surplus of feeling. An idea with real crime in it crossed her face.

Ji-yeon opened her mouth, and for once could not find the seam of the argument. A boyfriend wanting his girlfriend at the gate: nothing wrong with it. A working woman turning the car around because her company was being bought out from under her career: nothing wrong with that either. Half the boyfriends in the industry got out to nobody: a text, an apology, a reunion dinner. It was not a crime. It was just two rights, colliding.

The living room held a short, complicated silence, the silence of five women running that logic and finding no leak in it.

Soyeon nodded, and the tears went over again, quietly. Ji-yeon watched from the table with her chest packed tight.

There was a time this room ran like a wristwatch. Practice together, debut together, win together: one heart, five faces, stages lit like a row of mornings. The year of the scandal broke something in the works, and it never quite ran the same; every conversation since has had a draft in it from somewhere. But look at her. Look at us around her. Whatever cracked, it didn’t crack THAT. I forgot for a year that we were sisters first.

She crossed the room, went down on her heels in front of Soyeon, and took both her hands.

Soyeon looked up through the wreck of it. Ji-yeon smiled (the crescent-eyed one, the old one) and stood, and clapped her hands twice like a teacher calling the room to order.

The eyes went red again, differently, and the dorm got its breakfast at last, six steaming bowls of it, one for the patient first.

Ye-rim latched onto Joo-hyun’s other arm and simply vibrated.
