Chapter One Hundred Ninety-Three

Even If It Damns Us

May 21, 2013 · afternoon → night LOEN studio → a Gangnam private dining room the theme song recorded to the bone — and a line crossed for good

LOEN studio, two p.m.

Tiffany stood at the microphone in monitor headphones, lyric book dense with her own annotations. Beyond the glass he sat at the console, expression stripped down to pure attention.

“Begin.”

recording session · “Love Me Like You Do” · lead vocal

The piano intro unwinds. She closes her eyes, sets her breath — and opens on the first line, the one that names the beloved as both the light and the night.

“Stop.” Before the phrase even lands. “Too heavy. This is the tentative opening — not the declaration. Think: midnight, alone with someone, saying it almost under your breath.”

She resets. Second attempt — the line that calls him the cure and the pain in one breath. “Better. Not there. The pre-chorus is a transition — it has to gather and release. You’re gathering too tight and releasing too suddenly. Move like a tide.”

Her fingers whiten on the lyric book. She is one of Girls’ Generation’s lead vocals; she has survived a decade of impossible stages. And standing in this booth, under this particular scrutiny, she is — nervous. Genuinely, humanly nervous.

Third take. Fourth. Fifth. The same passage, rebuilt syllable by syllable. He stopped her on single vowels to renegotiate their placement; he ran one long note six ways hunting the most natural decay; he leaned into the register breaks with a jeweler’s loupe until the seams disappeared.

“Chorus — the title line. Rebalance the mix of your voice. True voice down to about sixty percent; add head resonance. It needs to pierce without pushing. The feeling is tender devotion — not fever.”

She obeyed. And discovered, again, that his ear was almost frightening — he caught the finest flutter of breath support, named the most private hedges in her emotional delivery, and for every flaw handed back a precise, workable fix.

Three hours in, she’d emptied five cups of warm water, her throat was tightening — and the session had cleared barely a third of the song.

“Ten minutes.”

She came out of the booth and leaned against the wall, exhaling from the floor up. He handed her a fresh cup.

“Brutal, right? But it’s working. That last chorus — the full-body devotion in it. That’s exactly what the song needs.”

She took the cup; her fingers brushed his in the handoff. Both sets of fingertips ran cool from the long session — and the contact still jumped like a spark off winter wool. She pulled her hand back fast and drank.

“…Thanks.”

The recording resumed. And the deeper into the song she went, the harder it became to keep herself out of it. The lyrics kept holding up mirrors.

The line about fear — about not caring.

Didn’t she care? No. She cared. She cared about Taeyeon — about thirteen years of friendship. And yet.

The line about being so far gone into someone.

Gone. That was the accurate word. What she carried for Ji-ho was exactly that: a sinking with no handholds.

On the final chorus take she stopped fighting the undertow — closed her eyes and let everything through. Her voice poured out of the monitors, restraint and hunger audibly at war.

The last note fell away. The studio rang with silence.

He didn’t speak. He looked at her through the glass. She looked back — and the look held too much: impulse, discipline, love, resentment, and a struggle worn nearly to despair.

“Perfect.” His voice, when it finally came, had sand in it. “That’s — today.”

✦   ✦   ✦

Seven p.m. A private room in an upscale Gangnam restaurant — soft light, wine-dark walls, a thread of diffuser scent in the air; silver on white linen, the decanted red throwing gem-light under the lamps.

She sat across from him, two glasses in, cheeks warmed, eyes loosening at the edges.

“You sang beautifully today.” He raised his glass. “To a perfect performance.”

She didn’t lift hers. She looked at him instead.

“Ji-ho. Do you know what I was thinking about, while I sang that song?”

“What?”

“You.” Her eyes had gone deep and unfixed. “About all the thoughts I’m not allowed to have. About what happens if Taeyeon finds out. About whether — I’ve lost my mind.”

He set down his glass, rose, and crossed to her side of the table.

“You—” She hadn’t finished before his hand closed over hers.

She pulled, weakly. “Let go.”

“Nuna.” Low, and completely steady. “You crossed the line first. This — you lit it first.”

“That— that was the alcohol—”

“I’m drunk now too.”

He leaned in; their breath tangled.

“Liar. You’ve had two glasses.” Her voice wavered as she said it, her eyes moving over the face an inch from hers — the deep-set eyes, the clean nose, the mouth. “Two glasses is nothing—”

He didn’t answer. He breathed her in instead — tuberose over wine-fruit, sweetness with the burn folded in — and then he stopped holding back: pulled her up into him and took her mouth mid-word.

“Mm—!”

She fought it — really fought it, fists against his shoulders. He held on, and the kiss burned through the struggle — fierce first, then, past her guard, achingly gentle. And by degrees the fists stopped landing. Her hands closed on his shirt instead of striking it. Then her arms were around his neck.

He smelled like clean woodsmoke. He kissed like a man reading her mind off her lips. And the worst of it — the unforgivable core of it — was that nothing in her wanted him to stop.

She began answering.

When they finally broke, she was breathing high and fast, face burning. The wine-heat receded; sense clawed its way back up. She shoved him back a step, voice shaking.

“We can’t do this— we can’t do this to Taeyeon.”

He drew her straight back in, chin coming to rest on her hair, breathing her shampoo. “Hearts don’t take orders, Fany. If feelings stopped on command, this world would have no tragedies in it.”

“So WHAT?” Her voice cracked open. “So you’ll just — comfortably wound two women at once? Ji-ho, you BASTARD! You philandering— how do you face Taeyeon?!”

“I know what I owe her.” Level. Quiet. “And I cannot let go of you.”

“So—” She wrenched her head up, eyes flooded. “So you’ll date me WHILE dating her? Shibal! What do you take us for?!”

He cupped her face in both hands and wiped the tear-track from one eye-corner with a thumb.

“From that kiss just now — I know you love me. You’re not denying it. Are you?”

She bit down on her lip and said nothing.

She wasn’t denying it. She loved him uncontrollably — the face, the talent, the fixed attention when he worked, the way his eyes went soft. He was nearly every private specification she’d ever drafted for a man, made flesh and standing too close.

“We love each other. That’s the whole fact.” His voice stayed even. “Taeyeon nuna’s side — I’ll explain it. I need time.”

“TIME?” A bitter laugh tore loose. “What does time fix? Time just stacks the lies higher and cuts the wound deeper!”

He answered by kissing her again.

This time she didn’t fight at all. Her eyes closed, and she let herself go under — all the way under — into the dangerous warmth of it. The body had already voted, well ahead of the heart.

When they separated, her lips were kiss-swollen and her gaze had gone soft-focus. She murmured, barely above breath:

“Ji-ho. You know this sends us both to hell, right?”

“I know.” No hesitation in it at all. “And I still can’t let you go.”

A tremor ran through her, head to heel.

“Nuna. Stop carrying all of it.” He took her hand. “Trust me. Taeyeon’s side — I will find the way.”

He drew out his phone, unlocked it, opened the banking app — and turned the screen to face her.

The string of zeros ran long enough to blur.

“This is — what?”

“Four hundred sixty billion won.” Flat calm. “My personal assets. Investment returns.”

Her eyes went wide. She counted. Recounted.

460 billion. All nine of them, everything they’d ever earned, doubled — didn’t reach it.

“Your career — I’ll carry it.” The voice kept coming, low and warm and remorseless. “The contract negotiations. The solo plans. Anything you ever want to build. All of it.”

“All you have to do is live inside this — and give me your heart.”

She looked at the impossible number. Then up, at the eyes above it.

Reason was screaming: this is wrong, this is betrayal, this is the unforgivable thing. And feeling answered underneath, soft and certain: he’s serious. He can protect you. He can give you everything.

And the body — the body had never once lied: pulse sprinting, palms damp, face hot.

He put the phone away and opened his arms, and this time she walked into them and pressed her face against his chest and stopped arguing with any of it.

Beyond the window, Seoul burned like a spilled galaxy. On the wall, two shadows folded into one shape.

She knew exactly what this moment was: the lip of a whirlpool with no way back out.

And she no longer wanted the way back.

The song had already said it for her — that she was too far gone into him.

Gone, then.

Even if it damned them both.
