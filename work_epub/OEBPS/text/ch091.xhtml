Chapter Ninety-One

Scandal

The car door opened; the back seat received them.

Taeyeon pressed herself against the far door, gaze pinned to the window, painstakingly not meeting his eyes.

Ji-ho let the corner of his mouth curl — silently — and folded himself in.

“Ji-ho, where do you live?”

Seohyun’s eyes found him in the rearview mirror.

“Songpa-gu, Samhaksa-ro 19-gil — Building 6.”

The car slid smoothly into the night.

Up front, Seohyun and Tiffany murmured over tomorrow’s schedules. Taeyeon contributed the occasional syllable, voice drifting somewhere else entirely.

Ji-ho watched the Seoul lights stream past the glass — and let his gaze drift back to her, naturally, again and again. Her lips, carefully repainted, caught the low light with a soft, water-bright sheen.

She was watching the window. She was also, out of the very corner of her eye, tracking every one of those glances — and her ears were slowly catching fire. At last she couldn’t stand it, whipped her head around, and glared at him with maximum ferocity.

She received, in return, a smile of even more innocent warmth.

Two meetings in half a month of murderous schedules — and both times, he’d kissed her until her whole body went soft.

What are we, exactly — me and Ji-ho?

She didn’t know. She only knew that every day apart had stretched like taffy, its gaps packed solid with unbearable missing-him.

She wanted, recklessly, to be with him — openly, in daylight, as his girlfriend.

But reality was ice water: he was about to debut, and her own dating ban still hung overhead like a suspended sword. One step past that line and — the fans, the company, the storm that would follow—

She was not unafraid.

And yet: not take the step? Her heart and her body had already surrendered without consulting her. Against him, she had no defenses at all.

She caught her lower lip in her teeth, watching neon smear backward past the glass, eyes lost.

What am I supposed to DO?

Seohyun’s car was new — clean, understated interior, none of the expected girlish clutter; a faint elegant fragrance from some diffuser, the kind that quiets the mind. She drove the way she did everything: steady, unhurried. About thirty minutes later, the car eased to a stop outside Ji-ho’s apartment complex.

“This is your building?” Tiffany had her face half out the window, peering up at the apartment block.

“Mm — Building 6, apartment 801.” He unclipped the seatbelt, stepped out, and bowed slightly beside the car. “Taeyeon nuna, good night. Tiffany nuna, Ju-hyun nuna — thank you for the ride. Good night.”

“Bye-bye~ get in safe.”

The car pulled gently away.

Through the window, Taeyeon watched the figure under the streetlight shrink — hand still raised in farewell — and something inside her snapped taut. An impulse seized her with zero warning.

“Wait! Stop the car!”

Screech— Seohyun feathered the brake and turned around, startled.

“Eonni, what’s wrong?”

Her heart was pounding. She grabbed the first excuse in reach.

“Ah— that— there’s something I forgot to tell Ji-ho. Wait for me, it’ll be quick!”

She shoved the door open and ran — small, quick steps — toward the figure just beginning to turn away.

He heard it: hurried footfalls, and a slightly flustered voice.

“Ji-ho!”

He turned. Taeyeon was running at him, the night wind lifting the fringe off her forehead—

—and a few steps out, her foot caught on something. A startled cry, and she pitched forward.

“Careful!”

He was already moving — one long stride, one long arm — and caught her cleanly, fully, against his chest.

Warm and soft and smelling like everything familiar, she landed in his arms; his arms tightened before he could think about it.

“Nuna, are you okay? What’s the emergency?”

Heart still skittering, cheek pressed to the heat of his chest, she could hear both their heartbeats scrambling. She took two seconds to find her breath — then tilted up fast and light, lips at his ear, and whispered in a rush of air meant for exactly one person:

“June 24th. I’m going home to Jeonju. About three days.”

Then she tore free of his arms like she’d touched a stove, backed up a step, and pitched her voice loud — imperious, to cover the panic:

“Did you get all that? Do it PROPERLY!”

He blinked once — then nodded along, the picture of obedience.

“Ne, got it, nuna. I’ll prepare thoroughly.”

She didn’t dare look at him one second longer. She turned and walked fast back to the car, breath still uneven.

“DaeDae, you okay?”

“Fine — just tripped.” She buckled in, dragging her voice level. “Told him what he needed to know.”

The car merged back into traffic and dwindled away.

None of them noticed the black sedan sitting motionless in the shadow of the roadside trees — or the faint glint, inside its window, of a camera lens catching the light. Once.

Ji-ho watched the taillights vanish around the corner, ran June 24th through his head several times until it was engraved, and went inside.

The elevator climbed to eight. The corridor held nothing but his own footsteps.

He unlocked the reinforced door of the rental; the living room sat in darkness. The mysterious roommate’s door was shut as always, not a thread of light under it. Nearly three months in this apartment, and his early-out, late-back schedule meant he had never once laid eyes on whoever lived behind that door. His entire evidence of their existence: the occasional, very soft click of a latch on weekend mornings.

He went to his room, switched on the light, stepped into his slippers, pulled a bottle of cold water from the mini-fridge, and drank half of it in one tilt.

The chill slid down his throat — and did nothing whatsoever about the warmth still living on his lips: sweet, faintly strawberry, entirely hers.

June 9, 2012 · Cloudy, Turning Overcast SM Entertainment — Executive Conference Room the report goes upstairs

The air in the room had weight.

Jo Woo-chul, head of Production Center Team One, distributed copies of the contract draft to everyone at the table: Sajang-nim Kim Young-min; Director Lee Soo-man; Kim Ah-ju for Legal; and the other two production-center heads, Kim Wook and Kwon Yoon-jung.

He cleared his throat and delivered his report on the negotiation with trainee Ji-ho.

“Those are his core demands. In summary: independent ownership of his own compositions, with the company holding exclusive agency rights only — major decisions subject to his consent, agency rights expiring with the contract. And on songs he both writes and performs, a total share of no less than forty percent.”

BANG.

Kim Young-min’s hand came down hard on the table, his face thunderous.

“Forty percent? Independent copyright? Who does he think he is? A trainee who hasn’t even debuted, daring to name terms like that?”

Disbelief and banked fury filled his voice.

“Bujang-nim, did you not explain the rules of this industry to him? Every single person who reaches a debut crossed a single-log bridge over an army of thousands!”

“Getting the debut at all should have him on his knees in gratitude! Revenue shares? Independent publishing? Those are negotiating rights for artists with YEARS of results and standing!”

He swept the table with a hard look.

“On this peninsula — at SM — the company sinks enormous capital into training, packaging, promotion, and carries every won of the risk. Trainee-era costs, future production budgets, marketing spend — which of those is not astronomical?”

“We provide the platform, the resources, the opportunity; the artist contributes talent and effort. That is the foundational model, and the market has validated it a hundred times over.”

“A rookie’s first job is to survive. To get exposure. To build a fanbase. Not to lie back on his so-called ‘creative’ laurels and try to fly before he’s learned to walk! That mentality is a textbook failure to know his own place — a boy drunk on one season of talent!”

Lee Soo-man had said nothing, fingers tapping lightly on the tabletop, eyes unreadable behind his lenses.

Did he prize the talent? Genuinely. He admired the boy’s creative firepower and that clear-eyed, faintly ambitious gaze. The potential coiled inside “Some” — he could see all of it.

But what Ji-ho had done today wasn’t merely bargaining. It shaded into leverage. Setting individual creative value above the company’s entire operating logic struck at the root of what SM was: a star factory. Stable. Controlled. Hierarchical.

A rookie this incapable of knowing when to yield — if he actually became something, how would anyone manage him? And where would that leave the company’s authority?

“Where does his confidence come from?” Lee Soo-man spoke at last. “Just ‘Some’ and ‘A Boy’? Or does he believe SM cannot do without him?”

“His posture in the room was absolute,” Jo Woo-chul answered quickly. “He invoked ‘value’ and ‘timing’ — and even implied his songs don’t necessarily have to ring out from SM’s deck.”

“Per our earlier discussion, I disclosed the plan to pair Taeyeon with him on ‘Some’ — used the opportunity and the platform as persuasion. But he… doesn’t seem satisfied with even that.”

Kim Ah-ju pushed up her glasses, voice cold.

“From a legal and contractual standpoint, his demands overturn the rookie framework entirely. Independent copyright means the company is constrained at every turn of song operations and derivative development. Yes, our splits run ten points under other agencies — but no other company on the market matches SM’s promotional and album investment.”

“Concede once, and it becomes an extraordinarily dangerous precedent. If every trainee with a little writing ability follows suit, the management system itself comes under siege.”

Kim Wook, head of Production Two, frowned.

“Did we perhaps treat him too generously? Early promotion to the pre-debut team, free run of the production rooms — it may have taught him the wrong lesson about his position.”

Kwon Yoon-jung looked more thoughtful.

“But his work… genuinely has hit written on it. And he’s prolific — reportedly in the production room until all hours. That appetite and that output, in a rookie, is rare. It’s only the temperament…”

The room lapsed into weighing silence. Kim Young-min, the rule-keeper, wanted the sprout crushed early. Lee Soo-man swung between commercial appetite and the need for control. The others ran their own ledgers.

And that was when the knocking came — fast, urgent.

A PR office director entered white-faced, barely managing the bow, and thrust a tablet in front of Kim Young-min and Lee Soo-man.

“Sajang-nim — Director! It’s broken! A dating scandal involving our artists just detonated online — it’s spreading at speed!”

On the screen: last night. The streetlight. Ji-ho with his arms full of a stumbling Taeyeon; Taeyeon tilted up, leaning close; the angle vicious, the frame slightly blurred — composed, to any stranger’s eye, like two lovers a breath from a kiss.

Kim Young-min’s face went stove-black. Lee Soo-man’s brow clamped down.

“Taeyeon?! With SONG JI-HO?!” It came out of Kim Young-min as a near-growl. “When did this happen?! Why am I hearing about it NOW?!”

“The photo is from last night. The paper bypassed the usual channels and published straight to the web. By the time our people caught it, it had already… already spread.” Sweat stood on the office director’s forehead.

No one needed the implications spelled out. Taeyeon: the soul and main vocal of Girls’ Generation, mid-ascent as a solo presence, with a fandom enormous and fiercely loyal. Any dating scandal would hit her and the group like a shell — and the other party was a trainee with no results to his name.

Almost in unison, phones began buzzing around the table — other departments, press contacts, industry friends, the bombardment beginning.

“Activate the emergency protocol! Contact that paper — find out what else they’re holding!”

“Monitor sentiment on every major forum and SNS platform!”

“And get Taeyeon and Ji-ho into this building. NOW!”

The office director fled with his orders.

Dead quiet in the conference room. The contract argument of five minutes ago lay drowned under the tidal wave — and yet somehow it coiled back around the table, sharper than before.

Kim Young-min stared at the photo, then thought of the boy across the negotiating table who had not yielded a millimeter, and his chest heaved.

A rookie who squares off with the company over splits and copyright before he’s even debuted — and by nightfall he’s tangled in a scandal with the leader of our flagship girl group?

“This Ji-ho!” The words came out from between his teeth. “He is a walking COMPLICATION!”

Lee Soo-man leaned back and kneaded the bridge of his nose.

A deadlocked negotiation. And now, on its heels, a scandal built to detonate a fandom.

Six months ago, the name Ji-ho had swept the country as a hero — the trainee who saved Sulli. Now the same name was about to sweep the entire net a second time, hotter and far less controllable — and this time the discussion wouldn’t be about character or courage, but scandal, blame, and the fury and heartbreak of a million fans.

The executive meeting scattered into emergency deployments. But the storm had already made landfall.
