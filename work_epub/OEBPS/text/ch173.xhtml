Chapter One Hundred Seventy-Three

Kissed Ten Times

April 2013 The newlywed kitchen set one simple good-morning kiss, nine unsimple takes

The shoot rolled on, and the story rolled with it — marriage registered, honeymoon phase engaged. Today’s set: the couple’s snug little kitchen, morning light through the window, the air sweet with milk and cereal.

The scene was peak newlywed sugar — everyday intimacy, hinged on one simple, essential good-morning kiss. Kwak Jae-yong’s brief: “Natural. Domestic. Love leaking out of the frame. Nothing intense — but one second in, the audience has to FEEL the honeymoon.”

Sulli had been ready for an hour: soft no-makeup makeup, cream knit and cotton lounge pants, hair loosely gathered with a few strands escaping down her neck — the complete adorable-young-wife package.

She stood on her mark with a heart rate worse than the first kiss scene’s.

For one excellent reason: today she had arrived with a mission statement.

Last time: one take. Catastrophic loss. This time — the opportunity WILL be maximized.

Her eyes kept drifting to where he stood confirming blocking with the director — gray heather tee, track pants, offensively good side profile in the morning light.

“The Girl in the Sun! Scene forty-seven, take one!”

“Action!”

Take one.

Back to camera, he worked at the open kitchen’s counter, breakfast in progress. She shuffled in sleep-soft, wrapped her arms around his waist from behind, cheek nuzzling between his shoulder blades. He turned, smiling, jam toast in hand. She tilted her face up, eyes fogged with morning, and puckered — subtle as a billboard. He bent and kissed her, light, sweet, one touch and apart. They grinned at each other; the air filled with visible pink.

“Cut! The feeling’s right — but Sulli, the face-tilt read a little deliberate. More natural, like you’re genuinely half-asleep and demanding it on autopilot. Again!”

“Ah — sorry, Director!”

The apology was instant and sincere-shaped. The heart under it did a small victory lap. An excellent start.

Take two.

She recalibrated: the back-hug landed boneless, the upturned face came with eyes half-lidded, maximum drowse. He bent; this contact ran a half-second longer, close enough to catch the lashes lowering, the soft curve arriving at the corner of his mouth.

“Cut! Better! But Ji-ho — the toast grazed her hair on the turn. Watch the prop. Again.”

Take three.

The turn came clean. As the kiss landed she tilted her head — “unconsciously” — nose brushing nose, a small itch of intimacy, the kiss going faintly, unscriptedly lingering.

“Cut! The nose thing is GOOD — very lived-in! But smaller, Sulli. Too big and it reads designed. Keep it accidental. We keep this one as backup and go again.”

Take four.

She worked very hard at accidental — possibly too hard. As his mouth came down, her body stiffened an unfindable half-second, the geometry misfired, and her teeth clicked gently against his lower lip.

“Cut! You all right, Ji-ho?”

“Fine.”

He touched his lip and glanced at the girl whose face had gone suspiciously rosy.

“Sorry, Oppa…” she offered, tiny-voiced.

Contact!! That counts as a French kiss, right? Technically? The delivery method was unorthodox but—

Take five.

Apparently to atone for the error, she now performed with extraordinary commitment. When the kiss arrived she not only received it but answered it, gently, unmistakably — and the standard-issue good-morning peck acquired several degrees of entanglement.

“Cut!” Kwak scratched his head. “Sulli-yah… this — the morning kiss doesn’t need that much depth. Fresh. Sweet but restrained. Again.”

Take six.

The kiss: duly restrained. Her hands: not so much. Wrapped at his waist, they wandered quietly upward, fingertips tracing — barely, deniably — along the firm line of muscle at his side, under the hem’s shadow. Mid-kiss, one of his eyebrows performed a very small, very controlled twitch.

“Cut! Sulli — hands STATIONARY, please!”

The director was entering the helpless phase.

Take seven.

Hands: exemplary. But as he finished the kiss and began to withdraw, she — pure improvisation — chased upward and pressed one extra peck to the corner of his mouth.

“Cut! …Hm.” Kwak studied the monitor, rubbing his chin at the unplanned, faintly impertinent, curiously sweet addendum. “That little chaser is… actually sort of charming? Very young-wife. Very spoiled-and-adored. BUT we need a standard version first. Again!”

Take eight.

Perhaps worn down by the campaign of small ambushes, this time HIS aim drifted — the kiss landed off-center, mostly corner.

“Cut! Position. Again.”

Take nine.

She appeared to be in deep, sincere reflection. The kiss came off textbook: one touch, apart, fresh, sweet. But in the after-beat her eyes went glossed and over-bright, cheeks flooding, the whole face broadcasting kissed-and-swooning at a wattage no acting teacher assigns.

“Cut! The emotion is GORGEOUS, Sulli — but we want everyday sweetness, not the morning after the wedding night! Dial it down! DOWN!”

Nine takes. And the NG log, read honestly, was a catalog of one actress’s micro-choices, micro-expressions, and unauthorized improvisations. The strange part — the part that had Kwak second-guessing his own cuts — was that every single “flaw” made the footage weirdly better: livelier, warmer, more like an actual marriage caught by accident. He was beginning to wonder whether some of this imperfection should simply stay in the picture.

The crew had traveled from professional focus to a set-wide epidemic of knowing smiles. Everyone present was an industry adult. The precise species of “difficulty” the leading lady kept having was visible from space.

He had traveled from cooperative to resigned. Of course he’d noticed. Lately she’d all but stopped bothering to hide it.

As the ninth “cut” rang out and the gaffers moved in to trim the lights, he dipped his head, and at a volume engineered for an audience of one: “Sulli-yah. Keep NG-ing and the director’s going to start doubting your professional competence. Also—” a beat, “—my lips are about to file for workers’ compensation.”

Warm breath on the shell of her ear, carrying that clean scent that was specifically his.

Her ear went scarlet on contact, the color marching down her neck. She shot him one lightning glance, met the laugh sitting in his eyes, and her heart dropped a beat — the full, scalding shame of a scheme read in its entirety by its target.

She stared at the floor and produced, with no conviction whatsoever: “I— I’m genuinely trying to get the scene right…”

He said nothing more. He just reached out and, with perfect naturalness, tucked the escaped strand of hair back behind her ear — one fingertip grazing the molten lobe in passing.

She shivered head to toe like a struck wire.

“All right! Positions! Take ten!” Kwak’s voice rolled across the set. “Sulli — relax. You’re just married, you’re happy, it’s an ordinary morning, that’s ALL it is! Ji-ho — you too, keep it easy! Action!”

Maybe the whispered warning worked. Maybe the hair-tuck had discharged the static. Or maybe — nine kisses deep — some private quota had finally, temporarily, been met.

Take ten.

She folded against his back and nuzzled in, and it was simply affection, nothing performed about it. He turned with the breakfast in his hand and saw her face tipped up — bare, luminous, eyes full of starlight — and the smile that broke across his face was the involuntary kind, the one reserved for the sight of someone dearly loved.

He bent and kissed her.

No engineered accidents. No designed micro-beats. Lips met, warmth traded, one touch and apart — and the current of feeling running between their eyes was thick enough to grade and cut.

They separated. They smiled at each other — hers sweet and sated with a spoiled-wife softness; his indulgent and warm, open fondness sitting plainly at the bottom of it.

Everything exactly where it belonged.

“CUT!!! Beautiful! That’s the take! MOVING ON!” Kwak released a lungful of relief and finally smiled. “Should’ve been like that from the start! Hold that state!”

Laughter and applause loosened across the set.

She unwound her arms from his waist, touched her faintly buzzing lips, and found the ledger fully balanced. She looked up at him, eyes glittering, and said, small: “This time — it really passed.”

He looked down at her, reached out, and lightly pinched one still-pink cheek — teasing, with something unreadable folded in. “Mm. Passed. After this many rounds of ‘instruction’ from Teacher Sulli, my lips were about to unionize.”

The blush deepened; the laugh escaped anyway — and inside, a whole jar of honey tipped over and kept pouring.

Mission accomplished. And the process… the process was VERY good for the heart. Also — Oppa didn’t hate it. He didn’t hate it at ALL.

That one conclusion floated her through the entire rest of the day — the afternoon’s setups sailed, and the sweetness kept escaping from the corners of her eyes and mouth no matter how professionally she pressed it down. The crew, watching the “newlyweds” drift between scripted and unscripted pink, traded the smiles of people who had all signed the same unspoken NDA.

Inside the film, Seo Yeon-woo and Go-i’s newlywed days ran sugared and warm.

Outside it, something unnamed kept growing in the space between takes — NG by NG, glance by glance — clearer every day, and more combustible.

When the shoot wraps — I confess. For real. From little sister to girlfriend. Hehe.
