Chapter Twenty-Five

The Drug

Ji-ho stepped out of the break room, swept the corridor once, and picked the direction where the light ran thin and yellow — old habit; quiet phone calls lived in dim corners. A short walk brought him to a storeroom off the kitchen. He pulled out his phone and returned Wendy’s call.

Outgoing call · Wendy

“Hey — Seung-wan-ah.”

“Ji-ho! Where did you disappear to? I looked all over for you at free practice!”

“Working. Part-time gig. Why — miss me already?”

“It’s not— you bought me coffee this morning, so I was going to buy you one back. That’s ALL.”

“Wait. Don’t tell me that’s been sitting on your mind the entire day.”

“It has NOT! I was just— it was just a question!”

“And here I thought a girl finally cared about me. Tragic. Truly.”

“YAH! Ji-ho! I’m hanging up!”

— call ended —

He pocketed the phone, entirely unaware he’d just spent two minutes flustering his newest friend into orbit. Years of easy living had left him with exactly one default setting around pretty girls: tease first, think later. Debt to her helper’s heart: one coffee, compounding daily.

He was turning to leave when footsteps sounded outside — close, coming closer. Then a voice:

“Yah — the stuff. Where is it?”

“Right here, right here. Choi hyung, aren’t you supposed to be all-in on Sulli these days? Been forever since you came out hunting with us.”

Drugs? SULLI?

Ji-ho’s whole body went cold and fast at once. He dropped behind a corner shelf stacked deep with clutter, folding into the dark like he’d been born there.

K-chak.

The door opened. Two men came in, one behind the other.

“Yun Seong-sik. Less commentary. Hand it over.”

The man called Yun Seong-sik held up a small pill vial, smirking — and when Choi Jae reached for it, snatched it back out of range.

“Ehh — hold on, hold on. You’re not planning to dose Sulli with this, are you?”

Choi Jae. The vial. Sulli. The pieces slammed together, and Ji-ho understood precisely what he had walked into. He eased his phone out, thumbed the camera to video, zeroed every sound in his body, and aimed through the gap in the shelving.

“What are you spouting? It’s for a little model I’ve got my eye on. Fresh debut.”

“Nah. Nah, that doesn’t wash. You told the group chat this morning — tonight’s your Sulli night, don’t wait up. Choi hyung. It’s ME. How many times have we run this play together? You’re really going to keep secrets from your own brother?”

The two of them had run enough dirty errands side by side that neither could sink without drowning the other — the kind of “trust” that only exists between men holding knives to each other’s ribs.

“Enough talk. The vial. Give.”

No denial. Which was its own confession.

Yun Seong-sik’s lip curled — all that big talk in front of the brothers, “three months and Sulli’s mine,” and here he is needing chemistry after all — but what came out was oily concern.

“Choi hyung, if it’s really Sulli, I can’t hand this over. She’s not some nobody, not some fresh college kid. Anyone who’s climbed to her level of fame is sharp as they come. This isn’t a girl you smooth over after the fact with an envelope and some sweet talk.”

“And what would you know about it?” Smugness fattened every word. “We’re not even dating and she’s already in love with me. Trusts me like a child. Tomorrow morning I pet her hair, tell her it’s all fine — and I save myself the confession and the gifts.”

“HAH! Sulli? In love with YOU?” The laugh was open mockery now. “Hyung. Lie to the brothers all you want, but don’t start believing your own material. Look at yourself. You’ve got twenty years on her, no money, no face. What could she possibly want from you?”

“The twenty years IS what she wants.” He preened as he said it. “Her dad divorced out when she was seven — gone, vanished. Her mother shipped her to SM to earn and never looked back. That girl is a stray dog on a cold street, you understand?”

“Reach for a stray too fast, it bites you. But toss it a bone — feed it a little, sweet-talk it daily — and soon it’s rolling at your feet begging to be touched. Walk it home on a string. And at THAT point—” the voice turned syrupy with satisfaction, “—you can kick it, scream at it, doesn’t matter. It’ll decide it deserved it, and grovel twice as hard to win you back.”

“All I did was ask about her day. Warm words, morning and night, cost of zero. She was so moved she bought me the newest iPhone for my birthday — unprompted. She’s a stray terrified of being abandoned, and I’m the one who picked her up. Watch how I train her after tonight.”

“And her name prints money, brother. Her fame is about to become MY career.”

Shelf-shadow held Ji-ho perfectly still while his blood moved like ground glass. The phone hand did not waver.

Yun Seong-sik’s grin had drained away somewhere in the middle of the speech — not out of conscience, but calculus. Sulli. THE Sulli. This toad is actually going to land her? The envy in his eyes was nearly chemical. He produced the vial slowly, still hedging.

“Hyung, if she’s really that obedient already — why risk the stuff at all? If this goes sideways, it goes all the way sideways.”

“Because tonight she humiliated me. In front of everyone.” The words came through clenched teeth, and for one unguarded moment the mask was fully off. “I don’t swallow that. It gets settled tonight. Besides—” a shrug, obscene in its lightness, “—she’s depressed. Certified. She already takes sleeping pills by the fistful. Even if she turns out to be some chastity martyr and runs to the police — nothing traces. Nothing sticks.”

Depression? If anything, Yun Seong-sik relaxed — the last risk factor, resolved. He held the vial out.

“It’s yours. One condition.”

“Name it.”

“Good hyung eats the meat…” — hands rubbing together, the grin sliding into something that belonged in a sewer — “…good hyung leaves his little brother a taste.”

Choi Jae’s face went iron-dark. His fist closed on Yun Seong-sik’s collar and yanked.

“Yah — you ssibal son of a—”
