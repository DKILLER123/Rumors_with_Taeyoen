Chapter Two Hundred

Six of Girls’ Generation at His Door

June 4, 2013 · 23–24°C, scattered rain LOEN lounge → SM practice room → the hillside villa one “housewarming,” six volunteers, and a doorbell he will remember forever

June 4. Seoul sat muggy under scattered rain, the air thick enough to lean on.

Early afternoon, in the LOEN lounge, he scrolled the official account of The Girl in the Sun. Since the first making-of wave on June 1, and his and Sulli’s synchronized stills-and-reflections posts on the 3rd, the comment sections had caught fire.

Sulli’s fans, notably, kept voting to hand her over. HIS fans were staging an armed counter-protest. The comment wars raged on schedule.

His phone buzzed — Dong-hwan, forwarding a headline.

Daily Entertainment · front page · reporter Park Min-hwan
A Brilliant Half-Year: Korean Film and Television, 2013 — Records, Tears, and the Return of Pure Love
DAILY ENTERTAINMENT · June 4, 2013

If one phrase can hold the first half of 2013, it is: an embarrassment of riches. Miracle in Cell No. 7 passed twelve million admissions to become the all-time domestic box-office champion — a father, a daughter, a prison cell, and every theater in the country converted into a public tear duct: proof that the plainest feelings carry the greatest force.

It did not reign alone. The Berlin File drew over seven million with hard-edged espionage action — overseas critics are calling it “the new benchmark for the Asian spy film” — while New World, the licensed remake of Infernal Affairs, took 4.68 million admissions into the gray zones of the undercover soul. Lee Jung-jae, Hwang Jung-min, Choi Min-sik: every glance a scene.

Television kept pace. That Winter, the Wind Blows paired Jo In-sung and Song Hye-kyo to a 15.3% peak; Gu Family Book held ten straight weeks atop the Mon–Tue slot at 19.5%; and just yesterday SBS premiered I Can Hear Your Voice — Lee Bo-young and Lee Jong-suk, a 7.8% debut and word of mouth already compounding. The industry consensus: summer’s next big thing.

It would be. A certified monster hit, in fact. Say what you would about this peninsula — THIS, it knew how to make.

He was mid-scroll when the call banner dropped from the top of the screen: Taeyeon. The smile arrived before the thumb did.

Voice Call · Taeyeon ♥

“Ji-ho-ya — what are you doing?”

“Checking how the film promo’s landing. Nuna?”

“Walking concert blocking. Mid-break.” — her breath still slightly quick from rehearsal. “Missed you. So I called.” A pause, and then, carefully casual: “Are you… free tonight?”

“I’m free right NOW.”

“Now?” — a blink he could hear.

June 8 and 9: the world tour’s opening shows at Olympic Gymnastics Arena, and after that a calendar with no seams in it. His film promo launched tomorrow. Which made tonight — arithmetic any couple could do — possibly the last real evening for two months.

And the members had been drowning in the practice room for weeks. Half an afternoon off wouldn’t sink the tour… right? Work-rest balance was PERFORMANCE SCIENCE, actually.

Her heartbeat picked up a little on the other end. She put on the pout he could also hear.

Voice Call · continued

“I’m off this afternoon too and — I’ve never even SEEN your house. Today I’ll come do a housewarming for you. Yes?”

Housewarming.

The smile froze on his face while his stomach performed a controlled demolition.

His house. The hillside villa. Currently featuring: Joo-hyun’s body wash and lotion in the shower rack, her skincare samplers colonizing the vanity, her sleepwear in the closet, her slippers in the entry cabinet—

The refusal rose — and died in his throat. A girlfriend asking to see her boyfriend’s home was not merely reasonable; it was overdue. Any deflection would glow in the dark.

Between one spark and the next, he pivoted to delight.

Voice Call · continued

“Really? PERFECT! I’ve been meaning to invite you forever!” — a beat; a measured wrinkle of difficulty — “Only, I just remembered: some film-promo process stuff needs confirming. Tell you what — I’ll send the address. Come around four? I swear I’ll have everything wrapped by then.”

“OK!” — audibly satisfied, sweetness rising through the line. Then, suddenly low and fast: “Yoon-a’s coming — hanging up. Mua~ saranghae-yo~”

“Mua.”

He hung up, stood in one motion, and was moving before the chair stopped rolling. Past Dong-hwan’s temporary desk he dropped exactly one sentence:

“Hyung — emergency at home, going back!”

“Eh? What emergency? The fan café — you promised you’d show your—”

The elevator had already eaten him.

Home. NOW. Operation Deep Clean — maximum classification.

✦   ✦   ✦

SM Entertainment, the Girls’ Generation practice room.

Taeyeon hung up in an excellent mood, turned — and met Yoon-a’s face at point-blank range, wearing the exact smile of a small fox that has located the henhouse.

High ponytail, full clean forehead, swan neck; the famous doe eyes glittering with mischief.

“Eonni~~ that was Ji-ho on the phone, wasn’t it? SO sweet~”

“You knew. Why ask?” The mood was too good for combat — which freed up capacity for mischief of her own.

She rose without warning and — with the speed of a striking snake — delivered one measured swat to the seat of Yoon-a’s jeans.

“AH!” Yoon-a clutched the afflicted region, cheeks flaming, staring at her leader. “Taeyeon eonni! You— PERVERT!”

Interior monologue, at volume: this ABSOLUTE tyrant. Abusing leader privileges!! If it weren’t for basic respect for the office, I’d have you over my knee — see if I don’t—

“Yoon-a.” Deeply satisfied with the tactile report, Taeyeon issued orders through a grin. “Round everyone up. Announcement.”

“…Ne, eonni.”

The rank-and-file scattered like sand on breaks, phones optional — but Yoon-a knew every member’s habitat. In minutes she had retrieved: Tiffany savoring coffee in the pantry; Yuri wind-bathing on the roof; Jessica starfished flat on the floor in exquisite blankness; even Soo-young, eavesdropping on trainee gossip through a bathroom wall.

“Haam~” Jessica arrived mid-yawn, expensive as a cat, fresh rose lipstick doing wars against her pallor. She propped herself on the mirror wall. “Taeyeon-ah. What does the company want NOW?”

Taeyeon surveyed her assembled members and felt, briefly, the guilt of a leader about to commit abuse of office. She cleared her throat and installed the official face.

“Ahem. So. These weeks — everyone’s genuinely worked hard for the concert. Our formations and blocking are solid; from tomorrow it’s venue rehearsal anyway. THEREFORE—” she paused for effect, watching curiosity climb, “—this afternoon: FULL REST. Free activity. Recover properly, fight better tomorrow!”

One beat of silence.

“REST?!”

“WAAAH!!!”

The room detonated into cheers and applause, exhaustion converting instantly to joy.

“Taeyeon! THE GREATEST!”

“Taeyeon! Crispy-bones!”

“Milk-skin Taeyeon! Beautiful AND benevolent!”

Tiffany blinked her signature crescents, pleased but dutiful. “DaeDae — the encore stages matter SO much this tour, I really think a few of the key transitions could use another pass—”

“YAH! Tiffany! Shut UP!”

Soo-young crossed the room in approximately one stride and clamped a hand over Tiffany’s mouth from behind, all limbs and outrage.

“YOU don’t have a boyfriend to date. I DO! If you’re bored, go shopping, go drinking — do NOT sabotage my precious date window!”

Who says I don’t have a boyfriend, Tiffany argued from behind the hand — and her eyes flicked, involuntarily, to Taeyeon, and dimmed one stop. Right. Can’t say it. That’s all.

“Having a boyfriend is NOT a personality.” Yuri peeled Tiffany free of the maknae-line’s grip. “Fany — if you’re free this afternoon: shopping with me, drinks tonight. Yes?”

Shopping? Drinks? The crescent eyes re-lit at once. As nominal discipline officer she had principles — several — none of which had ever survived three minutes of direct temptation.

“Yes yes YES!”

Yuri nodded, satisfied, and began the roll call. “Hyo-yeon? Boyfriend this afternoon?”

“Obviously.” Zero hedging, full grin.

“Taeyeon?” One raised eyebrow. “Look at that face — off to the land of two, clearly?”

Taeyeon smiled with visible sugar content. “Mm… I’m going to Ji-ho’s house. To do a housewarming.”

“He MOVED?”

“Aniya~” A shake of the head; more color in the cheeks. “It’s just — my first time going to his place.”

“YAH.” Yuri executed a theatrical eye-roll, hands on hips. “Your FIRST visit is not a housewarming.”

“Mind your business!” The pale neck stiffened with full righteousness. “This way it’s MORE commemorative. MORE romantic. A problem?!”

Yuri deployed the internationally recognized expression for “I decline to argue with this person,” clapped twice, and summarized: “FINE. Status confirmed: Kim Hyo-yeon, Choi Soo-young, Taeyeon — three traitors to the organization, defecting to coupledom for the afternoon. Remaining members of the singles’ alliance: who’s joining me and Fany for shopping and drinks?”

Ji-ho’s HOUSE?

Sunny — silent this whole time, observing from the mirror wall like a small well-fed strategist — lit up, eyes spinning visibly.

His house. His HOUSE. Would the height secret be IN the house?? Some kind of device? Special milk in the fridge?? Reconnaissance opportunity: PRICELESS.

Her hand shot up at maximum velocity. “ME! ME!”

“Excellent! Sunny, one!” Yuri beamed. “Who else?”

“Ani—” Sunny waved both hands urgently. “Not the drinking party. I mean — I’m going to Ji-ho’s housewarming!”

“…Mwo?!”

Taeyeon’s head snapped around, eyes locking onto Sunny with open menace. What is this woman UP to now?

And while that stare was still landing, Yoon-a’s eyes performed several fast rotations of their own.

Ji-ho’s house? PERFECT. Last month: her birthday. Which that WRETCH forgot. Completely. Last year he remembered — sent a gift and everything. THIS year she’d been touring Japan on his birthday and still hand-picked a present and had it couriered. And him? Film promo and NOTHING else on his plate, and not even a TEXT. Stood on an Alp saying he liked me — PAH. Today, accounts get settled. In person.

“Me!” Her hand went up, voice ringing, smile sweet and harmless, one glint of cold light passing across the bottom of her eyes. “I’m also going to Ji-ho’s housewarming.”

Taeyeon stared at her, fully bricked. And YOON-A?? What is HAPPENING?

And before she could process THAT — a cool, unhurried voice:

“I’ll go too.”

The practice room went silent as a sealed vault. Every head rotated, spotlight-style, to Jessica.

A HOUSEWARMING? JESSICA?? The registered spokesmodel of the national lying-down movement — will recline if sitting is possible, will sit if standing is threatened, hobbies: blankness and sleep — VOLUNTEERING for a social visit?? Had the sun risen in the west?

Under the combined disbelief of seven stares, Jessica pressed her lips together, one very faint pink crossing her face before the cool mask reasserted — with a defensive bristle.

“WHAT. Ji-ho is a talented person with real ideas. That — that little misunderstanding from before is long resolved; we’re FRIENDS now. A friend attends a friend’s housewarming. Is there a PROBLEM?”

Delivered almost smoothly — except for the flicker in the eyes and the half-beat rush in the cadence, which leaked exactly one drop of Not Usual.

Meanwhile, one seat over, a different calculus was collapsing toward a decision. Taeyeon gets to go to his house, Tiffany thought, with the sour bubbles already rising — his actual girlfriend gets the daylight version of him, and I get the shadows, no standing, no visiting rights. …But if Sunny’s going, and Yoon-a, and even SICA — then one more body in the crowd is invisible. And I get to see him. Legitimately.

“I— I’m going too!” Her hand shot up. “Ji— Ji-ho wrote me that whole song! For something as important as his housewarming, how could I NOT—”

The nickname nearly escaped containment; she swapped in the full name at the last available frame, heart hammering.

Taeyeon stood in the center of the room experiencing complete cognitive gridlock.

No. NO. “Housewarming” was code. It was a TWO-PERSON romantic-progress event. How has it become a GROUP EXCURSION?? Fany — Yoon-a — Sunny — SICA?! Are you people applying for positions as human light bulbs?!

She inhaled slowly, reaching for leader-grade composure.

“Eonni-deul—” Seohyun’s gentle voice rose; she was sitting with textbook posture, hands folded.

“YOU TOO?!” Taeyeon rounded on the maknae with wild eyes.

Seohyun shrank back a centimeter and waved both hands quickly, voice small. “A— aniyo. I was going to say — I’m going home to spend the evening with my mother.”

Taeyeon exhaled, long and slow, one vertebra at a time. Thank god. ONE normal human remains in this organization.

The exhale was not yet complete when Yuri — who had been spectating with rising delight — stroked her chin, looked from face to face, and let a grin of pure appetite spread.

Ji-ho’s house, versus ordinary shopping? A cast like this under one roof — entertainment GUARANTEED. Possibly premium alcohol as well.

“Sooo—” she flashed the full row of teeth, “—if all of you are going, there’ll be drinks, right? Count me in too?”

“KHUK— khk—!!”

The breath Taeyeon had been finishing went down the wrong pipe entirely. She coughed until her eyes watered, and through the tears surveyed the situation: Yuri’s face, on which was written in block letters I WANT THE SHOW and BRING ME ALCOHOL; and behind her, five members already self-organized into the Ji-ho Residence Housewarming Sightseeing Corps.

A profound powerlessness rolled through her entire body. Fine. FINE. When you owe eight debts you stop counting; when you host five bulbs, what’s one more. Yuri — whatever. WHATEVER.

She waved a limp hand: motion carried.

✦   ✦   ✦

Two-thirty p.m. The hillside villa. He hit the front door at a run.

Inventory of evidence, bedroom through bathroom: her silk robe over the chair back. Her skincare regiment on the vanity, in parade order. Her toothbrush standing in the cup — beside his. Her slippers in the entry cabinet.

“It’s over. It’s OVER—”

He narrated doom while executing: full bedding swap; a neutral-scent atomizer deployed against any lingering trace of gardenia; every personal item of Joo-hyun’s gathered, boxed, and locked into the guest-room cabinet. He circled the house twice, confirmed zero visible female presence — exhaled—

—and then thought: Taeyeon is not a careless woman. One missed hairpin. One sample-size lotion behind a mirror. One long dark hair on a cushion—

He rubbed his temples and initiated Plan B: the phone, the department store two neighborhoods down.

“Yes — one complete set of women’s toiletries. And a few sets of women’s loungewear— Size? Mm… about 163 centimeters, slim. As fast as possible. The address is—”

Thirty minutes later a courier delivered one large box, and he staged it through the master bath and bedroom with a set-dresser’s care: a prepared-for-Taeyeon domestic tableau.

Thus: any overlooked artifact of Joo-hyun’s now had a cover story with a receipt.

Three-thirty. He collapsed onto the sofa, regarded the mountain view, and confronted the structural issue.

Was this not — the textbook comorbidity of rowing multiple boats? And the acute complication: what if Joo-hyun and Taeyeon ever arrived the same night? What if they both wanted to STAY?

“…Need to buy real estate. Urgently.”

He said it to the empty room. Not one property. Several. And frankly, 2013 Seoul residential was a screaming buy — Hannam-dong, Cheongdam, Nonhyeon — the appreciation curve ahead was practically vertical. Pure asset allocation. Sound portfolio construction. The fact that it also prevented catastrophic girlfriend collisions was — a minor ancillary benefit. Mostly. Three units, then: one for Fany. One for Joo-hyun. One for Taeyeon and himself—

He was two listings deep when the doorbell rang.

3:50. Early — eager. Cute.

He straightened his shirt, calibrated the welcome-to-my-home smile, and opened the door.

The smile died where it stood.

On the doorstep: Taeyeon at point, cheeks faintly pink, eyes sweet with one visible gram of apology suspended in them. And deployed behind her in loose formation — Yoon-a. Sunny. Jessica. Tiffany. Yuri.

Six women, six entirely different styles, one collectively excessive quantity of dazzle — arrayed across his doorstep like a weather event.

Each carrying, with ceremony, a gift-boxed bale of toilet paper.

Six pairs of eyes: curiosity, investigation, mischief, anticipation, cool appraisal — and one unmistakable ration of vinegar.

All of it aimed at him, simultaneously.

His scalp went numb in a single continuous wave, crown to nape.

What. — WHAT. — What is this situation???
