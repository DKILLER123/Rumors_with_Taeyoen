Early Next Morning Girls’ Generation Dorm a guilty conscience, and a measuring tape

Taeyeon turned the dorm lock like a safecracker and slipped inside.

Silence. Curtains open, light bright. Yuri was in the kitchen with bluetooth earbuds in, humming, assembling one of her alleged health drinks. No contact. Taeyeon exhaled and glided to her room.

He’d been IMPOSSIBLE this morning — clingy beyond reason — and they’d fooled around so long she’d had to run for it without even showering. Where did the man get the stamina? Her sample size was exactly one, but she was fairly sure he outperformed the films she wasn’t supposed to have watched.

Door shut, one ear cocked: Sunny’s breathing from the far bed, even and slow. Still asleep. Into the bathroom.

The hot water ran a long time. She was thorough.

Half an hour later she stood at the mirror, wiped the fog away, and found a woman with scandalously good color looking back. She checked the faint pink mark near her collarbone. High collar today. Sealed and airtight.

She toweled off, opened the closet, and chose her favorites: light skinny jeans, a red-and-white checked high-neck blouse with puffed sleeves. Great cut, great stretch, legs for days. She pulled the jeans on, zipped, buttoned—

And stopped.

She looked down. A small strip of bare ankle showed above the hem — the cuff riding distinctly higher than memory placed it. She straightened, and the wrongness sharpened: through the thighs and seat, the lines the denim drew seemed… longer? Not weight — the fullness ran VERTICALLY, a strange taut unfamiliarity, as if the bones themselves had quietly renegotiated their spacing.

“…Shrank in the wash. Right?”

She frowned and rotated in the mirror.

“Oh? Our Taeyeon’s home~?”

She nearly left her skin. Sunny leaned in the doorframe, short hair at maximum entropy, eyes half-open, smile fully loaded.

“You SCARED me! Do you not make sounds when you walk?!”

“Me? I’M the quiet one? Somebody came home like a trained thief.” A yawn; an unhurried amble into the room; one slow scan, head to toe. “So~ where were we last night? Phone rang and rang, nobody home.”

“I went HOME. To my house. Is going to my own house strange now?” She addressed the mirror with tremendous casualness, adjusting her hair.

“Hooome?” Sunny stretched the vowel like taffy, eyes narrowing. “That house you JUST bought — that’s the one you can’t stay away from? Or is it… someone IN the house?”

“Yah! Lee Soon-kyu! What man would I even HAVE!”

Sunny had, notably, not said “man.” The lady’s own guilty conscience was doing the prosecution’s work. And the answer to “who,” Sunny was fairly sure she could already sketch. But — adults. Taeyeon didn’t want to say; Sunny wouldn’t dig.

“Fine, fine. Home is home.” She padded over barefoot to check her bird’s-nest hair in Taeyeon’s mirror—

—and mid-yawn, stopped.

She blinked. Looked at the mirror. Looked sideways at Taeyeon, currently tugging at her too-short cuffs.

Wrong. The ANGLE was wrong.

Sunny unconsciously straightened her full 155 centimeters. She knew this geometry cold — years of standing next to slippered Taeyeon, the exact familiar tilt of looking slightly up. And right now the tilt was… steeper?

“What are you staring at?” Taeyeon tugged her collar higher on reflex.

Sunny didn’t answer. Eyes narrowed, she orbited Taeyeon once, slowly, like a customs inspector circling a suspicious crate, gaze pausing pointedly at the waist and legs.

“You—” chin in hand, “—are you wearing LIFTS?”

“Lifts? In my house slippers?” Taeyeon lifted one foot and waggled the thin-soled slipper. “Look at it! Am I insane?”

True — the cat-face slippers were last month’s joint purchase; Sunny had the matching pair. But the discrepancy was getting LOUDER. She stepped in abruptly.

“Shoes off!”

“Huh?”

“Off! Now!”

Rattled — is there some other MARK I missed?! — Taeyeon kicked off the slippers and stood barefoot. Sunny seized her, spun her, and slammed them back-to-back.

“Don’t MOVE!” Spine at maximum extension, occiput pressed to occiput.

The data came through immediately, and it was wrong. The old differential — a familiar two, three centimeters — had become an unmistakable SHELF. Five centimeters if it was one. And Taeyeon felt it too: the crown of Sunny’s head, riding lower against her own skull than memory allowed. The too-short cuffs. The tight jeans. The robe that had migrated from heel to ankle. A thought — impossible, ridiculous — lifted its head.

“No way—” Sunny turned, staring up at her face with naked shock. “Taeyeon-ah. Did you… GROW?”

“Don’t be absurd! I’m twenty-four!” — with a voice that betrayed structural uncertainty.

“But it’s WRONG! It’s absolutely wrong!” On height differentials — particularly within the short-members’ union — Sunny’s instruments were calibrated to the millimeter. She cast around the room. “The height chart! We bought that stick-on wall chart, where—”

A frantic excavation of the storage cabinet produced the dusty roll. Sunny slapped it against the wall.

“Quick! Stand! Back straight! Heels to the wall!”

Taeyeon swallowed and took position — hope and absurdity wrestling openly on her face. Head, shoulder blades, heels to the wall. Sunny drew the slider down flat onto her crown, and followed it to the number.

And froze.

“How much?”

Nothing. Sunny’s face was performing several incompatible emotions at once.

“How MUCH already!”

Taeyeon twisted to look.

160.

The line sat clean and unarguable at one hundred sixty centimeters.

The air congealed. Taeyeon’s mouth opened. She blinked hard, twice. The number stayed.

“That’s impossible.” Sunny inspected the chart itself, laughed the laugh of a woman whose physics had failed her, and got loud. “160?! YAH! Taeyeon! You measured 158! The last physical said 158! What IS this?!”

“I— I don’t know—”

They measured again. 160. Again. 160. Four times, five — Sunny stole a second tape from someone else’s room on suspicion of instrument failure — 160. 160.

After the final reading, Taeyeon stood dazed against the wall while Sunny sat down flat on the floor, staring up at her like a newly documented alien species.

“160…” she breathed, cosmology visibly rearranging. “You really… grew two centimeters. At twenty-four. GREW.”

She scrambled up and grabbed Taeyeon’s arm.

“Yah! Confess! What have you been EATING? Some miracle exercise? Did you get your bones done?? No— you can’t stealth-grow two centimeters from surgery, that’s not—”

Taeyeon barely heard her. The world’s audio had dropped out, leaving only her own accelerating heartbeat.

160. Not 158. Not 159. 160. Since seventeen, the number had never moved. Seven years, countless measurements, a private fantasy every single time — and the answer had always been the same cruel 158. And now—

A thin wire pulled taut in her mind, and that morning flashed through whole: him coming out of the kitchen with the milk. The “dream.” “When you meet the person you’ll love for a lifetime… the body secretes a special hormone… you can even grow again.” That gentle smile. And the sentence that had set off a tsunami in her chest: “I’ve met MY nuna.”

It wasn’t sweet talk. Or — not ONLY sweet talk.

A shiver of pure electricity climbed her spine, tailbone to nape.

Not an illusion. Not a measurement error. It was Ji-ho. It was that glass of milk.

She slid slowly down the wall and hugged her knees, cheeks flushing with something enormous. Fate had put its omen in a dream. Their meeting was DESTINY. Hormones, second growth phases — every clinical word now wore its true, romantic meaning: fate, made flesh. A magic that broke the rules of the ordinary world, and manifested for exactly one person. Her.

Every small anxiety — the age gap, the uncertain future — dissolved without residue in the face of an actual, measurable miracle. If her own body could change because of him, toward something better — then what exactly was impossible? Their love carried heaven’s stamp. All changes that followed from it were gifts. And any “bad woman” attempting to approach him was now, by definition, a ridiculous creature setting herself against DESTINY ITSELF.

She buried her burning face in her knees. The small anxious, prideful animal that had paced inside her chest for months finally lay down, purring.

She thought of the morning’s overbearing kisses; of the marks she was hiding under a high collar; of the shameless skill with which he could take her apart; of the near-cardiac event triggered by the word “wife.” And now all of it had a BODY. She had grown taller. Because of him.

An uncontrollable, thoroughly silly smile spread across her face. Would he notice, next time? Would he watch her with those laughing eyes, as if to say: see? I told you. Nuna is my destiny.

Sunny’s interrogation? Anyone else’s questions? She lifted her head, met her own spring-lit reflection in the mirror, and produced one soft “hmph.”

This is ours. Fate notarized it. What could anyone else possibly understand? I’ll simply drink every glass of milk he hands me — and wait, in perfect security, for the rest of the destiny to arrive. The height. And the life.

Ji-eun. With WHAT, exactly, were you planning to beat me?
