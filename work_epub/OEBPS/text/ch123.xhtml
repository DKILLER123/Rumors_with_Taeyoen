Chapter One Hundred Twenty-Three

“Strawberry Gummies”

The arrangement for the morning-glory song took him exactly one day. He sent the full instrumental to IU — the lyric baton now formally hers — and turned to the mountain that actually mattered: his first full album.

Field Notes

The four album formats, for the record: the single (one title track, maybe an instrumental — fast comebacks, market tests, drama tie-ins); the mini-album (one title plus two to five tracks — the idol industry’s standard vehicle, cheaper and more frequent); the FULL album (one or two titles plus eight to thirteen tracks — the complete artistic statement, the sales vehicle, the tour foundation); and the repackage (an existing album plus a few new songs — a second promotional life).

A soloist’s first statement, with “Some” money fattening the production budget, could only be one thing: a full album.

He settled the title after one thought: SUNYE — his fans’ own name. Let the first album arrive as a gift addressed to them.

Twelve tracks planned. In hand: “If You” and “Eyes, Nose, Lips” as double titles, “A Boy” as a B-side. Nine slots open — and he had no intention of padding them with filler. The plan: mine his memory for the highest-grade ballads the next decade would produce, and add one original of his own as a test of his growing craft.

The original’s subject, naturally: love.

Why did ninety percent of gifted singer-songwriters live such colorful lives? Because nothing generates raw material like romance — grief, joy, bliss, ambiguity, despair, the whole palette on demand. At his current level, writing a sweet little love song wasn’t hard; he and Taeyeon were a whole songbook of material. Writing a GOOD one — that took work.

8:00 PM LOEN — Production Room a floor of crumpled paper

Ji-ho ripped off his headphones, crushed another sheet of manuscript into a ball, and pitched it at the floor, where a small landfill of its siblings already waited.

“Wrong. ALL of it’s wrong.”

Every draft was sweet — sweet like a formula. Chord progressions off the shelf, melody lines trying too hard; he could hear his own frown in them. What he wanted: simple, instantly catchy arrangement, lyrics that stuck on one listen. And here was the ceiling of pure theory — guitar and piano were the obvious sugar, but making those ancient instruments GRAB was another matter entirely.

Mid-frustration, his phone lit.

He grabbed jacket and guitar case, cleared it with his manager, and drove out alone.

Two blocks from the company, Star Counter-Surveillance pinged. He checked the mirror: a black sedan easing out of a parking spot behind him.

Oho. Paparazzi. Officially famous enough to be followed. A milestone of sorts.

Expression unchanged, he drove on — and stopped at a fruit shop, cap and mask on, and bought a bag of tangerines, a box of strawberries, a box of flat peaches. All her favorites.

“Imo, could you bag these for me? Oh — and one more bag of pears, separately, please.”

“Of course, sonnim.”

He came out carrying the fruit, located the tail car parked at an amateurish distance, and — without a moment’s hesitation — walked straight at it.

Inside the sedan:

“Hyung, that’s his car right there — so where’d HE go?”

“How would I know where he went!” The wiry man in the passenger seat was in a mood. “I TOLD you to stay close! CLOSE!”

“You literally told me to hang back—” the chubby driver muttered.

“What was that?”

“Nothing, nothing.”

Knock, knock.

Both men levitated. Ji-ho stood at the window, mask and sunglasses off, smiling pleasantly. Utterly Unremarkable: he had walked the entire way in plain sight.

“Aish— when did he get HERE?!”

The window came down with great reluctance. The wiry man attempted “delighted surprise.”

“Oh! You’re— you’re that celebrity, what’s-his— Song something—”

“I know you’re reporters.” He passed the bag of pears through the window with both hands, courteous as a holiday visit. “You’ve worked hard, both of you.”

“Well then — I’ll be going first.”

Mask up. Gone. Two men sat blinking at a bag of pears.

“Hyung… so what now? Keep following?”

“Follow WHAT.” The wiry man looked down at the pears and sighed from the depths. “The man personally asked us to leave.”

890309. The lock clicked. The apartment was lit; the shower was running. He set everything down, took the sofa, and pulled out paper and pen, scribbling the night’s keywords: staircase. lip balm. strawberry.

He tried a few progressions on the guitar. Close. Not there. The sweetness kept snagging on something and refusing to pour.

“Haah.”

He flopped over on the sofa, chin on his arms, writing and crossing out. The living room went quiet.

The bathroom door opened. Taeyeon padded out barefoot in a cream bathrobe, skin flushed and citrus-scented—

Odd. The robe — brand-new, worn twice — seemed shorter? It used to brush her heels. Now: ankles. And those jeans a few days ago had gone strangely tight and uncomfortable—

She scratched her nose — and then spotted the familiar figure sprawled on her sofa, and every wardrobe mystery lost its funding. Her mouth curved. She crept close, and bent to look over his shoulder.

“Strawberry Gummies.”

The title sat neat and centered at the top of the page.

Her breath stopped.

She held the bent posture, collar slipping, collarbones still wearing beads of water, and read down the scattered fragments of draft: a secret kept on the seventh step… your favorite gummies… strawberry arriving before the kiss…

Her ears went red at observable speed.

That night. The first kiss. The strawberry gummies he’d brought her. The stairwell landing — seven steps up. Where everything between them quietly turned true.

He remembered. All of it. In finer grain than she did.

Something molten went out from her heart along every vessel to her fingertips. She wanted, suddenly and physically, to hold him — to DO something with all this heat.

He was frowning at the empty chorus staves, pen hovering, landing nowhere.

“Inspiration. Inspiration, inspiration…” He scrubbed at his hair.

The creative wall was total; he never sensed her standing there.

She straightened, and withdrew to the bedroom on silent feet.

Five minutes later: two pale arms slid around his neck from behind. He smelled damp citrus first — and then a thread of strawberry, sweeter, closer, warmer.

“Close your eyes.”

Her lips were at his ear, warm and slightly wet.

He turned over and obeyed.

The next second, the softest possible weight landed on his mouth — carrying a half-melted strawberry gummy, ridiculously sweet. She pressed the melting candy gently across; strawberry bloomed through both their breaths, tangled with the steam still rising off her skin — something exactly between innocence and its opposite folding around him.

The kiss lasted until the candy was entirely gone.

She drew back a few centimeters, breathing unevenly, eyes enormous and water-bright.

“Now — any inspiration?”

He stared at her: robe gone loose at the shoulder, damp fair skin, lips glossed pink with sugar, a waterdrop tracing down from her collarbone toward points unrecorded.

His throat moved. He shook his head. Honestly.

She laughed — turned, took another gummy between her lips, and this time settled astride his lap, took his face in both hands, and kissed him properly. Deeper. Slower. The candy melted between them into warm syrup; every renegotiated breath carried its own small current. Her hands found their way under his T-shirt; fingertips grazed his stomach; the shiver that went through him was full-body.

The kiss changed category. The robe’s belt resigned at some unrecorded moment. The sofa registered formal complaints. Manuscript paper and pencil slid to the floor, unmissed. His palm crossed the warm smooth landscape of her back; she hummed against his mouth — a small cat-claw of a sound that raked directly across his least defended nerve.

When everything had settled, only the lamplight was left standing.

She was asleep where she’d curled on the sofa, thoroughly spent — robe loosely gathered, faint warm marks at her shoulder, a last unfinished trace of sugar at the corner of her lips.

Ji-ho — having labored honorably for the better part of an hour — was, by contrast, blazingly awake. The creative pressure that had been jammed all evening was now moving through him like floodwater with somewhere to go.

He tidied quietly, eased himself up, retrieved paper and pencil from the floor, and began to write like a man taking dictation.

All of it went in — transposed into melody and image: the soft weight of her settling astride him; the warmth of breath when a kiss turns serious; the small hum in her throat as the candy gave way; and the sleepy, unconscious syllables of his own name as she’d nuzzled into his neck at the end. The chorus assembled itself around the seventh stair and the melting candy and the strawberry balm she’d once worn because he’d said he liked it — sweetness stacked on secret on sweetness. The bridge went quiet and remembering: a dark stairwell, a counted heartbeat, a girl stopping on the seventh step to look back — and strawberry arriving a half-second before the kiss.
