Chapter Two Hundred Fifty-One

The Sixth Lover

Ji-ho’s hillside villa · late morning the first draft closes, the real company begins, and a wager becomes a relationship

Jessica thought of the fashion proposal she had carried into the room with such confidence.

Now, it seemed painfully naïve.

She had assumed that loving fashion meant she could build a fashion company. Passion had carried her as far as brand colors, silhouettes, and campaign ideas—but she had never forced herself to answer the harder question: what could the product do better than anything already on the market?

“I…” Her throat felt dry. “I did make it too simple.”

Ji-ho’s expression remained gentle.

“You weren’t simple. You wanted to succeed so badly that you mistook the first direction you loved for the only direction available.”

“You have taste, resources, relationships, and a recognizable name. What you lacked was a model that combines those advantages instead of wasting them.”

Jessica lowered her eyes.

“You’re right. I understand styling, not technical design. I kept telling myself they were almost the same because admitting otherwise felt like giving up.”

When she looked up again, admiration had replaced the last of her defensiveness.

“Thank you for making me face it.”

Ji-ho shook his head.

“I didn’t reveal anything you didn’t already know. I only said the part you were avoiding.”

Jessica stared at him, then laughed.

“You really don’t know how to soften a sentence.”

“I do when softness helps. We’re discussing your future. Politeness that protects a bad plan is expensive.”

She drew a deep breath and sat straighter.

“Then we do it your way. Beauty and skincare. We either acquire a small brand with strong products or develop our own line with professionals.”

She hesitated before naming the figure.

“I can invest three billion won.”

Ji-ho raised an eyebrow.

“That much?”

“Savings from the past several years. I meant to use it for the fashion label.” She smiled ruefully. “If I hadn’t just bought a house, I could contribute more.”

“Three is enough. I’ll put in five. Eight billion won gives us room to make the first decisions properly.”

“We’ll settle ownership after we define responsibilities,” Ji-ho continued. “If you want to run the company, prepare to learn operations. If you don’t, we hire an experienced chief executive.”

His tone became more serious.

“But you cannot become a decorative founder. You’ll test products, document feedback, attend formulation reviews, lead campaign decisions, and put your own reputation behind the result.”

“Consumers should feel Jessica in the brand—your standards, your restraint, and the things you refuse to compromise.”

She nodded.

“I understand.”

“Quality is the floor, not a slogan. If we save money with poor ingredients, rush stability testing, or make claims the evidence cannot support, we won’t merely lose a product line. We’ll damage your name.”

Jessica watched him with an emotion she could not reduce to one word.

“You’re taking this seriously.”

“I agreed to build it with you. Why wouldn’t I?”

Her smile came quietly.

That was when she knew she had chosen correctly.

Not because he had accepted her proposal, and not because he had money she needed. He had not indulged her weak assumptions or treated the company as the price of access to her. He had examined the idea, exposed the risks, and begun protecting a future that did not yet exist.

A man like this…

She could already feel herself moving closer.

Perhaps three years would prove unnecessary. Perhaps the day she stopped resenting the other women—and admitted what he had become to her—would arrive much sooner.

Sunlight crossed the coffee table and warmed the closed fashion proposal between them.

Jessica touched its cover and laughed.

“I’m keeping this. When the beauty company succeeds, I’ll look back and remember how foolish my first plan was.”

“It wasn’t foolish. Without the first plan, you wouldn’t know what the second needed to solve.”

“When do we begin?”

Ji-ho considered the sequence.

“Now. First, a complete proposal. Then we decide whether acquisition or internal development is stronger. After that: management team, research partners, manufacturing, claims review, product architecture, and launch schedule.”

He met her eyes.

“There is a great deal to do.”

Jessica’s eyes curved.

“Then let’s start.”

✦ ✦ ✦

For several seconds, neither reached for the laptop.

Ji-ho’s mouth tilted with mischief.

“Someone told me that for three years I would help build her career, while she tried to fall in love with me. She also promised to behave like a girlfriend—within the boundary she set.”

Jessica looked at him.

Then, before pride could interfere, she leaned across and pressed a quick kiss to the corner of his mouth.

Her face turned scarlet immediately.

“I keep my word.”

Her voice softened.

“And today… I think I like you a little more than I did yesterday.”

Ji-ho drew her into his arms.

Jessica gave a startled breath, suddenly close enough to hear the change in his heartbeat.

“Yah—what are you doing?”

He answered by kissing her.

Surprise held her still for one heartbeat. Then the patience of the kiss gave her time to choose.

She did not pull away.

Her fingers loosened against his shirt. Her eyes closed, and the tension in her shoulders slowly disappeared. When she finally returned the kiss, the response was inexperienced but unmistakably sincere.

Morning light filled the room. For a brief interval, the company plan, the difficult future, and every woman neither of them wanted to name fell outside the circle of the sofa.

When they separated, color still warmed Jessica’s cheeks. She pressed her lips together and refused to meet his eyes.

Ji-ho smiled and brushed one finger lightly over the bridge of her nose.

“Cute.”

She looked up long enough to glare.

“Don’t tease me.”

“You kissed me first.”

“Who gave you permission to turn it into… that?”

Ji-ho sighed theatrically.

“A woman makes the first move, enjoys the result, and then opens an investigation.”

Jessica’s eyes narrowed.

“A woman? That sounds like extensive field experience, Chairman Song.”

“We were discussing the company,” he said quickly.

“Coward.”

He reopened the laptop.

“Whatever happens between us, you still have your own career to build. This will be difficult. You’ll meet chemists who dismiss your instincts, operators who care more about margins than skin, and retailers who value your fame but not your judgment.”

Jessica’s expression steadied.

“I know. I won’t retreat.”

“Good. Then we start with the proposal framework. What do you want the first customer to see?”

She thought for a moment.

“Accessible prestige. Women from twenty-five to forty—old enough to demand performance, young enough to still experiment, and with enough purchasing power to build a routine rather than buy one novelty item.”

“And the promise?”

“Gentle, transparent, effective. A fresh and natural image without pretending that ‘natural’ automatically means safe.”

Ji-ho nodded approvingly.

“Better. Every safety or efficacy claim must be substantiated. What products first?”

Jessica’s eyes brightened.

“Sheet masks. Hydrating toner. A serum with a clear function, not ten vague promises. And eye care—performers learn early how quickly exhaustion shows there.”

Ji-ho recorded the points.

“Online, we use social media and credible beauty reviewers,” Jessica said. “Offline, selected department-store counters and launch events. We could also work with reputable skincare clinics for controlled sampling—not endorsements disguised as medical advice.”

“Good. That distinction matters.”

Her chin rose with returning confidence.

“My direction was wrong before. That doesn’t mean I know nothing about beauty.”

“I never said you knew nothing.” Ji-ho reached over and ruffled her hair. “Your first assignment is naming: legal company name, consumer brand, and a logo direction. Then we register the company and begin acquisition research.”

“Me?”

“It’s your business, isn’t it?”

He closed the laptop again, but his next words carried a different weight.

“One condition, Sica. This year, the company cannot interfere with Girls’ Generation’s schedules.”

Jessica frowned.

“No interference at all?”

Ji-ho rose and walked to the window. Forest filled the view beyond the glass.

“Girls’ Generation contains thirteen years of your life—training, debut, success, and the years you survived together. You, the members, and the fans would all regret an ending that could never be repaired.”

Jessica joined him, puzzled.

“What ending? The group has already reached the top, and now my second career has a direction. What are you expecting to happen?”

He did not explain.

“Follow the rule.”

“All right.” She nodded, then hesitated. “But why only this year?”

Ji-ho looked through the glass at a future only he remembered.

“Because next year, I’ll be an SM shareholder.”
