Chapter Eighty-Five

Promotion to the Pre-Debut Team

Finally, Lee Soo-man turned to his music director.

“Young-jin — you’re right that caution is available to us. But sometimes TIMING matters more than perfect preparation. Right now he has the songs, the drive, and a startlingly clear read on himself. That IS the timing. Wait, and we may grind that edge off him — or watch the window close. As for sustained output…” a rare smile surfaced, “…I trust my eye. I trust pressure and environment to pull more out of him. And we have you, and KENZIE, and the entire production floor to catch him if he stumbles — don’t we?”

One more beat, delivered almost as an aside:

“And note which way the political wind is blowing. The administration is pushing hallyu’s global expansion harder than ever. An artist built for the international market, launching NOW — that alignment doesn’t come along twice.”

The speech had done what his speeches did: answered every objection on its own terms while reframing the entire board — weaknesses re-read as distinctiveness, a risky debut re-cast as a lever in the company’s longest game, with even a nod to the policy climate that everyone in this industry had learned to read like weather.

The room went quiet again — but the temperature had changed. The objectors wore thinking faces now. Lee Soo-man was founder, chief producer, and largest shareholder; when he backed a gamble THIS explicitly, the scales moved.

Jo Woo-chul broke first, deliberating aloud.

“If… it genuinely starts as a low-cost digital single — strict initial budget, clear stage-gate KPIs, further resources contingent on market response — then it could run as an experimental project.”

Yoo Young-jin nodded slowly.

“Musically, I concede the potential. If you judge the timing right, hyung — I’ll stand behind the production. I’ll help him polish the record myself.”

With Team One and the director aboard, the remaining heads fell in line — targeted training, program scouting, pre-heat opportunities, each pledging their lane. Even Kim Young-min — no natural ally of the founder’s, but reliably aligned wherever the company’s interest pointed — judged the move playable.

Watching the consensus set like concrete, Lee Soo-man smiled and added the last stone.

“One more thing. ‘Some’ is a male-female duet. And doesn’t this company happen to employ the perfect partner for it?”

Yoo Young-jin caught it instantly.

“Taeyeon.”

“Exactly.” The glasses were adjusted with the serenity of a man holding a winning hand. “Same teacher — they’re formally senior and junior disciples under The One. The story writes its own headlines. And she recorded the demo herself; I’ve heard it. She isn’t a good option. She’s the DEFINITIVE one.”

Kwon Yoon-jung hesitated.

“Director — I’ve heard ‘Some’ several times now. It’s a song about two people circling a relationship. If he wrote it… is there any chance he’s WRITING what he’s living? Is the boy dating?”

Lee Soo-man had, of course, already asked himself the same question.

“The conduct-monitoring office ran a quiet check. His closest associations in the building are Irene and Wendy. No indication of romance found. Irene we all know — the probability of her dating approaches zero. As for Wendy—” a slight frown, “—have someone deliver a reminder. To both of them. The company’s rule stands: the trainee path, the artist path, or romance. Choose one. Smart children know which to pick — and a child who picks wrong isn’t worth developing further.”

Kwon Yoon-jung nodded, message received.

Lee Soo-man surveyed the table one final time, and brought the gavel down.

“Good. The solo debut project for trainee Ji-ho is hereby on the official calendar. First: promote him to the pre-debut team. Legal drafts the contract; Woo-chul contacts him tomorrow to walk the terms. Once he signs, the solo planning begins in earnest. Production Center leads — with Marketing and Planning — I want a preliminary proposal in one week: staged debut plan, intensive training program, image positioning, initial promotion strategy, budget assessment. Kim sajang controls the purse; Director Yoo controls the sound.”

“Adjourned.”

The room emptied, every departing head quietly re-pricing the trainee named Ji-ho. And the founder stayed in his seat a moment longer, regarding the photograph and the two song titles on the screen, smiling faintly to himself.

Once again — the great ship turns toward new water.

The Morning After the Somaek Night SM Entertainment — Team One Director’s Office the paper that changes everything

Ji-ho had woken in IU’s guest room, was still assembling a strategy for facing Jiyeon over breakfast — when the Production Center called: be at the company within the hour.

Honestly — a mercy. Whatever last night’s game turned into, a cooling-off period serves everyone.

He rushed home, changed, and made for SM.

The director’s door clicked shut behind him, sealing out the corridor noise. Inside: Jo Woo-chul; a stern middle-aged woman in fine-framed glasses — Kim Ah-ju, of Legal; and the director’s assistant.

“Ji-ho. Sit.” The director indicated the chair, smiling warmly. “Congratulations. As of today, you are formally promoted to the pre-debut team.”

He had braced for it. The joy detonated anyway.

Debut. It’s REAL.

“Thank you.” The bow was deep and entirely sincere.

“Today, we discuss the working framework.” A thick document slid across the desk — Exclusive Artist Contract (Draft) stamped across the cover.

“Before signature, we’re obligated to walk you through the key terms.” Kim Ah-ju’s voice was courtroom-flat. “All provisions follow company standard procedure and the spirit of the standard contract under the Popular Culture and Arts Industry Development Act — structured, of course, to protect a long and stable partnership for both parties.”

He drew a breath and opened it. Dense clauses marched down the pages under the title: Exclusive Entertainment Management Contract.

Her fingertip landed on Chapter One, Article Three, Paragraph One.

SM Entertainment · Exclusive Artist Contract — Key Terms
Article 3.1 · Term

This contract takes effect from the trainee’s official debut date, with an initial term of seven (7) years.

“The SM standard term — and the baseline framework recognized by the Korea Fair Trade Commission.” — Legal

Supplementary Provision · Term Extension

Should the Artist fail, within the initial term, to complete the minimum album releases specified in Appendix Four (e.g., three full-length albums) or the core activity targets designated by the Company, the contract term shall automatically extend until said obligations are fulfilled — all original provisions remaining in force throughout the extension.

Field Notes

The fine print behind the fine print: the KFTC had indeed capped debut contracts at seven years. But policy above breeds countermeasures below, and every major agency had its instruments for stretching the practical term — SM’s favorite being the split structure: seven years domestic, with overseas activities signed under separate “extension agreements” that quietly added three more.

And now, in small print beside the term clause, sat the other classic lever — the minimum-obligation extension, ticking silently under Appendix Four.

Ji-ho read on, very carefully.
