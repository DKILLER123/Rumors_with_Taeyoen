Chapter Two Hundred Thirty-Four

Taeyeon and Tiffany, First Crossing of Blades

August 5, 2013 A fan meeting hall · Grand Walkerhill, the Moonlight wing — a private garden above the Han nine girls, one boyfriend, six candles’ worth of history — and two smiles that mean war

August 5, 2013. Girls’ Generation’s debut anniversary.

Nine members, birthdays scattered across eight different months — with schedules like theirs, gathering for each one was impossible; most birthdays got a gift and a group-chat cake emoji. Tiffany’s birthday was the sole exception, attendance mandatory by unwritten law. Reason one: she was, by loving consensus, the only GIRL in Girls’ Generation, and everyone indulged her. Reason two: her birthday sat conveniently against the debut anniversary, so the two celebrations merged. Reason three: she was a pure product of America and constitutionally incapable of not organizing a party.

The daytime belonged to the fans — a sixth-anniversary fan meeting, loud and bright and warm. Toward evening, the members trailed out to the vans through the last of the summer heat.

Taeyeon walked at the rear of the line, white lace blouse faintly damp, black hair swinging with her steps. Ahead of her, Jessica was waving goodbye to the send-off crowd, white chiffon blouse tucked loosely into wash-blue jeans.

Taeyeon was tired. Her eye fell — idly, and then not idly — on the swaying line of Jessica’s silhouette. Her expression acquired intent. Her hand rose.

THWACK.

“Ya. Walk faster.”

The crowd went off like a stadium.

“AAAAAAH~ TaengSic!! TAENGSIC!!”

Jessica spun around clutching the point of impact, iconic frost-glass eyes flooded with outrage, cheeks blazing.

“Can you STOP hitting my butt?!”

“Hehe. Sica-yah, I think it’s gotten perkier. The rebound is excellent.”

“Go DIE! You’ve lost your mind!” The blush ran all the way to her ears. She swatted Taeyeon’s arm. “Pervert-yeon!”

Taeyeon looked deeply, radiantly pleased with herself.

“Tsk tsk tsk. Aigoo, aigoo.”

Yuri — mint-green camisole, healthy tan, hair loosely knotted with strands escaping down her neck — shook her head at the two of them with the weariness of a long-suffering referee.

“Tonight, the fan-fiction boards gain another hundred TaengSic epics.”

“And how would YOU know that?” Sunny’s eyes narrowed as she angled herself behind crossed arms — canary-yellow tee, and a silhouette that made loose fabric irrelevant. “Don’t tell me you READ them.”

“YA! Who reads— WHO would read those!!” Yuri’s ears went visibly red; the denial arrived at twice necessary volume.

“Nobody said you did. Why so loud?”

“I’m not loud, I’m HUNGRY.” Yuri executed a tactical subject change so abrupt her camisole strap slid off one shoulder and had to be retrieved. “Fany-yah! Where’s tonight’s party?”

Tiffany’s eyes curved into their crescents — cherry-blossom pink off-shoulder dress, shoulders bare and bright.

“A resort~ With a big pool.”

“Seriously?”

“Of course.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Grand Walkerhill sat on the wooded slope above the Han, its garden-style grounds folded away from the city’s noise, its pool courts hidden inside landscaped privacy and keycard gates. Ji-ho had booked out the entire Moonlight wing — private, secure, and decorated to specification: pink balloons everywhere, pink roses banked in every corner, food and wine in formation, and the pool holding the sunset like a bowl holds wine.

Tiffany saw it and lit up like a struck match, gathering her hem and breaking into a two-step run, pink skirt flaring.

He’d done all this — and in front of her sisters. The face he’d bought her tonight was worth its weight in anything.

Ji-ho was already there. And the first thing he did, in front of everyone, was walk to Taeyeon and fold her into his arms, tight and unhurried.

She stiffened — then the familiar scent closed over her and the weeks of held-back missing broke their banks all at once. Her arms went around him hard, small fists knotting the back of his shirt.

Sunny and Yoon-a watched Tiffany with surgical care. Sunny’s eyes ticked between the three of them, mouth curled with interest; Yoon-a’s lips pressed together over an expression with too many layers to name. Tiffany’s smile-eyes stilled for exactly one frame — then resumed as if nothing had happened. The two observers exchanged a glance of reluctant professional respect. The man was, no argument, a scoundrel. But holding Taeyeon in front of Tiffany, and Tiffany HOLDING — that took a quality of nerve you almost had to admire.

“Ooooh~”

“So sweet~”

Choi Soo-young and Yuri started the heckling in stereo — Soo-young draped against the table edge, long legs crossed, grin fully loaded.

Taeyeon went red to the ears, detached herself hastily, shot one covert glance at Tiffany — who was studiously arranging her hem, unreadable — and then, deliberately, took Ji-ho’s arm and wound her fingers into it.

“Annyeonghaseyo. It’s been a while.”

“Ne~ annyeonghaseyo.”

The members returned the greeting with varying degrees of starch. Girls’ Generation did not, as a rule, get stage fright — but nobody had yet located the correct etiquette for ‘member’s boyfriend, also a chairman, also the man who wrote our friends onto Billboard.’ Even the hecklers had gone quiet. The silence risked setting.

“To think you were hiding THIS much.” Yoon-a stepped forward, smile at maximum charm — white tee, pale blue skirt, deer eyes bright. “So what do we call you now? Ji-ho? Or Song-hoejang-nim?”

“I like ‘brother-in-law.’”

“Ha. Honestly—” and the knot in the room dissolved, because THERE he was, the same shameless person. She planted her hands on her hips. “I’m four years older than you. Call me nuna.”

“Yoon-a. Go grill the meat.”

“YA! I said call me NUNA!”

With Yoon-a as the icebreaker, the room thawed fast, and the questions converged on him from all sides.

“That Apink song — what did you charge for it?”

“Forgot. A few hundred million, somewhere in there.”

“You LOST money.” Yoon-a pressed her hand to her forehead, physically pained. “It’s on SEVEN music-show wins. It’s a national epidemic. You undercharged by half.”

“Mm. I don’t really care about money.”

Yoon-a’s breathing audibly changed gears.

INFURIATING. He did it AGAIN. She stalked off to the grill, skirt snapping, before she did something the fans would read about.

Seohyun rotated into the vacancy — pale blue floral dress, posture impeccable, eyes shining with the pure academic hunger of the room’s one true student.

“Congratulations, truly. Those two songs — they’re extraordinary. How do you write like that?”

“They’re all right. You get used to it.”

Her mouth twitched at the corner. It was TRUE, which somehow made it worse.

“I’ve been trying to write as well. I finish a song, I listen back, and I… can’t continue listening.”

“I mostly just write whatever comes. Talent, probably?”

The nation’s model student walked away puffed like an offended dove.

After that, mercifully, the room had the sense to leave the couple alone. Soo-young and Yuri colonized the dessert table; Yoon-a attacked the grilled meat with prejudice, deer eyes flicking over at intervals.

Ji-ho pressed Taeyeon’s soft hand.

“Finally. Everyone’s gone.”

“You chased them off on PURPOSE.” She gave him a sidelong look.

“Obviously. I want to talk to my nuna.” He laced their fingers. “Walk in the garden with me?”

She looked at him, then across at Tiffany — who was eating, and whose eyes kept arriving here between bites — and nodded.

“Okay.”

✦ ✦ ✦

The stone path wound off under the trees, August night-wind carrying green smells, her small hand folded entirely inside his.

“Mianhae, nuna.”

“For what?” She tipped her face up.

“The acquisition. I should have told you long before I did. But I never had it fully in hand — and if I’d told you early and then failed, I couldn’t stand the thought of you being disappointed.”

“Disappointed… that’s not the thing I was disappointed about…”

The words came out under her breath, mostly to the path.

“Mwo? Nuna, what did you say?”

“Hm? Oh— I said—” she skipped two steps ahead, turned, and walked backward facing him, eyes soft in the dark, “—even if you weren’t LOEN’s chairman, you’d still be the most extraordinary person I’ve ever met. Why would I be disappointed about a failed deal?”

“Honestly — that you even got the CHANCE at a deal like that is incredible. That’s SK. I’m proud of you.”

The truth was, it HAD been sitting on her chest for days. Lying awake, turning it over: he loves me — doesn’t he? Then why did I hear about the biggest moment of his life from a NEWS PUSH? Why did Fany know first? The suspicion had grown teeth in the dark: had he grown tired of her — was he drifting toward Fany — was the coldness the beginning of the ending? Every version of the thought ended somewhere that hurt to breathe around.

He knew her. Knew that she asked nothing and stored everything, and that unspoken small things compounded, in her, into unspoken enormous things. So — apart from the one subject he could not open — he opened everything else, deliberately, first. And sure enough, spoken to first, she spoke.

They came out at the pool — this one empty, the water a black mirror holding a thin moon and a scatter of stars. He sat on a lounge chair and gathered her into his lap — light as a cat, citrus-sweet, the warmth of her settling against him like something clicking home.

“Nuna. Do you know how I actually made the money?”

“Tell me tell me—” She came alive instantly, eyes bright, face tipped up. “The internet’s FULL of theories. One article says you’re secretly a chaebol kid.”

She knew his real story — the LA years, the four thousand dollars — which made the mystery of the fortune positively itch.

“Investments.”

“Investments?”

“Mm.”

And he walked her through it — the regime change in Japan, the yen sliding, the currency position, the market surge — attributing, with a perfectly straight face, the Wealth Robot’s inhuman precision to his own genius eye for macro timing. One window, correctly read, and the LOEN war chest had existed.

“Wah… daebak…”

She lay against his chest, finger tracing idle circles around his shirt button, feeling like she’d been read a bedtime story in a foreign language. SHE had seen the same news about Japan’s new administration — seen it, registered nothing, moved on. And her Ji-ho had looked at the exact same headline and extracted a fortune she couldn’t earn in ten lifetimes. Three million won to hundreds of billions. One year. HOW. How was one person like this?

“Ji-ho-ya. You’re really… too much.” Her voice had gone soft and full.

“I’ve told this to exactly one person, nuna. You.”

He lowered his head until their foreheads touched — hers cool, fine-grained.

“In my heart you’re not in the same category as anyone else. When I was a trainee with nothing, you were Girls’ Generation’s leader — the top of the entire sky — and you chose me anyway, exactly as I was.”

“What you and I have is the pure kind. So whatever I become — chairman, anything — this doesn’t change. It stays what it always was.”

Only told ME. I’m DIFFERENT from the others. …Yes. What she and Ji-ho had — who else could ever understand it? A man this extraordinary would always have women orbiting; that was physics. But the one he LOVED — that was her. That had always been her. The doubts that had circled her bed for a week thinned like morning fog. Whatever games he played, he had never actually left. She would hold this. She would HOLD it.

She shifted, turned, settled astride his lap, and kissed him first.

Lip to lip — and the current went through her, fingertip to sternum.

A small helpless sound escaped her; her whole frame went soft as warmed cream, arms winding his neck, fingers sliding up into his hair. Too long — it had been too long — since when was she THIS sensitive? Her breath shortened; heat climbed her throat in a slow pink tide; the night narrowed to the point of contact.

And into the middle of it, mild as milk:

“Khem. Excuse me~”

They broke apart fast. At the mouth of the path stood Tiffany — pink dress stirring in the night wind, the trademark sweetness in place, her smile-eyes pooled with shadow too deep to read.

“Ji-ho. Daedae. Time for candles.”

Sugar-sweet. Absolutely opaque.

Taeyeon’s eyes narrowed a fraction. Her chin came up a precise centimeter.

“Fany-yah. Mianhae~ Ji-ho and I hadn’t seen each other in so long — we got a little carried away.”

“Gwaenchana.” A wave of the hand, bright as ribbon. “Come quickly. Everyone’s waiting.”

“Arasseo.”

And then Taeyeon caught Ji-ho’s face in both hands and kissed him — thoroughly, audibly, at length.

“Mianhae, sweetheart. Tonight I’ll make it up to you properly.”

Tiffany had believed she could watch this with equanimity. She was, after all — she made herself think the word — the interloper here. But her chest closed like a fist anyway, and the smile took real load-bearing effort now, and she had no standing, none at all, to mind. She turned her face away.

And Taeyeon, rising, smiled a very small private smile. Fany-yah. While I’m here, there’s no opening. I don’t want to end up somewhere we can’t come back from — you’re my sister, and I’d like you to still be my sister on the far side of this. So please. Read the board. Retreat while it’s still graceful — before faces get torn that can’t be mended.
