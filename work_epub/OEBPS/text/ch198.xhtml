Chapter One Hundred Ninety-Eight

The Negotiation

June 2, 2013 Yeouido — SK Group headquarters, 28th floor the buyer walks in wearing his own face

Long after, the study went quiet again.

Joo-hyun emerged flushed, smoothed her hair and the silk robe back into order, and drew a tissue from the desk box with as much dignity as the moment allowed.

He pulled her into his lap and kissed her forehead. “Go rinse, then sleep.”

“And you?”

“A little longer. I’ll be quick. Promise.”

When she came back he was already shutting the laptop down.

“Really stopping?”

“Mm.” He took her hand. “Past a certain hour, thinking just digs trenches. Tomorrow— no. It’s already today. Today I’ll think.”

They lay down tangled together, her finger drawing slow circles on his chest.

“Ji-ho.”

“Mm?”

“Whatever it is you’re doing — I’m for it.” The murmur came soft against him. “So don’t carry all of it alone.”

The sentence hit somewhere unarmored.

He tightened his arms. “Okay.”

“Sleep.”

“Good night.”

✦   ✦   ✦

The next three days, his life ran on two parallel tracks that never touched.

Daylight: the star. Promo shoots for the film, press interviews, stills and reflections posted on schedule. To every visible eye, a leading man sprinting toward his release date.

Night: the plenipotentiary of Dreamland Capital — remote sessions with SHIN & KIM, diligence reports, negotiation trees, and financial clauses memorized like choreography.

June 1, late evening: final valuation and script arrived. On the video call, Lee Myung-ha’s face ran serious.

“Mr. Song — diligence came back better than the public numbers. Melon’s share rose from fifty-two to fifty-eight percent over the past year; stickiness climbing. Which means SK’s ask will likely run higher than modeled.”

“How much?”

“Initial read: their floor sits between ₩280 and ₩300 billion for the fifty-five percent.” A pause. “The good news — our channels say Starr Investment has paused its bid. Internal process issues, apparently.”

The Wishing Orb, filing its first progress report.

“So we’re the only live buyer?”

“Not the only — but one heavyweight lighter,” Kim Min-ji added. “Internally SK is split: one camp wants international capital for the ‘globalized’ optics; the other wants a domestic buyer who knows the market.”

“And Kim Sung-hwan?”

The strategy attorney pulled a memo. “Delicate. One insider quotes him from a recent internal meeting: ‘LOEN doesn’t need a new owner. It needs a new vision.’”

His eyes lit. Exactly the seam he’d bet on.

“I’ve read the script,” he said. “Professional — and too standard. I want to adjust some of the language.”

“Adjust how?”

He shared his own draft — the vision passages. The lawyers’ faces did complicated things as they read.

“Mr. Song,” Park Sung-jun ventured, “these are — moving. But Kim Sung-hwan is a data animal. Sentiment in a deal room usually—”

“Which is why we balance it.” He cut in. “Open with data and logic — establish the professional floor. Thread the vision through the middle — build the human bridge. Close with hard terms. Attorney Lee?”

Lee Myung-ha thought a long moment. “It can work. IF the timing and dosage are perfect. One whiff of manipulation and it backfires completely.”

“I understand.”

“Tomorrow, ten a.m., SK headquarters, thirty-second floor. We arrive thirty minutes early for final prep.”

“I’ll be on time.”

✦   ✦   ✦

June 2, six a.m. He woke ahead of the alarm.

The sky was barely paling; Seoul still slept. He rose without waking the warmth beside him, showered, and dressed in the new bespoke suit — deep navy, cut clean, shoulders squared.

He was at the mirror with the tie when she stirred and sat up, rubbing her eyes — and stopped at the sight of him fully assembled.

“This early?”

“Mm. Big day.” He turned and smiled at her. “Sleep more.”

Instead she slid out of bed, crossed to him, and took over the tie — small hands working the knot straight.

“Is it — the big thing you mentioned?”

“Yes.” He caught her hand. “Today we talk.”

“Will it be hard?”

“Maybe.” Honest. “But I’m ready.”

She looked up at him — then rose on her toes and pressed a light kiss to his mouth.

“I believe in you.”

Four plain syllables, and the whole day’s armor arrived at once.

“Wait for me.”

“Mm.”

✦   ✦   ✦

Nine-thirty a.m. The SK Group tower on Yeouido — fifty floors of glass burning in the morning sun, the power totem of the peninsula’s third-largest chaebol, dark-suited people moving through its doors at speed.

He met the three lawyers at the entrance.

“Final confirmation.” Lee Myung-ha, low-voiced. “Today’s shape: I open with background and the framework. You speak in the middle — vision and the operating plan. Any specific number, any clause — leave to us.”

“Understood.”

“And—” Kim Min-ji added, “—per our intel, strategy-investment people may sit in beyond Director Kim. Any blade-edged question: don’t answer directly. Route it to us.”

“Got it.”

The lobby: a crystal chandelier the size of a small car, marble polished to a mirror, one long wall charting the corporate ascent — a small textile shop in 1953 becoming a transnational at the top of the display.

Reservations confirmed; a suited secretary walked them into a dedicated elevator; twenty-eighth floor.

“Executive Director Kim is already in the conference room.”

The corridor ran silent except for carpet-swallowed footsteps, modern art on both walls, none of it inexpensive.

The door opened on a room bigger than the model in his head — a table cut for twenty, five people at it.

At the head: the man from the file. Square face, short hair, dark-gray suit, no tie — casual in a way that read as rank, not relaxation. He was reading and looked up only when the door moved.

And there it was, unmistakable: one full second of open surprise.

SHIN & KIM had disclosed that the buyer’s representative would attend in person. They had not disclosed which face he’d be wearing.

“Director Kim, good morning.” Lee Myung-ha led, card extended. “Lee Myung-ha, SHIN & KIM, representing Dreamland Capital. And this is Mr. Ji-ho — Dreamland Capital’s plenipotentiary representative.”

Kim Sung-hwan rose. His gaze stayed on the younger man’s face several seconds too long before the hand came out.

“Ji-ho-ssi. So it’s YOU. Genuinely surprising.”

The grip was strong; the eyes were surgical.

“Director Kim. A pleasure.” Level, unbowed. “Life is a journey made of miracles and surprises — isn’t it?”

“Hahaha. True enough. Sit.”

Introductions ran down SK’s side of the table: Park Jae-ho, head of strategic investment; Lee Sang-min, chief financial officer of the division; two note-taking secretaries.

“Begin.”

Lee Myung-ha took the projector and laid out Dreamland Capital — capital strength, long-horizon thesis, conviction on the Korean entertainment market — clean, dense, professional. Kim Sung-hwan listened and wrote, and when the deck ended, cut straight in.

“Attorney Lee. Dreamland is Cayman-registered. Who is the beneficial owner? And the specific source of funds?”

“Under client confidentiality, the beneficial owner cannot be disclosed. On funds — we provide Citibank certification showing in excess of four hundred million dollars in cash reserves, fully sufficient for this transaction.”

“Four hundred million—” the CFO turned it over. “Roughly ₩460 billion at current rates. Ample for the controlling stake. My question: equity or debt? If debt — rate and repayment schedule?”

“Pure equity. Dreamland injects via capital increase into the SPV. No debt component.”

“Then—” the strategy head, Park Jae-ho, “—the buyer’s valuation of LOEN? Premium expectations?”

“Per our diligence,” Kim Min-ji answered, “we assess fair value of the fifty-five percent block at ₩260–270 billion. Given control premium and strategic value, our offer will be competitive.”

“Competitive meaning WHAT?” Kim Sung-hwan spoke at last — and moved his eyes off the lawyers entirely, setting them on the young man across the table. “Ji-ho-ssi. Since you came in person — I’d like YOUR view.”

The pressure transferred cleanly. Every gaze in the room followed it.

Lee Myung-ha drew breath to interpose — too late.

“Director Kim. Before numbers — one question of my own.”

“Ask.”

“Is SK selling LOEN’s control to satisfy the Fair Trade Commission — or because it genuinely wants out of the entertainment business?”

Too direct. The strategy head’s face flickered; the CFO’s brow set.

Kim Sung-hwan’s expression did not move. “Is that relevant to this negotiation?”

“It is.” He held the man’s eyes. “If this is only regulatory compliance, SK needs nothing but the highest bidder. But if you still hold expectations for LOEN — then the selection standard should be different.” A beat, voice even: “It should be — the buyer who can build what you never got the chance to.”

One flicker, deep in the eyes across the table.

A short silence.

“Go on.” No emotion in it either way.

The hinge moment. He adjusted forward.

“Director Kim. Three years ago, when you took the digital media division, you said in an internal meeting: ‘SK’s future is not only energy and chemicals — it is content and culture.’”

The pupils tightened, fractionally.

“You drew four roads for LOEN. One: lift Melon from a music vendor to a digital content ecosystem. Two: build original production, stop being merely a channel. Three: push Korean music and film outward, into export. Four—” a measured pause, “—prepare an acquisition of JYP.”

The strategy head’s head snapped around toward his director. That fourth item had never left the building.

“And for three years, those roads stalled. Not for lack of ability — but because inside a chaebol built on heavy industry, entertainment is forever the side business. It never gets the real allocation.”

Park Jae-ho moved to interrupt; Kim Sung-hwan lifted one hand and stopped him.

“Ji-ho-ssi.” Slowly. “Your research is thorough. But knowing the past doesn’t change the future.”

“Which is exactly my point.” He took the sentence like a handoff. “Keep LOEN inside SK, and it remains the least-favored son — capped resources, capped horizon. Hand it to someone who will raise it as the FIRST-BORN—” a beat, every word set separately, “—and it has a chance to become the digital entertainment giant of the peninsula. Of Asia.”

The room went pin-silent.

Kim Sung-hwan looked at him for a long, unhurried moment. “What is your concrete plan?”

First door: open.

He pulled up his own deck — not the lawyers’ spreadsheet armor; something cleaner, visual, built to carry.

PROPOSAL — THREE PHASES

LOEN under new ownership · presented June 2, 2013

PHASE 1Fortify the core — rebuild Melon’s product experience, expand the rights library, deepen retention. Market share 58% → 65%+ within one year.

PHASE 2Extend the borders — original production: music, film and drama investment, artist development. Direct creative participation; international resources brought in.

PHASE 3Go global — LOEN as the platform for Korean cultural export. Overseas revenue above 30% within three years.

“A grand plan.” The strategy head, skeptical by portfolio. “And your guarantee of execution? You are — an entertainer.”

“PRECISELY because I’m an entertainer—” the interruption came smooth, “—I know what this industry actually needs. I know what platform creators are starving for. I know what listeners will pay for. I know exactly where overseas markets stand on Korean content — I’ve been performing into them all year.”

He turned back to the head of the table. “Director Kim. The things you didn’t get to build — I want to try. And I have grounds for confidence.”

Kim Sung-hwan said nothing. His finger tapped the table, twice.

“The offer. Numbers.”

The negotiation dropped into machinery. Lee Myung-ha took it: “Based on Mr. Song’s vision and LOEN’s assessed value — ₩265 billion for the fifty-five percent. That stands ₩15 billion above the level Starr had indicated, as a statement of seriousness.”

“Too low.” The CFO, flat. “Internal valuation puts the block at three hundred minimum.”

“Director Lee—” Kim Min-ji opened a binder, “—referencing the last eighteen months of Korean entertainment M&A comparables—”

The tug-of-war ran a full hour. ₩265 billion against ₩285; models against models; precedent against precedent — stalling, finally, in the neighborhood of ₩275 billion, neither side signing it, both sides circling it.

He stayed silent through all of it. This layer belonged to the professionals.

“One further question.” Park Jae-ho again. “If the deal closes with SK retaining twelve percent — board composition? Management rights?”

“Proposed: a seven-seat board,” Lee Myung-ha answered, ready. “Four appointed by Dreamland, two by SK, one independent. Day-to-day operations under a CEO nominated by Dreamland, ratified by the board.”

“And the CEO candidate is?”

Every eye in the room made the same journey.

“On closing,” he said, calmly, “I take the CEO seat myself.”

“You’ll manage it PERSONALLY?” The strategy head’s brows drew hard. “Mr. Song. Management and artistry are different disciplines. You’re certain you’re qualified?”

“I’m confident. And through the transition I’ll build a professional management bench — including retaining the strong performers on SK’s current team.”

For the first time all morning, Kim Sung-hwan’s face openly changed — surprise, then something slower and deeper behind it.

“I need to consider,” he said at last.

“Of course.”

The meeting wound down. Kim Sung-hwan rose.

“Today’s discussion was constructive. We’ll respond within three days, after internal review.”

“I’ll look forward to it.”

At the handshake, the older man’s grip held one extra second.

“Ji-ho-ssi. You’re unusual.”

“Thank you. Director Kim — would you have a drink with me?”

A blink of genuine surprise. Then a nod.

“Seven tonight. KEKU.”

“Done.”

✦   ✦   ✦

Half past noon, outside the tower, the sun at full glare and Yeouido roaring around them.

“Mr. Song—” Lee Myung-ha let out a long breath. “That went — better than modeled.”

“No rejection on the spot is a win,” Kim Min-ji added. “And that last remark — he rates you. Personally.”

He nodded, and kept his own tension where it was. Round one. The real weather was still ahead: SK’s internal committees, whatever bidders the vacuum attracted, and the number itself.

“Next moves?” Park Sung-jun asked.

“We wait — actively. Attorney Lee: start drafting the definitive agreement. And I want a full file on LOEN’s current management — every key person we might keep.”

“Understood.”

The four of them split into the noon crowd.
