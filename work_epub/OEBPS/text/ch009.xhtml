Chapter Nine

Limited-Edition Outfit: Bunny Girl

“Aaah—!”

At the same instant, Irene — standing there with a cup of coffee in each hand — shrieked, scared out of her wits right back. Her hands, however, were rock steady: not a single drop spilled.

Ji-ho thumped his chest, dragged his composure back into place, and bowed in his most careful Korean.

“Sunbae-nim. Hello.”

“Ne… hello.”

Irene startled easily — famously so. The trainees had a whole drawer of nicknames for it: Bunny Bae, Jumpy Bae. Her heart was still hammering triple-time; she managed the polite nod and made to escape back into the practice room.

Ji-ho’s eyes landed on the two coffees, and a headache bloomed instantly. Those were not two cups for two people. Both of them were hers.

It’s HALF PAST TWO. Two coffees?! Look, I know this country eventually evolves past the need for sleep, but that’s supposed to take another decade!

For the sake of ending tonight’s siege at a humane hour — and claiming his quest — he decided to weaponize his face and try his luck.

“Ahem. Sunbae-nim.” He called after her.

She turned, those wide bright eyes full of question marks.

He produced a yawn — mostly genuine — and pitched his voice into studied nonchalance.

“Actually, sunbae-nim, I came down this way to ask… where does one get coffee around here?”

“The first-floor pantry — free coffee, cups are there too.” She explained with great patience: “Take the stairs up, turn left, then right, then left again, straight to the end, then right — it’s diagonally across from the restrooms.”

Ji-ho’s face went carefully blank. Anyone who had ever dated knew this truth: asking a woman for directions was a form of gentle torture. Besides, his actual objective was never the coffee. It was subtracting one cup from her total.

“Sunbae-nim, I only arrived from the States yesterday — I don’t know the building at all yet. Could I trouble you to walk me there once? Please?”

And as he said it, he stared — pointedly, shamelessly — at the coffee in her hands. Subtlety had left the building.

Irene was a slow-warmer by nature: sunshine itself around close friends, socially rationed around strangers. Her first thought was that this hoobae was being remarkably forward — commandeering a senior as a tour guide, at this hour. But then again… he was an international kid, fresh off the plane, unfamiliar with the culture and the building both. It was forgivable. Probably.

The real problem was math: basement three to the first-floor pantry and back was a twenty-minute round trip — which was precisely why she’d brewed two cups in one go.

Caught in the dilemma, she glanced up — and finally registered the direction of his laser-locked stare. Hold on. You’re not lost at all. You’re after my coffee!

She took a longer look at him. Sword-straight brows, starlit eyes, a face with genuinely criminal bone structure. …Ugh. Fine. Consider it a tax discount for being handsome.

She held out the cup in her right hand.

“The pantry’s a long way from here — have someone show you tomorrow, you’ll learn it in one trip. Here. Take this one.”

Mission accomplished. Delight flashed through him as he accepted the cup with both hands.

“Thank you, sunbae-nim!”

“It’s nothing.”

Cradling her remaining cup, she padded to the center of the practice-room floor, sat down, and sipped in small, unhurried mouthfuls.

Ji-ho couldn’t be bothered to trek back to his own room anymore. He picked a corner and settled in, running quiet calculations: the moment you leave, sunbae-nim, I need exactly ten more minutes, and today’s quest is mine.

His casual squatting of her practice room needled her, just slightly. No rule assigned rooms to anyone — but the company ran on seniority in all things, and the nicer rooms went to seniors by ancient unwritten law. Three years into her training, she’d long held the best room on the floor, sharing it only with trainees of similar tenure.

Then again — she was close with the overseas trainees, Zhang Yixing and Lu Han especially; she’d trained alongside Yixing for three years now. Kids from abroad never seemed to keep score on these unspoken rules. She let it go.

If Ji-ho could have read her mind, he’d only have grinned wider. It’s 2:30 in the morning and you’re drinking double coffee. Grind all you want — somebody’s got to make you take care of yourself. He raised the cup and drank.

“GAH— it’s so bitter! Is there even sugar in this?!”

The bitterness detonated across his tongue — and slapped his exhausted nervous system wide awake.

Watching his face crumple like a kicked paper bag, Irene hid a smirk behind her cup. She’d gone light on the sugar on purpose — it was functional coffee. A small, sparkling vein of vindication warmed her. Hmph. That’s what you get for mugging me for my coffee.

Ding— “Milestone achieved: Indirect Kiss — sharing the same drink, or tasting the same food.”

The night flowed on, silent and still — but inside Ji-ho’s chest, a river had just burst its banks.

Fwish! A light-screen only he could see unfurled across his vision, line after line leaping out like sprites:
