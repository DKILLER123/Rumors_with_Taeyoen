Chapter Two Hundred Seventeen

Cry, Fight, and Threaten the Rafters

The same morning Girls’ Generation dorm, Seoul · a resort suite, Jeju the phone hits the floor in Seoul — and a nineteen-year-old rewrites the rules of the war

“Ji-ho… and Fany—”

For one instant the world spun off its axis.

The phone slipped from Taeyeon’s hand and hit the floor with a dull crack. Her slight body swayed, as if every bone had been drawn out of it at once, and slid down the cold wall until she was sitting on the floor. Black pulsed at the edges of her vision. Her ears rang. Taeyeon’s world lost all its sound.

So that was it.

No wonder “Love Me Like You Do” burned the way it burned. No wonder Fany came back from that recording with her eyes lit up like festival lanterns. No wonder she changed the subject — that awkward half-beat too fast — every time his name came up. Every detail she’d waved off now turned around and came back with a knife.

Her boyfriend was extraordinary. So extraordinary that she had allowed herself, now and then, a secret little vanity: enjoying the members’ envy and their awe, enjoying — yes, even enjoying — the way her sisters’ hearts sometimes skipped around him, because it fed the warm, possessive certainty of HE IS MINE. She had been certain of Ji-ho’s love: he would never betray her. She had been certain of her sisters: they would never cross the line.

Reality had just slapped her across the face with both hands.

How could they? How DARED they? Fany — one of the sisters she trusted most in the world. Ji-ho — the man she had bet everything on believing, on loving. Why did it have to be those two, driving the deepest blade into her together?

Heartbreak was too small a word. It felt as if her whole chest had been scooped hollow and packed with cold broken glass, every breath dragging its edges. The tears came without any warning at all, scalding, flooding her vision, streaming down cheeks gone icy and soaking the front of her nightshirt. She wanted to scream; grief had her by the throat and would not allow it. What came out instead was the choked, hitching whimper of a small animal, her body twitching for air.

“Sulli. Why would you do this?”

On the other end of that phone line, in the hotel room, Choi Sulli watched Ji-ho’s face go abruptly, perfectly still — not one muscle of expression left on it — and her heart shivered once, an animal instinct of cold.

But she drew a deep breath. And in those eyes that always carried candy-sweet laughter, something obsessive glittered instead. She met his gaze dead-on, unnaturally calm.

“Oppa. You were never going to keep this from Taeyeon eonni forever. A wound you keep covered just rots. Instead of dragging it out, letting it wear you thinner on both ends — lance it.”

She stepped closer, the words coming faster now, hotter.

“A man like you doesn’t need a woman you have to tiptoe around — someone to coax, to hide from, to fight with over her jealousy.”

“What you need is someone like me. Someone who can hold ALL of you. Who doesn’t need titles or status. Who only needs one small corner of your heart — and as long as you love me, I will do anything for you!”

Her voice had started to tremble, and there was fever under it now, something past the border of ordinary sense.

“I, Choi Sulli, do not care how many women stand around you. As long as you love me. As long as you need me!”

“Oppa, you don’t have to grovel your way around all of them! The ones who can accept you will stay — and the ones who can’t? If they leave, so what? It’s not YOU who loses anything!”

Her pale cheeks burned with an unnatural flush; possessiveness churned openly in those clear, innocent-looking eyes. This wasn’t a normal person’s love — it was devotion bent into something else. Or perhaps it was love past normal capacity. Just… a little mad.

Ji-ho looked at her and said nothing. The silence spread through the room like cold tidewater.

Then he turned, walked straight to the door, took his jacket off the chair back, and put it on with quick, economical movements.

That calm frightened her more than any shouting could have.

“Oppa!”

She flew at him, small cold hands clamping his wrist, fingertips like ice.

“Are you— are you angry with me?”

The crying edge was in her voice now. The noble varnish — I did it for you — had peeled away entire, and underneath there was only the enormous, naked terror of being thrown away.

He didn’t shake her off. He stopped, turned his head, voice level.

“What right do I have to be angry at you? But right now, I have to get back to Seoul.”

Back to Seoul. To Taeyeon eonni. Her panic went vertical. No. He could not leave. If he went back now — carrying his disappointment in her and his guilt toward Taeyeon eonni — what would happen? She didn’t dare finish the thought.

“NO!”

She threw herself against his back, arms locking around his waist with everything she had, cheek crushed against his spine as if pure grip could anchor him to the island.

“Oppa, don’t go! I’m begging you, don’t go!”

His body tensed slightly. He pried her hands from his waist, and without turning around, said in a voice with weight in it:

“Sulli-yah. Taeyeon nuna chose me back when I had nothing at all.”

A bucket of ice water, straight over her last ember of hope.

This was the thing she feared most — that old debt of shared hardship, the love that predated the money and the fame. The calmer he got, the more terrified she became. She had no one else. Only this man. She could not lose him. And out of that crushing panic, her cornered mind threw off one twisted spark of brilliance.

She grabbed his arm again, words tumbling.

“Oppa, you SHOULDN’T go back! If you go back now, you get shouting and tears and nothing else gets solved! You should act like you know nothing!”

He finally turned, brow creasing. “Meaning what?”

She tipped her face up, urgent.

“Taeyeon eonni was always going to find out sooner or later — and what you want is for her to eventually ACCEPT it. Right? Then she cannot face you at the exact moment she’s angriest and most broken! You need time. And you need… a little outside help.”

She wet her dry lips, voice dropping low, coaxing, serpentine.

“Eonni is going to call back any minute. She just took the worst hit of her life — right now what she needs most is someone to pour it all out to. And whose words does she trust most right now? Mine. The witness. The good little sister. When a person is most helpless, the voice they trust is the only voice that works. Let ME talk her down. Let me hold her steady for you. Okay?”

She stared into his eyes, hunting for one millimeter of wavering.

And as if summoned to prove her point, her phone on the bed began to convulse. The name jumping on the screen: Taeyeon.

Sulli snatched it up, shot Ji-ho one look, dragged in a breath — and answered on speaker, her voice flipping instantly into tearful, frantic worry.

Voice Call · Taeyeon eonni — on speaker

“Yeoboseyo? Eonni?”

“Sulli-yah! Where are they— where are they RIGHT NOW?! Tell me! I’m coming there NOW!!” — hoarse, ragged, the heavy nasal tremor of a voice that has been screaming into a pillow.

The despair and the hate in that voice dropped through Ji-ho’s chest like a stone.

Choi Sulli, unnervingly composed, flashed him a small stay-calm hand signal — and spoke into the phone with the measured patience of a hostage negotiator.

Voice Call · continued

“Eonni, breathe first. Do you know how the original girlfriends who LOSE — the ones who end up walking away while the other woman wins — do you know how they lose?”

“…Mwo?” — a stunned hiccup.

“Exactly like what you’re about to do. Storming over like a madwoman. Screaming the house down. Men hate one thing above everything: exhaustion. Endless fighting. Even the deepest love gets ground down to nothing under hysterical confrontation after hysterical confrontation. And at the end of it, he’s just TIRED — and the gentle, understanding one who never pressures him starts looking awfully good. THAT’S when you’ve truly lost, eonni.”

She paused — then dropped the only question that mattered.

“Eonni. Ask your own heart. Do you actually want to LOSE Oppa? Do you really want to shove him into someone else’s arms with your own two hands?”

Break up. The words landed on Taeyeon’s chest like a hammer. Break up with Ji-ho? — The late nights tangled close, the sweet unhurried kisses. The love songs written for her and no one else. His back, straight and unflinching, planted between her and the world. The moment fate itself bent and made her taller, because he wished it. Drop by drop, all of it had soaked into her marrow. It wasn’t part of her life anymore. It WAS her life. That wasn’t a breakup. That was asking for her life back.

Down the line, the dam failed completely. Taeyeon wept — huge, tearing, helpless sobs that shredded everything they passed through.

And listening to it, Choi Sulli’s own eyes flooded instantly, fat tears rolling as she cried right along, words breaking apart around her own hiccups.

Voice Call · continued — both ends crying now

“Eonni— don’t cry— crying doesn’t— doesn’t solve anything—”

“Who— who CRIES— to solve— PROBLEMS?!” — gasped out between sobs, unanswerable.

“Eonni, listen to me.” — a hard swipe of tears; the voice firming. “You absolutely cannot come here. If you show up and they deny everything to your face, then what? And you’d burn ME — your eyes on the inside! Once they’re on guard you’ll never catch proof of anything!”

She dropped her voice lower.

“I’ll help you. I’ll build you a moment where you catch him red-handed — no denying, no wriggling out. And THEN, eonni, you run the old playbook on him: cry, fight, and threaten the rafters. Make all the scene you want!”

“…Cry, fight, and— threaten the rafters?”

The sobs had shrunk, baffled into partial silence by vocabulary.

“Yes! V-mom taught us this back in the dorm days — she said it’s the ancient wisdom of Chinese women, three moves, undefeated for a thousand years!”

“Move one — CRY. But never, EVER in front of the other woman. That only feeds her. You cry when it’s just you and Oppa. Quietly. Wronged. Fragile. His feelings run deepest for YOU — one look at you like that and his heart scrambles. Guilt. Ache. Checkmate of the heart.”

“Move two — FIGHT. Meaning: no matter what happens, the word ‘breakup’ never leaves your mouth. You hold the line. Let the woman who’s desperate to be promoted do all the screeching and all the fighting with Oppa. And when she’s worn him to the bone? That’s when you appear — soft, warm, understanding, asking for nothing. Side by side like that, who does he run back to? You. And he throws the exhausting one away himself.”

“And move three — THREATEN THE RAFTERS—” — the voice climbing with enthusiasm now — “if it turns out Oppa’s got other little side-flowers besides Fany eonni, you don’t even take the field yourself. Send ME! I’m the tactless little sister, I don’t know any better~ I’ll ‘accidentally’ set them against each other until they tear each other apart — and you, eonni, sit back and collect the winnings!”

Both ends of the line — and one tall man standing frozen in the middle of a hotel room — fell into a brief, dazed silence.

Cry, fight, threaten the rafters… it’s a WAR MANUAL?

Taeyeon’s hiccuping slowed. Unbidden, she thought of Ji-eun — of the two times she and Fany had run into her, and the sharp, shameful satisfaction of watching Fany step forward and defend her, claws out, on her behalf. Ji-ho never fought with anyone. He hated noise, hated scenes, walked away from mess on principle. So… what Sulli was saying… actually held together?

Like a drowning woman closing her hand around driftwood, Taeyeon asked, half-believing:

Voice Call · continued

“Sulli-yah… all of this… it would really work?”

“Of course! As long as Oppa still has you in his heart — still loves you — this combination is unblockable! Guaranteed!”

“Then— then how do I know… whether he still loves me?”

“Easy! Watch what he does next. If his guilty conscience makes him better and better to you — gentler, warmer, more attentive — he loves you so much it terrifies him to lose you. But if he goes cold… distant… then…” — the sentence left unfinished, its meaning finishing itself.

Silence from Seoul. Only suppressed breathing, and under it, a violent, invisible struggle: love and unwillingness to let go, the fury and pain of betrayal, and a desperate half-belief in a nineteen-year-old’s crooked scripture — all at war inside one small chest.

Choi Sulli waited, phone in one hand, the other hand unconsciously twisting the hem of her dress. Sunlight cut her profile in half — one side lucid and bright, the other drowned in shadow.

She had just used Taeyeon’s most broken moment to pin her in place — and lashed herself twice as tightly to the center of this whole burning chessboard while she did it.

She lifted her eyes to Ji-ho, and gave him a small, careful smile, mouthing the words without sound: See, Oppa? I can help you.

At last, Taeyeon’s voice came back — bitter as unsweetened medicine.

Voice Call · continued

“Sulli-yah. Whatever happens over there… you’ll tell me first. Promise me.”

“Don’t worry, eonni. I’m on YOUR side. You’re the only sister-in-law I have.”

“Mm. …Thank you, Sulli.”

She even said thank you. Sulli lowered the dead phone and laughed at herself, quietly, without any joy in it.

“A bad man and a bad woman. We really are a perfect match — aren’t we, Oppa?”

Ji-ho said nothing.

Because he had no better plan. That was the truth of it. Love bought with money, fame, talent — you could service it with money, fame, talent; you could pay its emotional invoices, buy its compromises. But love that had been given for NOTHING — given to the broke nobody in the bar with four thousand dollars to his name? He had no idea what that love would forgive. So he had stalled. And stalled. And told himself he could keep stalling. Now Sulli had put a knife through the beautiful lie, and it was deflating in front of him.

Sulli rose to her feet.

“Oppa. Taeyeon eonni’s heart is soft. Softer than anyone knows.”

“Just be good to her. So good it makes her feel guilty. Do that, and for the rest of her life — she’ll never get the word ‘breakup’ out of her mouth.”
