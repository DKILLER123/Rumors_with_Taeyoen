Chapter Forty-Two

A Small Scheme

The One taught out of the new building, and the corridors there came stocked with celebrities; Ji-ho bowed his way down the hallway with impeccable manners, humility set to maximum.

The One’s reputation ran industry-wide: severe, exacting, allergic to wasted time. He was already waiting in the vocal studio when Ji-ho arrived.

“Seonsaeng-nim. Good evening!”

A bare nod from the unsmiling face.

“Come here.”

No preamble; the man was constitutionally incapable of it.

“Your foundation is solid, so we’re skipping ahead. Tonight: mixed voice. Pay attention with more than your ears.”

And then The One simply took Ji-ho’s hands and pressed them flat — one to his own abdomen, one to his chest — and began to sing scales: first in pure chest voice, then in properly produced mix, letting the student feel the difference in the muscle states directly through his palms. Sound as anatomy.

The clock touched eight. The God of Learning switched on like a lamp.

Ji-ho closed his eyes and let the tactile data burn itself in — every subtle shift of support and release archived permanently, deeper than memory.

“Now you. Try.”

He set his breath. The One’s hands took up position — one at Ji-ho’s abdomen, one at his chest.

“Ready — begin!”

Field Notes

The One’s mixed-voice method — first principles:

“Stop trying to SING it. Try to SIGH it.” A demonstration: a long falling ah, breath-borne, effortless. “Feel the sound slide down the airflow on its own — not shoved out, carried out. That release is where the mix begins.”

“Breath is a leaking tire. Now pinch it with the vocal folds — gently, the way a finger narrows a hose until the water runs even instead of spraying. Fold closure versus airflow: two forces, one balance.”

“Hold that ‘si—’ thirty seconds. Follow me: soft! Hard! Soft! Long! Short! Long! The breath obeys YOU — never the reverse. The charm of a voice isn’t a pile of tricks. It’s truth, held steady. PINCH that airflow!”

Ji-ho followed, adjusted, followed again — and The One’s eyebrows betrayed him twice in one hour. A first-timer had no business calibrating this fast. Between a teacher who held nothing back and a student inhaling knowledge like oxygen, sixty minutes vanished without either noticing.

“Enough. That’s today.” A hand clapped his shoulder, warm past the gruffness. “Your vocal folds finished their workout — now they rest and rehydrate, and tomorrow they’re stronger. Overwork them and you’re the unstretched runner attempting a marathon: guaranteed injury.”

“Remember this. The greatest singer is not the one with the most beautiful voice. It’s the one whose voice is still healthy when the long career finally ends.”

“Thank you, seonsaeng-nim.” The bow came from the floor of his heart.

“Go.”

Ding— First session under a master instructor, amplified by God of Learning: an exceptionally practical vocal technique acquired. Vocal +1 → 75.

He eased the door shut behind him and let out a long breath. One of the Peninsula’s true master coaches — and it shows in every minute. The stat-tick had him walking on springs. He headed for the elevator, already budgeting a few more hours of dance drilling into the night—

“Ji-ho?”

A clear, warm voice behind him. He turned — Taeyeon, standing poised in the corridor light.

A pale-yellow blouse with a doll collar, delicate lace at the throat and cuffs — springtime rendered in fabric. A navy high-waisted A-line skirt swaying gently with her step, fair legs flashing in and out of view. Mary Janes adding the final note of playful charm.

“Nuna.”

“What are you doing here?”

“Nuna, you forgot?” He was already falling in beside her. “Today’s my first lesson day with seonsaeng-nim.”

“Ohh—” It came back to her; she’d been in the room when it was decided. The new album had simply eaten her memory whole. Her junior, though — her hoobae-disciple — merited proper concern. “How was the first class?”

A thumbs-up, entirely sincere.

“Even better than I hoped. Seonsaeng-nim is everything they say — SM’s ace coach, no exaggeration anywhere.”

“Obviously!” She nodded with proprietary pride. “So — what did he teach you today?”

“Mixed voice.”

“Mixed voice?” Her brows rose. “That’s genuinely difficult. How did it go?”

“Oh, I—”

The boast was locked, loaded, and halfway up his throat — and then his eyes happened across the delicate oval face tilted up at him from arm’s length, and the entire strategy rewrote itself mid-sentence. His shoulders drooped; gloom assembled itself across his features with theatrical precision.

“—I struggled, honestly. Maybe I’m just slow. Seonsaeng-nim explained it beautifully and it still wouldn’t click for me.”

“Hey — don’t be discouraged.” The comfort arrived instantly, reflexively. “Mixed voice is high-level technique. It takes talent AND years of grinding. Nobody gets it day one.”

“Thank you, nuna.” A pause. Then, with exquisitely calibrated hesitance: “Um… nuna, are you… heading home now?”

“Mm. Why?”

“It’s just — during the lesson, seonsaeng-nim kept saying you were the most gifted disciple he’s ever taught. That you mastered this exact technique almost immediately. So I thought — maybe — I could trouble you to show me how you did it.” And then the withdrawal, gracious and utterly weaponized: “But if you’re heading home to rest, forget I asked. I can’t go wasting your time on my problems.”

Inside Taeyeon’s chest, several flowers bloomed at once.

Seonsaeng-nim, honestly — yes, fine, the talent is real, but praising me like THAT just piles pressure on the poor kid. And look at him — struggling, and still thinking of MY rest first. This considerate little… Well. Today, of all days, the First Disciple clearly has a duty to perform.

“Come with me. Your nuna has accumulated a few insights about mixed voice, you know.”

“REALLY? Thank you, nuna! Beautiful, brilliant, AND generous — Taeyeon truly has it all!”
