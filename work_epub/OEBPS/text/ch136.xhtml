Chapter One Hundred Thirty-Six

The Boyfriend Halo

The car rejoined Seoul’s glittering night, and the cabin stayed very quiet.

Irene leaned into the seatback, face turned to the streaming lights, the flush not yet fully gone from her cheeks. Her slim fingers worried the edge of the seatbelt without her knowledge; her lips still carried the memory of temperature and touch. Everything had happened too fast — fast enough that her reason never finished building its wall before her heart had already walked through the gap.

Ji-ho glanced at her quiet profile at intervals, and the knowledge settled in him with enormous satisfaction: the face of a generation — and she was his.

He’d expected the shoot to ferment what was already between them. He hadn’t expected the window paper to tear TONIGHT, in this exact way. But love is impulsive and hearts are weather — and if tonight’s momentum went unclaimed, a Irene given three cold days to run the cost-benefit math might not permit herself the thought again for five years.

“Tired?”

She shook her head lightly and turned to look at him — eyes unusually clear in the dim cabin.

“It doesn’t feel real yet.”

He laughed low, took one hand off the wheel, and closed it over the hand resting on her knee. Her fingers flinched — and then, slowly, turned palm-up, and laced through his.

That one small answer softened something in his chest completely.

“Scared?” A gentle squeeze.

She was. Of the company finding out. Of the debut wobbling. Of this sudden closeness knocking over the balance she’d bled to keep. And yet, with her hand held like this, the fear kept getting drowned out by the older, larger current underneath — the one that wanted to move closer, and refused to let go.

“Would you let the things I’m scared of happen?” — half test, half lean.

“No.” His grip firmed. “You keep doing exactly what you do — work like before, shine like before. Everything else, leave to me.”

Overbearing — and it landed exactly where her anxiety lived. She thought of the trainee who’d out-worked everyone, and the artist who’d detonated on debut, and allowed herself to believe he might genuinely have the power to shelter what he chose to shelter.

The car stopped in a quiet lane short of the dorm — her requested distance. He killed the engine but not the locks, and turned to her, serious.

“From tomorrow: practice ends at eleven. No later.”

Her mouth opened; he overrode it.

“I’m deputizing Seung-wan to enforce it.” A beat. “And when you’re tired — message me. Call me. Or let me drive you somewhere with wind in it, like tonight.”

She looked into his eyes and nodded. He understood her pressure better than she’d guessed — and understood the shape of support she could actually accept: measured company, and one small gap to breathe through.

“Then — what are we, exactly?” The question finally made it out.

He smiled — gentle, and not entirely readable in the low light.

“A let-it-flow relationship.” Then, before the words could sting: “But about you — I’m serious. So don’t worry, and don’t carry it like a weight. We go at whatever pace you can accept.”

Half the steering wheel, handed back to her. The last thread of hesitation dissolved.

She nodded and unbuckled — and as her hand touched the door, he caught her wrist, lightly.

“Goodnight, nuna.”

And he pressed a kiss to the back of her hand.

Her heartbeat skipped one full beat. She looked back at him — his outline unreasonably sharp in the dark. Handsomer than yesterday? An illusion? Or just the girlfriend filter installing itself?

“Goodnight.”

She slipped out. He watched until her figure vanished toward the dorm before restarting the car. A message from Taeyeon glowed unread on his screen; he glanced at it and let it wait. The wheel turned a smooth arc toward home, and the warmth on his face folded itself away into level, working calm.

Joo-hyun had been outside the plan — but not outside control. The ledger, updated: Taeyeon was mutual love, the girlfriend who needed emotional harbor. IU wasn’t love — or was covetousness of his talent with a percentage of love dissolved in it, mirrored on his side by career math and the conqueror’s itch; their interests would only knot tighter. And Joo-hyun was the trainee who needed steady ground and a locked door — outwardly cold, inwardly scalding, fine-nerved, private to the bone. She would guard this the way she guarded any precious secret; Seul-gi herself would likely never hear a word. And Taeyeon — a homebody by nature, with Girls’ Generation mid-transition and a calendar like a minefield — had a buffer built in.

He wasn’t naive enough to believe the balance would hold forever. But with enough care, enough attention, enough safety dispensed to each — the crash risk stayed manageable. A message from Tiffany arrived mid-thought — arrangement talk again, the run-hot-run-cold rhythm of a woman who suspected her best friend’s claim and couldn’t put the curiosity down. He answered briefly, lukewarm by design. Every woman in his life was a different piece of music, and he intended to conduct each one on the correct stage, before the correct audience. It wasn’t fake, what he gave them — his capacity was simply larger than standard, and his allocation method… particular.

The elevator climbed. He messaged Joo-hyun: Home yet? Sleep early. No secret practicing.

The reply was nearly instant: Home. I won’t. Today… tired me out. Goodnight.

Mm. Goodnight. Remember to miss me.

Five full minutes later: You sleep early too… The weather’s turning. Have your manager stock cold medicine. Album prep means recording marathons — keep throat-soothing drinks ready. And mind your etiquette. I know you grew up in America — but you’re on the peninsula now.

He laughed out loud at the itemized nagging. Bae-eomma, exactly as advertised. The moment a woman’s heart settles, the caretaking instinct deploys.

ABSOLUTE FOCUS. On a stage, under lights — the fangirls in the pit stood no chance whatsoever. And Face 9: one point off the theoretical cap. Certified divine-visual territory.

He was at the bathroom mirror before he’d decided to stand. The face looking back was still unmistakably his own — brow, nose-line, jaw, all familiar — and yet everything had been… optimized, a hair at a time. The faint dullness of late nights: gone; the skin clean and lit from underneath. The eye-line adjusted by some divine sub-millimeter hand — sharper shape, brighter focus. The lips fuller in color. The whole face rebalanced into a coherence so complete it hit like force.

He looked at himself, understood the trait’s name from the inside — magnet to iron filings, brightest star in a dark sky — and grinned. The mirror grinned back, and even HE felt his mood lift at the sight. On a lit stage, behind a spotlight, this face plus this trait was no longer “likely to be popular.” It was “engineered to cause incidents.” From now on, he too qualified for the old line: as long as the face holds, the kingdom holds.
