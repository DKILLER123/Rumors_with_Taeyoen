Chapter One Hundred Fifty-Two

Ji-eun

Deep night · Hong Kong His hotel suite the answer, delivered by perfume

It was deep night by the time he got back to his own hotel.

The entry light woke as the key card clicked. He draped the suit jacket over the sofa arm, loosened his collar, and went straight to the suitcase — navy silk sleepwear, folded neat. He shed the evening a layer at a time and was headed for the shower when—

Knock. Knock. Knock.

He froze, pulled the sleepwear on fast, cinched the belt, and checked the peephole.

Ji-eun stood in the corridor. The ceremony gown was gone; she wore soft grey cotton loungewear.

He opened the door.

“You’re back? How was the night out?” He stepped aside to let her pass.

The corridor air came in with her — carrying a distinct edge of alcohol.

“It was fun.” Her mouth curved, technically. She walked in, steps not quite level. “We went to Victoria Harbour. The night view’s gorgeous — the Avenue of Stars, the Star Ferry… it earns its reputation.”

She sat down on the edge of the sofa.

He shut the door, took a bottle of water from the minibar, cracked the cap, and sat beside her.

“Drink some water.”

“Thanks.” She took it and sipped, small.

Quiet, for a few seconds.

Then her eyes lifted — and stopped on the navy silk. Held there for two seconds. Darkened.

“Your friend tonight— did you have a good time?”

He paused half a beat, then nodded.

“Mm. It was good.”

“Is that so?” Her fingers tightened, unconscious; the plastic bottle creaked. “That’s nice.”

She set the water down. Then she moved — folding softly against him, forehead into the hollow of his shoulder, breath warm through the silk. Her voice came out muffled, roughened at the edge by the alcohol.

“Now… can you finally give me your answer?”

His arm settled around her shoulders. He didn’t answer. He asked instead:

“Ji-eun-ah. Have you ever thought about what comes after?”

“After what?” She didn’t lift her head.

“Do you want to work for someone else your whole life? Have you ever considered—” his voice slid into that patient, coaxing register — “why the two of us don’t—”

But she wasn’t listening.

Her nose was working, quietly, against the skin of his neck — parsing the layered signals there with a specialist’s precision. First: white magnolia over a soft milky warmth. Taeyeon’s “Magnolia Poem.” She’d smelled it that very afternoon; the memory was hours old. Then: gardenia and dark ebony wood — a bath product. Not remarkable in itself, hotels used similar lines — except THIS Four Seasons stocked white musk. And his own soap, the one he traveled with, was cedar over sandalwood-amber. So where had gardenia-and-ebony come from? The question answered itself.

And the finishing detail: Taeyeon’s perfume was still readable on him AFTER a shower. Gardenia and ebony should have buried the magnolia — but her scent clung to his skin, lifted brighter by post-bath warmth. Which was only possible if the perfume had transferred at zero distance. Skin to skin.

The image forced its way in — a dim hotel room, steam, a pale arm hooked around his neck, a whispered word, a smile with victory in it— They met. They — the needle went through the last of her restraint, and the alcohol carried the fire straight up.

“Shibal— you son of a—”

“Mwo?” Mid-pitch about ambition and futures, he looked down, blindsided.

Her head snapped up.

The eyes that always held laughter or cleverness held neither. They were rimmed red, whites shot through, pupils churning with betrayal and the humiliation of having been played. The alcohol had burned off every layer of poise.

“AISH!!!”

She shoved him with everything she had. He went back into the sofa cushions, collar falling open — and there, at the collarbone, a shallow reddened mark. Scratch or kiss, it hardly mattered which.

Her eyes nailed to it. Her breath stopped.

Then she was on her feet, hand already swinging.

His pupils snapped; pure reflex jerked his head aside — her fingers grazed his cheek, close enough to leave a line of heat.

“Ya! Have you lost your mind?!” He pushed himself up, shock and anger arriving together.

“Mwo? I’VE lost MY mind?!”

She snatched the water bottle off the table — the one he’d just handed her — and swung it in an arc without even looking.

Uncapped. The cold water cut a bright line through the air and took him across the face and chest. The silk soaked through instantly, ice against skin, water beading down his jaw onto the dark velvet of the sofa.

“Ssss—!” He flinched hard, swiped the water off his face, and stared at her in disbelief. “Ji-eun! You—”

The half-full bottle came at his head.

Too close to dodge — his forearm came up on instinct.

Thunk.

Bottle and water together, square on the forearm bone. The pain flashed white.

“Ah! Aish!” He shook out the arm, temper catching. “What is WRONG with you?!”

“You’re right! I HAVE lost my mind!”

Whatever reason remained had burned to the socket. She grabbed the sofa cushion — a fat, soft down pillow — and came at him swinging it like a weapon, blow after blow after blow.

“You bastard! You trash! You animal who PLAYS with people!”

“Shibal!”

“You son of a—!”

A curse per swing. Down feathers burst from the seams and hung in the air like a private snowfall. The blows didn’t truly hurt — but the fury behind them was the kind that wants to level cities.

“You went to see Taeyeon, didn’t you?!”

“You SHOWERED! Did you sleep together?! SAY IT!!”

Her voice was shredding, and she kept going.

“What ARE you two?!”

“What am I to you?!”

“You came back wearing her PERFUME — to disgust me?!”

“Shibal! SHIBAL!!”

Feathers in her hair, on her shoulders, on the soaked sofa, on his silent face. Her hair had come down; the grey collar hung askew; she looked wrecked. And the fire in her eyes only climbed — aimed at this man who would not defend himself, or could not.

He had blocked at first, arms up around his head. Then he stopped. He simply sat there and took it, swaying with each impact, soaked silk clinging, water still dripping from his hair down his neck. No guilt on his face. No panic. Barely even surprise. Only a flat, bottomless calm — and calm like that reads as confession.

Which broke her worse than anything.

At last her arms gave out. The pillow dropped to the carpet with a soft whump and a final puff of down. She stood gasping, chest heaving, red eyes locked on him while tears ran without sound. The room held nothing but her ragged breathing and his.

He raised his eyes slowly to the girl coming apart in front of him. His mouth opened — something about to be said.

Nothing came out.

And that quiet gaze was colder than any denial or any shout could have been. Because she understood, suddenly and completely: the answer had been written all along — in the perfume at his neck, in the mark at his collarbone, in this exact silence. She had never needed to ask. The moment the question left her mouth, the only person who lost was her.

But she could not accept it. The nights in the studio until sunrise. The arguments over a single melody line that ended in laughter. Watching him climb from unknown trainee to the soloist the whole country watched. The talent she looked up to; the profile she’d memorized; the late-night calls; the accidental touches that were never accidents; the long kisses — everything, everything short of the final line, they had already done. Was silence supposed to settle THAT account? Was her heart something he could just… handle?

“Say something! Ji-ho!” Her voice shook itself hoarse. “Aren’t you the one who’s so good with sweet words? In bed— on the phone— was all of it fake?!”

“SAY SOMETHING!”

She sank onto the rug, undone.

“Please— say anything— tell me it isn’t what I think—”

He pressed two fingers to his temple and let out a slow breath.

“You’re right. Taeyeon nuna and I are together.”

The air went out of the room. Her sobbing stopped mid-breath. She had known — and hearing it still turned her blood to ice water.

“Shibal-nom—” It came out through her teeth. “Then what are WE? What do we count as?”

She pushed herself up to kneeling, face tipped up at him, his features swimming familiar and foreign through the blur.

“In your eyes — what exactly am I, Ji-eun?”

He held her gaze for two seconds.

“Someone I like. Obviously.”

“Like?” She grabbed the word like a rope — and watched it fray in her hands. “Then Taeyeon? Will you— are you going to break up with her?”

He shook his head.

“No.”

Two flat syllables, and the last thread of maybe was cut clean through.

“Mwo…? …Shibal.”

The absurdity crested over the anger, big enough that she almost laughed. She stared at this man — terrifyingly unfamiliar, pitifully deranged.

“So what you’re saying is—” her voice trembled with the sheer scale of it — “you intend to date me AND Taeyeon? At the same time? To feed a man’s greed and his trophy-hunting?”

A twisted, bitter laugh escaped her.

“Who do you think you are? Samsung’s Lee Jae-yong? SK’s Chey Tae-won? Even THEY don’t put it on the table and call it— call it reasonable.”

She dragged herself upright on unsteady legs, seized the other cushion, and hurled it at his infuriatingly calm face with everything left in her.

“What do you think you ARE?!”

He caught it one-handed, without hurry, and dropped it on the carpet.

“Samsung’s Lee Jae-yong. SK’s Chey Tae-won.” He repeated the names, and something odd crossed his face. “You reached for THOSE two comparisons without thinking. Which tells me something, Ji-eun — in your own head, what you’re rejecting isn’t the sharing. It’s that Ji-ho, at his CURRENT altitude, hasn’t earned the rules those men live by.”

He leaned forward, wet hair shadowing his eyes, and pinned her flustered stare.

“And the gym, that first time. The deliberate display — that particular outfit. Wasn’t the seed of it Taeyeon? A little provocation, a little scorekeeping, a little test? Your approach had a win condition built into it from day one. Didn’t it?”

“How DARE you—” Her face changed color — the humiliation of being read correctly braided through the fury of being read wrong. “Fine — yes — at the very start there was some of that. But after— after—”

“After, what?” He didn’t let her finish. “After, you decided I had real talent, real upside — worth investing feelings and a future in — and the act became sincere? Ji-eun. Don’t package yourself as the pure-hearted victim. Neither of us is that person.”

She wanted to argue. Her throat wouldn’t cooperate. Because the memory WAS that clear: it had begun with not-losing. With the faint threat that hung around Taeyeon. The wanting came after. But the late-night calls, the leaning, the falling — was all of that fake? Was it all scorekeeping? She no longer knew where one ended.

“If sharing were truly unacceptable to you,” he pressed on, each step measured, “you’d have slapped me, walked out, called me trash, and never once looked back. That’s what that woman does.”

“You didn’t. You asked me what I DESERVE. You cited Lee Jae-yong and Chey Tae-won. Why those names?”

“Because you grew up inside this industry, inside this society, and you’ve seen enough to know exactly how it works: the closer you get to the top of the pyramid, the further the rules drift from ordinary morality. You accepted that reality a long time ago. So when you caught me reaching past the line, your first instinct wasn’t ‘love is sacred.’ It was ‘YOU? You’re not tall enough yet.’”

“Ji-eun.” His voice found a strange, resonant register. “Underneath, we’re the same animal. Ordinary beginnings — humble ones. A little talent, and a work ethic that frightens people, and a fixed intention to climb out of the pit and hold our own fates with our own hands.”

He paused, watching the storm turn behind her eyes.

“That refusal to lose — that ambition — it’s the brightest thing about you. It’s also why this hurts the way it does. You saw through the rules earlier than the romantics ever do. And now the rules and the feelings are tearing you in half.”

“Admit it. That’s who you are.”

She stood frozen. The tears had stopped without her noticing. His words went off in her skull one after another — because yes, she had envied and despised and UNDERSTOOD, all at once, the women who attached themselves to chaebol heirs; she knew where money and power pooled, beauty followed; she knew the world’s operating manual was nothing like the fairy tale on screen. So how much of her refusal was faith in pure love — and how much was pride’s arithmetic: Ji-ho, you haven’t earned my compromise?

“Fine.” Her own voice came out dry and scraped. She wiped her face hard with the back of her hand. “Say you’re right. Say that’s what I am. So what?”

She straightened her spine.

“And what exactly are YOU right now?” The mockery came back into her voice, keen-edged. “A soloist who’s been famous for two months? A clever boy who writes hit songs? You don’t actually believe that’s bought you out of the entertainer class, do you?”

She took a step forward.

“Smile on stage, smile off stage, bend at the waist for the money and the seniors — at bottom, you and I are PRODUCT. Merchandise that reads the room for a living.”

She laughed once, cold, with something like grief underneath it.

“And with THAT résumé, you fantasize about living like the men who actually own things — keeping me AND Taeyeon? On the strength of that face? On one weightless little ‘I like you’? Ji-ho, don’t make me laugh.”

Her gaze raked the soaked silk, the ruined hair, the face that stayed unfairly beautiful through all of it.

“If your grand ambition tops out at collecting women, it isn’t just laughable — it’s pitiful. You have no idea what real power even looks like.”

“You’re right,” he said, and nodded, entirely without heat. “At my current standing, what I’m proposing is delusion. It’s an insult. Agreed.”

He rose and walked, unhurried, to the floor-to-ceiling window. Hong Kong blazed below — neon rivers, towers drawing the skyline of money and power in light, and beneath all of it, the black water and the chasm nobody photographs.

“Which is why I asked you the question you didn’t listen to. Ji-eun-ah — have you ever thought about what comes after?”

He turned. The wet silk clung to the line of his shoulders; the city glow rimmed him in a blurred halo; his expression was unreadable.

“You ask what I deserve. What I have is the same ambition you do — and possibly a hotter strain of it.”

“I will not spend my life as a performer who smiles for a living.”

A beat. Then, with precision:

“So tell me, Ji-eun. Inside that rulebook you already believe in — the brutal one — what do I have to become, how high do I have to climb, before ‘having you and Taeyeon both’ stops being a joke and starts being something you could consider?”

Her eyes narrowed, the way you look at an unfamiliar animal.

“Lunatic.”

He was unbothered. He set a name on the table, flat as a card.

“If I get where Shin Min-hyuk is — command of our schedules, my own production division, a real voice — is that enough?”

Shin Min-hyuk: real influence in the industry, nowhere near real power. She actually laughed — the laugh you give a fantasist.

“Then Park Sung-hoon. Running an entire major agency. Enough?”

Park Sung-hoon. LOEN’s sitting CEO. Her boss. Genuine authority, genuine resources — the power to move artists’ fates and billions in production money, a seat near the actual capital. Her eyes flickered, once. If Ji-ho stood THERE— no. Even there. Park Sung-hoon was a salaried manager under SK Telecom. A board above him. A headquarters above the board. Reports. Compromises.

He read the hesitation and nodded slowly.

“Still short, then. All right. And if I buy LOEN?”

“Buy… LOEN?”

It was the funniest thing she’d heard all year, and the absurdity of it actually diluted the rage.

“Ji-ho. Do you know what that COSTS? Three hundred billion won. Why would SKT ever sell? With WHAT would you ever buy? Do you understand what kind of being writes a check that size? Do you imagine men like that are short on women?”

“You. A singer who’s been hot for eight weeks. Standing in a wet pajama set, announcing an acquisition of LOEN.”

She shook her head, and suddenly the whole night tasted like ash — she had lost her composure, her dignity, and apparently her judgment, over a man who lived inside a fantasy. She was tired. Body and heart both.

“Forget it.” She turned for the door, legs still unsteady. “Tonight never happened. My eyes were bad — I misread a person, that’s all. From now on we’re colleagues. And when it’s not strictly necessary — not even that.”

Her hand closed on the cold metal of the door handle — the chill of it steadying her half a degree.

And from behind her, perfectly clear:

“Call me insane if you want. But the LOEN acquisition — I’m going to do it.”

Her hand stopped on the handle. She didn’t turn around.

“And, Ji-eun — remember tonight. The day I get it done, I’m going to ask you again.”

The arrogance in it finally found one last nerve. She spun, eyes red-rimmed and burning all over again, mouth curling.

“Ha— get it done? If you, Ji-ho, ever actually own LOEN—”

She bit off every word, self-immolating and contemptuous in equal measure:

“—then I, Ji-eun, will come to your door myself and BEG you to keep me.”

Silence. Outside, the neon turned over; a car’s lights crossed the dark like a brief meteor.

He looked at her for a few seconds, eyes deep in the shadow of his wet hair.

“Then remember what you just said.”

She gave him one last look — the man standing at the seam of light and dark, soaked through and spine perfectly straight — then wrenched the door open and was gone into the dim corridor.

Click.

The room went completely quiet.

He walked to the window and stood there a long time, watching the ferries drag ribbons of light across black water, until the silk had half-dried against his skin.

Then he turned toward the bathroom.

This time, he genuinely needed the shower.
