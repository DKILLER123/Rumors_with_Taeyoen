Chapter One Hundred Thirty-Seven

Across the Water

Shoot Day Udo, Jeju — “Spring Day” MV Set white sand, black lava cliffs, and one very distracted actress

Morning on Udo: salt-damp wind with flowers hidden somewhere in it, white sand against black volcanic rock, the sea scattering morning light like coins.

Kim Ji-won waited at the edge of the crew in a pale beige cardigan and fringed skirt, watching Ji-ho confirm blocking with the director — and made a small involuntary sound in her own head.

Sixteen days since the set visit. And he was… different. Not the hair, not the clothes — something finer than either. Same face; same clean bones. But when he turned to point at the far cliffs and the morning light landed along his cheek, she forgot, briefly, how breathing worked. A handsomeness that HELD the eye. Clean, translucent — and impossible to file under “background.”

“Ji-won-ssi — first setup is the bicycles,” an assistant murmured.

She surfaced, cheeks warm, and walked to the white bicycle with her heart still misbehaving. How does a person like this exist? Standing NEAR him is nerve-wracking.

“Nervous?” He’d materialized beside her with a bottle of water, voice mild.

Her fingers brushed his taking it — a faint static prickle.

“A little—” she admitted honestly, glancing up and immediately down. “It’ll pass once we’re rolling.”

And it did — that was her professional wiring: blush-prone in private, instantly transformed the moment a lens found her.

Performance

“ACTION.”

She rides the coast path, wind lifting flax-pale hair and white fringe, smile bright and unforced — spring itself on two wheels. He appears from a crossing lane; they “happen” to meet at the junction. In frame, his eyes carry first-encounter wonder; hers answer with a flush so natural that she herself briefly loses track of which part is acting.

“CUT! Lovely! Ten minutes — then the hand-holding walk.”

She dismounted and exhaled from her toes. That eye contact had nearly knocked her off the script — too focused, too penetrating, a gaze that went straight through to the floor of a person. She had MET handsome men. Why was today like this?

“That was good. Very natural.” He handed her a clean towel.

“You too—” she said, small, noting the fine sweat at his temple catching sunlight, deciding firmly not to have the thought she was having, and having it anyway.

The hand-holding takes went smoother — she poured herself into the girl-who-met-someone-in-spring, and the genuine tremor when her fingers settled into his palm earned an enthusiastic “REAL! Very good!” from the director.

Between setups they sat in the tree-shade, and she gathered her nerve.

“Ji-ho-ssi… has something changed lately?”

“Changed?”

“Just—” she sketched vaguely with one hand, “—you seem more… radiant? I can’t explain it.” Regret arrived instantly: it sounded like the worst pickup line in Jeju.

He laughed — unfairly attractive in the dappled light.

“Probably just Jeju sun.” And he slid the topic gently to her upcoming auditions, and listened the way he listened — attentive, funny in the responses, never too warm, never letting silence go awkward — and she discovered that talking to him was simply… easy.

The last setup was two silhouettes on the cliff at sundown — no lines, just sea wind and the sky changing color. At wrap she bowed her thanks with complete sincerity, added several points to an already generous opinion — professional, considerate, and that face, which was frankly a museum piece — and looked back once from the car window: his profile against the last of the light, cut sharp enough to remember.

Message · Kim Ji-won → Ji-ho

Thank you for taking care of me today — it was a really happy shoot. Safe travels!

Reply, near-instant: “You too. Get home safe. See you next time.” — and she stared at “see you next time” smiling until she caught herself, clapped both hands over her hot face, and issued an internal citation: Kim Ji-won, you are being COMPLETELY abnormal today.

“That fast?”

Kim Ji-soo. The eyebrow went up on its own. In his first life’s memory, that particular face wasn’t due on screens for years yet — and here the timeline was bending itself politely around him.

Next morning she was on set: eighteen, white shirt and jeans, hair soft on her shoulders — the features already fully arrived, barely a degree greener than the future’s polished version, with a trainee’s carefulness living in her eyes. At the sight of him she visibly froze one beat, then bowed fast.

“Hello, sunbae-nim. I’m Kim Ji-soo, a YG trainee. Please guide me well.”

Crisp voice, flawless etiquette — and ear-tips going pink, gaze bouncing off his on contact. YG’s house aesthetic ran to strong personalities over pretty faces; but a face currently running ABSOLUTE FOCUS sat outside everyone’s calibration, industry veterans included — let alone an undebuted eighteen-year-old.

“No need to be nervous.” He kept it warm. “Read the script?”

“I did.” She produced a copy creased soft from handling. “I mapped the emotional shifts — though a few places I’m still not sure—”

And the greenness fell away the instant she talked craft. Prepared. Thinking. Interesting.

Performance

“MOONLIGHT” · the treatment: lovers parted by a misunderstanding retrace their memories under moonlight, and reconcile before dawn — a night beach, an abandoned lighthouse.

Evening: white dress, makeup pared down to clear eyes and natural youth. First setup — the beach meeting, first-flutter and shyness required. She’s stiff on the rehearsal pass — until he leans close and low: “Don’t act it. You’re really on a beach at night, and you’ve just met someone who makes your pulse jump. Good moon. Soft wind. And then — you see him.” Her eyes soften and brighten on the description itself.

“ACTION.” — light-footed approach, one upward glance carrying startled wonder, a smile like a struck match. “CUT! Ji-soo-ssi has real gifts!”

(She was barely acting. The wonder was sourced locally.)

The night runs long and smooth: little experience, fast uptake — and a born instinct for the lens, always knowing her angle, always finding the frame. The acting: adequate. The camera-sense: gold.

In a break under the lighthouse, cocoa in hand, she asked quietly:

“Sunbae-nim… was I really okay just now?”

“The acting’s average.” Honest, as ever. “But you know how to hold a camera. That’s a genuine talent.”

Her face warmed in the dark.

“Honestly, I was so nervous… especially playing opposite sunbae-nim.” A breath, then courage: “You’re really… very good-looking. It’s distracting sometimes.”

The bluntness made him laugh.

“That’s supposed to be MY line. YG’s been hiding a trainee like you — and Yang Hyun-suk sajang-nim just lets you out for an MV?”

“Sajang-nim said…” she pressed her lips, “…it’s a very good opportunity. And that working with sunbae-nim — the company takes it seriously.”

Message received: YG wanted his heat to pave her road early — and his being CHOSEN for that meant his weight in their building was already real.

Four AM; last shot down; the east going fish-belly pale. Wrapped in a blanket, eyes still bright, she bowed deep at the parting.

“Thank you for today’s guidance. I learned so much.”

“You brought the talent yourself. Train hard. I’ll be watching for your debut.”

“I will.” Her eyes steadied. “And when it comes… I hope we meet on a stage, sunbae-nim.”

He watched her car vanish into the sea fog. Eighteen, and already the silhouette of the future was legible: beautiful, diligent, and precise about what she wanted. And he — apparently — had just become a small, early hand on the wheel of that career.

Post-production went to the company; his own calendar detonated into promo season. A second Running Man invitation. Radio Star. Happy Together. The variety machine had discovered that he was reliable television — and every appearance, ABSOLUTE FOCUS did its silent work: once he entered frame, eyes simply stopped leaving. The face itself became a fandom talking point; the surgery whispers lasted about a day before his own fans buried them — the man is on camera weekly; nobody heals THAT fast.

And the bigger itinerary waited across the water.

And Ji-ho — the LA-raised phenomenon whose overseas fan communities had been building since the Sulli story broke — was the invitation they wanted most.

Arrival Changsha Huanghua Airport several hundred reasons to believe the numbers

The arrivals gate stunned him slightly. He’d expected a crowd; he had not expected several HUNDRED — lightboards and banners with his name, and a wall of sound the moment he appeared.

“SONG JI-HO! SONG JI-HO!”

“IU! IU!”

“Ji-ho-ya — over HERE!”

He kept the warm smile up the whole gauntlet, security parting the crowd. IU leaned close, under the noise:

“You’re THIS popular here?”

“They’ve been with me since before the debut,” he said, smiling. “When your people watch you make it somewhere far away — they show up.”

Some had found him through “Some”; the larger share had followed him since the Sulli story and the scandal cycle — the subtitle-team faithful, converting all that history into airport turnout.

Twenty chaotic minutes later they were in the car. Tomorrow’s taping was discussed; the show’s veteran host was, he assured a visibly nervous IU, famously professional and famously kind to foreign guests — and anyway:

“You’ve got a live-in translator. Treat it as a vacation.”

Though privately he knew the stakes: this appearance was the door into an enormous market, and a clean first impression would save a hundred later headaches. That evening the program’s writers walked them through the flow; the young staffer’s parting remark — “our whole group chat is discussing you” — suggested the door was already half-open.

That night, three photos on his phone: Joo-hyun’s sweaty practice-mirror selfie (“working hard” — reply: “beautiful. stretch.”); a city-night shot sent to a silent, busy Taeyeon; and one line to IU — rest early, hwaiting tomorrow — answered in seconds: you too. goodnight.

He stood at the window a while. The river city’s lights were nothing like Seoul’s — and nothing like LA’s either — and carried, strangely, a familiarity anyway: the smell of the old-quarter food streets had drifted up even to here, and somewhere in it was a thread that led all the way back to a kung-fu video store in a Chinatown ten thousand kilometers and one lifetime away.

Tomorrow, a stage. Walk up. Become the light. SUNYE has to sell — the plan runs on money now.
