Chapter One Hundred Nine

First Variety

KBS2 Studio “Yoo Hee-yeol’s Sketchbook” — Backstage enter stage left. or was it right.

Ji-eun stood shoulder to shoulder with him, wearing a smile that was not entirely a smile.

“First time. Nervous?”

“Claiming otherwise would be a lie.” One deep breath.

“Relax. Just don’t enter from the wrong side.” She patted his shoulder and extended one porcelain arm to point. “You go in from the LEFT. I take the right.”

“Off you go.”

He made it two steps before the conversation’s aftertaste caught up with him, and he turned to give her a long, suspicious look.

The delicate, immaculately innocent face blinked back.

“Mwo?”

“…Nothing.” He turned and walked to his mark.

Behind him, IU smirked in quiet triumph.

Post-explosion, her schedule had bent to accommodate his: joint music shows, promo stages, corporate events, threaded around her own first solo tour. Same company, same itineraries, one extremely ambiguous duet — familiarity had been inevitable. And with familiarity had come the revelation: beneath the national little sister’s pristine face lived a spectacularly unfiltered sense of humor. A connoisseur of off-color jokes, gender-blind in her teasing — spending her Kakao hours needling Sulli, or needling Jiyeon, and now, having grown comfortable, needling Ji-ho at a minimum of twice daily, apparently for health reasons. Pure and demure in front of strangers; a full-blown Lich King behind the curtain.

Women like this are the most dangerous kind.

Staff fitted their earpieces and mics. Out front, the host began; Ji-ho and IU started their walk toward the small stage planted in the audience. The fans were electric — and impeccably, respectfully silent.

“The two guests about to appear are the gods of love responsible for this month’s sharp nationwide increase in sweet couples.”

“Messengers who make you want to date the moment you press play.”

The hall’s two fandoms broke into cheers.

“Please welcome — IU, and Ji-ho!”

Performance

The light finds him first.

He opens with the ballad standard of another hemisphere — the late-century classic about oceans between two people and a vow to wait, however long, right where he stands. He keeps it in the low-middle registers, no vocal fireworks at all: clear tone, warm middle, the highest note a feather of soft mixed voice, the slow 4/4 rolling underneath like a tide. It’s a singalong in waiting — and by the second chorus, half the hall is mouthing the promise with him.

The English is flawless — no accent to detect, because there is nothing to detect: it’s his first language, the sound of the LA radio stations he grew up under. The realization moves through the crowd as a second, separate wave of appreciation.

He bows and steps toward the stage. Somewhere, quietly: “…This kid.”

IU bit her lip. Should have sung FIRST. Tactical error. Pressure being fuel, she inhaled, reset—

Performance

The light crosses to her.

Her signature hit — the one about a sky too blue and a wind too perfect on the day a girl decides not to confess. Two years past its release, she is simply better now: riper control, deeper story. The famous three-step high-note staircase arrives — and lands, all three landings, effortless.

The hall erupts. Honors even. Possibly.

Amid the cheering she crossed to the stage and took the seat beside Ji-ho; the host settled next to them.

“Wah — up close, Ji-ho-ssi is genuinely TOO handsome. And IU today, lovely as ever. With you two seated there, the frame looks like a pictorial. Everyone — IU and Ji-ho!”

“Annyeonghaseyo!”

Greetings in order — his wave and bow drawing a decibel spike with an unmistakably female frequency profile — and then Yoo Hee-yeol turned to IU.

“IU-ssi, brutal schedule lately, no? Promotions AND a first solo tour.”

“Ne, truly busy. But — happily busy.”

“Of course happily. WAH.” A theatrical headshake. “‘Some’ is EVERYWHERE. The cafe by my house is playing it when I go to work — and when I come home, it is STILL PLAYING.”

The audience’s laughter had the ring of lived experience.

“And I hear the words and music are Ji-ho-ssi’s own work?”

“Ne.”

“Genuinely extraordinary. Could you share the — hm — the inspiration?”

A question he’d war-gamed long ago.

“Honestly, ‘Some’ was born like an accidental encounter. One day in the practice room I stumbled on a chord progression, and it suddenly reminded me of that state between people — near but not near, far but not far. So much to say, and all of it stopping at some invisible line.”

“I wanted the melody to capture that feeling of an unfinished conversation. It was harder than it sounds — luckily a teacher helped me, and shared her own story.”

“The best part is watching listeners write their own endings onto it. Every time someone says ‘this song is MY story’ — that’s the whole meaning of it.”

“Ahh, I see.” A nod, and a pivot. “IU-ssi — your partner debuted after three months of training. How long did YOU train? Remember?”

“I passed LOEN’s audition in October 2007, and debuted September 18, 2008 — about ten months.”

“Both absurdly short. Both absurdly talented.” He consulted his cards with a glint. “And apparently these two share ONE more thing… a truly impressive number of FAILED auditions.”

Two simultaneously sheepish faces. The audience leaned in.

“IU-ssi. Is it true? Many failures?”

She covered her mouth, laughed, and confessed to what her fans already knew from last year’s talk show.

“Before LOEN… I failed exactly twenty auditions.”

“TWENTY? Really? Why?”

“At the time I was, um, a little chubby. And my fashion sense was… mature beyond its years.” The blush was real. “So. Twenty.”

“I see, I see. And Ji-ho-ssi? With that face and that voice — how does one FAIL?”

IU turned to stare at him with predatory curiosity.

“Mm… about twelve times, I think. The thirteenth was SM, and I passed.”

“Ani — WHY?” IU’s enormous eyes went full question mark. “SM is supposed to be the HARDEST one.”

“Probably the face,” the host cut in gravely. “Ji-ho-ssi is genuinely too handsome. Isn’t Lee Soo-man seonsaeng-nim famous for loving pretty children?”

The hall cracked up. IU examined the sculpted profile at close range and privately conceded the hypothesis was not without merit.

“…Could that actually be it?” Ji-ho looked genuinely thoughtful.

“Ne?!” Host, partner, and three hundred audience members startled in unison — then dissolved.

“A joke.” He straightened. “Truthfully: my age was wrong. Nineteen, with zero trainee history — already past the window. And back then, my dancing was poor and my singing was nowhere near what it is now. Company after company thought about it, and passed.”

Strictly true. Without a previous lifetime’s twelve years of bar stages folded into his vocal stat, the actual nineteen-year-old Ji-ho would never have cleared a single audition.

“I knew SM’s bar was the highest — I went as a last shot. And I sang with everything I had left. Maybe it was exactly that desperation that moved the judges, because they kept me as a probationary trainee.”

“In the conversion evaluation, my vocal examiner was Taeyeon nuna.”

The name alone raised a cheer from the hall.

“Nuna saw potential in my voice. I practiced like my life depended on it, my vocal score came back perfect, and I entered SM properly. Later I also became a disciple of The One seonsaeng-nim.”

Ahh, the audience nodded — so THAT’S the special history with Taeyeon. No wonder they’re close.

He knew, with complete certainty, that she’d be watching tonight. So he threaded her through the answers — openly, warmly, on the record. Vaccination, too: establish the closeness in daylight, and a future photograph becomes a footnote instead of a bomb.

The host seized the opening gracefully.

“Then, Ji-ho-ssi — why DID you leave SM? The internet has developed some… conspiracy theories.”

The theory in question: the dating was real, so SM expelled him — because why else release someone this valuable? Right, and wrong, in the usual proportions. The rumble was loud enough that answering here, once, was the whole reason for tonight’s booking.

“Honestly, the reason I left SM…” He let the pause do its work. “…is very simple. We couldn’t agree on the contract.”

Surprise — then a ripple of dawning, almost comic comprehension. SM’s stinginess was a national institution. A rookie who writes his own All-Kills asking for better terms? And SM balking? PERFECTLY plausible. Case closed, apparently.

Performance

The talk ends; the lights fall to pink; the duet begins — the promotion’s whole point.

His soft opening line; her narrating answer about pacing an empty room while yesterday’s drama replays. They drift toward center; she takes his hand — HER move, made in full view — and the two of them face each other on the small stage as the hook rises: the lover-not-lover circling, the words “just friends” gone suddenly unbearable, the wish to wake to a message every morning — all of it delivered in two braided voices and one held look, charisma at saturation.

First variety appearance: cleared.
