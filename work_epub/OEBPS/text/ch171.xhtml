Chapter One Hundred Seventy-One

A Class for Best Friends

March 2013 · night, after wrap The set → LOEN headquarters one tutor, one eager student, and one extremely reluctant auditor

The long, full shooting day finally lowered its curtain.

Nine p.m. On Kwak Jae-yong’s called wrap, The Girl in the Sun set dissolved into a chorus of “good work today”s, crew peeling away into the night in twos and threes.

Out of costume and into his own jacket, he walked for the exit kneading the stiffness out of the back of his neck.

One full day inside the machine, and his respect for the Korean film industry’s precision had gone up another full notch.

Growing up in LA, watching the K-dramas his grandmother taped and the films that crossed the Pacific after them, he’d assumed the industry’s Asia-wide dominance ran on top-shelf directors and writers, fearless subject matter, and a creative leash long enough to barely qualify as a leash.

Standing inside it now, he understood: that was maybe forty percent of the answer.

The rest was this — a mature industrial system, ground and polished for over a decade by everyone working under the country’s culture-first national strategy.

The makeup artists building character into every detail of a face; the stylists dressing not bodies but temperaments. The DP composing light and frame like a draftsman; the gaffers conjuring romance or dread or warmth out of nothing but color temperature and angle. The props team resurrecting whole eras with the right kettle on the right shelf—

Every gear machined to spec, every gear meshing.

Seoul itself was a city of crowding and age and gloss all at once — plenty of its corners were frankly grubby. But run it through this system’s sleight of hand — clever set dressing, engineered light, fluid camera moves, the grade and the cut — and what reached the audience was something dreamlike, romantic, filtered at the source.

That was the power of industrialization.

Since The Girl in the Sun sat squarely in Kwak Jae-yong’s pure-love wheelhouse, once cameras rolled, the set was effectively a monarchy. Shots were added and trimmed, lines adjusted, and the screenwriter was rarely consulted — and the screenwriter had zero objection. At Kwak’s level, the personal style was fully formed and repeatedly proven: rhythm, atmosphere, the tuning of actors, all of it ran on a system of his own. His register lived between art-house romance and light comedy, taut and slack in the right proportions, always leaving the audience room to feel a character change. The script had only ever been a skeleton and a blueprint; the flesh and the spirit were carved on set, by the director and the whole crew together.

Evenings belonged mostly to the two leads — the other actors had schedules to chase. That was the great perk of shooting in Seoul: with commutes this short, a good supporting actor could juggle three productions at once.

Sulli, having shed her early habit of glancing at the lens, was flowing now. They shot until nine before the day let them go.

“Sulli-yah, where’s your manager?”

She had come skipping over alone, hands clasped behind her back, planting herself in front of him with a face full of expectation.

“I sent eonni home first.”

“Eh? Then you? How are you getting back?”

“Me?” Her eyes curved into crescents, voice dipped in syrup. “I have Oppa!”

Stated as natural law.

“Me?” He had to laugh.

“Yes, you!” She closed a small half-step, chin tipped up, expression going earnest. “I know my acting still has holes. A film is honestly a stretch for me — but the tricks Oppa taught me today actually WORKED. So — so I want to ask for more! And of course!” A hand shot up in a solemn oath, followed immediately by a small, pained wrinkle of her nose. “I’ll pay tuition! Tonight’s late-night snacks — on me!”

“My exclusive acting curriculum is worth one round of snacks?”

“Aaah~” Both hands came together in front of her face, the classic begging pose, lashes fluttering at maximum wattage. “Oppa, you KNOW I’m still paying off what my mother owes the company~ pleeease? When I’m rich I’ll buy you the most expensive dinner in Seoul, promise! Yes? Yes~?”

That she could mention the past this lightly now — it warmed something in him. It meant she was genuinely walking out of the shadow. He’d planned to coach her through the shoot anyway, so he produced a long-suffering sigh of a man making an enormous concession.

“Fine, fine. Seeing as you’re this cute and this pitiful, I’ll sacrifice my precious rest hours. Let me think where—”

“Oppa’s place!” she proposed instantly, delighted with herself.

Thock.

His hand rose and delivered a measured knuckle to that flawless forehead.

“Ow—!” She clutched it, staring up at him in wounded incomprehension.

“Are you insane?” He kept his voice down, exasperated. “You want tomorrow’s headline to read ‘Day One of The Girl in the Sun: Leading Man Takes Leading Lady Home for the Night’? And our statement says what — we pulled an all-nighter studying the script? Deep exploration of craft?”

She pouted, muttering at a volume not quite meant to be heard. “It’s not like it’s impossible… I wouldn’t mind anyway…”

“What was that?”

“Nothing! Nothing!” Hands flapping, eyes doing a quick, bright orbit. “Then — Oppa’s company! LOEN has practice rooms and meeting rooms, right? Perfect — I haven’t seen Ji-eun in forever. We can drop in on her!”

That was, in fact, the sensible version of the plan. He thought a moment and nodded.

✦   ✦   ✦

The van glided toward the LOEN building. At night the company ran quieter, but a scatter of floors still burned. They went straight up to the practice-room floor IU favored — and sure enough, before they’d even reached the door, a voice was leaking through it, rising and falling with worked-up feeling.

He pushed the door open.

There she sat: oversized gray-blue hoodie with a cartoon print, the no-pants-visible school of loungewear, bare legs folded up on a stool, hair in a careless bun, snacks and a script arranged in front of her — mid-recitation of a scene from You’re the Best, Lee Soon-shin.

“Ji-eun-ah!” Sulli lit up and sang it across the room.

IU turned at the sound, froze one beat at the pair in the doorway, then bloomed into open delight. “Peaches!” Script down, quick trot over — two best friends colliding into a hug, patting each other’s backs like long-lost war buddies.

“It’s been forever! How’ve you been?”

“Shooting the new drama — You’re the Best, Lee Soon-shin. My head’s about to burst from memorizing lines.” A laughing complaint, then the return volley. “You?”

“Also shooting! First day today — Ji-ho Oppa’s movie, The Girl in the Sun.” Sulli nodded importantly, then performed a full-body collapse of exhaustion. “Film is HARD. I can never stop knowing where the camera is—”

“RIGHT? Exactly that! I feel it in my bones!”

Instant resonance. Only after the catch-up ran its first lap did IU’s gaze finally travel to the man standing off to the side, watching them with a small smile.

Their eyes met. The air snagged — one brief, particular beat.

Since the wager, the two of them had lived on a strange, blurred border. Closer than friends — they’d kissed, after all, and there’d been that night of the perfume forensics when precious little of her had escaped inspection. But his incorrigible track record, plus one unfinished bet, drew an invisible line straight through the middle of it. Around him she was never entirely at ease — and, in the way of someone who’s already been seen through, oddly relaxed at the same time.

“And what brings YOU to my floor?”

The tone came out normal. The eyes were pure investigation.

Before he could answer, Sulli beat him to it, sparkling. “Ji-eun-ah, Oppa’s teaching me acting! I NG’d so many times today, and he gave me these little techniques and they actually work! So I made him keep tutoring me!”

“Him? Teaching YOU acting?”

The pitch climbed with unconcealed disbelief — and one other note, faint, not quite filed in time. Sour.

Her own acting got roasted often enough, fine — but Peaches was a former child actress with better fundamentals than hers. SHE needed lessons from a man who’d wandered in off the music charts? …Unless. Unless this silly peach was using tutoring as a pretext to get close to him.

The alarm system in IU’s head lit every panel at once, followed by a hot, unreasonable surge of discomfort. One Taeyeon was already more than her nerves could file. Now her own best friend was circling this — this certified philanderer?

Absolutely not. Supervision was required. No best friend of hers was walking into that fire pit unescorted.

In the space of a breath she’d found the perfectly noble name for it: protecting Peaches. A sacred duty.

“Right?! Oppa teaches SO well!” Sulli, oblivious to all of the above, was still glowing. “Ji-eun, want to listen in? You’re shooting too — it might help!”

“Oh? Is that so?” IU’s face assembled itself into we’ll-see-about-that, and she turned on him with a chin-first little challenge. “Ji-ho-ssi is so accomplished he teaches acting now? Careful you don’t lead the youth astray. Peaches acts circles around you.”

He shrugged, declining the bait entirely.

Her eyes did one quick calculation, and then she was all warmth, towing Sulli by the arm. “Come come come — there’s an empty acting room this way, great soundproofing, huge mirror, perfect for rehearsal. I’ll take you!”

Full-session supervision. Zero exploitable openings. That was the mission.

✦   ✦   ✦

The small practice room had everything they needed. Having discharged her duties as guide, IU installed herself in a chair against the wall, arms folded, radiating the authority of a construction-site foreman.

“Here to steal the class? If you want to learn, just say so. It’s not like I’d refuse you.”

She came up off the chair like a cat whose tail had been stepped on, chin high, one contemptuous scoff. “Chi! Who wants YOUR teaching? With your amateur two moves? I’m staying to make sure you don’t wreck our Peaches with bad instruction. And—” a sniff, “—to watch you embarrass yourself.”

Delivered with total righteousness, as if it were even half true.

He couldn’t be bothered to spar. He turned to Sulli and the playfulness dropped away. “Let’s start. First — the ‘reunion gaze’ you NG’d most today. The emotion climbs in stages—”

He taught, and he demonstrated.

How the eyes alone carry a progression — spark of excitement, the covering of it, longing pressed flat into calm, then the long, deep undertow of missing someone. How to govern the small muscles of the face so surprise never tips into mugging and tenderness never curdles into grease. How breath and micro-movement — a tremor in the fingers, a half-step that refuses to finish — externalize a storm the script never says out loud.

Sulli absorbed it with total focus — mimicking, asking, adjusting. He answered and corrected, hands-on, angling her shoulders, resetting her eyeline. One taught in fine grain; one learned with her whole attention.

And on the chair by the wall, the audit was not going according to plan. IU’s expression traveled, station by station, from front-row-for-the-comedy to surprise, to open disbelief, and finally to something she could not quite keep off her face — frustration, edged with envy.

Because he was GOOD. Genuinely good.

When he demonstrated, eyes, face, body all clicked into place instantly — any emotion on demand, each one worn like it had grown there. And the psychological scaffolding he attached to every technique sounded so RIGHT that she caught herself secretly trying the exercises along with them — and they worked better than anything her own expensive coaches had ever sold her.

No — how does one person get the face, the songwriting, the voice, the screenwriting, a first acting job this polished, AND the ability to teach it as a system?

She grumbled internally — and had to concede the corollary: maybe this was exactly why someone as proud as Taeyeon had folded. And why she herself had come within one bet of being collected.

What made sitting still truly impossible, though, was that Sulli’s learning process was clearly not confined to learning. Every few minutes a vivid demonstration would earn a peal of giggles and a two-armed swing off his arm. Every “Oppa, like this?” somehow required a distance of thirty centimeters and a direct upward deployment of the eyes. The dependence, the worship, the faint and deniable flirtation in that gaze — to a fellow woman it was all legible in ten-foot letters.

That silly peach. Fully bewitched.

Vinegar and worry churned together. She could NOT keep sitting. But having sworn on her dignity that she wanted none of his teaching, walking over now would be a face-plant of historic scale. Her eyes made one quick circle. A plan arrived.

“Wait!” She stood abruptly and marched over. “This part — the emotional pivot. Your method is wrong.”

Grave face. Purely academic objection.

“Oh? Wrong where?”

“It should be — more restrained. Carried in micro-expression and the tremble of the gaze, not by slowing the movement.” She pressed on before he could answer. “I’ll demonstrate.”

A deep breath, and she launched into Sulli’s reunion-gaze beat.

But — whether it was the nerves of having barged in, or wanting too badly to prove a point in front of this particular audience — she overcooked it. Badly. The performance came out green and stiff, eyes swimming, face abandoning management entirely, tilting toward outright comedy.

A strangled “pfft—” escaped Sulli, who clapped a hand over her mouth, shoulders quaking.

He extended no such courtesy. He laughed — a full, delighted, ringing laugh that bounced off the practice-room mirror.

IU’s face went scarlet on contact. But she gritted through the scene to the end, every mortifying second of it, then turned with manufactured composure, chin aloft. “See? THAT’S — that’s how it’s done.”

He got his breath back and applauded with vigor, mockery entirely uncamouflaged. “Excellent! Truly! That performance had — enormous individual character! Overflowing feeling! ‘Most Spirit-of-Breakthrough Rookie’ — the award is yours, uncontested. My respect. My deepest respect.”

“PFFHAHAHA—” Sulli lost the battle completely and folded in half.

IU turned a shade that threatened actual blood, glaring pure murder, molars audibly grinding. Hateful man. Not one shred of face spared.

Sulli laughed herself out, then looked at her best friend — rigid with mortification, dying to learn, constitutionally unable to ask — and her heart went soft. She slid over, wrapped both arms around his arm, and swung it gently, voice going full spun sugar. “Oppa~ stop teasing Ji-eun. Look, she’s shooting a drama too, she needs this. Be nice and teach us both~ mm? Please~?”

Upturned face, eyes enormous and liquid. A weapon with no known countermeasure.

He looked at IU — desperate to learn, strangled by pride, hopping mad — and found the whole tableau deeply entertaining. He stroked his chin, performing deliberation. “I COULD teach her. However—” the drawl stretched luxuriously, “—I’m very expensive.”

IU watched Sulli hang off his arm, watched the arm decline to escape, and felt her temper double. She yanked her friend back and rounded on him. “You’re charging MONEY?”

“Obviously. Knowledge has a price; nothing could be more natural. Besides, with that attitude, I might not teach you even if you paid. Sulli — shall we continue?”

He turned as if to resume.

“You—!”

Sulli hooked IU’s arm and stage-whispered the counsel of a very smooth little fox. “Ji-eun-ah, he’s the real thing, I promise. I got stuck all day; one round of his notes and I passed. People talk about both our acting, you know they do — and here’s someone who actually knows how to teach and is willing. When does that ever happen? I paid tuition too!”

“You paid? How much?”

“I’m covering his late-night snacks.”

Some interior spreadsheet relaxed. Snacks. That wasn’t expensive. Her face eased a millimeter and she turned back, tone still fully starched. “Then I — I’ll buy you two coffee! What do you want? Peaches, latte, right?”

She polled Sulli first, on principle, then made herself look at him.

He shook his head, smiling at some private altitude. “As I said. At the moment, money may not secure my services.”

“You—!”

Her breath jammed. Deliberate. Teaching Peaches for snack money and making HER grovel? This absolute — when he’d been holding her and kissing her he hadn’t mentioned any RATES — the moment the pants are back on he doesn’t know you — pah! She violently disowned her own metaphor.

“Oppa~” Sulli redeployed the aegyo, gazing up at him with maximum pitiable wattage.

He allowed himself to be visibly softened, sighed, and turned to IU. “Fine. On Sulli’s face, not yours. Apologize sincerely, ask me nicely, and I’ll consider it.”

“In your DREAMS!”

Reflexive, instant. Bow her head to this menace? No door. No window. Not a crack.

“Ji-eun-ah~” Sulli swayed her arm gently, dropping to the register of a co-conspirator. “We’re girls~ bending a little, saying one tiny sorry — it costs nothing. Think about it: You’re the Best, Lee Soon-shin MATTERS. Nail this role, get the acting recognized, and the whole road widens — your fans get to be proud. And think of the antis who dragged your acting — imagine face-slapping them with the WORK. How good would that feel?”

There is no fortress that falls faster than one with a sweet-voiced friend inside the walls. The firm position began to slide. Peaches was… not wrong? Face was temporary. Craft was permanent. For the sake of the acting, one small, strategic head-bow… survivable? Nobody was even watching—

The internal war lasted about four seconds. Hunger to improve — and a pointed refusal to be outpaced by Sulli — carried the field.

She inhaled, fixed her eyes on a neutral patch of wall, and released, at speed, at the volume of a mosquito: “S—sorry. Please teach me.”

“What? Can’t hear you.” He excavated an ear.

She shut her eyes, burned the boats, and raised the volume. “I’M SORRY! Please teach me!”

Two red clouds promptly annexed her cheeks.

“Better.” He nodded, wearing the smug benevolence of a satisfied schoolmaster — then pivoted without warning. “Tuition’s due, by the way. Three hundred thousand won per lesson.”

“THREE HUNDRED THOUSAND?!” She nearly left the floor. “Why not just rob me! And WHY does Peaches pay one round of snacks while I pay three hundred thousand?!”

Finger extended at Sulli. Face at full mutiny.

His gaze toured the two of them without urgency. “Sulli is adorable and obedient; I’d teach her free. Now examine yourself.” An unhurried up-and-down. “Three retorts per sentence. Attitude: abysmal. Three hundred thousand is the friends-and-family rate. Too expensive? Don’t learn.”

Her chest heaved. The finger redeployed at him. “You — have you forgotten what we — what we—”

What we did, you want a receipt?! — but the sentence died on the launchpad. Too mortifying to fly.

“What we…?” he inquired, at leisure, eyes glinting with mischief.

“NOTHING!” One savage glare, and inside her skull an entire hate-choir performed. Philanderer! Menace!! “My private coach charges two hundred thousand a session!”

“Two hundred is the market rate. This is the Ji-ho Bespoke Breakthrough Intensive — emotion management, camera-fear elimination, micro-expression masterclass included. Three hundred. Take it or leave it.”

Not one millimeter of ground given. The money itself meant nothing to him — what he wanted was the habit. Get a person used to yielding to you, once, twice, until it becomes reflex — and the scales of any relationship tilt quietly, permanently, toward your side of the table.

Her small fists clenched. Refuse, and the apology was a sunk cost — the groveling wasted, catastrophic loss. Accept, and she was officially a hostage of this insufferable — the INJUSTICE of it.

“Three hundred thousand it IS!” she bit out — then jabbed a finger in hard appendix. “BUT! If the teaching disappoints me, or I decide there’s no effect, I reserve the right to dock your pay!”

The yielding was all he’d wanted. Objective achieved, his face flipped instantly to sunshine, the entire skirmish apparently erased from history. “Deal! Then shall we begin? Time is money, student Ji-eun.”

Private Tutoring · Session 2 · Two Students

Same room, different prescriptions

SulliCamera-awareness detox, continued — plus the staged build of the reunion gaze, now decomposed into four workable rungs.

Ji-eunComic timing for Lee Soon-shin — letting the funny arrive from earnestness, not from pushing; kill the overcooked take before it’s born.

BothBreath before line. Fingers and feet tell the truth the face is hiding — put the storm there, in the small joints, and let the lens find it.

(One student decorates every correction with a giggle and a captured arm; the other pretends her furious note-taking is casual.)

Tutor’s margin: teach them both, charge only one, and never explain the pricing model. The pricing model is the point.

From there he ran the two of them in parallel — different roles, different weak points, different drills. Sulli learned loudly, punctuating his notes with little gasps of revelation. IU began at maximum starch and then, despite herself, was pulled under by how clean and usable the instruction was; somewhere in the second hour the grudge fell off her lap and she forgot to pick it back up.

Only — the room never quite lost its faint, tart bouquet of vinegar.

IU watched Sulli manufacture question after question that somehow each required physical contact — the arm-hug, the shoulder-bump, the full fangirl lean — and felt her mood fray in a way she declined to examine. So, with great naturalness, under flags of convenience like “demonstrating the two-hander” and “adjusting the blocking,” she began inserting herself bodily into whatever gap existed between the two of them — or “accidentally” brushing him just as Sulli mounted her next offensive.

He, meanwhile, stood in the crossfire of two entirely different but equally lethal styles, nose full of two competing perfumes — Sulli all sweet orchard fruit, IU a cooler citrus over a thread of milk — forearms registering periodic soft collisions from alternating directions. His pedagogy remained impeccably professional. His pulse, less so.

When the midnight class finally let out and he walked to the elevator, he carried it with him, woven into his shirt — one mixed, unmistakable fragrance belonging to two beautiful women at once.
