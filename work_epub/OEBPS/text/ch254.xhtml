Chapter Two Hundred Fifty-Four

I Said I Love You, Song Ji-ho

Outside the T-ara dormitory · evening a pink dress beneath a streetlamp, a white handbag, and the truth hidden inside an investigator’s file

Ji-ho saw the pink silhouette before he reached the dormitory.

Jiyeon stood beneath a streetlamp, searching every approaching car. When his vehicle slowed, she smoothed her skirt, checked her hair with one hand, and only then hurried over.

She opened the passenger door, slipped inside, and removed her mask.

Ji-ho paused.

The girl who usually favored simple rehearsal clothes had transformed herself for the evening. Her soft pink dress, carefully arranged hair, and restrained makeup made her look less like T-ara’s powerful center and more like the young actress from an editorial spread.

“Why are you staring?”

Jiyeon lowered her head and pretended to concentrate on the seat belt.

Ji-ho started the car.

“Because you look beautiful.”

“Was I not beautiful before?” she muttered.

The corners of her mouth had already betrayed her.

The car entered Seoul’s evening traffic. Jiyeon stole one glance at his profile and immediately looked away. Her perfume mixed with his clean, cool scent, making the enclosed space feel much smaller than it was.

At a red light, Ji-ho reached into the back seat and handed her a cream-colored shopping bag.

“This is for you.”

“What is it?”

She opened the bag and found a white patterned box bearing the Hermès name. Her eyes widened.

“Hermès?”

“Open it.”

Inside lay a milk-white Birkin, its leather soft and precise, the hardware muted beneath the car’s interior light.

Jiyeon inhaled sharply.

“Why would you suddenly buy me something this expensive?”

“I passed the boutique, saw it, and thought it suited you.”

He said it as though buying a Birkin on impulse were a minor scheduling decision.

Jiyeon traced one careful finger over the leather. She loved beautiful bags as much as any young celebrity with eyes, but she rarely allowed herself purchases at this level.

“Thank you.” Her voice became very small. “I love it.”

Ji-ho rested his hand briefly on her hair. She accepted the touch without protest, cheeks warming.

“Hungry?”

“Mm.”

“Chinese food.”

Her eyes came alive.

“Really? I love Chinese food. Tangsuyuk—and…”

She caught herself, coughed, and attempted dignity.

“Anything is fine. I don’t eat much.”

Ji-ho laughed.

✦ ✦ ✦
Private Chinese restaurant · Seoul red lanterns, carved screens, and a very small appetite that does not survive the first course

The restaurant’s exterior was restrained, with red lanterns beneath the eaves and dark timber framing the entrance. Inside, carved screens divided private rooms furnished in polished wood.

A server in a qipao guided them to a secluded table and presented a menu written primarily in Chinese.

Jiyeon stared at the page.

“Is there a Korean menu?”

“I’ll order.”

Ji-ho took the menu.

“What do you want most?”

“Tangsuyuk.”

“And?”

“I told you, I have a tiny appetite. Choose whatever you like.”

Suppressing a smile, Ji-ho ordered several dishes in fluent Mandarin. The server withdrew.

“You didn’t need to order that much,” Jiyeon said. “Honestly, I barely eat.”

“There’s a saying: a good appetite is a blessing. You don’t need to perform elegance for me.”

Her eyes curved.

“In that case, I won’t hold back. I really do love Chinese food.”

Ji-ho poured her water. She touched the cold glass, then quietly pushed it aside.

“One day I’ll take you to China and show you the real thing.”

“Promise?” Jiyeon leaned forward. “I want to see Jiuzhaigou, Zhangjiajie, and that enormous wall…”

“The Great Wall?”

“Yes! I heard it’s incredible.”

The food arrived quickly. Crisp sweet-and-sour pork took the center of the table, surrounded by dishes Jiyeon could not name.

“Wah…”

Ji-ho placed the first piece in her bowl.

“Eat.”

Her declared tiny appetite disappeared on contact. The coating cracked beneath her teeth; the meat inside remained tender, the sauce bright without being cloying.

“It’s so good.”

She ate with her cheeks full, caught him smiling, and frowned suspiciously.

“What?”

“Nothing. Have another.”

Under the warm light, Ji-ho looked different from the man who commanded stages and boardrooms. Here, with his sleeves relaxed and amusement softening his face, he seemed real enough to touch.

“Why did you stop eating?”

Jiyeon lowered her gaze.

“It feels unreal, that’s all.”

He touched her hair again.

“Don’t keep doing that. I’ll stop growing.”

“How tall are you now?”

“One sixty-eight.”

She said it proudly.

“Then you have nothing to worry about.”

Jiyeon narrowed her eyes, uncertain whether he was teasing. His innocent expression gave her no help.

✦ ✦ ✦

After dinner, Ji-ho did not turn toward the dormitory.

Jiyeon watched unfamiliar roads climb into the hills. Her palms began to dampen against the new handbag.

His house?

She was not unwilling. She was simply not physically comfortable enough for anything beyond closeness tonight, and embarrassment tied every possible explanation into knots.

The car entered a private villa compound.

When Ji-ho took her hand, he felt the moisture in her palm.

“Are you feeling unwell?”

“Ani. I’m just nervous.”

“There’s nothing you have to do.”

The answer loosened her breathing.

Inside, the villa astonished her into silence: a high living room, a wall of glass overlooking the garden, a small pool catching the night light, a piano, several guitars, and shelves of handwritten scores.

“This place is enormous.”

“Sit wherever you like. What do you want to drink?”

“Water.”

He brought it warm.

Jiyeon wrapped both hands around the glass and looked at him in surprise.

“Those are old songs,” he said when he noticed her studying the manuscripts. “Early drafts.”

She imagined him alone at the piano, writing the music that had changed his life.

“You can wash up upstairs if you want. There’s a new robe and unopened toiletries. The guest room is yours if you’d rather sleep alone.”

The explicit choice mattered more than he knew.

Jiyeon nodded and went upstairs.

Warm water eased the day from her shoulders. She changed into the robe, attended discreetly to her period, dried her hair as thoroughly as she could, and studied her own nervous face in the mirror.

Nothing had to happen. She was safe.

When she returned downstairs, Ji-ho had washed as well. His dark hair was still damp, and a charcoal robe softened the angles of his frame without hiding them.

“Finished?”

“Mm.”

He touched one damp strand near her shoulder.

“Your hair isn’t completely dry.”

“I tried.”

Ji-ho searched her face before moving closer.

“Jiyeon-ah.”

“Mm?”

He kissed her.

The kiss was warm and patient, leaving her room to retreat. She chose not to.

Her hands came to rest on his shoulders. Surprise became trust; tension slowly gave way beneath the clean scent of soap and the steady care with which he held her.

When he drew back, Jiyeon’s face was bright with embarrassment.

“Don’t be afraid.”

“I’m not.”

He led her to the sofa and settled her against his chest. Outside the windows, Seoul glittered below the hill.

His fingers moved gently through her hair until her heartbeat began to match his.

“Still nervous?”

“A little.”

Ji-ho held her more securely and asked for nothing else.

Jiyeon looked up at him when she thought he was watching the city. He turned at exactly the wrong moment and caught her.

“What are you looking at?”

“Nothing.”

She hid her face against his chest. He laughed and lightly pinched one reddened earlobe.

For several minutes, there was only warmth.

Then Ji-ho’s expression changed.

“Jiyeon-ah. Has anything unusual happened at the company recently?”

She looked up.

“Unusual how?”

“I’m not sure yet.” He reached for the investigator’s envelope. “Read this.”

Jiyeon shifted away enough to open the file.

The Same File · What the Pages Held

SenderRyu Hyo-young

Sent toLee Ah-reum, T-ara

Date27 July 2012 — before Music Bank

Transcript · Excerpts “I’ll come to Music Bank and beat you to dust. Just wait.” “I’ll scratch up that face of yours so you can’t get on a broadcast again.” “A dog only wakes up when it’s beaten.” “You bitch. Try getting caught by me.”

The father of Ryu Hwa-young: a director of Beomseobangpa.

Source: former T-ara staff. Witnessed and recorded as sent. Name withheld; will not testify publicly.

One page was all it took.

Color left her face. The further she read, the colder her expression became.

“They threatened Ah-reum?”

She stood abruptly, breath coming too fast.

“Why didn’t she tell us?”

Anger broke through the shock.

“Hyomin eonni and I wanted to speak publicly. Soyeon eonni said to think of the group and wait for the company to solve it.”

Her hand pressed against her chest as if she could physically contain a year of buried emotion.

“We were telling the truth. We were—”

The sentence failed.

Tears arrived instead.

Ji-ho rose and pulled her into his arms.

“Cry. You don’t need to make sense of it tonight.”

Jiyeon clung to him and finally released everything the past year had forced her to swallow.

T-ara had fallen from the edge of the summit into nationwide contempt. Every performance, every camera angle, every ordinary expression had become evidence for strangers determined to hate them.

Ji-ho did not offer empty reassurance. He held her and moved one hand slowly across her back while grief did what explanation could not.

Eventually, the sobbing weakened into exhausted breaths.

“Tired?”

“A little.”

He lifted her carefully.

Jiyeon gave a startled sound and caught his neck.

“Upstairs. You need sleep.”

He placed her on the bed, wiped the remaining tears from her cheeks, and drew the blanket over her.

“Sleep. I’m here.”

“You’ll help us?”

“I will.”

Ji-ho lay beside her without asking for more and let her curl into his arms.

It was the first time Jiyeon had shared a bed with a man. Strangely, embarrassment had disappeared. What remained was safety—the sense that her fear finally had somewhere to rest.

Midnight · Spoken Into the Dark

“Song Ji-ho. Saranghae.”

“Mm. I know.”

“Saranghae.”

“I know. Sleep now.”

“I said I love you, Song Ji-ho.”

“And I love you too, Park Ji-yeon.”

She finally — content to the very tips of her fingers — closed her eyes.
