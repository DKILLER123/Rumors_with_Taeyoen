She took the four bars she had brought with her and played them, and played them again with the third note a semitone lower, and looked up at him over her shoulder to be told which one she had invented.

“The second,” said Song Ji-ho, from directly behind her, which was where he had been standing for four minutes. “You only wrote it once. The other one you wrote now, to see whether I would notice.”

“Did you notice because it was worse?”

“Because it was braver, and you don’t write brave by accident at nineteen-forty in a room full of cameras.” He put his hand past her shoulder, not touching, and wrote on the pad propped against the music rest: keep the leap, lose the run. “Everyone in this country can hear you arriving at that note. “Rolling,” said Oh Sang-hoon, quietly, from somewhere, and they did it again for the camera, and this time when she looked up over her shoulder he was already there.

In the doorway, out of the frame by a margin that had been measured with a tape, Kim Taeyeon watched the height of him behind the smaller woman, and the way the two heads came together over the same square of paper, and the pencil going.

It was, she told herself, a scene. It was a scene she had read in a binder on a Friday, in a van, at a speed of roughly four pages a minute, and in that binder a man leans over a woman’s shoulder and improves a bar of music and she looks up at him and the camera holds, because the song is about the fact that a person can be changed by being understood by somebody who listens properly, which is the entire point of the reel and had been the point since the first day in a corridor, and it was no secret that he was good at it or that he had written the thing, and the least secret thing in that building was that the woman on the couch was the best friend she had on earth.

So why does it feel like a tooth being worked loose with a cloth?

Jealousy was a word she refused to use, on the grounds that a word used correctly is a small door opened, and she had no intention of walking through it in a building with forty-one crew in it. She had a better argument, and she ran it the way she had run it that morning in a mirror and the night before in a car and twice in the last five minutes with increasing lack of conviction.

He is mine. That is not a feeling, that is a fact with paperwork. He is mine, and Mi-young is my best friend, and this is work, and there is nobody in the world who knows better than I do what that man is like in a room, because I have been in a room with him since he was seventeen years old and had one jacket.

He draws women to him the way a lamp draws moths, and he cannot help it, and he does not encourage it, and the one who goes home with him at the end of the night is the one who came into the corridor first. Her father’s chest had been a thing nobody in her family had discussed for a year. A cough that behaved, a test nobody had booked, and a daughter standing in the kitchen of the Kim family house in Jeonju between the rice cooker and the back door, being rude enough to be obeyed: get him looked at, this week, the proper way, with the pictures, and say that I made you do it. He had said it first, months before, in his own way, once, in a car — but he is not a son, and a warning from a man who is not a son goes into one ear of a Korean grandfather and out of the other, and a demand from the youngest of three does not. At the review in the summer the cardiologist had used the phrase well controlled in front of the patient, on purpose, in the sort of voice you use when you want a father and a daughter to hear the same thing at the same time.

Hwang Mi-young had done that. Not by design, not with a receipt, not in a way that could be repaid in any currency at all; she had taken one sentence she had been given in a corridor and had gone and shouted it, with affection, in a kitchen in Jeonju, and had then never once in the eleven months since let anybody make it into a debt.

Which means that what I am feeling in this doorway is not only ungrateful, it is invented.

She put both arguments together, sat on the floor of the room with her back against the wall because there was a cable there and she needed not to be standing, and worked on them for twenty minutes with an expression of grim application, in the way she had once worked on a harmony. The song was not a clever one, and the crew who had heard it at eleven hours’ volume had stopped using the word simple about it somewhere on Friday, because simple is what a person calls a thing that has done work they cannot find.

It went like this. A child in a hallway, running, all noise, a small animal with a future entirely unwritten; and the piano under it doing the thing a heart does when it is not thinking about itself. Then the same child at fourteen, in a room with a mirror, certain of exactly two things, that he is going to be somebody and that he is already too late. Then the years of being looked at, and the melody lifts and thins and gets loud, and there is a line in it about a stage that a person has to be carried onto. Then the middle of a life, where the tune comes down to a walk again and the words change tense. And at the end — and this is the part nobody had been able to argue about — the oldest version of the same man, with white in his eyebrows, going back for one thing only, and it is not a stage and it is not a room. It is a hallway. It is the noise.

There is nothing in it you can put on a poster. It is a life, put in order, in four verses, by somebody who is supposed to be the person in it.

Kim Taeyeon had come down to that basement with three expressions in her repertoire and a note from a man with a red pen that said second one, hold it four frames longer, and had every intention of giving him exactly that. Somewhere in the second verse her chin did something she did not author. In the third, her hands stopped picking at the cuff of a cardigan that was not hers. By the fourth her eyes were hot, and then they were full, and then a strand of it came down the side of her face and she turned her head away from the camera at the precise moment she was told to look into it, which is not a thing you can do on purpose, because a person performing the concealment of a feeling and a person concealing a feeling are two different faces and the camera is a machine that has never once been fooled.

What they had not said, either of them, was what easy was supposed to look like, and there was no version of it available in a kitchen with the sun on the table and a man refilling a cup before it was empty, and so the reckoning did not come.

Kim Taeyeon looked down at a piece of bread cut off-centre and ate it in small bites with her ears hot and her hair still damp at the ends and did not once manage to arrange her face into anything she would have chosen.

Hwang Mi-young sat with a fried egg in front of her and poked at it for a while with the tip of a fork, not thinking about the egg, and about the middle of it she looked up and said, to no particular purpose:

“You can cook, which is the worst thing about you.”

“I can cook one thing,” said Song Ji-ho, “and there is a man in Los Angeles who has been paid four thousand dollars to teach me one thing, and I have been using it on this country since I was seventeen.”

And he looked at the one of them on his left, and then at the one of them on his right, and then he went back to his own plate, and the corner of his mouth did what it does in a room when he has decided something and is not going to say it out loud in front of witnesses.

Slowly. There was no hurry at all.

He had, as it happened, the whole of the rest of his life, and forty-one minutes before a car needed to be outside the gate of a broadcast centre, and two women in his kitchen who had not yet said a word to each other about either of them, and a record that came out on the third of October that was going to be the reason that nobody in this country ever again asked him a question he had not already decided the answer to.

He had time. That is the only thing a man like that ever needs, and on a Monday in September, on a hill where every other window was dark, he had it.
