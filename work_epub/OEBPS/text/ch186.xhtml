Chapter One Hundred Eighty-Six

Hello, Sister-in-Law

May 18, 2013 His apartment → a private dining room in Haengdang-dong a belated birthday by candlelight — and one word that stops Taeyeon cold

Taeyeon stood in front of the familiar apartment door with her heart trying to exit through her ribs.

One deep breath. Fingertips — trembling slightly — reached for the keypad.

Beep. Beep. Beep. Beep.

The tones rang absurdly loud in the quiet corridor. And on the final digit, the door opened from the inside.

He stood in the doorway smiling — plain white cotton shirt, sleeves rolled to the elbow, forearms bare — and for one second the hallway lighting seemed to have been redesigned around him. Months apart had thinned his face a little, sharpened the lines, aged the eyes a degree — and left completely intact the boyishness that had undone her in the first place.

What had actually tormented her these months wasn’t the schedule. It was this — the missing of him, arriving in tides. Once upon a time, a phone had been enough to fill any solitary hour. Now the moment she was alone, the missing grew over everything like a vine with no pruning season.

“You’re home.”

Two words, gently said — and every dam she owned failed at once.

She launched herself into his arms. The door clicked shut behind her, and her mouth had already found his — soft, scorching, desperately impatient. He caught her, turned, set her back against the wall; her legs wrapped his waist on pure instinct, the whole of her winding around him like a vine finding its trellis. They kissed in the entryway like people trying to refund four months of separation with interest.

“Missed you…” she managed between breaths, voice fraying at the edges. “So, so much…”

He rested his forehead against hers, their breathing tangled. “Me too, nuna.”

“Ji-ho-ya. Carry me to the room~”

“Nuna — close your eyes first. Okay?”

She shot him a look of soft complaint — and obeyed.

“No peeking.”

He carried her; she felt the turns. When her feet touched floor again, his voice arrived, warm at her ear. “Open.”

She opened her eyes.

The living room was full of candlelight — tall white tapers in proper holders, squat scented candles scattered across every surface, the whole space washed amber. And the flowers. Red roses, pink, champagne — vased, heaped, strewn loose across the coffee table and the rug, some petals still holding beads of water. The air was roses and warm wax, braided into something that made the knees unreliable.

Her gaze reached the dining table: white cloth, careful place settings, a burst of white lilies at the center—

She froze. Then burst out laughing.

“Mwo-ya…” She pointed at the plates, torn precisely down the middle between being moved and being unable to breathe. “A candlelit lunch of… pork cutlet?”

On the plates: golden fried cutlet, sliced in tidy strips, fresh salad alongside, a small dish of gochujang. The plating was genuinely earnest — someone had carved tiny flowers out of radish — but it was, incontrovertibly, pork cutlet. Also, it was not yet noon, and the sun was out, and the room contained approximately forty lit candles.

He rubbed his nose, faintly sheepish. “Because you love pork cutlet. I made several attempts. This is the most successful one.”

He led her to the table and drew out her chair. “And it’s not an ordinary lunch.”

She sat, and looked up at him. Curtains drawn, candlelight moving on his face, his eyes gone very soft.

“Last month, your birthday — you were in Japan, I was on set, and all you got was a phone call.” His voice stayed low. “I decided then: the moment you came home, you’d get a proper one.”

He’d thrown Sulli a set birthday. Joo-hyun — same birthday, as it happened — had her gift arranged in advance. Only Taeyeon had gotten a rushed phone call.

The guilt had been sitting with him for a month. No favorites. The ceremony would be paid in full.

He took a deep-blue velvet box from his pocket and set it in her palm.

“Happy birthday, my nuna. It’s late. The feeling behind it isn’t.”

Her fingers shook slightly. She opened the box — and her breath caught.

A Louis Vuitton Blossom necklace lay on the black velvet: a fine, precise chain, and a pendant carved from black onyx in the shape of an open flower, a ring of small diamonds tracing the petal edges — glinting in the candlelight with a restrained, aristocratic fire.

Women are perhaps born without adequate defenses against jewelry. Jewelry from the beloved hand has no recorded survivors.

“Wah. Daebak. It’s so beautiful—”

The longer she looked, the wetter her eyes got. “Ji-ho… this is too expensive…”

“Not as expensive as you.” He rose and moved behind her chair. “Shall I put it on?”

She nodded hard, and the tears finally went over. She tipped her head forward, baring the pale line of her neck; he lifted the necklace out with great care, the cool chain sliding against her skin, the onyx blossom settling just below her collarbones — where it promptly made her twice as difficult to look away from.

“Is it pretty?” she asked, very quietly.

He wrapped his arms around her from behind, chin at her shoulder.

“Pretty enough that I can’t look anywhere else.”

A kiss landed at her ear.

✦   ✦   ✦

Lunch proceeded in a fog of sugar. She fed him strips of cutlet and watched him chew with her eyes bent into crescents; he cut a piece and returned fire.

“Well? Is it actually good?”

“Ji-ho made it. Of course it’s good.” A beat. “Though — honestly — there’s room for growth.”

“YAH.” Chopsticks down, wounded dignity fully deployed. “Nuna. Your cooking has not yet earned mocking rights over mine.”

“Fine, fine.” She patted the air, diplomatically. “Neither of us mentions cooking again. Ever.”

“Going forward — delivery.”

“An outstanding proposal.”

They high-fived on it, a bilateral treaty, and talked through the months apart — tour stories, set stories, all the small daily things phone calls never had room for — while the candles moved and the roses breathed and time went kind and slow.

After, they cleared the table together. She insisted on washing up; he wrapped himself around her from behind, chin on her hair, and supervised each plate.

“Taeyeon-ah.”

“Yah, you! I’m NUNA to you!”

“I love you.”

Her hands stopped in the suds. She turned in the circle of his arms, rubber gloves and all, a fleck of foam on the tip of her nose. He wiped it away with one finger, laughing. She rose on her toes and kissed his cheek.

“I love you too.”

They brushed their teeth side by side at the mirror — one blue brush, one pink, motions accidentally synchronized — until she caught sight of their matching foam-faced reflection, laughed, choked on toothpaste, and had to be rescued with a cup of water and back-pats.

Then the shower ran warm, and the mirror fogged over, and the small room filled with steam and closeness — and the rest of the afternoon belonged to the two of them.

✦   ✦   ✦

Later, wrapped in towels and drowsy warmth, he dried her hair, and she stood pliant and boneless under the dryer’s hum, occasionally reaching up to touch his face. He bundled her in a fresh towel like a gift being wrapped and carried her to the bed over a protest that contained no sincerity whatsoever, and she promptly burrowed into his chest and found her spot.

“Ji-ho-ya.”

“Mm?”

“Today’s birthday… I loved it.” Her voice came muffled from his shirt. “Loved the cutlet. Loved the roses. Loved the necklace. But mostly — you were here. That I loved most.”

His arms tightened. “Every year from now on. No — not just birthdays. Every ordinary day we get, I want to make it feel like this.”

She tipped her head up, eyes shining. “You mean that?”

“I mean it.” He kissed her forehead. “Promise.”

She sighed, content, and resettled. A while later, surfacing from the warmth:

“Oh — tomorrow, Fany’s bringing Sica to the recording.”

His fingers paused mid-stroke through her hair. “Sica? Why would she — she doesn’t exactly—”

“Fany invited her.” A beat. “Honestly… I don’t think Sica dislikes you so much anymore. After that whole thing, something seems to have — settled in her.”

“Good.” The smile was audible. “I’d rather not fight with one of your best friends every time we’re in a room.”

“YOU’RE the one whose mouth starts wars.”

“I was wrong. Henceforth: gentleness and courtesy to all of nuna’s friends.”

“That’s more like it…”

He took the opening. “Also — nuna. Tonight, Sulli wants to take us to dinner.”

“Sulli?” A pause. “Why suddenly dinner? And why is the invitation coming through YOU?”

He sat up a little, entirely casual. “Last time you and I were on the phone, she overheard. You two were roommates, I’m her oppa — she found out we’re together, and she wanted to host us. That’s all.”

She thought about it — and found she liked it. She loved this man past all sense; it was time, slowly, to start introducing him into her world. She nodded. “Okay. It’s been ages since I saw Sulli anyway. What time?”

“Seven.”

Which left five whole hours. To fill with… what, exactly?

Her gaze traveled to him — half-propped against the headboard, all lazy lines and unfair geometry — and her answer arranged itself. He caught the look, read it fluently, and leaned to her ear, delighted and shameless.

“Nuna. Say ‘yeobo.’”

“You— in your DREAMS!”

“You said it once before~ once more. Hm?”

She wavered — then clamped her teeth on the duvet cover and shook her head with the resolve of a martyr.

“Then just ‘Oppa.’ Compromise.”

“You… you’re insane…”

“‘Oppa.’ Quickly. Or we get dressed right now and go eat dinner early.”

“Wait— fine, I’ll say it!”

A pause. A very small voice.

“…Oppa~”

6:50 p.m. Haengdang-dong, Seongdong-gu — twenty-second floor. D.A: a private dining room helmed by a Michelin-starred chef. Thirty pyeong of hush and understatement, members-only reservations, a guest list shorter than most elevators.

In the private room, Sulli waited with her pulse misbehaving. She’d dressed with intent tonight: an off-shoulder white puff-sleeve top, playful with a measured edge of daring, over pale-blue high-waisted denim shorts and a very great deal of long, bare leg.

Taeyeon eonni’s legs are short. Mine are a strategic asset. Deploy accordingly — quietly.

“This way, please.”

The server led two masked figures down the corridor and knocked softly.

They’re here.

She inhaled once, rose, and installed the sweetest smile she owned.

“Come in.”

The door opened; the server bowed out and drew it closed. The two of them stepped in, pulling off masks and caps.

“Sister-in-law! Oppa! Please, sit!”

Sister-in-law?

Taeyeon stood perfectly still.
