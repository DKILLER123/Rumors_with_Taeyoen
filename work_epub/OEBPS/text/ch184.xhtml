Chapter One Hundred Eighty-Four

Sister-in-Law Taeyeon

May 18, 2013 f(x) dorm → Seoul Hotel grand ballroom the wrap party — couple watches, flawless PR, and a request for tomorrow

On the afternoon of May 18, the last harmony pass of Apink’s “NoNoNo” was signed off in the booth. He’d barely cleared the goodbyes when his phone buzzed: Sulli.

“Oppa~ what time does the wrap party start again?”

“Sign-in at six. Doors at six-thirty.”

“It’s five now…” The voice tilted into a practiced pout. “Manager eonni got pulled away — she’ll be late picking me up. Oppa. Come get me?”

A few seconds of silence on the line, then the soft rustle of fabric — the unmistakable acoustics of someone rolling over on a bed for dramatic effect.

“I’m already IN my dress. Waiting all alone. It’s very lonely.”

He sighed — and his mouth curved anyway. “Understood. Send the address. Twenty minutes.”

✦   ✦   ✦

At the f(x) dorm entrance, the door was already open, and she was waving from it.

Champagne satin, cut with severe simplicity, following every line it touched; thin straps over pale shoulders; collarbones that belonged in an exhibit. No coat — only a whisper-weight cashmere shawl, hair loosely gathered, a few strands falling along her neck against skin the dress made even brighter.

“Oppa.”

She laced their fingers together as if that were the door’s standard greeting and towed him inward.

“You wore a SUIT today. So handsome.”

Head tilted up at him, eyes glittering.

“It’s a wrap party. One dresses.”

Her room was aggressively girlish — pale pink walls, a bed under strata of plush toys, cosmetics ranked with parade discipline, air layered with fruit and flowers. On the duvet, one soft white cat lifted an eyelid at them, assessed, and declined further involvement.

“Lucky-yah, look who’s here.” She scooped the cat up and delivered it into his arms. “Play with him a minute. I’m fixing my brows.”

He sat on the bed’s edge; Lucky, a diplomat by nature, was purring inside a minute. At the vanity, she leaned in and drew two practiced strokes, adjusted, approved. Then she rose and turned a slow circle in front of him, skirt catching the lamplight.

“Oppa. How do I look?”

For a moment he genuinely had no sentence.

The girl stood in the gold of early evening, radiating that unfair, unbottleable aliveness — a smile brighter than the season. For a moment the little room seemed lit by her instead of the sun.

“I passed a big flower shop on the way,” he said finally, quietly. “Roses, lilies, tulips — all in full bloom. Not one of them comes close to you right now.”

The blush arrived at observable speed. She pressed her lips over the smile, crossed the room in small quick steps, and settled beside him, head finding his shoulder like a homing pigeon.

“Did Oppa drive himself here?”

“Mm.”

“Why do you ALWAYS drive yourself?” Face tipped up, eyes transparent. “Almost no artist self-drives to schedules.”

A pause. His hand moved down the cat’s spine.

“Because… I’m a man with many secrets.”

“Secrets?” Her eyes made one quick orbit — then she darted in and pressed a fast kiss to the corner of his mouth. “Am I one of Oppa’s secrets?”

Soft lips; peach-flavored gloss.

His arm settled around her waist and he looked down at the flushed, flower-bright face. “Weren’t you the one insisting on ‘little sister’?”

“Can’t a little sister kiss her oppa?” Both arms looped his neck; the voice went small and silken. “‘Little sister’ is what we tell sister-in-law Taeyeon. I’d never make trouble for you. I’m this well-behaved — doesn’t Oppa like me?”

The eyes were innocence and mischief in one solution — a kitten fully aware that being adorable was a license.

Like her? Yes. Lying about it: unavailable.

He coughed once, and reached for higher ground. “Ahem. So — is Ha-ra nuna still angry?”

“Obviously. She said she’s calling sister-in-law Taeyeon to file a report.”

His body committed the tactical error of flinching, visibly.

“Oppa’s SCARED?” She was openly delighted.

“Not scared, exactly.” He sighed; the brow creased. “This day always comes eventually. It’s just — Taeyeon nuna feels everything at double resolution, and her heart knots easily. Without groundwork, she’ll spiral somewhere dark and live there. That’s what worries me.”

“Okay, okay — stop worrying.” She reached up and smoothed the crease from his brow with two gentle fingers. “I told Ha-ra eonni everything about the sea. She’s talked down. Still angry — but she promised she won’t get between us. Feel better?”

“Really?” Light came back on in his face.

“Mwo-ya, Oppa.” Wicked and sweet at once. “Didn’t you just say you weren’t scared?”

He was cornered, and there was exactly one dignified exit. He took it: he tipped her chin up and kissed her mid-smirk.

Her lashes fluttered down; she answered in kind. His hand traveled the satin at her waist — cool fabric, warm girl.

“Oppa…” A breath between kisses. “The dress will wrinkle…”

“Then we change it.”

“No time…”

The protesting hands had, notably, no force in them whatsoever.

From the vanity, at some point, Lucky observed the entangled pair of owners, yawned at length, and went back to sleep.

✦   ✦   ✦

Outside the Seoul Hotel ballroom, the film’s poster hung enormous under a “PRODUCTION WRAPPED — GOOD FORTUNE” banner. The sign-in desk had gone quiet; staff were tidying the stacks of souvenirs — crew tees, custom tumblers, photo books stamped with the title.

Inside, the noise was fully airborne. At the head table Kwak Jae-yong held court with Kim Hae-sook and Sung Dong-il, veterans trading war stories; one table over, whatever Lee Kwang-soo had just said had Chae Soo-bin laughing behind her hand. Blooper-reel cuts looped on the screens — flashes of the two leads mid-scene drawing periodic waves of laughter.

Producer Shin Min-hyuk checked his watch, frowned, and drifted to the manager standing near the door. “Weren’t you and Ji-ho coming together?”

“He sent me ahead — said he was picking someone up.” Ryu Dong-hwan checked his own watch, less serene than he looked. “I’ll call and hurry him—”

The sentence died in a small commotion at the doors. Reporters who’d been idling by the wall suddenly shouldered cameras and moved.

“It’s Ji-ho and Sulli!”

“This way! Both of you — this way!”

The flashes fused into one white wall.

He stepped from the car in a deep-gray suit cut close and clean — then rounded the hood and opened her door. She rose out of it like the shot every photographer had come hoping for: champagne satin running with light, one hand settling into the crook of his arm, smile flawless. The valet bowed away the keys.

“Ji-ho-ssi! First film, straight into a leading role — how does it feel?”

“Sulli-ssi! Any unforgettable moments from the shoot?”

“Question — why did you two arrive TOGETHER? Are you dating?”

At the last one she stopped, turned to the cameras, and delivered a smile of textbook, luminous ease.

“Aniya~ Oppa and I are very close chingu. We’re the same age, but I’ve always called him Ji-ho Oppa — he’s exactly like a real older brother to me. On set, off set, he’s helped me with everything. I’m endlessly grateful.”

And then, for the lenses, she ran the full best-friends repertoire: tugging his arm, throwing a peace sign against his shoulder, pinching his cheek with theatrical menace. He supplied the long-suffering expression on cue. The photos came out warm, funny, and utterly, expensively innocent.

The moment they cleared camera range she pinched the arm for real.

“Villain. Your fault I had to redo makeup AND steam the dress.”

“Remind me who wouldn’t let go from behind.”

“YAH!”

They reached sign-in mid-squabble; the staff visibly decompressed. “Ji-ho-ssi, Sulli-ssi — you’re the last two. Sign here, please.”

The book was a jungle of flourishes. He signed; she leaned in against his shoulder and signed beside him — two names pressed close on the page.

“Souvenirs.”

Two paper bags. She excavated hers immediately. “Tee, tumbler… ah — the photo book!” First page: the poster shot, the two of them laughing at each other on the shoreline, sea light everywhere behind them.

“It came out so well,” she said, suddenly quiet.

✦   ✦   ✦

Speeches had begun; Kwak nodded them in from the podium as staff steered them to the head table through a corridor of greetings.

“Late arrivals owe penalty drinks,” Kwang-soo stage-whispered.

“Sulli looks lovely tonight,” Kim Hae-sook said, warm as ever.

They took their seats as the director wrapped, and then it was the leads’ turn on stage. One shared glance; they climbed into the light — familiar faces upturned all across the hall.

“First — sincerely — I apologize for arriving late.” A small bow; friendly laughter rolled back. “This was my first film. I had far to go, and everyone here carried me — the director’s patience, the seniors’ generosity, every department’s labor. The Girl in the Sun isn’t just a film to me. It’s a treasured season of my life. Thank you.”

Her turn: hands wrapped around the mic, eyes shining.

“In three months of shooting I learned more than I can count. Beyond the director and our seniors — I have to thank Ji-ho Oppa specially.” She turned to him, smile at full sweetness. “A rookie actress gets lost a LOT. Oppa always ran lines with me, broke the character down with me, waited for me to find it. Truly — thank you.”

Down in the hall, the assistant director leaned toward the DP. “Those two are performing right now, you realize. Completely different animals on and off stage.”

The DP grinned. “Youth.”

Glasses rose; the clink ran around the room like a bell choir, and the party found its cruising altitude.

✦   ✦   ✦

The toasting circuit began. As the male lead he moved table to table, glass in hand, all courtesy.

“Good work in there, Ji-ho. Let’s do it again.” Sung Dong-il thumped his shoulder. “And when a good script crosses your desk — you remember my number, eh? Hahaha.”

“With your acting, sunbae-nim, there’ll be no shortage of reasons to call it.”

Sulli, meanwhile, had been annexed by the actresses.

“Sulli-yah, the dress is gorgeous — whose is it?”

“A small independent designer’s.” She answered smiling — while her eyes, unsupervised, tracked one particular figure through the crowd. He stood in profile with the director, the light doing what light did to that jawline. As if the gaze had weight, he turned — and smiled at her across the whole room.

Her heart missed once. She ducked into her juice.

“What’s wrong? You’ve gone red.”

“N-nothing. It’s warm in here.”

✦   ✦   ✦

Mid-party came the games. For Pictionary, the two leads landed on one team — the prompts all pulled from the film.

First prompt, whispered to her by the host: the place the leads fell for each other.

She thought two seconds, drew a wavy sea, added one heart.

“The first kiss at the shore!”

“Correct!”

Applause. The next prompts fell in the same rhythm — near-instant answers, zero misfires. On the final one — the film’s theme — she drew a sun, and under it two small stick figures holding hands.

“The Girl in the Sun.”

First place, uncontested. The prize: a pair of couple watches from the sponsor.

“Such teamwork,” the host needled, presenting the boxes. “You’re CERTAIN you two aren’t a couple?”

She raised her wrist, new watch catching the light, smile unbreachable. “We ran lines together every day for three months. I just know how his brain works.” A turn. “Right, Oppa?”

“Mm.” He accepted the second watch. “Thank you.”

Back at the table she leaned close, voice under the music. “Oppa. The watches…”

“Keep them,” he said, level. “They’re good-looking.”

She watched him fasten his onto his wrist. Then she bit down on a smile and fastened hers.

Chae Soo-bin approached with a glass and started, gently, “Thank you. If it weren’t for you—”

“Aren’t we friends?” He tapped his glass to hers. “Drink the toast and retire the speech.”

“…Ne.”

She smiled — soft, and something underneath it — and drank.

✦   ✦   ✦

Toward ten the party wound down; groups splintered off toward second rounds. The director caught the two of them on the way out.

“Ji-ho-ya. The theme song — where are we?”

“Girls’ Generation lands back home tomorrow. I’ll have it recorded and delivered as fast as humanly possible.”

“Good. The MOMENT it’s mixed, send it.” He turned. “Sulli — solid work this time. Keep climbing.”

“Thank you, Director.”

They walked out toward the lot. The night wind had a chill in it; her arms crossed on reflex — and in the next second a suit jacket settled over her shoulders.

“Oppa…”

“Wear it. Don’t catch cold.”

In the car she turned the new watch on her wrist, around and around, and then, out of nowhere:

“Oppa. Tomorrow — can I come with you to see sister-in-law Taeyeon?”

Several seconds of engine hum.

“Can I ask why? Sulli — the truth is, we—”

“Oppa. I really do just want to be the little sister.” She watched the streetlights strobe past the glass. “You can have many girlfriends. Girlfriends fight, and sulk, and leave. A little sister doesn’t.” A pause, and then, quietly: “Oppa. You’ll only ever have ONE little sister. Right?”

He didn’t answer at once. The light ahead went red; the car eased to a stop; and only then, softly:

“Sulli. I understand.”

Green. The car slid back into the river of taillights.

“My only little sister,” he said. “And someone I can’t let go of.”

“Come with me tomorrow. We’ll see Taeyeon together.”

She turned to look at him — the dashboard glow soft on the lines of his face — and decided, once and finally, that her plan was correct. Don’t think about tomorrow. Don’t count his girlfriends. Don’t weigh jealousy, or morality, or right and wrong. Tonight she was the treasured little sister — and the sweetheart underneath the word.

✦   ✦   ✦

The car stopped below the f(x) dorm. She unclipped the belt — and didn’t reach for the door. Instead she leaned across and kissed him, once, lightly.

“Good night, Oppa.”

“Good night.”

He waited until the building had taken her fully out of sight before pulling away.

On the passenger seat, left behind: one peach-flavored lip balm. He picked it up, smiled at it a moment, and slid it into the inner pocket of his jacket.

Upstairs, at her window, she watched the black car shrink into the traffic. Fingers to her lips. Then a glance at her wrist.

Tonight, at least — I really was his girl in the sun.

Sister-in-law Taeyeon. Your little sister-in-law is coming. I truly cannot WAIT to meet you tomorrow.
