Chapter Two Hundred Twenty-One

Beautiful, Brilliant Women Are a Man’s Oldest Fuel

Late July 2013 LOEN Entertainment · Ji-ho’s studio — and a riverside apartment above the Han a national little sister stops pretending — and a trainee receives a set of keys

And what about her?

Ji-eun had clawed her way to a full-length drama lead with everything she had, and every single step of it had cost blood. She was happy for Sulli — truly — and the envy underneath the happiness gnawed at her heart like a snake anyway. Because she had been that close to him once. If she’d known — if she’d had any idea he carried this world-tilting talent for film and music — would she ever have turned him down? Would she have made that ridiculous wager?

Taking a man. Please. Who couldn’t do THAT? Was she, Ji-eun, going to lose a man to Taeyeon, the resident homebody? …Peaches. Fine. Peaches was a genuine threat.

Either way, the wager barely mattered anymore. Whether Ji-ho could buy LOEN inside three years was a far less practical question than what he could do for her career RIGHT NOW. She’d come today to shelve the bet and simply enter the race — and walked into that corridor scene instead.

Of course. A man proud down to the marrow — how would he ever bend? But… Vice President Yoo was not a man you crossed for free.

Her insides churned — furious at his recklessness, unable to stop worrying about him, both at once. And while she stood there deciding whether to approach, Ji-ho had already turned toward his studio, back straight, not one gram of panic in it.

Go. She had to go. A man in “trouble” — there would never be a better moment to show up with coal in a snowstorm and repair everything at once.

She stopped hesitating and went after him at a quick walk.

He’d barely pushed the studio door open before Ji-eun slid in behind him like a nimble little fish and shut it with a backward press of her hand.

“How did you—”

He was surprised she’d appeared at all — more surprised she had evidently seen everything.

She had no patience for pleasantries. The delicate face tipped up, brows knotted hard, and she went straight at it, urgent and scolding in the same breath.

“Why? Why would you refuse Vice President Yoo to his FACE? Do you have any idea what kind of man he is? There is nothing — NOTHING — you gain by making an enemy of him!”

He looked at her — cheeks flushed from urgency, eyes broadcasting an exasperated why-are-you-like-this — and found himself, unexpectedly, entertained. He crossed to the sofa, sat, and shrugged.

“No special reason. I just don’t like his face when he talks.”

“You—!”

The sheer childishness of the answer nearly knocked her over. She paced two agitated circuits on her heels, then stopped dead in front of him and seized his wrist.

“Up! You’re coming with me!”

“Where?”

“To Executive Director Kim Jun-myeon!” She was already hauling him toward the door. “He runs music production, digital distribution, physical distribution, AND business partnerships — his real power isn’t one won less than Yoo Jae-ha’s! And most importantly, the two of them have NEVER gotten along! I’m on decent terms with Director Kim. I’ll take you to him, he can step in and smooth this over — at the very least, Yoo Jae-ha can’t be allowed to trip you at every turn!”

But Ji-ho didn’t move. Instead his hand rotated and caught hers — warm, steady, closing around the cool slender wrist.

“Ji-eun-ah.”

He looked at her, eyes deep, voice level.

“It’s because the film blew up and the OSTs swept the charts, isn’t it? My market value went up. High enough to be worth setting down all that pride and principle from before. High enough to be worth — competing with other women for?”

Her whole body went still.

She bit her lower lip. And then, all at once, she was done pretending. The smash-the-jar recklessness surged up, dragging years of compressed grievance, ambition, unwillingness, and this afternoon’s fear along with it.

“That’s RIGHT!” Her face came up, voice trembling. “It’s exactly because you’re worth more now! Worth me, Ji-eun, throwing away my pathetic scrap of self-respect to fight other women for! Satisfied? Are you HAPPY now?”

Her eyes reddened fast, tears standing bright in them, stubbornly refusing to fall.

“Does it feel good, seeing me like this?”

“Look at her! The Ji-eun who turned you down — the so-called nation’s little sister—”

“Reduced to THIS for your songs, your scripts, and that face of yours — this petty! This calculating! This— MERCENARY!”

“And it’s your fault! You and your wandering heart and your bottomless greed — YOU made me into this!”

“Satisfied? HAPPY?”

The tears broke loose at last, rolling down her smooth cheeks. Everything came pouring out now, her voice cracking, climbing.

“Yes! This is who I am!”

“I can’t AFFORD to lose!”

“My life has to be a success — it HAS to!”

“Every choice I make gets weighed. Every single one has to point HIGHER!”

“There! You’ve seen it! Are you satisfied now?!”

She cried until her shoulders shook, the immaculate makeup smearing under the tears — and somehow that ruined, undefended face was more striking than the perfect one had ever been.

He let her empty it all out, watching — and the last small burr of distaste her calculation had left in him quietly dissolved. Because he recognized every gram of it.

He sighed softly, raised a hand, and wiped the tear tracks from her cheek with the pad of his thumb.

“Ji-eun-ah.” A small smile. “Three days from now, you’re going to be very glad you made today’s choice.”

She hiccuped, blinking up through wet lashes, lost.

“What… what does that mean?”

“People who work as hard as you do are never truly laughed at, Ji-eun.” His gaze was steady, serious. “I told you once — under the skin, you and I are the same kind.”

“A family drowning in debt. Living in other people’s houses. Choosing this industry from the bottom of a corner — and getting rejected at more than a dozen auditions without quitting.”

“Debuting into a wall of mockery about your weight, your face — and smiling into the cameras anyway, then turning around where nobody could see and grinding yourself down to nothing: the dieting, the skincare, the expression training. Ruthless with exactly one person. Yourself.”

“Working. Staying humble. Enduring. Climbing with your teeth clenched until you’d paid off your family’s debt and held up their whole sky.”

Sentence by sentence, he recited the road she had walked.

Ji-eun stood frozen. She had forgotten to cry.

“How could a person like that be ‘petty’?” He shook his head. “You’re just more awake than most people. You know exactly what you want — and you have the nerve to go get it.”

“As for you and me—” He paused, gaze going long. “Everyone figures it out eventually. A future matters more than love, because survival is instinct.”

“But love is RARER than a future — because a true heart is a luxury good.”

“And the right person doesn’t make you choose. The right person understands your future, and stands inside it, and walks it with you.”

“So no — I don’t think there’s anything wrong with your choice. Weighing the odds in front of reality, fighting for better resources for your career — that’s not a sin.”

He held her eyes, open and unguarded. “And I don’t think there’s anything wrong with mine, either. I have my own road, and my own lines I don’t cross.”

A future matters more than love… love is rarer than a future… and the right person stands inside your future— She repeated it soundlessly, and the tears slid again — but not the same tears. These were the shivering kind that come from being understood all the way down.

Yes. The career she’d bought with blood and dignity — the tower she’d built out of her own hide — how could that, how DARE that, be wrecked over a man? Over so-called love, that fades on a schedule nobody controls?! But— but what if the man was himself standing in the middle of her future’s only road? What if he WAS the shortcut — faster, steadier, higher — what if he was part of the dream itself? Then why on earth should she pass him by? Hand him to someone else? For a “first principle” reality had already sanded off? For MORALS? Ha. Hilarious.

The flood broke the last levee. Ji-eun stepped in, threw her arms around his neck, rose on her toes — and aimed for his mouth.

A warm palm landed gently against her forehead and held her exactly one kiss short.

She opened her eyes, uncomprehending, and met his laughing ones.

“Ji-eun-ah,” — pure mischief — “our wager isn’t finished yet.”

His palm slid up, brushing her bangs aside, and the mischief in his eyes dropped away into something deeper.

“You’re too clever. Too formidable. The little fools around me put together probably couldn’t handle you.”

“So before any of this truly begins—”

He bent his head, breath warm at her ear, voice low.

“I need you to surrender to me completely. And of your own free will.”

Her heart went off like a drumline. She stared at the face inches from hers, at the ambition running fathoms-deep behind his eyes, and understood at last: this was no longer the “talented good-looking guy” she’d met a year ago. He was the eye of the storm. He was the storm. And she had one foot in the vortex already — and no desire whatsoever to pull it back.

Ride the wind up, or be torn apart by it — she couldn’t know. But life IS the wager. The ones who won’t bet have already conceded the whole game in their hearts. Not me. I bet on myself. I win the career AND the man.

“Where are we?”

Irene turned a slow circle, taking in the apartment — finished to the last centimeter, quietly expensive, and beyond the floor-length windows, the Han River laid out end to end.

Arms folded around her from behind; a voice grazed her ear.

“Do you like it?”

Her knees went soft on contact, breath catching. “Mm— jagi-ya, don’t…”

“That’s not an answer to my question.”

“I… like it. I like it.”

“Then it’s yours. From today.”

“Mwo?”

She twisted free and turned to stare at him — and had the sudden, vertiginous feeling of not recognizing the man in front of her at all.

She’d assumed he’d bought the place for himself and wanted a girlfriend’s opinion on it. Instead — a gift. Why? What had she ever done to deserve— unless he meant something by it. What kind of problem gets solved with an APARTMENT? The word arrived unbidden and ugly: kept. No. No, he wouldn’t. But the canyon between their stations, and this apartment, and the fact that it wasn’t a proposal, it was a DEED— panic rose in her like cold water.

And into her spinning, wide-eyed confusion, he lowered his head and kissed her.

Electricity — instant, total, unfair. Her heart slammed; heat flooded her face. How— how could one kiss— Even missing him this badly, even after weeks apart, surrendering to ONE kiss? Or was it the apartment — the sheer satisfied weight of it — that made the surrender come this easy? Am I really… that vain a woman? Her body, at least, had stopped taking her calls entirely.

She hated admitting the rest of it, but it was real: the gap between them was no longer a feeling, it was a fact. The redder he burned, the higher he climbed — the smaller she stood in this relationship, month after month.

He drew back from her lips, studied the impossible face in front of him, and pressed down gently on her shoulders. And she sank — of her own accord and not — until she was kneeling on the warm floor, heart in complete disarray, understanding exactly what was being asked of her.

Not the apartment. Not the river view. Him. All of him, on his terms.

He bent, one hand tilting her chin up.

“Irene. That kiss just now — I felt it like a live wire. Did you?”

She nodded before she could decide to.

He smiled.

“Then it’s decided. This whole life of yours — you’re my woman.”

“Saranghae.”

And that one word took the last wall down.

An apartment worth billions of won. A man she loved. A man who ran current through her with a kiss. A man she could not refuse — had, if she was honest, surrendered to long ago.

He smoothed her hair back from her face, expression unhurried, entirely content. The rest of the evening belonged to the two of them and the river light.

Money and position really were remarkable things, he thought. And beautiful, brilliant women — they were a man’s oldest fuel. The original engine. The reason the whole machine ever turns over at all.
