Chapter Forty-Five

A Boy

Advanced Rap Mastery included composition — but a piece rushed out on his current runway wouldn’t detonate a room. For detonation, he went where he always went: the vault. Twelve years of residency work had stocked his memory with an ocean of songs, and he trawled it now, methodically. Rap. Rap. Rap…

Then the light went on.

“A Boy.” Of course. OF COURSE.

The song was — would be — one of G-Dragon’s signature works: written from his own life, the story of a kid who started down the music road at thirteen and carried it on stubbornness alone for a decade, GD on the pen with Choice37 on the composition. A testimony disguised as a track — and the shape of that testimony mapped onto Ji-ho’s own life almost line for line. All it needed was the details swapped for his own: the bars, the failed auditions, the ocean crossed alone.

As for GD — a talent that size wouldn’t miss a few songs. The man was a fountain; the fountain would keep flowing.

Decided. Work.

Professional arranging software cost money he didn’t have, so the first pass went down the ancient way — staff paper, the classical piano line sketched by hand, the original structure mapped, and then verse by verse, the lyrics rebuilt around his own scars. He brewed a lavender tea, sank in, and let the hours dissolve — memory supplying the blueprint, the system’s rap-writing knowledge fact-checking every bar.

An entirely disproportionate little thrill fizzed through her, and the announcement quietly canceled itself.

At 3:55, Taeyeon came down the third-floor corridor at speed — and spotted him from a distance, leaning against the wall beside Production Room 3, waiting.

Her hand went to her hair without consulting her. Her pace gentled itself into a stroll. Her voice arrived ahead of her, soft as the corridor light.

“Ji-ho.”

He turned toward the sound — and open admiration crossed his face before he could file it.

The fair oval face, lifted by immaculate makeup; rose-pink lip color with a wet-petal sheen. A cream knit cardigan slouched soft off her shoulders over a matching camisole; washed-blue micro-flare jeans cropped at the ankle, tracing the line of her legs down to a flash of slim ankle. Her short gold hair was parted and pinned into two loose, curling twists behind her ears, a small purple flower ornament and a blue curl-tie tucked into them — romance and sweetness braided directly into the hair.

She stopped in front of him, and the scent arrived a half-second later — tuberose and jasmine, a rich white-floral sweetness that made the flowers seem like the ones being flattered.

“Nuna. You look really beautiful today.”

She had heard that sentence, in all its versions, thousands of times across the years — long past the point of feeling it. But this once, for no reason she was prepared to examine, it landed somewhere ticklish and new, and a bloom of shyness got past her guard.

She pressed her lips together, turned to the door, and pushed it open.

“Get in here. I booked the whole afternoon.”

He followed her in, eyes sweeping the racks of professional equipment — DAW workstation, MIDI keyboard, monitor speakers — and settled at the console. FL Studio blinked awake. He laid his handwritten pages — arrangement sketch, finished lyrics — on the desk beside the keyboard.

He selected the tempo in the toolbar, pulled his instruments one by one from the browser, then dropped into the piano roll and began laying chords and melody with total absorption — pausing only to nudge faders on the mixer, chasing balance element by element.

Taeyeon watched him work, curiosity practically luminous — but too respectful of the trance to interrupt. Instead she lifted his draft pages, quietly, and began to read.

The intro: a restrained, gentle drum pattern under layered, hazy electronic color. The arrangement was clean to the point of audacity — no clutter, no showing off, every element pulling toward a hook that grabbed the ear on first contact and a rhythm you could hang laundry on.

This… what IS this? Ji-ho wrote THIS?

Her own résumé scrolled past for comparison: SM Academy vocal training from middle school; Jeonju Art High School, singing and dance in parallel; fifteen years old, alone at the 8th SM Youth Best Contest, walking out with the grand prize in singing; then years under The One, a decade-plus of systematic training end to end.

And with all of it — every year, every award, every hour — she could not have written this page.

The arrangement looked so simple. And the simplicity was the trick — some quiet magic in the spacing of it that hooked the ear and would not let go, the kind of less-is-more that took most producers half a career to trust.
