Chapter Two Hundred Fifty-Nine

Old Tricks Are Fine If They Work

August 20, 2013 · morning Girls’ Generation dormitory two closed hearts, eight worried members, and a familiar man waiting downstairs

The dormitory felt too quiet.

Members sat or stood around the living room, their attention drifting repeatedly toward Taeyeon’s bedroom door. It had remained closed since the previous evening. Now and then, a muffled sound from inside tightened every face in the room.

Tiffany was no easier to understand. She occupied one corner of the sofa with a cushion held against her chest, eyes swollen from crying, gaze fixed on nothing.

Jessica leaned toward Sunny.

“Yah. What happened to those two?”

Sunny lifted one shoulder.

“I don’t know. But Taeyeon went to LOEN yesterday to record, and came back like this. I’d bet Ji-ho is somewhere in the middle.”

Ji-ho.

Jessica’s attention sharpened, though her expression did not.

“I see.”

Sunny studied her in return.

“And what are you doing lately? Practice ends and you vanish. Every day, some secret schedule.”

“I’ll tell everyone near the end of the year.”

Jessica moved quickly to safer ground.

“We’re rehearsing together this morning, right?”

“Mm. The first solo concert in Indonesia needs proper preparation.”

Jessica nodded while reorganizing her private calendar. The executive course she had enrolled in would have to wait until the afternoon.

Studying had never come easily to her.

She was doing it anyway.

Taeyeon’s door finally opened.

She emerged with shadows beneath her eyes and a voice roughened by crying.

“Is everyone ready?”

No one asked the question written across the room.

“Let’s go.”

The members filed into the elevator and descended to the underground garage. Taeyeon walked in front with her arms folded, head down, intent on reaching the van and hiding in its darkest corner.

Tiffany followed with equal silence.

As they rounded a concrete pillar, a man stepped into view.

Taeyeon stopped so abruptly that Tiffany nearly walked into her.

Ji-ho stood beside a black sedan.

“Come with me.”

Surprise ignited into relief—and relief into anger.

“You waited until now?”

“I waited until you had slept.”

“I have rehearsal.”

“I cleared two days with SM.”

Taeyeon stared at him.

“Without asking me?”

“Yes. That part was wrong.”

He held out one hand.

“If you tell me to leave, I’ll leave. If you get in the car, I’m asking for two days to apologize properly.”

Behind Taeyeon, eight women watched in complete silence.

She looked at his hand, then at his face.

“I’m still furious.”

“I know.”

“I haven’t forgiven you.”

“I know that too.”

Taeyeon placed her hand in his.

“Two days. Then I decide whether your apology was worth hearing.”

Ji-ho opened the rear door. She entered with her chin high and refused to acknowledge how violently her heart was beating.

Tiffany met Ji-ho’s eyes before the door closed.

“There’s something for you at the villa,” he told her. “Take it when your schedule ends.”

The sedan pulled away.

The remaining members watched it disappear between the garage ramps.

“Was that reconciliation,” Yoon-a asked, “or a corporate extraction?”

“She got in voluntarily,” Yuri said.

“After he rearranged her schedule without asking,” Seohyun replied.

Sunny sighed.

“Whatever it was, we’re rehearsing without our main vocal.”

✦ ✦ ✦

In the car, Taeyeon sat rigidly beside Ji-ho.

“I’m angry.”

“You should be.”

“You don’t get to decide that for me.”

“Then decide it yourself. I’ll listen.”

That answer deprived her of the argument she had prepared.

Ji-ho’s phone rang. Lee Soo-man’s name appeared on the screen.

Taeyeon looked at it with sudden interest.

Ji-ho enabled speakerphone.

VOICE CALL · LEE SOO-MAN

“Chairman Song. What exactly do you mean by removing Taeyeon from rehearsal for two days?”

“Seonsaengnim, I revised the OST arrangement and need her for additional production work.”

“That explanation convinces no one.”

“Her next immovable schedule is in three days. She’ll be back before then.”

“You are not her agency.”

“Correct. That is why I asked for the adjustment rather than issuing one.”

A long silence follows. Both men understand the request is personal.

“Two days. The third morning, she returns. Do not affect the Jakarta preparation.”

“Understood. Thank you, seonsaengnim.”

The call ended.

Taeyeon looked out the window so Ji-ho would not see the smile trying to form.

He had come.

Late, infuriatingly, and with the subtlety of a headline—but he had come for her.

That did not mean she would forgive him easily.

Absolutely not.

✦ ✦ ✦
11:00 a.m. Mountain campsite outside Jeonju the place where their relationship first changed

Three hours later, Taeyeon stepped from the car and stared.

She knew this place.

Their first private trip. Their first real date. The night their relationship had crossed from possibility into fact.

The campsite had been rebuilt with far more competence than their original attempt: shade canopy, chairs, cooking equipment, an insulated tent, books she loved, and enough supplies for two days.

Ji-ho spoke to Dong-hwan through the driver’s window.

“Return in two days. Keep the lower access road private, but don’t block emergency services or authorized park staff.”

“Understood.”

The car departed.

“Two days?” Taeyeon demanded. “What about your company?”

Ji-ho turned to her.

“The company can function without me for forty-eight hours.”

“That isn’t what you were going to say.”

“No.” His gaze softened. “I was going to say the company matters less than this.”

Her heart missed one beat.

Taeyeon looked away.

“I don’t believe you.”

“You don’t have to yet.”

He offered his hand again.

“Stay anyway.”

Her mouth remained stubborn. Her hand entered his.

The large tent contained portable cooling, soft floor mats, pillows, and several books she had once mentioned in passing.

“There’s air-conditioning in a tent?”

“Now do you appreciate the planning?”

“A little.”

Her smile escaped before she could stop it.

“Rest. I’ll make ramyeon.”

Ji-ho went outside.

Taeyeon picked up one book, turned two pages without reading, and looked through the open flap at him working over the stove.

He let her cry all night, then rebuilt their first date.

A terrible man with excellent production values.

✦ ✦ ✦

After lunch, Ji-ho led her toward the river.

“Aren’t you worried someone will photograph us?”

“The campsite is reserved. Security is posted below, and the park’s authorized staff know we’re here.”

“Your other girlfriends might still be devastated.”

Ji-ho did not miss the bitterness.

“They know you are my girlfriend.”

They.

Taeyeon’s jaw tightened.

Some disloyal corner of her mind produced the words first and official as consolation. She rejected both immediately.

Kim Taeyeon, do not crown yourself queen of a disaster.

The summer heat was heavy enough that Ji-ho removed his shoes, rolled his trousers, and stepped into the shallow water.

Taeyeon stayed on the bank.

“I’m not ruining my clothes.”

A handful of water struck her shoulder.

She stared at him.

Another splash caught her across the front.

“Song Ji-ho!”

Taeyeon kicked off her shoes and charged into the river.

The fight was immediate and total. Water flashed through the sunlight. Her shriek became laughter; his laughter turned into surrender when she attacked with both hands. Birds lifted from the trees while the two of them forgot, for several minutes, to be wounded adults.

By the time Ji-ho caught her around the waist, both were soaked and breathless.

“Mianhae,” he said against her damp hair. “Not for the water.”

Taeyeon’s smile vanished.

“I know.”

“I should have told you before truth became an ambush.”

She pushed him backward into the water.

“That apology is not enough.”

Ji-ho surfaced, wiped his face, and nodded.

“Then I’ll keep working.”

They returned to camp near sunset. Ji-ho handed her dry clothing and left the tent so she could change privately. When he returned after changing elsewhere, Taeyeon was wrapped in a light blanket, hair loose around her shoulders.

✦ ✦ ✦

Night cooled the mountain. A small fire burned at the edge of camp, and the scent of grilled meat drifted between the trees.

Taeyeon sat beside the flames with the blanket around her. Ji-ho picked up a guitar, tested the tuning, and looked at her.

“This is for you.”

The introduction began.

Stay With Me · Campfire · For Kim Tae-yeon

“I guess it’s true — I’m no good at a one-night stand
But I still need love, ’cause I’m only a man…”

The verse ran on — nights that never went to plan, a man who didn’t want her to leave, a hand held out and asking—

“Oh, won’t you stay with me
’Cause you’re all I need
It is love — it’s clear to see
My darling, stay with me.”

The new album’s title track — with one line taken to the knife.

Taeyeon rested her chin on one hand and watched him.

The tactic was shamelessly familiar.

The same mountain. The same man. A new song built to reach the place ordinary apologies could not.

Last time, he had sung “Eyes, Nose, Lips.”

This time, “Stay with Me.”

Her eyes filled before the song ended.

“What is it called?”

Ji-ho set the guitar aside and sat beside her.

“‘Stay with Me.’ One of the lead songs for the English album.”

Taeyeon let him place an arm around her shoulders.

“It’s beautiful.”

“Taeyeon-ah, I won’t deny what I am. I have loved badly and selfishly.”

He faced her fully.

“You were my first love in this life. You are still the person whose absence changes the shape of every room.”

“I don’t expect one song to repair what I broke. But I will not let your silence become the end of us.”

She lifted her head into his eyes. The firelight jumped. In the amber of his pupils her own face floated — so clearly that it seemed, in all this world, he saw only her.

System · Passive Skill — In Effect

Silver Tongue

The Player’s tone softens automatically; the gaze turns warm and radiates sincerity. Even lies are instinctively believed by lovers. Rapidly soothes a lover’s negative emotions — wounded feelings, anger, jealousy, insecurity.

Knowing did not make the words meaningless.

“I’m still angry.”

“Stay angry. Just stay.”

He took her hand.

“We imagine life as long because the number of years sounds large. Then work takes most of the days. Sleep takes another third. Parents, friends, distance, illness—everything claims time.”

“I don’t want to waste what remains by making you guess whether I’ll come after you.”

Taeyeon looked down at their hands.

“You came very late.”

“I did.”

“And you rearranged my schedule without asking.”

“I won’t do it again.”

“You’ll tell me the truth when I ask?”

He did not answer quickly.

“Yes. Even when the answer makes me look exactly as bad as I am.”

That, more than the song, loosened something inside her.

Ji-ho spoke again, more quietly.

“One day, if you still want it, I would like a family with you.”

Taeyeon’s eyes lifted.

“Children?”

“Not as another promise made for tonight. A real conversation, when the time is right. Your career, your health, and your choice first.”

She pictured a daughter with his amber eyes and her voice. A son with his height and the stubbornness they both possessed.

The image was dangerous. It also made her smile.

Ji-ho touched her cheek and waited.

Taeyeon closed the distance herself.

The kiss was slow, salted faintly by tears, and chosen without confusion.

When it ended, she kept both arms around his neck.

“I haven’t forgiven everything.”

“I know.”

“And tomorrow, you answer every question.”

“Every one.”

They entered the tent together.

The fire settled outside while the night became private. Whatever tenderness followed remained beyond the page, grounded not in surrender but in Taeyeon’s decision to stay for the conversation still owed.

✦ ✦ ✦
The following morning Mountain campsite one day remains

Sunlight entered through the tent seam and touched Taeyeon’s face.

She turned and found Ji-ho asleep beside her, expression peaceful enough to look innocent.

The owner of that face was a catastrophic flirt.

Taeyeon traced his features in the air—brow, nose, mouth—and had her hand caught before she could withdraw it.

Ji-ho opened his eyes with a smile.

“Were you watching me?”

“No.”

“Of course not.”

She tried to reclaim her hand. He held it gently.

“Hungry?”

“A little.”

“I’ll make breakfast.”

Ji-ho dressed and stepped outside.

Taeyeon remained beneath the blanket, listening to cookware, morning wind, and the first low line of the song from the night before.

“Sing it again,” she called.

His laugh came from beyond the tent.

“All of it?”

“All of it.”

Ramyeon began to scent the morning. Ji-ho’s voice followed.

Taeyeon wrapped the blanket around her shoulders and sat at the entrance, watching his back.

One day remained.

After that, there would be names to hear, boundaries to set, and consequences neither song nor kiss could erase.

But not yet.

For this morning, he was here.

So was she.
