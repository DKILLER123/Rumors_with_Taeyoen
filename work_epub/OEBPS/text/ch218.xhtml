Chapter Two Hundred Eighteen

Palace Intrigue

Dusk, the same day The seaside pension, Jeju no blood drawn, no voice raised — and the hierarchy quietly turns upside down

The car rolled slowly into the walled courtyard as Jeju’s particular orange-red spilled across the sky, gilding the two-story house and the tangerine trees. Waves murmured somewhere below. On the surface, a perfectly serene vacation scene.

Ji-ho had barely stopped the car before Choi Sulli hopped lightly out.

And saw, in one glance, the woman standing on the front steps.

Tiffany had clearly taken pains. The ivory casual dress was cut exactly right, making the most of her curves and the length of her legs; the chestnut waves lay soft and loose on her shoulders. Gentle. Lovely. Waiting.

“Fany eonni!”

The smile that bloomed across Sulli’s face outshone the sunset by several degrees. She jogged up the steps, arms wide, and wrapped Tiffany in a full, unhesitating hug.

Tiffany’s whole body, in the instant those arms closed, turned to wood.

Sulli? Why is SHE here? Does she know— would she tell Taeyeon? I haven’t even figured out how to face Taeyeon myself. I never meant to steal him. I just… couldn’t help wanting a small share. The panic came up her throat so fast she could only stand there like a mannequin and be hugged.

“Eonni came to Jeju to unwind too? What a coincidence!”

Sulli released her, stepped back half a pace, eyes curved into little crescent moons, sunset light swimming in the clear pupils. And her gaze slid — as if by accident — across the side of Tiffany’s neck, where the collar half-hid a faint, fresh mark.

Tiffany dragged her mouth into something dry that wanted to be a smile.

“Ah— mm, y-yes… Sulli, how are you—”

“Oppa and I are working here! The film promo just wrapped.” Sulli’s voice was all lightness, spoiled and easy. “Oppa said it’s quiet here and I should rest a few days. What about eonni? How come YOU’RE here?”

She tipped her head, voice going soft, the question tossed off like nothing at all:

“Don’t tell me… you’re keeping an eye on Oppa for Taeyeon eonni? In case some bad woman tries to slip in through a crack?”

Every word innocent. Every sentence warm. And every syllable landing like an open palm across the face. Keeping an eye on him FOR TAEYEON EONNI. A BAD WOMAN slipping in. The bad woman — wasn’t that exactly her?

The color drained out of Tiffany’s face completely, down to the fingertips. She felt stripped — stood up naked in front of those clean, clear eyes, every unshowable thought in her lit up with nowhere left to hide.

“I— I’m— that’s not—”

The words came apart as she said them. She couldn’t meet Sulli’s eyes. Shame closed over her head like water. In front of her former roommate — her own little sister — she stood exposed as a petty thief. A person who could never be shown in daylight. A… third wheel.

Sulli catalogued every inch of that stiffness, every flutter of panic, and a cool ribbon of satisfaction unspooled through her. So even you, Fany eonni. The dazzling one. The “LA gem” of Girls’ Generation. One lifetime of never quite standing straight in front of Taeyeon eonni — and now you can’t stand straight in front of ME either. That’s a thief’s fate, isn’t it? Steal a little sweetness and you still never get to stand in the sun with it. That marrow-deep, illegitimate cringe — it doesn’t wash off.

Thock.

A finger-flick — not hard, not soft — landed crisply in the middle of Sulli’s smooth forehead.

“Ah! That hurt! Oppa!”

She clapped a hand over her forehead, mouth crumpling, doe eyes filming over with instant professional-grade tears. Her skin was so pale the spot flushed red at once, maximally pitiful.

Ji-ho ignored the performance entirely. He walked past her, slid an arm around Tiffany’s rigid shoulders, and drew her chilled body against his side.

“Let’s go in. The wind’s picking up.”

Tiffany clamped onto his arm instantly, driftwood in open water. He was here. The man she loved was here. The panic in her chest eased down a single notch.

Sulli watched the two of them move inside pressed together, and laughed softly to herself. Oppa, Fany eonni seems… so scared. Is it because she can’t face Taeyeon eonni — or because she can’t face ME, the good little sister who loves Taeyeon eonni so much? Bloodless. A clean first exchange. All it had taken was “innocence” and “warmth” to punch straight through Tiffany’s teetering defenses — and in the same motion, cement her own position as the understanding one. The useful one. She savored the feeling, unhurried, and followed them in on light feet.

✦ ✦ ✦

Inside, the lamps came on, warm yellow pushing back the dusk. Ji-ho settled Tiffany on the sofa and went to pour her a glass of warm water.

“I’ll see what there is to eat.”

And then he was in the kitchen, and the living room held only the two of them.

Tiffany cupped the warm glass, lashes down, not daring to look at the girl curled comfortably into the armchair opposite — pale calves swinging, gaze wandering the cozy room with evident interest, utterly at home.

“Jagi-yah—”

Tiffany finally scraped the courage together, lifting her eyes, reaching for the old intimate nickname like a rope bridge.

Sulli turned her head at the sound. The gaze that settled on Tiffany’s face was calm, expressionless — simply waiting.

Tiffany’s heart stuttered under that look. She bit her lip, and forced it out:

“I’m… I’m sorry.”

The apology was vague and thin — but what explanation was there to give?

Sulli blinked. Then asked, out of nowhere, with no preamble at all:

“Eonni. Would you ever break up with Oppa?”

Tiffany jolted, chestnut curls swinging with the force of her headshake.

“Never!”

Iron in the word. Last night she had finally given all of herself, body and heart filled to the brim by that man — the joy and the belonging of it were branded into her bones now. Let go? Impossible.

“Theeen—” Sulli let the syllable stretch, leaning forward, chin in both hands, eyes fixed and unblinking. “Taeyeon eonni is a wall you’ll have to face sooner or later. What’s eonni’s plan?”

The question went straight into the deepest chamber of Tiffany’s fear. Her face lost another shade; her gaze skittered; her fingers wound the hem of her dress into knots.

“I— I don’t know… I never wanted to take him from her, I swear I didn’t— I just— I couldn’t stop—”

Watching her flounder — all that everyday confidence and shine stripped away — the cold little pleasure spread further through Sulli’s chest. Look at her. The radiant one. Helpless as anyone in the mud of it, shackled by the same guilt. Sulli sat above it all and enjoyed the view. Seniority, morality, emotional high ground — every one of them had quietly become a chip in HER stack.

“Eonni,” Sulli said, tone warming into concern, “you know Taeyeon eonni’s temper better than anyone. When she finds out, she’ll be heartbroken. And furious. And Oppa will be crushed in the middle.”

She paused, watched Tiffany go whiter still, and continued at her leisure:

“And if— I’m just saying IF— Taeyeon eonni gets angry enough to make Oppa CHOOSE?”

Tiffany’s breath stopped. Her face went paper-white.

That scenario was the one she never allowed herself to imagine. That scenario was hell.

Sulli read the terror off her like a page, and judged the temperature exactly right. She sighed, softly, as if this were all very hard for her too.

“Honestly… I don’t want to see any of us get hurt. Especially not Oppa.” Her eyes lifted, and for a moment something rapt and unguarded moved through them. “When Oppa’s happy, I’m happy.”

Then her gaze came back down onto Tiffany’s face, and the tone shifted into something more delicate.

“So, eonni… before Taeyeon eonni finds out — shouldn’t we get along? Really well? At the very least, Oppa shouldn’t have to worry about the two of US. Don’t you think?”

It sounded like kindness. Every sentence was a soft knife. “Get along” meant: know your place. Behave. Possibly even — court the favor of the one who KNEW. The little sister who looked like an ally and held the detonator. Tiffany heard all of it. Humiliation surged — and drowned, immediately, under the two bigger animals in the water: losing Ji-ho, and the unspeakable wreckage of exposure. For the first time she saw with total clarity that this girl — this young, lovely, harmless-looking girl — was deeper and more dangerous than she had ever bothered to imagine.

Under the peninsula’s iron seniority culture, the hoobae in this room owed the sunbae deference. And the ground had just quietly reversed. She, the eonni — in the wrong, and knowing it — now stood a full head shorter.

Tiffany fought it for a few seconds. Then the last scraps of pride broke ranks and fled before the fear.

She dragged in a breath and manufactured a placating — almost humble — smile, her voice coming out dry.

“Sulli’s right… we should— of course we should get along. We can’t make things… hard for him.”

She even reached out, picked the plumpest, brightest strawberry from the fruit bowl on the coffee table, and offered it across with careful hands.

“Sulli-yah, have— have some fruit? You were in the car all day. You must be tired.”

Sulli looked at the strawberry held out to her. At the eyes above it working so hard to hide their fright and sit still. At the faint tremble in the fingertips.

Then she smiled, sudden and sunny, and accepted it graciously.

“Gamsahamnida, Fany eonni~”

She took a small bite. The sweet juice stained her full lips a brighter red.

And she savored it — all of it. The taste of an eonni she’d once had to look up at, lowering herself, very nearly fawning. The weight of her own position on Ji-ho’s crowded board, growing quietly heavier by the day. Choosing “little sister” had been the right play after all: no frontal war required, the soft knife carves just fine. Seniority was natural camouflage — and a secret, held close, was the sharpest blade in the drawer.

Sulli licked the last bead of sweetness from her lip, and the dark little satisfaction inside her wound itself tighter, vine around a post. This game was getting more interesting by the hour — and she had found the way she liked to play it. Because she was NOT like Fany eonni. She wasn’t a third wheel. She was the one who had loved Oppa first, the one who counted no costs, the one fated to be standing there at the very end. Taeyeon eonni? Fany eonni? Could either of them match what she and Oppa had — a bond sealed the day they nearly died together? Could either of them accept him whole, bottomless, no lines drawn? She could.

Could you?
