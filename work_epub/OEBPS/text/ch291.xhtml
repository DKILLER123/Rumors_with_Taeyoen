“It’s over. It’s completely over. My sister, seen, by— and I’m the eonni in this scenario. What am I supposed to say at the next family dinner? ‘Pass the kimchi, and also—’”

“To be fair, not all of her.” He pitched it as comfort, warm and reasonable, both hands still in the notarized position. “I closed my eyes.”

Her head came up off the wall. She looked at him for a while, at war with several futures at once.

“…Do you really expect me to believe that?”

“I really closed my eyes.”

Three seconds of eye contact, in which both parties conducted complete discovery and neither side produced a witness.

“This is your fault!” Her fist landed on his arm, accomplishing nothing medical. “Wanting coffee! At midnight! Who taught you that!”

“You invited me.”

“I invited you to coffee. I did not invite you to audit my sister’s wardrobe change!”

“Which is precisely why it can’t be my fau— no. No, you know what, the cleaner line is: I closed my eyes.”

From the living room, at sister volume:

“Eonni, the coffee’s poured! What are you doing back there!”

“Coming, coming!” Then, back into the walk-in, at the lowest thread of voice she owned, delivered with the full finger: “You. Here. Unmoved. Not one step out of this closet until I come back for you, and that is after she’s asleep. Do you understand the schedule?”

“And how long is ‘after she’s asleep’?”

“I don’t know! However long sleeping takes! Stay!”

She got two steps, and the afterthought hit her, and she spun on her heel, finger up, adding clauses at speed.

“Nothing in this closet is for touching. Nothing on that bed is for lying on. Nothing in this room is for— for anything. Nothing!”

“Understood, understood.” He was nodding with the serenity of a man signing nothing.

She glared at him one last full wattage, and went, and the door clicked, and the room was his.

✦ ✦ ✦

Song Ji-ho leaned back against the rail of beige and looked down the racks of Jung Soo-yeon’s life, sorted by color, hung by system, a boutique with one customer, and the smile stayed on his mouth like a stamp that wouldn’t dry.

After a while he looked down again at the state of his trousers, and sighed, and let it be entered into the record that the situation had been thrust upon him.

It isn’t a discipline problem. Let’s be scientific about this. Twenty minutes ago I had Soo-yeon in my arms at her own counter, soft and sweet and giving as sunlight. Then the lock beeped, and now the visual file contains her sister, unguarded, at one meter, in the warm bulb light. The Jung sisters, back to back, inside one hour. There is no man alive built to spec for that gauntlet.

He dragged a hand down his face, and made an honest attempt at cool, and was midway through it when the idea arrived, fully staffed.

Wait. Run the position. I am locked in a perfumed bedroom. Sica is on sister duty and cannot extract me until the younger one sleeps, which by her own admission is an open schedule. The door is that one. The bed is this one.

He considered the bed. Pale bedding, plump to the point of opinion, a princess-adjacent arrangement that looked like it had never once been slept in impatiently.

The corner of his mouth went up, and this time it kept going.

Argue it any way you like, Song Ji-ho. Tonight, one way or the other, you are asleep in this room. Jung Soo-yeon either shares this bed with me, or she goes down the hall and squeezes into one bed with her own sister. Whichever fork the night takes, my position improves.

There are chess problems less clean than this.

He lay down on the princess bed, legitimately and at his leisure, exactly like a man checking into a hotel paid for by an opponent.

The pillow gave up its scent at once: shampoo, faint and clean, identical to the source, the whole evening in one breath.

Either she sleeps here, or she goes and squeezes in with her sister. Whichever way the night accounts for itself, I can’t lose. It’s rare, in this life, to be handed a position where every exit is a win.

He folded an arm under his head and looked up at the ceiling of Jung Soo-yeon’s bedroom, in the deep quiet of the perfumed dark, and there, by any honest measure, his mood finished the night at its highest mark of the day.
