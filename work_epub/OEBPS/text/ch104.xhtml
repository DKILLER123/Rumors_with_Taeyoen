And the core of the blast was the song itself.

The unhurried Urban R&B groove moved like a summer-night breeze, the piano hook threading the whole track. His low, magnetic entry rendered the restlessness of waiting alone so vividly it itched; her sweet, detail-controlled line answered with that maddening is-it-mine-or-isn’t-it pout. And where the two voices tangled in the hook — pulling, yielding, circling — the title’s whole thesis stood embodied: more than friends, less than lovers, unbearably in between.

Office workers, students, anyone who had ever hovered over a send button at 1 AM — the melody caught them, and the lyric finished them.

But inside the tidal roar around the song, the discussion of the SINGER — Ji-ho himself — swelled into something bigger, stranger, and more story-shaped, as the key threads braided together at speed.

They policed the discourse gently and vigilantly at once — savoring the explosion, terrified of the undertow: all that old baggage swirling in the flood of new attention, one bad current away from a narrative relapse.

In LOEN’s war room, the dashboards told a simple story: charts pinned at #1; search volume a cliff face; social mentions compounding geometrically. Talent, scandal, history — every thread wound around every other, and none of it drowned the song. The streams all fed one river, and the river had a name.

Shin Min-hyuk watched the numbers tick and allowed himself a settled, unhurried smile. He lifted the internal line.

“Notify Ji-ho and his manager. Next itinerary starts NOW. First stage: this Friday — ‘Music Bank.’ Get KBS on the phone. I want a special staging.”

The dawn of Ji-ho’s era arrived exactly as engineered — to the melody of “Some,” and utterly unstoppable.

From this moment, two syllables were going to sweep the entire peninsula’s music industry.
