Chapter One Hundred Fifty-Five

A Female Lead Named Sulli

January 2013 SM Entertainment headquarters a return visit, half a year later

The lobby of SM Entertainment was polished mirror-bright, the same as ever, reflecting the hurrying trainees and staff across its floor.

He stood at the revolving door, looking up at the familiar logo, a smile that wasn’t quite a smile at the corner of his mouth.

Half a year. His debut album had cleared six hundred thousand copies — past the carefully-built TTS album, past even SM’s proudest workhorse, Super Junior. The critics had settled on a phrase for him: “the genius SM let slip.” And the LOEN deal he’d signed was industry legend now — the most artist-favorable rookie terms anyone could document. Lee Soo-man seonsaeng-nim. CEO Kim Young-min. Are you still standing by that trainee contract?

“Mr. Ji-ho — this way, please.” The receptionist’s eyes were bright with open curiosity.

Of course she remembered him. The boy who always practiced latest and left anyway. Now the year’s biggest solo act, walking back through the lobby in charcoal Gucci, cut clean. Against the cheap training clothes of six months ago — a different person wearing the same face.

The elevator opened on the executive floor.

“Mr. Song, CEO Kim Young-min is expecting you.” The receptionist’s voice dropped to reverent.

He only smiled, and knocked.

“Come in.”

Kim Young-min sat behind the desk, head bent over a report, brow tight. At the sound of the door his face lifted — and moved through several expressions before settling on none of them.

The man had personally let go of a talent that outshone Park Jin-young and G-Dragon both. He knew it. Everyone knew he knew it.

“Sajang-nim. Annyeonghaseyo.” The bow was flawless — not one degree more or less than courtesy required.

“Ji-ho. Sit.” Kim Young-min adjusted his glasses.

He crossed to the conference table and drew out a chair.

“Your album… did well.” Each word seemed individually expensive.

“Thank you. Good luck, mostly.”

The silence that followed was the awkward kind. Both men knew precisely how much luck had to do with it: twelve self-written tracks, every one of them a standard-bearer. Luck was not the operative variable.

Kim Young-min cleared his throat.

“Let’s hear it, then. What brings you back?”

“A film.” He drew the file from his bag and opened it. “A LOEN and CJ Entertainment production. Pure-romance. I’m the screenwriter — and the male lead.”

A pause; his eyes crossed the CEO’s carefully mixed expression.

“And LOEN has given me advisory rights on casting.”

A rookie not yet a year past debut, holding casting input on a feature. Nearly unheard of. THAT was the scale of the frame LOEN had built him — and the CEO understood, again and freshly, exactly why the boy had chosen LOEN.

“So you’re here because…”

“I’d like Sulli for the female lead.”

Kim Young-min’s face gave nothing away.

“You really do treat Sulli well.”

“A large part of how fast I rose traces to the attention from Sulli’s incident, and from the scandal with Taeyeon nuna.” The smile stayed even. “So for this film, I want Sulli as the lead — and Taeyeon nuna singing the OST.”

Fingers tapped the desk, slow. The LOEN–CJ pairing meant real money and real promotion. The young man across the table had not missed once — not one step since the day he walked out. His songs held their value. SM lost nothing on this arrangement anywhere he could see, and for Sulli and Taeyeon both, opportunities like this weren’t common. The only open question: did the script actually suit Sulli?

“Sulli is an f(x) member. Scheduling has to be considered.”

“Understood. The shoot runs three months, and it needs the leads fully committed for the window.”

The CEO lifted the phone and dialed an internal line.

“Send in the planning team lead for f(x).”

The team lead arrived fast. The CEO passed him the script: “Film scenario. Does Sulli have schedule conflicts coming up?” The subtext was legible from orbit: if it suits her, there are no conflicts; if it doesn’t, there is nothing BUT conflicts. The team lead read carefully. An appealing picture, honestly appealing. He closed the script: “Sajang-nim, Sulli’s schedule can be adjusted.”

“Ji-ho. To a good collaboration, then.”

“To a good collaboration.” He rose. “One more thing, sajang-nim — the news. May I be the one to tell Sulli?”

The CEO’s brow creased a millimeter. If he didn’t know the boy’s girlfriend was Taeyeon, he’d have suspected something else entirely.

“You may.”

“Thank you. Then — I’ll take my leave.”

He turned for the door — and the CEO’s voice caught him at the threshold.

“Ji-ho. Do you hate SM?”

The room went still.

He turned back, wearing the same even smile.

“No. I’m grateful to SM.”

Not the answer anyone in the room was braced for.

“If I hadn’t left, I might never have had the chance to make my own music — never met a company that treats writers the way LOEN does.” His voice stayed light. “Sometimes the greatest luck a person gets… is being turned away.”

A shallow bow, and he was gone.

The door clicked shut. The CEO’s face darkened by degrees as he looked at the LOEN file on the desk — the project summary, and under it, one name listed twice: screenwriter, male lead. What actually stung wasn’t the file. It was that the opportunity inside it had been carried through his door by the trainee he’d let walk.

“Adjust Sulli’s schedule.” A pause. “And don’t tell her yet.”

“Ne, sajang-nim.”

Afterward, the CEO walked down the corridor to Lee Soo-man’s office and gave him the visit in brief. The founder was quiet a moment, then rueful: “What’s the use of saying it now? He’s LOEN’s. We built everything around teams — and a creator like that slipped through the mesh.” — “Do you think we should adjust?” The founder actually looked up, startled at the source of the question. The CEO caught himself, shook his head. “Forget it. I didn’t say it.”

He hadn’t left the building. He’d found a terrace instead, phone already at his ear.

Outgoing Call · Sulli 🍑

“Sulli-yah. Where are you right now?”

“Oppa?! Practice room — why, why, WHY?”

“Let’s meet.”

“Right now? What’s going on?”

“Mm — Hong Kong, remember? I promised you a surprise.”

“THE surprise?! You’re serious?! When?!”

“Good. When you’re done, I’ll come pick you up.”

He pocketed the phone, and stood a moment in the winter light.

And after that — Joo-hyun. It had been too long, and there are things text messages simply cannot carry.
