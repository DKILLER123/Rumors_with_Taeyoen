Chapter One Hundred Eighty-One

“I Envy You, Sulli”

The same night, continued Hara’s living room four beauties, one cat, one sentence that stops the room

At the news of who was at the door, Sulli lit up and skipped for the entryway like a small delighted bird.

“Ji-eun!”

“Ji-yeon!”

“Peaches!”

“Ahahaha—”

Four girls collapsed into one laughing knot of a hug.

The warmth of the scene sent a distinct chill down one particular spine in the living room.

Calm. Stay calm.

Inventory: with Jiyeon, exactly one accidental, heated lapse. With Ji-eun — a relationship too tangled for any three sentences, but she knew his true colors, and knew things with Sulli were murky. Sulli knew his nature too; what she didn’t know was that her best friend Ji-eun had received the same honesty he was so generous with.

Calm. No panic.

He tidied his thoughts, rose smoothly, and produced a warm, perfectly appropriate smile. “Ji-eun. Ji-yeon. Good evening.”

Jiyeon wore a vintage-print short-sleeve blouse over crisp white denim shorts, the lines of her — round, soft arms, precisely drawn collarbones — doing effortless damage. Ji-eun had come in a milk-white cotton shirt with the sleeves pushed carelessly up, wide khaki trousers brushing the floor, hair in a low tail: literary, unhurried. Ha-ra, in her gray-blue silk loungewear set, cropped top baring a slender waistline, cuffs rolled above ankles pale as porcelain. Add one soft-smiling Sulli in her house dress, and the room now held four entirely different species of beautiful.

He had grown into his own looks lately — taller-seeming, steadier. The instant Ji-yeon’s eyes touched his, they slid away, not quite comfortable.

“…Good evening.”

Ji-eun managed far more ease, lifting a hand in a breezy wave. “Chukhahae! The film wrapped.”

“And congratulations to you — the new drama’s sitting on top of the weekend slot.”

“For which I should thank a certain acting tutor,” she said, and meant it. “Extortionate rates. But — worth the money. Barely.”

Her first long-format lead had drawn early blood — the critics had teeth, and the green edges of her performance had given them plenty to bite. Then the tutoring: the crying scenes first, then the lived-in everyday register, and week by week the audience had come around. Privately she had to admit it — philanderer or not, the man was infuriatingly good at everything he touched.

“If you’re that satisfied, I accept performance bonuses.”

“In your DREAMS. Three million won for ten lessons and he wants a BONUS?”

Ha-ra’s head tilted in, curious. “What three million?”

“Oppa gives Ji-eun acting lessons,” Sulli supplied, sunny. “Three hundred thousand per session.”

“That much?” Ji-yeon’s eyebrows went up.

“But it WORKS.” A small proprietary pride crept into Sulli’s voice. “Everyone online is praising Ji-eun’s acting now. All thanks to Oppa’s teaching.”

“Yah, Peaches!” Ji-eun’s hand shot out for her cheek. “That was MY hard work! His contribution is — at MOST ten percent!”

“Tch. Some people collect the goods and deny the receipt.”

She stuck her tongue out at him and pulled a face. “Nyeh. If you’re so aggrieved, try being a girl for a day.”

“You—”

“Okay, okay, children.” Ha-ra broke it up, laughing despite herself. “The food and drinks are ready. Sit. I’ve been waiting long enough.”

They arranged themselves around the low table. Ji-yeon’s spicy squid and kimchi-tofu took the center; he lifted a piece of tofu dripping kimchi brine and the salt-heat of the kimchi against the sweet, custardy tofu opened the appetite by force. He went back for a second piece.

Ji-eun, chin propped on one hand, let her gaze travel from him to her best friends and back — and déjà vu tapped her on the shoulder. The last girls’ gathering had featured this exact configuration.

“Hold on.” She blinked at him, suspicion assembling. “Why is it that OUR girls’ nights always seem to include YOU?”

He set down his chopsticks and regarded her with luxurious patience. “Ji-eun. Has it occurred to you that every single time, the plan was made between me, Sulli and Ha-ra nuna first — and that the late add-on to girls’ night is, in fact, you?”

She opened her mouth. Rewound the record. Closed it. It was — irritatingly — true.

And there sat Peaches, tucked in at his side with the settled, doting naturalness of a little wife at her own table. Something sour rose in Ji-eun’s chest — she declined to investigate on whose behalf — and she solved it by physically repossessing her friend, pulling Sulli over and nuzzling her shoulder.

“Peaches-yah, we haven’t played together in FOREVER. I missed you.”

“We’ve all been buried in filming~” Sulli patted her back, soothing. “My movie’s wrapped now. There’ll be time. Lots of it.”

The word movie tripped a memory; Sulli sat up. “Oh — eonni, where’s Lucky?”

“Ah, right! Where DID Lucky go?”

“Who’s Lucky?” — in stereo, from Ji-eun and Ji-yeon.

“The cat I adopted. Ridiculously pretty.”

“A CAT?”

Chairs emptied. The search began.

“Lucky~”

“Kitty kitty~”

“Myau~ myau~”

The answer drifted from the guest room: one entirely white cat with clear blue eyes, curled at the corner of the bed, face engineered for mass destruction. It monopolized the room’s attention in under a second.

“WAH! So pretty!”

“Let me hold it! ME!”

A period of concentrated group adoration followed, at the end of which Ji-yeon — by straightforward superiority of grip strength — emerged holding the prize, stroking its back with deep satisfaction.

“Sulli, where do you even BUY a cat this beautiful?”

“Not bought.” A distinct note of pride. “It’s an actor. It’s IN our movie — I adopted it when we wrapped. You’ll see when you’re in the theater.”

“An actor-cat?”

“Mm. Very important role. No spoilers.”

“Of course we’re supporting your movie!” Ji-eun hooked an arm around Sulli’s neck, genuinely delighted. “Our Sulli, a FILM actress now. Look at you.”

“Really enviable,” Ji-yeon said, softly.

Sulli beamed and wound her arm through his, light spilling from every angle of her face. “It’s all Oppa. He wrote it — I didn’t even have to audition.”

Ji-eun’s gaze settled on him, long and level and dark at the bottom.

This absolute menace. Stringing ME along with one hand and plucking at Peaches’ heart with the other. Then again — the face, the talent, and a literal life-debt on top. Of course Peaches fell. Taeyeon. Sulli. Me. That’s THREE. This — this—

Ji-yeon had gone quiet, fingertip working under the cat’s chin.

A film lead. Must be nice. T-ara’s calendar is a desert — a few scattered Japan appearances and nothing else — and every one of us is carrying the same quiet dread about what comes next.

Ha-ra watched Sulli hang on his arm — the ease of it, the sweetness of it — and something wistful crossed her face and softened into a smile before it could become anything sharper.

“I really envy you, Sulli. Finding a boyfriend as exceptional as Ji-ho.”

The sentence landed.

And the room went suddenly, comprehensively silent.
