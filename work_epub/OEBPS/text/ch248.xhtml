Chapter Two Hundred Forty-Eight

The Canary and the Businesswoman

1:00 a.m. Seoul · a city still wide awake pride drinks too much, courage arrives uninvited, and a future is placed on the table

By day, Seoul belonged to office towers and pressed suits. After midnight, the same workers shed their uniforms, put on the clothes they had chosen for themselves, and disappeared beneath neon signs—pouring a week’s worth of swallowed frustration into music, laughter, and alcohol.

Jessica lay sideways across Park Min-young’s sofa with a glass trapped between both hands. Her words had begun to blur at the edges.

“He wasn’t like this before. He used to be warm with me. Then he suddenly went cold. No calls, no replies. Whenever I ask to meet, he’s busy.”

Real
Park Min-young
Television Actress
Known for Sungkyunkwan Scandal · City Hunter · Dr. Jin
Born: March 4, 1986

A quick-witted leading actress and Jessica’s trusted drinking companion, equally capable of blunt romantic advice and dangerously enthusiastic matchmaking.

Min-young rolled her eyes.

“Yah. You’re Girls’ Generation’s Jessica. You’re supposed to leave men waiting—not sit here because one left you waiting.”

“Maj-a.” Jessica pushed herself upright and raised her glass with wavering dignity. “I’m Jessica of Girls’ Generation. I refuse to be controlled by a man.”

“Exactly. No man controls you.”

“Cheers.”

They drank.

When Jessica sank back onto the sofa, her confidence went with her.

“But he’s different.”

Min-young’s teasing expression faded.

“He’s the most capable man I’ve ever met.” Jessica stared into the remaining liquor. “I need his help. I may have found a way forward, but I still don’t know whether I should do it.”

Min-young gave her shoulder an exasperated shove.

“What is there to hesitate over? Good men don’t remain available while everyone politely considers their feelings.”

“You think I should make the first move?”

“Of course. You’re beautiful, successful, and impossible when you’re being proud. Maybe you held yourself so far above him that he decided reaching up wasn’t worth the trouble.”

Jessica was silent for two seconds. Then she began searching through her phone.

“I’ll call him.”

Her thumb found the number and stopped.

“No. He’ll only say he’s busy again.”

She tossed the phone onto the cushion and stood with both hands braced on her knees.

“I’m going to his house.”

Min-young sprang up.

“Sica. Soo-yeon-ah. You can barely walk straight.”

Jessica was already moving toward the door.

A sober Jessica could be reasoned with. A drunk Jessica had the resolve of a landslide.

After two failed attempts to stop her, Min-young grabbed her coat and followed. If nothing else, she intended to discover the identity of the man who had reduced the Ice Princess to this.

✦ ✦ ✦

Ji-ho was asleep with Irene tucked against his shoulder. The long day and the difficult conversation between them had finally settled into quiet.

His phone rang.

Irene stirred with a soft complaint. Ji-ho reached for the device, saw the caller’s name, and frowned.

INCOMING CALL · JESSICA · 1:07 A.M.

“Yah. Open the door.”

“Jung Soo-yeon, have you lost your mind?”

Two seconds of wounded silence.

“You… yelled at me.”

“Do you know what time it is?”

Rustling. A muffled question away from the receiver. Someone holds up one finger.

“One?”

“Then whatever this is can wait until morning.”

“No. In the morning you’ll be busy.”

“Open the door. I’m outside your house.”

Ji-ho sat upright.

Irene shifted closer, still mostly asleep.

“Who is it… this late?”

“Nothing you need to worry about. Go back to sleep.”

He patted her back until her breathing evened out again, then carefully freed his arm and pulled on a robe before stepping into the living room.

“My house? Do you even know where I live?”

“Your house is…” Jessica faltered. “That villa you brought me to last time.”

Ji-ho exhaled.

“I moved weeks ago.”

“Moved? Oh…”

The call ended.

He stared at the screen, irritation turning abruptly into concern.

The old hillside villa district was nearly deserted at this hour. Apart from residents, private security, and the occasional night runner, there would be no one around. Jessica was a famous, visibly intoxicated woman standing somewhere outside a darkened property.

He regretted, briefly and sharply, how far he had pushed the distance between them.

There was no time to examine that regret.

Ji-ho returned to the bedroom. Irene remained deeply asleep. He changed without waking her, took his keys, and left.

✦ ✦ ✦
Hillside villa district · fifteen minutes later an old address and two women beneath one streetlamp

Across from the villas, Jessica sat curled on a set of stone hiking steps, her forehead resting against her knees. She wore a pale blush pleated dress and looked very small beneath the amber streetlight.

Min-young crouched beside her in a cap and mask, holding one arm.

“Sica, can you finally tell me his name?”

No answer.

“Sica?”

Nothing.

“Yah.”

Min-young sat beside her with a sigh. The neighborhood was unnervingly quiet; only leaves moving in the night wind disturbed it. She had to admit that the location was impressive.

A car stopped near the villa gates.

Ji-ho got out quickly and surveyed the empty road. When he saw no one, he called Jessica again and began walking.

Min-young answered.

“Yeoboseyo?”

“Who is this?”

She raised an eyebrow. The voice was warm, clear, and faintly familiar.

“I’m Sica’s friend.”

Ji-ho rounded the bend and looked up. Two women sat beneath the streetlamp; one had folded herself inward, while the other waved from beneath a baseball cap.

Relief arrived alongside annoyance.

Jessica had not been reckless enough to come alone. He had worried for nothing—but he was grateful to have worried for nothing.

Min-young studied the approaching man: baseball cap, mask, tall frame, and a limited-edition jacket recognizable even in poor light. Jessica’s taste, apparently, was excellent.

As he came closer, the eyes above the mask became more familiar.

Ji-ho recognized her first.

“Park Min-young?”

She stood slowly.

“Ne. And you are…?”

Ji-ho removed his cap and mask and offered his hand.

“Song Ji-ho. Thank you for staying with her.”

Min-young covered her mouth.

“Ohmo—Song Ji-ho-ssi?”

Her surprise became delight. She took his hand in both of hers and shook it enthusiastically.

“I love your music. I bought the physical album—the collector’s edition too.”

A genuine physical-album buyer qualified as a genuine fan.

“Thank you for supporting it.”

“Could I have an autograph later? And is the second album already in production? Have you chosen a release date? Are you producing the whole—”

“Min-young-ah…”

The faint voice behind her restored Min-young’s memory of why they were there.

Jessica raised her head. Her eyes were red when they found Ji-ho.

She tried to stand and immediately tipped sideways.

Ji-ho caught her before she fell.

“You really were home.”

He and Min-young looked at each other.

There was no useful answer to that.

Min-young hurried to support Jessica’s other side.

“Sica, are you all right? Do you know who I am?”

“I’m fine.” Jessica looked at her with solemn drunken affection. “You can go now. I found Ji-ho.”

Min-young stared.

Abandoned for a man in less than ten seconds. Friendship was fragile indeed.

So this was the person Jessica had drunk herself miserable over. Looking at Ji-ho’s steady hold on her friend—and remembering that he had driven out in the middle of the night—Min-young concluded the interest was not entirely one-sided.

Ji-ho looked at her.

“You’ve been drinking too. I’ll take both of you home.”

“That isn’t necessary. My manager is waiting in the car below.”

“She’s heavily intoxicated. Are you comfortable leaving her alone with a man?”

Min-young’s respect for him rose another degree.

“Then send me your number. I’ll call her in ten minutes, and she can stay in your guest room until she’s sober.”

“That works.”

She transferred Jessica’s coat and bag to him, then bent close to her friend.

“Sica, I’m leaving with my manager. You will answer when I call. If you don’t, I’m coming back with security.”

Jessica made an indistinct sound that might have been agreement.

Min-young turned to Ji-ho, eyes sparkling with equal parts warning and fandom.

“Please keep her safe. And don’t forget the autograph.”

“I won’t.”

She hurried down the steps. A minute later, a waiting car pulled away.

Ji-ho sat beside Jessica beneath the streetlamp.

AFTER-MIDNIGHT JESSICA OFF DUTY

Dress · pale blush pleats, elegant rather than stage-bright

Details · delicate straps, simple heels, a light floral fragrance

Impression · the Ice Princess with every public defense lowered by exhaustion and wine

Jessica leaned against him as if she had forgotten how to hold herself upright. The careful poise she wore before cameras was gone. Under the amber light, she looked less like an icon and more like a tired woman who had run out of ways to pretend she was not afraid.

“Why are you avoiding me?”

Ji-ho did not answer immediately.

“I asked you a question.”

She tugged weakly at his sleeve.

“What is wrong with me?”

“Nothing.”

“Then why?” Her voice began to break. “You were warm before. Then you stopped answering. Coffee, dinner—always busy. What are you so busy doing?”

“I am busy.”

“Liar. You’re avoiding me.”

He did not deny it.

After several seconds without an answer, her eyes filled.

“I know you bought Tiffany a villa. It must have cost billions.”

Ji-ho’s expression changed.

“At airports, the loudest cheers used to be for Taeyeon, Yoon-a, and me. This time they were for Tiffany—because of the songs you gave her.”

Jealousy sharpened each word, but pain gave it weight.

“Career, affection, security. No wonder she can stay without a title.”

“Jung Soo-yeon.”

“You hate hearing me talk about her.”

Jessica stood too quickly and swayed. She leaned toward him, impulsive and uncertain.

Ji-ho turned his face before the attempted kiss could land and steadied her by the shoulders.

“You’ve had too much to drink.”

Humiliation broke through the last of her composure. Tears slipped down her cheeks.

He lifted a hand to wipe them away. She turned aside.

His hand fell.

“Because you’re too proud,” he said.

She looked at him without understanding.

“Forget it. I’ll take you somewhere safe. We’ll speak tomorrow, when you’re sober.”

Jessica stepped into his arms before he could rise.

“I’m not leaving until you answer me.”

She was warm with wine and trembling with determination.

“What answer do you want?”

Jessica looked up. Alcohol had stripped away caution, but not the ambition beneath it.

“A man like you is difficult not to want. If you pursued someone seriously, almost no woman could refuse you.”

Her breath shook.

“I won’t pretend I feel nothing. Even knowing you’re unfaithful, I’m drawn to you. Pathetic, isn’t it?”

“Soo-yeon—”

“You said you were a businessman. So am I.”

She straightened as much as the alcohol allowed.

“I have two offers.”

JESSICA’S PROPOSAL · SPOKEN DRAFT
Option One · The Canary

Five years as his private companion. In exchange, Ji-ho finances and supports the business she intends to build. At the end of five years, she leaves with control of that enterprise.

Her valuation: the name and market power of Girls’ Generation’s Jessica.

Option Two · The Partner

Three years to build the business together and discover whether affection can become love. She will offer loyalty, companionship, and every ordinary duty of a girlfriend except physical intimacy until she gives it freely.

If she cannot accept his complicated private life after three years, she keeps the business but repays his investment with interest. She also offers a limited final concession—an emotionally reckless term Ji-ho refuses to discuss while she is intoxicated.

“You are not bargaining with your body tonight,” Ji-ho said. “You are drunk.”

“The offer is still mine.”

“Then make it again tomorrow.”

Jessica’s eyes burned beneath the streetlight.

“If all you see is Jessica’s beauty and fame, choose the first offer.”

She paused.

“If what you want is Jung Soo-yeon—the person—give me three years.”

“You have remarkable faith in this business.”

“I have faith in you.”

Her voice steadied.

“A life you refuse to wager is a life you have already decided to lose. I gambled thirteen years of youth and won my first career.”

Night wind lifted the hem of her skirt. Her eyes were swollen, her chin stubborn, her spine trembling but straight.

“Now I’m wagering my judgment. I believe you can help me build the second.”

For that moment, she was not Girls’ Generation’s Jessica. She was simply a woman willing to place her name, labor, and future behind a decision—and accept the cost if she was wrong.

Ji-ho watched her in silence.

DREAM LIFE · CHALLENGE QUEST TRIGGERED

Ding—Challenge Quest: The Canary and the Businesswoman

Path One · CanaryAccept Jessica’s first proposal. Beauty, fame, and dependence may produce the perfect ornament—but an ornament remains inside its cage.

Path Two · BusinesswomanAccept Jessica’s second proposal. Her commercial instincts may be unproven, but her resilience and appetite for risk may create both a trusted partner and a formidable ally.

Selection deferred. A binding answer should not be taken from an intoxicated participant.

Two roads opened before him beneath the indifferent Seoul night.

Ji-ho did not choose.

Not yet.
