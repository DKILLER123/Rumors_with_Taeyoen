Chapter One Hundred Two

The Debut Plan · Into the Booth

He delivered Taeyeon safely back to her family’s neighborhood in Jeonju, then pointed the car at Seoul and didn’t stop.

At the LOEN building, he went straight up to Artist Development. Chief Lee Ji-yeon’s face produced its professional smile on sight.

“Back already? Everything handled?”

“Ne, all handled, Ji-yeon nuna.” Respectful posture, fresh smile. “Three days apart and nuna looks even more radiant — years younger, honestly. Did something good happen?”

“Aigoo, what good thing could happen to ME?” Her whole face relaxed anyway, visibly pleased, tone slipping into something warmer. “I just squeezed in some skin management last week. One little beauty injection.”

“So it actually works that well?”

He was mildly startled — he’d been dispensing standard social lubricant, and she’d gone and actually had the injection. Recovery was instant; the thumbs-up, sincere.

“Works? Nuna was already lovely — now you look like a graduating sunbae. Full of light.”

“Hahaha — that MOUTH of yours.” She laughed outright, her opinion of this rookie — skill and social IQ both fully online — climbing another notch. “Right, to business. The debut song. Where did you land?”

He straightened.

“I’ve considered the company’s recommendation carefully, and I’m taking it. ‘Some’ is the debut song. I’m looking forward to working with IU nuna.”

“The WISE choice!” Approval flashed through her eyes and she was already standing. “Come on — the bonbujang-nim’s office.”

“He said you’d choose this song. Guaranteed it.”

“…Why?”

Something reminiscent surfaced in her expression.

“Because the ambition in your eyes is exactly what IU’s looked like back then. Stronger, if anything.”

“Let’s go. From today, this whole floor shifts into high gear.”

✦   ✦   ✦

In the Music Division head’s office, Shin Min-hyuk sat behind the desk with Kim Si-won already present. Lee Ji-yeon brought Ji-ho in; pleasantries were brief; he restated his decision.

Shin Min-hyuk nodded slowly, satisfaction plain.

“Ji-ho-ssi. I read you correctly after all.”

“We all know what’s inside ‘Some.’ Pair it with IU’s pull, and this launch doesn’t just land — it detonates.”

He leaned in, hands folding on the desk.

“Direction’s locked. Time to build the debut plan. Kim siljang — production timeline?”

Kim Si-won pushed up his glasses.

“Recording is the crunch item. IU’s schedule needs precision coordination — we’re targeting both vocalists tracked and final edit inside two weeks. MV: I’ll engage the production team; shooting within a week of recording wrap. Full production cycle, one month, to guarantee ceiling quality.”

“Lee siljang — development and promotion?”

Lee Ji-yeon had clearly walked in loaded — whatever song won, the promo scaffolding had been drafted for weeks.

“Phase one: seven days out, we release ‘mystery rookie male vocalist’ concept photos and the IU collaboration tease. Melon homepage feature — our own platform — plus seeded music bloggers for organic-looking chatter.”

“Release day: audio and MV drop simultaneously; all channels fire at once — Melon’s full ad inventory, purchased SNS trend keywords, deep interaction with IU’s official accounts. Every Melon chart tracked in real time.”

“Broadcast: music-show appearances for both — my recommendation is a formal debut on ‘Music Bank.’ Post-debut, beyond promotion schedules, we screen one or two high-quality variety appearances for first exposure — focus locked on musical talent.”

“Variety selection needs to be surgical. He keeps the mystery,” Shin Min-hyuk added. “First-stage design?”

“I’ll negotiate directly with the network. Target: a special staging — a romantic duet set with IU, maximum talk-value.”

Shin Min-hyuk’s gaze came to rest, finally, on Ji-ho.

“You heard all of it. The company believes in you, and the investment will show it. Which also means the pressure will be unprecedented — debuting WITH IU means every eye and every comparison lands on you at once, day one.”

“Your job: at every single node — recording, shooting, stage — show ability and presence that make the word ‘rookie’ look like a clerical error. Are you ready?”

The air in the office was thick with pooled ambition. Ji-ho drew one breath and met the man’s eyes without wavering.

“Ne, bonbujang-nim. I’m ready.”

“I believe ‘Some’ will be the brightest song of the second half — and I’ll be the strongest rookie in it.”

“Good.” A real smile this time. “Meeting adjourned. All departments — detail your plans along today’s lines and execute immediately.”

“Ji-ho-ssi. Your first assignment: be ready to record with IU.”

“Welcome to the actual battlefield.”

Out in the corridor, Ji-ho exhaled long, and the light seemed to gain physical weight. Lee Ji-yeon clapped his shoulder, voice dropping.

“It’s going to be nonstop from here. Guard your body AND your head.”

“Oh — the company’s assigning you a manager, a makeup team, a stylist. A personal assistant we’ll provide short-term, but honestly? Hire your own.”

“Understood. Thank you, Ji-yeon nuna.”

✦   ✦   ✦

With the plan locked, time hit fast-forward. Lessons, meetings, makeup tests, shoot planning — each day meshed into the next like machined gears.

And then it was recording day.

flagship gear, floor to ceiling. no excuses available.

Ji-ho arrived an hour early to re-walk melody and staging already branded into his memory. Kim Si-won had beaten him there and was calibrating the chain; he glanced up and nodded.

“Condition?”

“Ne. Ready.”

He answered it seriously — he wasn’t just the vocalist here. He was the author, with a non-negotiable blueprint for every bar.

Half an hour later the studio door eased open. Ji-eun came in — comfortable hoodie and jeans, immaculate light makeup, hair soft over her shoulders, wearing her signature warm, faintly formal smile.

“Annyeonghaseyo — IU imnida.” The greeting went to the producer first; then her eyes found Ji-ho and the smile deepened. “Ji-ho. It’s been days. The song really is wonderful.”

“Let’s skip the ceremony.” He grinned. “Shall we?”

Work began at once. Both singers took the booth for level checks. Ji-ho’s opening section went first.

Performance

The opening verses — a man alone in his apartment, phone in hand, willing it to ring. His first pass is technically immaculate. And that’s the problem.

In the control room, chin in hand, into the talkback:

“Ji-ho-ya, technique’s rock solid — but it’s TOO solid. That itchy, self-mocking restlessness of waiting on a phone that won’t ring? Bring it down to street level. Live in it. You’ve been in that room, haven’t you?”

He nodded, closed his eyes, and went looking for it — and found it immediately, because he owned it: Taeyeon’s schedule devouring her days, his own debut devouring his; a couple newly addicted to each other reduced to texting across two-hour reply gaps.

The next take carried a thread of tiredness and dry self-mockery, a man genuinely muttering at an empty room — down to the last line about a phone that stayed silent until sleep took him.

That one felt right.

✦   ✦   ✦

IU’s sections came next — and she demonstrated exactly what a veteran’s effortlessness looks like: near-identical top-grade emotional output on every pass, without ever merely repeating herself.

At the line about suddenly hating the phrase just friends, she stopped mid-take, thought, and keyed her mic.

“Seonsaeng-nim — Ji-ho-ssi — can I try this line lighter? Almost muttered? That squirmy ‘won’t admit it but can’t deny it’ feeling — if I sing it too solidly it might read as performed. Brushing past it might actually itch more.”

Ji-ho lit up.

“Yes! That ‘don’t know WHY I hate hearing it’ is a flicker of private annoyance — half-voice will cut deeper!”

Kim Si-won signed off. The take landed.

Then came her big emotional line — the complaint that he was no good lately, colliding headlong into the confession that he was, nonetheless, all she had. Her delivery was flawless on paper: the reproach full, the dependence complete. In the control room, Kim Si-won was already nodding, pen moving to mark the take—

“Nuna — I’m sorry. One moment, please.”

IU blinked. Ji-ho left the booth, put on the monitor headphones, and listened with his eyes shut, brow drawing in — the fully submerged focus of someone who has left the room for the inside of the song.

“Is something wrong, Ji-ho?”

“That line, just now.” His words came slow with thought. “Nuna’s read expressed both halves fully — the blame AND the clinging. But the TURN between them… it’s missing its seam. It comes out smooth. Almost too smooth.”

Kim Si-won’s eyebrow went up. He leaned forward, interested.

“Missing its seam?”

“Yes.” Ji-ho walked into the booth and stood beside her, patient, precise. “The line’s whole tension is this: the front half is a small ERUPTION of stored-up resentment — an accusation with heat on it. But the back half isn’t surrender, and it isn’t sadness. It’s a blade turning in mid-air — it cuts through the complaint and lands on the real thing underneath: fear, and acceptance.”

“That emotional hairpin needs a tiny, audible fracture in the voice — a reset. What nuna did was beautiful, but the transition was too clean. It sounds… engineered.”

He demonstrated — humming the joint two ways.

“Glide through it, and the layers blur — the self-tearing pain in the lyric goes soft. What I need to hear: the tail of the complaint falls… then an almost imperceptible catch of breath… and then those next three words arrive in a DIFFERENT voice. Thinner. More broken. More decided. As if everything she just accused him of got swallowed whole by the admission.”

The note was microscopic — past pitch, past rhythm, down at the millimeter level of emotional plumbing.

IU listened in silence, arms folding, one finger tapping her arm — her deep-processing tell. She had climbed from a mocked, struggling teenager to the top of this industry on exactly two engines: work, and a merciless ear. Bluntness didn’t offend her. It fed her.

“…I see it,” she said at last, very quietly. “It’s not ‘complain, then depend.’ It’s ‘TRY to complain — and discover the complaint has no floor to stand on, because the dependence already ate it.’ So the turn isn’t a bridge. It’s a — self-demolition?”

“EXACTLY that.” His eyes went bright — the thrill of being precisely understood. “Self-demolition.”

“Again, please.”

She turned on her heel, walked back to the mic, and pulled on the headphones.

Performance

The track rolls. The complaint builds with its former heat — and at the very edge of the last syllable, her throat catches. Not a flaw: a genuine emotional stall, the sound of a sentence choking on itself.

Then the turn — and her voice is suddenly a different substance: drained of force, lighter by half, carrying the soft broken finality of someone laying down a weapon. The fall from accusation to total disarmament is steep enough to hurt.

At the console, barely audible: “…Daebak.”

Ji-ho watched her through the glass to the last decaying note, shoulders finally unlocking. He let out a long breath.

“Perfect, nuna. That’s the exact feeling. Thank you.”

He was harder still on himself. On the tangled run of lyrics about a person who seemed his, wasn’t his, and somehow belonged to him anyway, he finished one full take and his own frown beat the producer’s to it.

“I’m sorry. Again, please.”

The “seems mine” needs blurrier diction — a testing uncertainty. But the instant of “belongs to me,” the want has to spike — even if the difference is a tenth of a second.
