Chapter One Hundred Thirty-Eight

Happy Camp

Taping Day Changsha Broadcasting Studios the colorful wall, the famous five, and a full house

Blinding stage light; the iconic rainbow backdrop; not one empty seat. The five hosts landed their signature opening, and the senior MC lifted his microphone over the applause.

“Welcome, everyone, to this week’s Happy Camp!”

“Today’s guests are VERY special,” the show’s queen of chaos picked up, bright and quick. “One of them has come a long, long way from the peninsula — and the other one grew up with our fans cheering him from right here across the water!”

“Both of them are absolutely on fire in the Korean music scene right now—”

“—and their duet has been sitting on top of every chart!”

“That SUPER sweet one!”

“I genuinely cannot wait any longer!”

“Then let’s welcome them with everything we’ve got — IU! And SONG JI-HO!”

Performance

The duet’s intro rises; the lights go soft pink-violet; two figures enter from opposite wings — he in light grey casual over a plain white tee, she in a lotus-pink dress, hair in soft curls.

They meet center stage and sing. The hall very nearly comes off its foundations — and the instant his face hits the giant screen in close-up, a wave of audible gasps rolls through the seats.

“He’s TOO handsome—” · “A hundred times better than the videos—” · “Is that jaw line legally real?”

Song done, the senior host led the applause in, greeting them in Mandarin and then in carefully practiced Korean. IU pressed her palms together, bowed, and delivered her rehearsed Mandarin — “Hello everyone, I’m IU, so happy to meet you” — green at the edges but clear and charming, drawing warm laughter and applause. Ji-ho followed in fluent, unaccented Mandarin, greeting each host by name and the audience after.

The comedy anchor leaned in, clutched her heart theatrically, and inspected him at close range.

“Wah — up close it’s even WORSE! Ji-ho, have you noticed the studio lights are extra bright today? Are you REFLECTING them?”

“It’s probably your smile bringing the light over,” he returned, patting his own cheek — and the two senior hosts exchanged one appraising look. Fast, high-EQ, funny. Television-safe at every speed.

✦   ✦   ✦

The interview couch. The senior host steered, gently.

“Let’s start with IU — which visit to China is this?”

She listened to the interpreter’s earpiece, answered in Korean — third visit, long ago, delighted to be on this show — and then, by pure instinct in a foreign room, glanced at Ji-ho.

He caught the glance before the staff interpreter could inhale — leaned in, and rendered her answer into Mandarin, natural, quick, even preserving the small skip of excitement in her original tone.

“Is Ji-ho volunteering as translator now?” the host asked, delighted.

“IU nuna’s Mandarin is still in training. I’m just covering the gaps.”

Said lightly — and it warmed her straight through. In a foreign studio, one completely trusted relay changes everything; the safety of it was irreplaceable, and the look she turned on him carried gratitude with a new dependence dissolved inside it.

“This picture is SO sweet I can’t bear it!” the comedy anchor announced, chin in both hands — and IU, catching maybe a third of the words and all of the tone, went pink at the ear-tips.

▸ HOSTS’ TURN — “kimchi” lands fast; “hanok” gets a roof-shape and cross-legged tea-drinking, and the resident snack-loving host buzzes in with “TATAMI!” — chaos, foot-stamping, “KOREA! Korea, not Japan!” — before the senior host rescues it.

“Your chemistry is ILLEGAL,” the senior host declared. IU, on translation delay, laughed and threw Ji-ho a thumbs-up; he returned, low, in Korean: “nuna’s gestures were very vivid” — and the cameras ate the whole exchange.

Back on the couch, the origin story: she praised the demo’s hooks and the sweetness-with-a-complaint-in-it feeling; he translated — then added, facing the audience, his own coda: that her instincts in the booth had made the final version better, that her emotional precision was rare. Translation plus curation plus compliment, in one seamless motion — and though she couldn’t parse the words, she could read the room’s reaction and the warmth of the look, and her face heated again. When she impulsively added that she’d been delighted by the invitation because his songs were extraordinary — then flushed at having interrupted the flow — he converted it smoothly into a modest joke at his own expense that kept her flawless and the segment moving.

“I’m starting to think Ji-ho isn’t here as a guest,” the senior host said, “he’s here as IU’s official spokesperson!”

A mini skit followed — a couple’s quarrel-and-reconciliation over a late arrival and a strawberry cake, her Korean pout answered by his Mandarin coaxing, eye-lines and timing so natural the language gap simply stopped registering — and then the group photo, and the farewells: hers slow, careful, fully rehearsed Mandarin, thanking every host by name; his fluent and warm, closing with —

“—and thank you to the fans here, who’ve supported me since before anyone knew my name. Being able to stand on this stage for you feels like coming full circle. My first album goes live next month. Please look forward to it.”

✦   ✦   ✦

Backstage, the perfect broadcast posture came off her like a coat; she sagged, tired and shining. He appeared with two warm honey-citron teas.

“Hard day, nuna. You were excellent.”

“Today was all thanks to you.” Korean, soft, eyes very bright over the cup. “Without you I’d have frozen solid.”

“A singer who’s played arenas? Hardly.”

“It’s different.” She shook her head. “When the language is gone, a person gets so… unsteady. Every time you translated, the floor came back.” A pause; quieter: “And… you always seem to know what I need.”

A sentence with more than colleague in it, in a room containing only two teams’ worth of discreet assistants.

“A few more promo stops,” he said, gently even, “and then your tour finale. Let’s finish well.”

“Mm.” She held the cup in both hands, and the curve of her mouth was soft the whole ride home — where, watching neon slide past the window, she suddenly said it in Mandarin, the pronunciation markedly improved:

“Thank you.”

“You’re welcome,” he answered in kind, smiling in the dark.

✦   ✦   ✦

He was fresh from the shower and reaching for his phone to call Taeyeon when the knock came — quick, urgent.

At THIS hour?

He opened the door and IU slipped in on a gust of perfume. He shut it fast, voice dropping.

“Yah— have you lost your mind?”

“Scared?” She stepped in instead of back, eyes glinting, chin up.

Pure as porcelain in public; pure trouble in private. Two complete faces. He shook his head slowly.

“Why are you shaking your head?”

“I periodically suspect you of a theatrical personality disorder. The difference between your public and private selves is clinical.”

“The version for everyone else,” she said, blinking sweetly, “and the version for YOU. Should they be the same?”

“Oh? And what’s different about my version?” One skeptical pass over the pajamas: high-necked, opaque, fortress-grade.

“Want to find out?” Her eyes narrowed; her teeth caught her lower lip; one hand lifted the hem of the pink sleepwear a theatrical inch. “Look for yourself.”

His eyes darkened into something with edges. So — she’d decided something tonight. She was studying his face right back, with an open flicker of fascination: if she didn’t see him constantly, she’d have sworn he’d had work done. The face hadn’t CHANGED — it had simply become more itself, more coherent, more…

He picked her up bodily, crossed the room in three strides, and deposited her on the bed. She landed entirely unafraid, watching him with amusement as he followed, and looked up at him from the pillows with her arms flung open, defenseless on purpose, the air going warm degree by degree.

She glanced down once at her top button.

“Answer one question — and you can undo it.”

His hand rose, unhurried, closing the distance to her collar.

“What question?”

The blush was climbing her face now, but her eyes never wavered — watching his hand arrive, watching his fingers close on the button.

“That kiss of ours. What did it make us?”

His hand stopped. His gaze came up off her collar and met her eyes.

He knew the arithmetic: say “boyfriend,” and the button opens — possibly the whole evening opens. But IU was not Irene’s quiet keeping-kind. The girlfriend title was not a coupon to be handed out under duress.

The silence — and the stilled hand — dropped her temperature ten degrees. She shoved him off, yanked her hem down, and stood, arctic.

“Apparently that question is very hard for you. Sorry for the intrusion.”

“Not hard,” he said, unbothered, from the pillows. “Mistimed.”

“Mistimed?” She turned. “Meaning WHAT?”

“Mm~” Arms folded behind his head. “I think — after my first album finishes its run. THEN I can answer you.”

“After the album? …Why?”

“Nuna. Don’t you find women who ask too many questions lose a little of their mystery?”

Her breath caught; her eyes closed; one long inhale. Fine. No questions. I’ll SOLVE it. Why the album? What changes between now and then? …Ah. There it is. Right now she was the top solo queen and he was — hot, but hollow: two hit singles deep. The moment SUNYE landed — a full album of his own writing — he’d be the second half’s brightest artist, the producer everyone begged, artist-plus-hitmaker, a standing no longer beneath hers. He didn’t want to be handed UP to. He wanted to arrive level.

Hmph. Male pride. Prehistoric. …So why was something in her chest quietly delighted about it?

Her big eyes fluttered open, studied that increasingly unreasonable face, and she crawled back across the bed and pressed a kiss to his cheek.

“I’m pleased. That was your reward.”

He had no idea what she was pleased ABOUT — but a beautiful woman volunteering was not to be wasted. He caught her mid-retreat and pulled her back into his arms.

“Yah— let go!”

“No touching before you answer MY question. Do I look like an easy woman to you?”

“No,” he said, rolling her under him, studying the immaculate face at close range. “You look like a very beautiful one. Hence the greed.”

Beautiful. HE thought she was beautiful. Her entire mood lifted off like a balloon; the frown dissolved into shameless delight.

“Then… one kiss,” she said, blinking up at him, soft-voiced. “JUST one.”

The more beautiful the woman, the better the lie, he thought — and kissed her anyway.

And discovered, in short order, that the evening’s newest system acquisition had unadvertised applications: fresh, deft, and thoroughly disorienting for the other party — who forgot entirely to police his hands, went rigid, went soft, and ended up staring at the ceiling in a warm daze wondering how “just one” had come to this.
