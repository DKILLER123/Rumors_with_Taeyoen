Curiosity Killed the Deer

Saturday · 00:06 → 01:58 a dormitory elevator · a password door that opens before she can press the buttons · a living room under full lights · and one bed with a tablet on it one alibi borrowed from a sunbae, one court of seven eonnis, one confession she wasn’t supposed to hear, and a curiosity with hooves

The night deepened on its way down to the garage.

Im Yoon-a leaned against the wall of the elevator with her whole spine, and the exquisite little face that a nation had voted on was, for once, simply tired. She let out a sigh that started somewhere under the ribs and arrived with nothing left in it.

Tiffany eonni. Taeyeon eonni. Sica eonni.

Sica eonni couldn’t even stand him. Could she? Everyone in this dorm knows she can’t stand him. Of all nine of us (of the eight, of the seven, however you count the ones still awake) the very last person on this peninsula who would walk into her own sister’s feelings is Jung Soo-yeon.

So how. How does it happen that fast?

And underneath the betrayal of it, in the place where the annoyance had already stopped pretending to be annoyance, a colder piece of her mind was doing arithmetic.

There is something about Song Ji-ho that I don’t know. It isn’t status. Status makes women careful; it doesn’t make them quiet. It isn’t money; money makes women patient, and patience looks entirely different from what Taeyeon eonni’s face looks like these days. Three women that proud, arranged around one man that casually, means there is something in him that a woman cannot refuse. Something specific. What is it?

What does he have, that Taeyeon eonni can tolerate Tiffany eonni, that Tiffany eonni will stay without a name, that Sica eonni, the ice princess herself, would share?

She breathed in slowly, held it, and let it go again. On the fourth round, the tangle in her chest loosened enough to be carried.

At the dormitory door she took out her compact and put her face in order, item by item, like a soldier reassembling a rifle in the dark.

Hair: fine. Clothes: fine. Expression—

She tried the corners of her mouth. A smile arrived, manufactured, and sat there like furniture.

Too stiff. Once more.

Better. Nearly natural. A passerby would buy it; the problem is that the audience inside is not passersby.

She keyed the passcode. Beep, beep, beep.

“She’s back, she’s back.”

“Quick, quick, quick—”

The door was hauled open from the inside before her finger left the keypad.

Choi Soo-young stood in the gap with her arms crossed and a smile wearing far too much meaning for one in the morning.

“Yo. You’re back?”

Something in Yoon-a’s stomach went down a step it did not come back up.

This formation. This formation is wrong.

She angled her shoulders to slip past, and an arm came out and settled across the doorframe like a toll gate.

“Not so fast.” The eyes went down her and up again, itemizing. “First things first. Where?”

“Just… an errand.” The word came out with its shoelaces tied together.

“An errand.” One eyebrow climbed. “What kind of errand requires trying on fourteen outfits?”

…I’m dead.

When she was deposited on the living-room sofa, she understood that her estimate had been optimistic by an order of magnitude. This was not “wrong.” This was a full organizational incident.

Every light in the living room was on, all of them at once, the way a flat is lit for a burglary inspection.

In the center of the main sofa sat Kim Taeyeon, cross-legged, wearing the expression of a judge who has already read the file and is mainly curious how long the defendant will take to confess.

On her left, Tiffany had one knee over the other and did not blink at all. On her right, Kwon Yuri sat with folded arms and a stare that could open a tin. Jung Soo-yeon lay with her head in Yuri’s lap, wearing the open-handed delight of a woman at a tennis match. Sunny leaned on the far wall with a smile she was making no effort to disguise as anything else.

And in the corner (the corner!) the maknae stood folded into the smallest shape a tall girl can make, radiating guilt at her like a heat lamp.

Seo Ju-hyun. You sold me.

Sorry, eonni. Soo-young eonni was standing right there when I opened the door.

The eye conversation got exactly that far before Choi Soo-young pressed her down into the cushions and took the chair opposite the judge.

“Sit properly.”

Then Soo-young sat down beside her, on the escape-route side, and crossed her legs.

“Begin.” Kim Taeyeon spoke at last, in the voice that is neither loud nor soft and gives you no furniture to hold on to. “Where did you go?”

“To meet an investor. For a drama.” Yoon-a laid the words down carefully, one at a time, like cards she had practiced in the elevator. “There’s a script. We talked it through.”

“…Man.”

“A man?” Jung Soo-yeon lifted her head off Yuri’s lap an inch, suddenly vitalized. “Is he handsome?”

“Sica!” Yuri cuffed at her without looking. “Is that the question tonight?”

The ice princess shrank back onto the lap she had come from, unrepentant.

Look at them. Grilling me over a mystery investor, gobbling popcorn by the fistful. If this room had the faintest idea whose private dining room I stood in two hours ago, if Taeyeon eonni knew, if Sica eonni knew, if Tiffany eonni knew, the popcorn would be on the floor and the building would be structurally unsound.

Kim Taeyeon leaned forward, still a court in session.

“Yoon-a-ya. Say it again while you look in my eyes.”

Yoon-a lifted her chin and met them. Taeyeon’s eyes are pretty the way autumn water is pretty, still and clear and much deeper than they let on, and holding them was like standing on a glass floor over exactly that.

“It was an investor, eonni. Truly.”

“Then why fourteen outfits?” Soo-young, from the escape-route side.

“Because — because it was a first meeting. I wanted to leave a good impression.”

“A good impression needs two hours?” Tiffany, from the left.

“Doesn’t everyone do this?” Yoon-a parried, finding her footing at last. “Eonni, how long does your own mirror take before you’ll let a camera see you?”

Tiffany opened her mouth and then closed it, because she is, by her own long-standing certification (renewed annually, on camera), Girls’ Generation’s only girl, and girls of her standing do not answer procedural questions while presiding.

Yoon-a’s eyes went to the corner. The maknae looked at the floor as if it owed her money.

“All right.” Taeyeon recrossed her legs, a court rearranging its robes. “Then tell us the specifics. Which investor? Which production? What script?”

The pressure in Yoon-a’s chest tightened one turn. A person really cannot afford to do anything wrongful; she had been gone five hours, told one lie, and come home to a full bench.

Think. Whatever happens, the name Song Ji-ho does not leave this mouth. If it does, the fight starts here on this carpet, and I haven’t even done anything. Convicted of the affair without receiving any of the benefits. There are no words for how that loses.

“It came through Ye-jin eonni,” she said, levelly, committing to the tallest cover available. “A film script. It’s early; I can’t say much yet.”

“Ye-jin eonni?” Tiffany’s whole face changed channel. On this peninsula, say Ye-jin with no surname and no further wiring, and there is exactly one person it can be. “It was truly Ye-jin sunbae-nim who introduced it?”

“Of course!” Yoon-a turned the smile up to its press-setting. “You all know Ye-jin eonni and I get along well.”

Tiffany looked at her for three full seconds, and said nothing at all, which was worse than anything she could have said.

The face an entire industry describes when it says “elegant.” She took a single-scene cameo in the chairman’s first film and made a premiere audience gasp at the sight of it; she is one of the only names in the country that needs no surname. In this story’s present, she and Im Yoon-a are good friends, the kind whose friendship the future has already scheduled to deepen.

Because here is the thing about Son Ye-jin and Girls’ Generation that every gossip desk in the country knows by heart, and most of them got wrong.

SPORTS SEOUL · GOSSIP DESK · FILED 2011 · NEVER RETRACTED, NEVER PROVEN
Blind item: “Two top actresses, one girl-group idol: the A·B·C triangle”

Filed under letter codes, as the desk’s tradition demands: A, a top actress of the classic school; B, a top actress of the glamorous school; C, a member of the nation’s girl group. The claim, attributed to “an industry source with direct knowledge”: both A and B were, in the same season, pursuing C.

The internet did the rest inside forty-eight hours. A became Son Ye-jin; B became Han Ye-seul; C became Kwon Yuri. The details grew legs and then grew engines: an introduction brokered through intermediaries, a phone number obtained by unconventional means, party invitations, director introductions, a BMW Mini alleged to have changed hands, an overseas flight alleged to have been taken to visit a set. Two actresses, one idol, one triangle; a shape so satisfying that no one involved ever needed to confirm it for it to be true everywhere.

What the record actually shows: the two names at the top of the blind item have never shared a project of any kind (no film, no variety, not one advertising campaign) and have been photographed in the same frame barely at all. No agency ever confirmed a syllable of it. It was a slow gossip week, a desk with letters to fill, and ten thousand readers who each privately knew exactly who A, B and C had to be.

The friendship that is real, as it happens, is the other one.

Later (and this chapter is being told by a narrator who has seen the later) Son Ye-jin will have Im Yoon-a at film premieres on her personal invitation, will walk her through the industry’s back doors one by one, will introduce her to directors at dinner like a niece she is proud of.

Later, Yoon-a will be the one sending coffee trucks to Son Ye-jin’s sets with her own money and her own handwriting on the banners, and the two of them will keep a standing dinner that no manager of either of them has ever successfully cancelled. Later, the word for them will be best friends, and neither will bother correcting it.

Now, tonight, they are exactly what Yoon-a said: close seniors and juniors who get along well. Good friends. The depth is still ahead of them.

And here is the part that quietly holds the whole alibi up: if Son Ye-jin and Kwon Yuri had ever actually been anything, Yoon-a could not have been friends with either of them, because Yoon-a and Yuri are not the kind of close that the word “close” can carry. What those two are to each other has never had a public name, but it has had nine years of evidence, and any secret living inside either of them would have filed paperwork with the other one first.

Everyone in this living room knows that. Which is exactly why the name Ye-jin eonni, of all available names, was the one cover story that could not simply be laughed out of the room.

So the bench half-believed her, which in a dormitory court is a hung jury, and hung juries make these particular judges insane.

He spent the evening telling me, in front of my face, that he intends to have me. Also, Sica eonni, whose lap Yuri is currently guarding, is one of his girlfriends. Eonni — if one day you find out whose table I was sitting at tonight, will you still pat my shoulder like that? Will you still tell me to sleep early?

The living room finally went quiet around one girl who did not move. The big lights were still burning straight down on her, and she sat in them like an exhibit, and did not reach for the switch.

When she finally came into the bedroom, Jung Soo-yeon was propped against the headboard with the tablet on her knees, lit blue, reading something with her whole face.

Ever since Sica started voluntarily studying at night (a sentence that would have gotten a person institutionalized for saying out loud a year ago) she had moved into Yoon-a’s room, on the stated grounds of “the lamp in my room is wrong,” which are the grounds of a woman who wanted a witness.

And she has changed, is the thing. The historically certified Horizontal Sica, whose recorded three joys of life were, in order, fashion magazines, bullying Soo-jung, and sleep, has, for some weeks now, been voluntarily awake, voluntarily upright, voluntarily reading.

If the fandom could see this. If Soo-jung could see this. She’d bully herself to death with envy: eonni studying? Eonni? Studying?

The plan had been to lay it on the table after the world tour, next year, properly, with numbers. Im Yoon-a looked at the girl holding the tablet and revised her, quietly and completely, upward.

This was not a whim. This was preparation with its sleeves rolled up. Sica had decided, actually decided, and the decision had paperwork behind it.

Which left the worry, which arrived on schedule.

But is this road walkable? An idol’s education stops at whatever age the practice room got her. Sixteen years old and the company teaches you exactly one trade. Eonni has folders, but folders don’t formulate a cream; and the moment the brand is real, it collides with the group schedule. Front page of every schedule. That collision is not a folder problem. That collision is a life problem.

“Eonni.” Carefully. “You’re really going to do this?”

Jung Soo-yeon heard every layer under the question; she has never once missed a layer in her life.

And the honest answer, the one she gave herself first, was that without a certain man standing behind the whole thing as the floor under it, she would never have chosen cosmetics at all. She would have started where she is already the expert: fashion, accessories, the sunglasses line she had drawn up first, the things a girl from a girl group can launch with her own eye and her own allowance. Cosmetics was the harder, deeper, more expensive game. She had chosen it because someone had made the losing of it survivable.

She hesitated for exactly one breath, and then chose the honest version out loud.

“Yoon-a-ya. The truth — I’m not building it alone. There’s one other person in it. And he’s the reason I finally decided at all.”

The pieces fell with an audible click, the way they only fall when they have already been arranged.

“It’s Song Ji-ho, isn’t it.”

Jung Soo-yeon’s eyes went wide, genuinely and briefly.

Im Yoon-a can carry a drama that underperforms and still be handed the next script, has carried, has been handed. Soo-young, Yuri, Seo-hyun: does anyone in this room believe they never wanted the same door? They queued. The door is narrow.

I’d have gone upstairs with him that very night. It would have been my first time; I told him so, priced in. Even losing, I told him, I’d give him a month. For nothing.”

Yoon-a inhaled so sharply it hurt.

That’s — those are my words. That is, word for word, position for position, the offer I sat on that man’s knee and made him two hours ago. The body as the opening bid. The career as the term. The exit priced and signed in advance.

Because that is what this industry is, at a certain altitude. The thing you want is sitting on a table, and the table has a price, and the price is capital (fame, talent, family, connections) and when your column comes up short, there is one asset left that the table always accepts. Then it’s just a question of whether you can look at it as a line item.

Jung Soo-yeon, unaware of how precisely she had just described the inside of a private room in Gangnam, went on.

“Offer two. If what he wanted was the person, then three years. Three years of building the company together, and finding out whether what I feel can be grown into something that deserves the word. Every duty of a girlfriend, offered freely, except the bed; that door stays shut until I open it myself.”

She said it without hurry, a woman reading terms she had already notarized.

“And at the end of the three years, the test: if I can accept his other women and mean it, then this is my life and I’ve chosen it, and Jung Soo-yeon doesn’t go back on a chosen thing. If I can’t — then I keep the company, and I repay every won he put in, with interest, and we’re done.”

Yoon-a listened with her jaw slightly apart.

These — these are bandit words. Where did the ice princess get bandit words? Who taught my eonni to negotiate like a mid-sized shipping concern?

“You think I’ve lost my mind.” Jung Soo-yeon read her face with visible enjoyment. “Don’t you?”

“I — I only never imagined—”

“—that these sentences could come out of this face.” She took the words out of the air and handed them back, neat as a receipt. “Yoon-a-ya. Do you know what I genuinely cannot stand? Dithering. Weighing and re-weighing, hovering at the door, going in without committing and coming out without deciding. I despise it in menus. I despise it in men. I refuse to be it.”

The voice went quiet, and quiet in Jung Soo-yeon has always carried further than loud.

“I wanted him. There were other women already around him. So what? Something I decide I want does not become less mine because there’s a queue. And the reverse holds, Yoon-a-ya, and this is the half nobody believes: a man I haven’t chosen could be Jesus returning in a Bentley, and I still wouldn’t look at him twice, let alone keep him on the side. It’s not a price question. It never was. It’s a him question.”

“So —” Yoon-a worked through it slowly, “you made the offer. You went first. It wasn’t his terms.”

“It was mine.” The eyes were perfectly clear, and perfectly lit from somewhere. “He didn’t set conditions for me. I set conditions for him. I put myself up as the stake and I bet it on the man. If I win, I win a man and a company. If I lose, I start over, owing nothing but interest. I bet thirteen years of my youth on Girls’ Generation’s debut and beat odds that made bookkeepers laugh. You want me to fold now, at a table I chose, with a hand I dealt?”

Yoon-a had nothing left to say, and sat there in the lamp light revising the girl upward a second time, no: downward, back to the original. This was, after all, exactly the Jung Soo-yeon she had always known. The trainee who talked back to the company at seventeen. The ice princess who has never once lent a warm word to someone she didn’t rate, and never once pretended. Loves and hates at full size, and never, ever muddy.

After a long while, Yoon-a asked it softly.

“Then, eonni — right now. Are you winning, or losing?”

Jung Soo-yeon lowered her eyes. The line of her mouth went soft at one corner, a change of a millimeter that would have been invisible on any face that laughs more. She picked the tablet back up, found her place in the folder, and resumed reading about trademark classes as if the question had been about the weather.

“It isn’t settled yet.” A pause, a flip of the page. “But I have never lost a bet I made on purpose. And neither, as it happens, has he.”

Her ears, Yoon-a noticed, had gone quietly, thoroughly, absolutely red.

Yoon-a watched the red for a moment, and then, helplessly, started to laugh, and after a second the girl behind the tablet laughed too, silently, at the document, at one in the morning, at all of it.

Later, with the lamp off and the tablet dark on the nightstand, the laughter thinned out into something with more edges.

Sica eonni staked her whole self. Body, name, three years, the exit terms, everything on the table, eyes open, receipt signed. That kind of nerve… I stood in that room tonight and couldn’t even say his name out loud.

And I envy it. That’s the truth under the shock, and I might as well be honest at one in the morning in the dark: I envy the nerve and the clean hands. She gets to be entire about it.
