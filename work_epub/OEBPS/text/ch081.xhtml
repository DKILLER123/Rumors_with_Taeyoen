Chapter Eighty-One

Scatterbrained Jiyeon

“Seafood soup incoming! Quick, quick — make way — this thing is LAVA—”

Ji-ho emerged balancing the great bowl; Sulli hastily cleared the center of the table; he set it down and immediately pinched both earlobes to cool his fingertips — the exact same maneuver Hara had performed twenty minutes earlier.

“Oh… you look SO familiar.” Jiyeon studied the handsome stranger who had materialized in the kitchen doorway, frowning at her own memory. Someone THIS good-looking, I’d remember meeting… where have I—

THERE it is!

“You’re the one from the interview with Sulli — SONG JI-HO! The hero who saved her, right?!”

“Ne, Ji-yeon sunbae-nim. Ji-ho.”

He glanced over as he answered — and discovered that looking away required active effort. Fresh from the bath, in loose pajamas hanging carelessly off one shoulder and knee-shorts, wrapped in a faint drift of rose, Jiyeon was every bit the owner of the “luxury figure” the industry credited her with — and appeared entirely, obliviously unaware of the picture she made.

“What’s with ‘sunbae-nim’? Let’s just talk comfortably.” She smiled and dipped forward slightly, extending a porcelain-fair hand. “Introductions — Jiyeon, born ’93.”

“Ji-ho. ’94.”

They shook. At 168 centimeters to his 182, conversation required a small tilt of her chin upward and of his downward — and the loose neckline, at that geometry, was not doing anyone any favors. She had come to a girlfriend’s house never expecting a man on the premises, and had clearly dressed accordingly — which is to say, minimally.

Does this girl have any idea what she’s wearing right now? Blood pressure. Deep breath. DISCIPLINE.

Ji-yeon released his hand, plopped onto the stool directly beside him, and fixed the seafood spread with naked longing — every available brain cell rerouted to dinner, all other considerations dismissed from the building.

Ji-ho, meanwhile, quietly stood and relocated to the seat beside Sulli. An accidental glimpse was fortune; continuing to look was a character flaw — and as a healthy man with honest self-knowledge, he preferred changing seats to testing his own restraint.

IU raised her glass first.

“To US — first round!”

“GEONBAE~!”

“Woo-hoo~”

IU set her glass down, laid the prepared eel on the grill, and asked the room casually:

“Peach. NOW can you tell us what happened today?”

Sulli leaned into her shoulder.

“Ji-eun-ah… Mom and I are finished. Today…”

She told it all again. And when she finished, Jiyeon shot to her feet and brought her palm down on the table—

“HOW CAN AJUMMA BE THAT— AH— sss— OW!”

Table damage: +1. Ji-yeon damage: +5.

Ji-ho’s expression went briefly strange. …I have DEFINITELY seen this exact scene before. Same table. Different hand.

IU pulled Sulli in close — heartbroken for her, and simultaneously, visibly exasperated WITH her.

“Yah. On your BIRTHDAY I told you — take back control of your own money. You nodded. I thought you actually HEARD me.” Anger and urgency tangled in her voice. “Peach-yah. A woman guards her money before anything else. A woman’s backbone is her bank account — not her face, not her figure. Do you understand me?”

Sulli cried silently and said nothing.

“Ji-ho — swap seats with me.”

Hara rose, wedged herself bodily between him and Sulli, and evicted him to the sofa, gathering Sulli into her arms.

“We brought you here to COMFORT the peach — not to lecture her.”

“What good is comfort? Comfort is what friends do when they can’t actually fix anything.” IU took Sulli’s hand, dead serious now. “Peach. I know an excellent lawyer. Tomorrow I’m introducing you. Your money gets managed by YOU — are you hearing me?”

Sulli squeezed the hand back.

“Thank you, Ji-eun. I’d… already decided to, anyway.”

“THAT’S more like it.” The smile came back, relieved and warm. “Oh — and the next six months? I’m covering you. Call it a loan.”

Sulli didn’t refuse. She lunged instead, hugging IU hard and planting a loud kiss on her cheek.

“Mua~! Ji-eun, you’re the BEST. Thank you.”

“Aigoo — what’s with the formality? You’re my PEACH~”

“Okay, okay — DRINKING.” Hara stood and refilled the table. “Yah. Nobody sleeps sober tonight. We are washing every bad thing out of Sulli’s head~”

“GEONBAE~!”

The table dug in — seafood, soju, laughter slowly reassembling itself. And underneath it ran a quiet consensus nobody needed to say aloud: Ji-ho’s rescue of Sulli had banked him a reservoir of trust, and today’s three-hour dash to Busan had doubled it. Not one woman at this table felt anything but safe drinking into the night with this particular man in the room.

Three rounds became five; a dozen soju bottles and a small forest of beer empties accumulated underfoot. The table held its color — except IU, whose fair face had gone visibly rosy.

“We’re running low. I’ll get more.” She stood. “Though — I’m mostly out of soju and beer. Wine and whiskey okay?”

“ANY alcohol. Bring it ALL.” Ji-yeon, fully in the zone, simply got up and joined her. “Come on — I’ll help carry.”

At the liquor cabinet, Ji-yeon lit up like a festival.

“WAH— Ji-eun-ah, when did you build THIS?”

“Started stocking it the week I moved in.”

“And you didn’t TELL me?” Her fingers trailed along the bottle shoulders. “If I’d known you were sitting on this collection, I’d be here mooching drinks EVERY night.”

“Which is EXACTLY why I didn’t tell you.”

“MWO?!” One beat of shock — then she pounced, fingers finding IU’s waist and tickling without mercy. “You were DEFENDING against me?!”

“AHAHAHA— no— stop— yah, I’m fighting BACK—”

As T-ara’s resident enforcer, Jiyeon feared no counterattack ever born. The two of them dissolved into a shrieking tangle until IU, breathless, surrendered first.

“Okay — OKAY — I yield! Come drink whenever you want!” A gasp. “…But you bring your own anju.”

“NOW we’re talking.” Ji-yeon released her, thoroughly smug.
