Chapter Seventy-Five

Busan

Messages answered, he dialed Sulli.

The call refused to connect. His brow creased. No signal? Airplane mode? Still asleep?

He left a KakaoTalk message and went to dress: white fitted polo, light apricot casual trousers, white canvas shoes — the 182-centimeter frame doing its usual quiet work on the proportions.

Style Index: 75 · First-impression bonus: Sunshine

Ready to leave, he tried her again. Still unreachable. Message: still unread.

9:20. Not up yet? No — that’s wrong. They’d fixed 10 a.m. at Myeong-dong just yesterday afternoon, and no girl on earth skipped the makeup hour before an outing. The unease arrived quietly and set up camp. Sulli… nothing’s happened to you, has it?

A moment’s thought — then he called Victoria.

Outgoing call · Qian-jie

“Hello — Ji-ho?”

“Qian-jie, it’s me. Sorry to call out of nowhere — is Sulli at the dorm?”

“No — she went home last night. Why? Is something wrong?”

“We have a lunch appointment. Two calls won’t connect, and my message is sitting unread. I’m… a little worried.”

“Won’t CONNECT? …Hold on. Let me make some calls.”

Victoria hung up and worked the phone tree: Sulli’s line — dead. Sulli’s mother — no answer. The manager, Amber, Luna, Krystal — nothing; no one had heard from her. The worry jumped hosts. She called back.

Incoming call · Qian-jie

“Ji-ho — her mother isn’t answering either, and none of the members have heard from her.”

“Qian-jie — has her phone ever been unreachable like this before?”

“Artists don’t pick up during work — but the line is never DEAD like this.”

His stomach settled a few degrees colder. An adult out of contact for half a morning was usually nothing — work, errands, sleep. But this was Sulli. And between Sulli and her mother sat a relationship with edges he’d personally seen.

“Qian-jie. Send me her mother’s address.”

“You’re GOING? It’s in Busan — two and a half hours at best! Wait a little — maybe she just went home and overslept. She could be on the flight back to Seoul right now with her phone in airplane mode. You’d cross paths in midair.”

“…Busan. Okay — that’s fair. I’ll wait. But send me the address anyway. Our appointment is ten o’clock. If she’s still unreachable at ten, I’m on the next train.”

“Ten works — any flight back would’ve landed by then. And if she’s STILL dark… something’s genuinely wrong, and I’m coming with you.”

“No — better division of labor: you check the company and anywhere else she might surface in Seoul. Whoever finds her first calls the other. And — don’t alert the company yet.”

“Understood. Sending the address now.”

He headed for Myeong-dong, redialing as he walked. The appointed corner came and went empty. The line stayed dead.

Early Afternoon KTX — Busan Station 300 km/h, and not fast enough

Off the high-speed rail, into a taxi, address read out. He watched the unfamiliar streets slide past with his jaw set. She would never no-show. Never. Something happened. He’d pulled her out of Choi Jae’s path — but the other wound, the family, had never needed a villain from outside.

At the apartment complex he paid, climbed, found the door. Knocked — hard.

Bang! Bang! Bang!

“WHO IS IT— coming, coming!”

An irritated male voice; the lock turned. The door opened on a young man — who looked up at the visitor standing half a head taller, and visibly lost his momentum.

“You… who are you?”

“Is this Sulli’s family home?”

Wariness first — then squinting recognition — then the eyes flew wide.

“…Ji-ho? You’re the Ji-ho — the one who saved my sister!”

He seized Ji-ho’s hand with both of his, then turned and shouted into the apartment:

“Jin-ri! JIN-RI! Ji-ho is here!”

Quick footsteps — and there she was in the doorway, staring at him like an apparition, disbelief and something desperately relieved fighting across her face.

“…Oppa~”

Relief hit him first — alive, whole, standing — and then he registered her eyes: swollen, red-rimmed, cried raw.

“Sulli-yah. What—”

“OPPA!”

She crossed the threshold in two steps and crashed into his chest, arms locking around him, face buried against him — and the sobs came all at once, huge and ragged, weeks of something finally breaking open.

Her brother’s mouth opened, closed, and he stepped back with a defeated look, leaving them the doorway.

“Hey — hey. What happened?” He steadied her, taking in the tear-streaked wreckage of her face.

She only shook her head, hiccuping, refusing the question — and asking her own instead:

“Oppa… how— how did you even… know to come here?”

“We had a date to buy a car, remember? You went dark. I called Qian-jie — she gave me the address. She’s worried sick too, by the way.”

“…Eonni…” A wet sniffle — and she burrowed back into his chest. “Oppa. Take me away from here. Please.”

He didn’t ask anything else. He just patted her back, twice, steady.

“Okay. Pack your things. We’re leaving now.”

She peeled back from him slowly and attached herself to his arm instead, small and certain.

“Whatever oppa says.”

The living room, when they entered, told its own story — disorder everywhere, shards scattered across the floor. The fight had been a bad one. Her things were in the bedroom, but the route passed her mother’s door; she stopped, and looked at her brother instead. He nodded.

“I’ll get them.”

While he was gone, Ji-ho glanced down at her.

“Why has your phone been unreachable all morning?”

“Phone…?” She wiped her eyes, released his arm, thought — then crossed to the corner by the television wall and dug out a handset with a spiderwebbed screen.

Fresh tears welled as she held it up.

“She… she smashed it. Hukhuk—”

Family can be the deepest happiness a person owns — or the deepest wound.

He sighed, and ruffled her hair gently.

“Okay. Enough crying. I’ll buy you a new one today.”

“Mm~”

She leaned her head against his chest, small shaking sniffles slowly settling.

Her brother had barely been gone a minute when raised voices erupted from inside — and then a strikingly beautiful middle-aged woman burst into the living room. Her eyes found Sulli leaning against Ji-ho, and her face transformed.

“WELL. No WONDER you suddenly need to waste money on a CAR — you’re DATING!”

“And you come home and scream at ME — blame ME for spending YOUR money!”

“Your money’s too good for your own mother, but fine for THIS stray man?!”

What a blade of a mouth.

The woman raked Ji-ho up and down with open contempt.

“Look at this shabby thing. I birthed you THIS pretty so you could keep some pretty-faced freeloader?!”

“MOM! What are you SAYING—” The brother rushed out, grabbing her arm. “He’s—”

“I don’t CARE who he is! Sulli — you are an IDOL, have you forgotten?! You break it off IMMEDIATELY. You are NEVER seeing him again!”
