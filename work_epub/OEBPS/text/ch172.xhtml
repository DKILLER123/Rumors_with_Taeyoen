Chapter One Hundred Seventy-Two

The Kiss Scene

March 2013 · mid-shoot The Girl in the Sun set three coffee trucks, one girlfriend, and a scene that only needed one take

A few days into production, the shoot found its cruising speed.

The afternoon sun ran hot, and between setups the crew wore the particular tiredness of people being paid to stand in it. Which was exactly when a coffee truck — balloons on the mirrors, banner across the flank — rolled to a stop on the open ground at the edge of the set.

The banner, in a bouncy typeface beside a photo:

Coffee truck banner

For our dearest dongsaeng, Screenwriter Ji-ho — fighting! The Girl in the Sun, hwaiting! Everyone on set, thank you for your hard work! — From the Yoon-a nuna who will always be proud of you

Beside the truck stood a life-size standee of Yoon-a, smile at full sweetness, fists raised in a cheering pose. The truck staff began laying out drinks and snacks with practiced efficiency, and the smell of espresso went out over the set like a bugle call.

The assistant director confirmed the delivery, then announced it at volume: “Everyone! Girls’ Generation’s Yoon-a has sent a support coffee truck for Ji-ho and the whole Girl in the Sun team! Take a break — drinks and snacks for all!”

“Whoa — from YOON-A?”

“The connections on this man!”

“Girls’ Generation! Best day of the shoot!”

A ripple of cheers ran the set, fatigue dissolving on contact with surprise.

He had just come off a scene and was resting at the edge of it. At the news, he blinked once — then walked to the truck, read the sign-off, looked at the beaming standee, and felt his mouth curve without permission.

So she really did hear it.

That night his words had been sharp — but she’d clearly caught the care packed inside them. This truck wasn’t just support. It was a reply, gracious and unmistakable: message received; recalibrating. He raised his phone, shot the truck and the banner, and sent it with a message: “Coffee truck received. Genuinely surprised — thank you, nuna. The whole crew’s delighted. Dinner’s on me next time.”

The answer arrived at once, with a winking emoji: “Our dongsaeng’s first lead role! Of course eonni-nim supports~ (heart) Fighting on the shoot! I’ll be waiting for the premiere!”

He laughed and pocketed the phone, and his estimate of Yoon-a rose another notch. To take blunt medicine without flinching, and then answer it with this much grace — that was a caliber of poise and EQ you didn’t meet every day.

It was, however, only the opening move.

✦   ✦   ✦

A few mornings later, a second truck arrived — plusher, pink-and-white, its banner in flowing script:

Coffee truck banner · No. 2

Wishing our brilliantly talented composer-and-actor Ji-ho a triumphant film! The Girl in the Sun — smooth shooting, safety, and joy! — Always cheering for you, your Tiffany nuna

Tiffany’s photo shone from the truck’s side amid a weather system of glitter stars and ribbon bows — unmistakably her aesthetic down to the last sequin. The menu ran past standard coffee into her beloved iced Americanos and a lineup of specialty juices; the desserts had gone up a full tier of fancy.

“Girls’ Generation AGAIN!”

“TIFFANY?! My literal goddess!”

“How close IS he with them?!”

The set boiled over a second time, the younger crew hardest hit. He stood in front of this truck radiating Tiffany-brand enthusiasm and felt something in his chest unclench.

A good signal.

It meant the kiss that night hadn’t cost him her — or that whatever anger there’d been had burned off. She was still keeping him somewhere she checked on.

He sent his photo and his thanks: “Nuna — thank you for the support.”

And then: “When you’re back in Seoul, let’s talk. I have a surprise for you.”

Tiffany didn’t reply.

Back-to-back luxury support from members of the top girl group in the country gave the crew a whole new appreciation of their leading man’s social gravity — and the set’s mood, already good, turned buoyant.

✦   ✦   ✦

Three days later, late afternoon, as the day’s shooting wound down, a third truck appeared — quiet, understated, pale gold and white, banner brief and sure:

Coffee truck banner · No. 3

For our Ji-ho — fighting. The Girl in the Sun: may every ounce of effort come back as something beautiful. — Taeyeon

No riot of decoration — just a clean, luminous silhouette of Taeyeon in profile. But the coffee and dessert selection ran deep and immaculately chosen, tuned to the tastes of every department; beside the counter sat baskets of steam eye-masks and snack packs for tired eyes and long nights.

This time the crew’s reaction wasn’t simple excitement. It came with knowing smiles.

“Taeyeon too…”

“That’s the third one, isn’t it?”

“Is he collecting the whole group?? Are they really THAT close?”

“This isn’t PR-friendly, this is friend-friendly. Look at the eye masks.”

Even Kwak Jae-yong wandered over to needle him, grinning. “Ji-ho. This popularity of yours — I suppose that’s what I get for hiring the man who wrote our OST. Girls’ Generation is running a relay for you. That is an obscene amount of face.”

He looked at Taeyeon’s truck and his mouth twitched with suppressed laughter.

He’d have bet the Wealth Robot on it: his actual girlfriend, constitutionally unromantic in the performative ways, found coffee-truck theater fundamentally embarrassing. But Yoon-a had sent one. Tiffany had sent one. Therefore the GIRLFRIEND could not not send one — and hers, obviously, had to be the best of the three. The logic of a proud, secretly competitive little gerbil.

He retreated to the rest room, shut the door, and called her, voice going soft on the first syllable. “Saw the coffee truck. Every detail of it. Thank you, girlfriend-nim.”

Shy pleasure came down the line, badly disguised. “You like it? Shooting’s hard work~ the members and I wanted to send you strength. Yoon-a and Fany both sent one, so obviously I couldn’t fall behind.”

Then, smaller: “But mine’s the best one, right?”

He laughed quietly. Exactly as diagnosed. Awkward in her own particular key — and unbearably cute in it.

“Of course. Yours is the best. I’ll video-call you when I wrap tonight.”

“Mm. I’ll be waiting.”

✦   ✦   ✦

Support at that caliber, three times running, was never going to slip past the media. Within days, “Yoon-a sends coffee truck to Ji-ho’s set,” “Tiffany backs Ji-ho’s film debut,” and “Taeyeon’s thoughtful support” were stacked on the trending charts, fan photos and clips multiplying across every community.

SNS · the coffee-truck relay

@EntertainmentExpress Three separate Girls’ Generation members — Yoon-a, Tiffany, Taeyeon — have each sent a support truck to Ji-ho’s The Girl in the Sun. Not one group truck. THREE INDIVIDUAL TRUCKS. The man’s standing is unreal. The Taeyeon closeness rumors write themselves at this point. Anticipating the film!

@HallyuLoudspeaker Witness the friendship! First lead role and the sunbae nunas run a support relay. Officially the nation’s most-loved dongsaeng. (laughs)

@IsSNSDWorkingToday AAAH my women!! Why are you all cheering for that boy!! (sour) …okay, their friendship is real and he IS talented, forgiven. GRUDGINGLY. Wait — the OST. A Ji-ho song sung by a certain member?? Dreaming about it already.

@CPfanEuphoria (circles Taeyeon’s banner in red) “For OUR Ji-ho.” OUR!!! The phrasing!! The most low-key truck and the most married energy. Homemade-support feeling = insider feeling. JiYeon couple is REAL, I said what I said.

Three national goddesses’ worth of coffee trucks worked on the production like a course of adrenaline — not just the caffeine and sugar, but morale and free publicity in one delivery. The crew joked that working a Ji-ho set came with goddess benefits; veteran actors and rookies alike ribbed him with open envy. And Kwak Jae-yong was quietly delighted: public backing from artists at that altitude, mid-shoot, was pre-release word of mouth money couldn’t buy.

In that warm, watched, well-caffeinated atmosphere, the film rolled forward — and arrived at a scene the entire crew had circled on their schedules:

The leads’ kiss.

✦   ✦   ✦

The set: a small, tasteful lounge inside the hero’s company. Afternoon light strained soft and warm through venetian blinds. Kwak sat command, sculpting it by hand.

“Here — a little warm fill. Yes. That faint pink-gold, like the last of a sunset. Camera two ready on the eyeline exchange — lights, LIGHTS, softer — there. That’s it. Accidental romance. Natural. Beautiful.”

Grips whisper-shuffled tables and chairs by centimeters, cleaning the composition for every angle.

And in the standalone bathroom not far from set, Sulli stood before the mirror conducting classified pre-production of her own.

Mint mouthwash first, thorough enough to sting — the arctic blast through the sinuses actually helped her calm down. Spit, rinse. Then the travel-size breath spray from her bag, peach flavored, two precise pumps, sweetness misting over her tongue.

Finally, with ceremony, the Dior lip glow, #001 pink — the color-reviving balm that read your temperature and pH and bloomed accordingly. She applied it with a jeweler’s focus and pressed her lips together once, softly. In the mirror they came up full, glossed, naturally flushed. Inviting, if she did say so herself.

Then she looked at her own too-bright eyes and pink-dusted cheeks, and her heart resumed its unauthorized sprint.

It wasn’t the first time she’d kiss him. But to kiss him in front of everyone — under multiple 4K cameras — as Go-i kissing Seo Yeon-woo, on the record, sanctioned—

Openly. LEGALLY.

The thought lit her ears on fire; the flush outran the blusher. She pressed the cool backs of her hands to her cheeks, breathed her count, rebuilt the polite professional face, and walked back out with her spine straight and her mission locked.

He was already waiting at the dressed conference table — plain pale shirt, sleeves rolled, unfairly clean-cut in the soft light. He looked up as she approached and gave her a low, steadying smile. “Don’t be nervous. Just like rehearsal. Relax and play it.”

She sat down opposite him, fingertips curling in her lap.

Nervous? No, Oppa. The word is ‘ready.’

“The Girl in the Sun! Scene twenty-two, take one!”

“Action!”

The slate cracked. A bit player in office dress delivered two coffees on a tray. Script pages turned into speech: a work conversation, easy and fluent, the partnership recap.

Behind the monitors, Kwak murmured to his operators. “One — watch the rim light on his profile, touch more… good. Two, standby. After her line — eyes, close.”

In frame, he lifted his cup, drank, set it down, and let his gaze go earnest across the table. “This launch plan — honestly, it was all you. Without your support at the critical moment, we’d never have landed the partnership.” A beat, sincerity deepening. “Really. Thank you.”

He set down the cup and bowed forward, formal, deliberate.

And when he straightened, the air between them had curdled into something stiff — two people with too much history, suddenly strangers of politeness. She looked away toward the window, thumb moving on the cup handle. He cleared his throat and found somewhere else to put his own eyes.

“This feels… so weird.” She finally cracked, hand at the back of her neck, half-laughing, half-helpless.

The laugh escaped him too, and with it, the stiffness — something old and familiar flowing back in. “Yeah. It really does.”

Her smile tipped toward him, eyes going glossy. “But — this might be the first time Oppa’s ever thanked me. Seriously, I mean.”

“That can’t be right. I say thank you all the time.”

“As ‘transaction counterparties.’ Or ‘partners.’” She propped her chin on the back of her hand, performing recollection — but her gaze drifted somewhere real, and a thread of something low slid under the line. “This sincere? This formal?” Her eyes came back to his face and landed. “First time.”

Her lashes lowered; the next line fell half to herself, half to a gone decade. “In middle school… it was zero times.”

The smile faded off him, replaced by apology arriving years late. “I’m sorry. Back then — I was too cold to you.”

She lifted her eyes to him.

Oppa. You know what’s funny? You’re good to me now. Gentle. And sometimes it still feels cold — because what I want is how a man treats a woman. Not how an oppa guards a little sister.

The thought crossed and stung. She shook it off, pressed it down, brought the smile back up — but the eyes above it went deeper, as if looking through Seo Yeon-woo at somebody standing just behind him. “Don’t say that. I — I made it this far because you protected me. Over and over.”

And here the scripted gratitude of Go-i began to fuse with the unscripted gratitude of Sulli, and the emotional charge in her gaze climbed past acting into open ground. “You saved me. You let me—” the line, HER line, doubled its meaning in her mouth, “—be born again.”

Her eyes fixed on him and did not let go.

“Thank you, Oppa.”

That was not a performance.

In the film: the drowning girl, saved, given a human life. Outside it: the idol steered away from Choi Jae, cut loose early from a mother’s bleeding grip. This was Sulli, under cover of a character, paying her truest confession and gratitude to the man who’d pulled her back from the edge and handed her a new beginning.

Behind the monitor, Kwak’s fist closed. LOOK at that. The eyes. The immersion. She’s grown a whole new spine.

In frame, he met that shining, brimming gaze — one startled half-beat — and then Seo Yeon-woo’s gravity and Ji-ho’s genuine, unguarded response fused into a single register. His face went utterly serious, and the words came clear, one at a time. “I’m so glad — that we met again.”

“Even if it was only chance.”

“Just knowing you’re doing well now — I feel lucky. Thank you. For being okay.”

He wasn’t purely acting anymore either. Somewhere in it was the second life itself — Taeyeon, the whole impossible chain of meetings, and this girl whose fate had braided into his.

Sulli-yah. Thank you, too. You’re part of what made this second life mean something warm.

Between them, truth and craft ran together and could not be told apart — and the take turned into something magnetic: salvation, fate, and afternoon light on two faces. Trembling lashes. Sun through blinds. The camera drank all of it.

Kwak nearly cheered out loud.

Per the script, the goodbye came next. He gathered the documents and stood. “I should head back. We’ll sync on the proposal by email.”

She didn’t move.

Her face was changing — the deep water rising past the levees the scene had built: love, refusal to keep suppressing it, and the decision to push through to something further. Her eyes tracked his every movement, a cat flattened in the grass at the end of a long stalk, body coiling, quiet.

He picked up the last folder. Began to turn.

“It wasn’t chance.”

Bright. Absolute. He stopped mid-motion and looked back, questioning—

She came up off the chair in one move, palms planted on the table, body arcing forward, quick and sure as a pounce — and kissed him, precisely, without one millimeter of hesitation, full on the mouth.

Soft lips, peach-sugar and a ghost of Dior florals, pressed to lips cool and unprepared.

Time went taffy.

One second. Two. Three. Four. Five—

Multiple cameras drank the composition from their angles: the girl’s fearless offering; the man’s surprise melting into wonder into a deep, unhidden tenderness. Two beautiful faces, pink-gold light, late-afternoon sun.

She finally drew back — slowly, hands still on the table, leaning close, eyes locked on his at kissing distance. Cheeks flushed crimson; eyes wet-bright, impossibly alive, full to the brim with feeling and satisfaction. His gaze held hers, lit and soft at once.

And then her mouth curved, degree by degree, into a smile so radiant it seemed to burn off every shadow the story had hung over her — the clean bright arc of a rainbow after rain.

“CUT!!!”

Kwak was on his feet. “EXCELLENT!!! Perfect!!! ONE TAKE!!!”

“Both of you — emotion, rhythm, the visual — not one flaw! That take goes STRAIGHT in the film!”

Restrained, spontaneous applause broke across the set.

…One take?

Sulli surfaced slowly from the deep immersion, registered the director’s verdict, exhaled in relief — and then a delayed, catastrophic realization arrived and her eyes went perfectly round.

RUINED. Total loss!!! How did I pass in ONE?! I should have been nervous, missed my mark, blown the expression — NG’d two, three — no, DOZENS of times! Every NG a fully authorized, professionally mandated kiss!!!

She snuck a glance at him — reviewing playback beside the director, face all business — and her internal abacus clattered at full speed.

It’s fine. It’s FINE.

She dropped her gaze to hide the scheming glint, tongue-tip unconsciously grazing lips that still carried a faint electric hum.

The script has two more kiss scenes. Oppa, just you wait — next time I’ll be ‘off form.’ I won’t be able to ‘find the feeling.’ I will NG a dozen times MINIMUM, and recover today’s losses with compound interest.

She squeezed her small fists once, re-hung the sweet, harmless Sulli smile on her face — and underneath it, that hot little ember of anticipation and premeditation caught, and quietly began to burn.
