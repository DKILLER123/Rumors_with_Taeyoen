Chapter Two Hundred Fifty-Eight

The Tangled Little Lamb

LOEN · Studio A after the fight

Ji-eun avoided Ji-ho’s eyes.

Taeyeon did not.

Her eyes were red as she faced him across the studio.

“You still have the nerve to ask what happened?”

Ji-ho remained silent.

“Then I’ll ask the question.” Her voice trembled. “What are you doing with her? And with Fany? How many truths have you let me discover by accident?”

At Tiffany’s name, Ji-eun’s attention sharpened.

Tiffany too?

Ji-ho looked from Taeyeon to Ji-eun.

“Taeyeon-ah—”

“Why aren’t you answering?”

She crossed the room one step at a time. Tears spilled before she reached him. Her fists struck his chest—not hard enough to injure, but with every word she could not make orderly.

“Say something. Stop standing there and say it.”

Ji-ho did not block the blows.

“Yes,” he said at last. “There are other women.”

Taeyeon’s hands stopped.

“Ji-eun is one. Tiffany is one.”

For a moment, Taeyeon simply stared.

Then disbelief became a laugh with no humor in it.

“You’re astonishing. Honest only after there’s nowhere left to hide—and shameless enough to call that honesty.”

“You’re right.” Ji-ho took her hand carefully. “I have no defense that makes this fair.”

His grip tightened just enough to keep her from turning away before he finished.

“But you are still the person I love most.”

The words struck the weakest place in her anger.

A traitorous flicker of relief rose inside Taeyeon—and made her hate herself almost as much as she hated him.

“Let go.”

She tore her hand free and raised the other.

Ji-ho did not move.

The slap never landed.

Taeyeon looked at the face before her and could not force her hand to cross the final distance. The failure hollowed her out.

“I hate you, Song Ji-ho.”

She shoved past him and ran.

The studio fell silent.

Ji-ho took out his phone and called Ryu Dong-hwan.

Outgoing · Ryu Dong-hwan

“Taeyeon just left Studio A. Stop her before she reaches the street. You and two trusted staff members will take her safely to the dormitory.”

“Understood, Hoejang-nim.”

Ji-ho ended the call and drew one slow breath.

Ji-eun retied her disordered hair.

“You aren’t going after her?”

Her voice was quiet.

“She is the woman you love most.”

Ji-ho looked at her.

“That hurt you.”

Ji-eun’s eyes filled again.

“What woman wouldn’t be hurt? I knew I wasn’t first. Hearing you say it while I’m standing beside you is different.”

Her composure finally fractured.

“I trusted you completely. I love you. I’m part of this too.”

Ji-ho’s reply came too sharply.

“Enough.”

Ji-eun went still.

He closed his eyes briefly, then lowered his voice.

“Come here.”

She approached with the uncertain posture of someone expecting another wound.

Ji-ho sat on the sofa and looked up at her.

“You chose this relationship knowing it was complicated. That does not mean you’re forbidden to feel hurt.”

“But hurt does not give either of you permission to humiliate each other or turn my studio into a fight.”

Ji-eun lowered her head.

“I know.”

“And I will not buy your silence with work.”

She looked up quickly.

“The plans we made still stand because you earned them. Finish the album properly. Train your acting. Next year, I intend to build a drama around you if you’re ready.”

Hope slipped through her grief before she could stop it.

“A drama?”

“Music, television, and variety together. You have the range to become Korea’s most broadly recognized female soloist.”

“Will you be the male lead?”

“That is the current plan. But if your acting is not ready, wanting it will not make it good.”

Ji-eun pressed her lips together.

Ji-ho’s tone softened.

“I believe in you. Put the anger into the work instead of using it against another woman.”

“Remember?”

After a pause, she nodded.

“I remember.”

She sat beside him.

“Mianhae. I made everything harder.”

“We all did.”

Ji-eun guided his head onto her lap and began massaging his temples with practiced fingers.

“Rest for a minute. I won’t start another fight.”

Ji-ho closed his eyes.

Ji-eun responded to clear goals and visible progress. She was ambitious enough to turn pain into discipline—if he respected the ambition rather than treating it as a leash.

Taeyeon would be more difficult.

Her contradictions were not calculation. They were the knots of someone who felt everything deeply, resisted saying what she needed, and then suffered because no one guessed correctly.

Chasing her immediately would only reignite the confrontation. Leaving her entirely alone would turn anger into abandonment.

Tonight, she needed safety and distance.

Tomorrow, he would go to her himself.

✦ ✦ ✦
Girls’ Generation dormitory an empty apartment and a very loud pillow

Dong-hwan and two trusted staff members delivered Taeyeon safely to the dormitory entrance.

She barely remembered the drive.

The dormitory was empty. Jessica had become mysterious lately; Sooyoung and Tiffany were working; Sunny was elsewhere; the remaining members had their own schedules.

Taeyeon went directly to her room, shut the door, threw herself onto the bed, and buried her face in the pillow.

Only then did she let herself cry without restraint.

“That liar. That shameless, impossible—”

How could he admit to “many women” so calmly?

How could he say she was the one he loved most, watch her run, and still not chase her?

If she was truly first, why was she alone?

Taeyeon cried until the pillowcase darkened beneath her face.

Then the anger turned inward.

Kim Taeyeon, you are hopeless.

She had raised her hand and failed to strike him.

What was the point of lifting it? If you meant to slap him, you should have done it.

But that face.

Even furious, she had not been able to hurt it.

Taeyeon rolled over, shouted into the pillow, and kicked at the mattress until the pressure in her chest changed shape.

Eventually the crying weakened into occasional breaths.

Her mind replayed the studio.

She had not won the argument.

That realization was almost as offensive as the betrayal.

Ji-eun had gotten under her skin with the one line that mattered: What if distance makes you temporary?

And during the fight, Ji-eun had pulled her cardigan one more time than Taeyeon had pulled the ponytail.

Taeyeon sat up and examined the damage. The knit had wrinkled badly; the neckline had stretched.

“Aish!”

She fell backward again.

Next time, use words. Better words. Colder words. And never wear knitwear to a territorial dispute.

The thought itself was ridiculous.

There should not be a next time.

Then Ji-ho returned to her mind.

Why had he not called?

Taeyeon reached for her phone.

No missed calls. No messages.

She threw it aside.

Fine. Who cares?

Three seconds later, she retrieved it.

The last message from him remained: Sleep early.

Her thumb hovered over the keyboard.

Where are you?

Why didn’t you follow me?

Do you actually love me?

How many women?

Every question looked unbearably vulnerable once written.

She erased them all.

But one sentence continued circling her mind.

You are still the person I love most.

If it was true, she mattered more than Ji-eun. More than Tiffany. More than the unknown women hidden behind the word many.

Relief tried to appear again. Taeyeon crushed it immediately.

One line. One beautiful line, and you’re already weakening. Have some pride.

But he had looked directly at her when he said it.

What if it was true?

What if it was only what he knew she needed to hear?

True?

False?

True?

Her thoughts became unbearable.

Taeyeon opened Tiffany’s chat.

PRIVATE CHAT · TAEYEON & TIFFANY

Fany-ah, when are you coming back?
Tiffany
Still filming. What happened?
Taeyeon
Nothing. I was just asking.

Did you know Ji-ho had someone besides us?

I knew there might be others. Why are you asking now?
Taeyeon

Ji-eun? She too???
Taeyeon
Mm.
Tiffany
Taeyeon-ah, are you all right?
Taeyeon
I’m fine. Finish your schedule.

Taeyeon put the phone down and stared at the ceiling.

She was not fine.

She had fallen in love with a man whose heart seemed built without ordinary walls—and still could not imagine leaving him.

THE FIRST NIGHT SHE BELIEVED HIM

A dim tent beyond the city.

Ji-ho singing an apology instead of defending himself.

A kiss offered gently enough to refuse.

His arms around her afterward, making the dark feel safe.

None of it had felt false.

If those moments were lies, then he was the greatest actor alive.

If they were not lies, then the worse possibility remained: he loved her genuinely and still loved others.

Taeyeon had already known about Tiffany. At the time, she had told herself the wound had boundaries: Tiffany and her, two people with thirteen years between them, sharing one impossible man.

Now there was Ji-eun.

And the word many.

“Liar,” she whispered. “Terrible liar.”

Yet he had never said she was the only one.

She had not asked because she had not wanted the answer.

Could she leave?

Taeyeon imagined days without his calls, his voice, his arms, the music they built together, or the calm that came when he said nuna as if the word belonged only to her.

The thought hurt immediately.

Then accept it?

Acceptance meant Ji-eun. Tiffany. Others. Repeated negotiations over time, affection, and position.

Exhausting.

Also, apparently, dangerous to knitwear.

Taeyeon buried her face in the pillow again.

Fine. I won’t contact him. Let him discover what silence feels like.

She opened Ji-ho’s chat settings and enabled mute.

One minute later, she disabled it.

What if he messages and I don’t see it?

She enabled it again, reasoning that she would check manually.

Five minutes passed.

No message.

Ten minutes.

Nothing.

Half an hour.

Still nothing.

Taeyeon pulled the blanket over her head.

Maybe he doesn’t love me anymore.

Maybe I’m too difficult.

Maybe Ji-eun is easier.

Tears returned.

While crying, she began planning what she would do if Ji-ho appeared tomorrow.

Ignore him?

Demand every name?

Walk into his arms?

No. Absolutely not the last one. That would be humiliating.

Unless he apologized properly.

Unless he looked at her in that way.

Unless he used that gentle voice.

Taeyeon pressed her face deeper into the pillow.

Kim Taeyeon, you’re finished.

She fell asleep eventually—still waiting for the message she had decided not to want.

In her sleep, she murmured:

“Next time… I’m winning the argument…”
