Chapter Seventy-Three

The Rooftop Warning

“Ji-ho?”

A clear, sweet voice behind him. He turned — and brightened.

“Yoon-a nuna! What are you doing up here?”

“Came up for a coffee — and found someone loitering.” Yoon-a strolled to the rail beside him, gaze going out to the distant Han, tone dipping straight into mischief. “Look at you, all leisurely. At YOUR stage, I didn’t dare steal a single lazy minute.”

With her arrival came a drift of fragrance — fresh cherry blossom folded through bergamot brightness and a deep musk floor. Romantic, elegant. Exactly her.

“Is that so?” His smile acquired layers. “Because I heard there’s a certain someone who was famous as SM’s champion latecomer — AND who regularly recruited accomplices for secret snack operations. Would nuna happen to know who that is?”

“Haha. Ha.” The laugh came out at half power. “…No idea.”

Trainee-era Yoon-a had juggled school and training — sprinting to the company in her uniform, eating on the move, missing subway after subway, collecting tardies like stamps. And the in-house snack-smuggling record was, in certain circles, legendary. (Her metabolism had always been forgiving; the instructors, mostly, had been too.)

But the real question was HOW he knew. The clever head tilted; the answer arrived almost instantly. She turned.

“Taeyeon eonni told you.”

“So nuna admits the person I described is her?”

She didn’t answer — because the deduction had already completed itself, and it mattered more than the teasing. Everyone knew Taeyeon’s contrary, bottled-up wiring; things stayed sealed inside her by default. If Ji-ho was hearing her old trainee stories, then eonni had genuinely opened the door to him — past-sharing, the real kind. Two people falling into each other don’t narrate third parties by accident.

Good, she thought, with a small ache of relief. Eonni keeps everything inside until it rots. At least now someone’s been let in. And the someone is… well. Look at him.

She studied the handsome profile a moment, then changed lanes.

“The new song’s supposed to be excellent. You’re quite talented, apparently.”

“Nuna’s heard it?”

“No.” A small headshake. “Just manager-eonni gossip. They say it’s… an ambiguous-feelings love song?”

“You could say that.”

She turned her face back to the skyline. So THAT’S why eonni came home that night glowing through her own skin. She’d already heard it. The one he’s ambiguous WITH is her — which makes it her song. Written for her. A girl at the daydreaming age, career secure, the company’s leash loosening… meeting a person like this. No wonder she couldn’t stand firm.

A quiet moment passed. Then, without turning:

“Be careful.”

He looked at her, questioning. She kept her lovely profile to him, voice low and even.

“Songs are autobiography, most of the time. You’re a trainee of three months — whoever you’re writing about is either a trainee or an artist in this building. The company may already be mapping your relationships. If you want to DEBUT — don’t get caught. If you’d rather pivot to producing… well, for a writer this gifted, the company doesn’t police producers’ love lives.”

A warning, wrapped as advice, given freely. He had, in fact, already run this exact risk model — Seung-wan and Joo-hyun read as ordinary colleagues; TTS’s schedule meant he and Taeyeon almost never crossed paths on campus; Sulli was buried in her comeback. He wasn’t worried. But the heart behind the warning was real, and he honored it accordingly.

“Thank you, nuna. I’ll be careful.”

“Good that you understand.” She smiled at him — and their eyes held a beat.

Cameras had never done Yoon-a justice; the proof only existed in person. The vivid eyes, the porcelain clarity of her, the faint sweetness riding the air — the whole assembly operated at an unfair resolution.

“How does nuna have time to be at the company anyway?” He surfaced and found a topic. “Isn’t the drama schedule brutal?”

“How do YOU know I’m shooting a drama?”

“Taeyeon nuna mentioned it last night — said she’s watching it. ‘Love Rain,’ was it?”

Yoon-a blinked. Eonni SWORE she wasn’t watching it. A soft warmth spread through her chest. Always like this. Iron mouth, cotton heart. What a hopeless, backwards eonni.

“The shoot’s nearly wrapped.” Her smile gentled. “And tomorrow’s my birthday fan meeting — I came in to walk the run sheet with the company.”

“Tomorrow’s your BIRTHDAY?”

Her face flipped instantly to theatrical outrage.

“You didn’t KNOW? Hah. Wounding. Truly wounding.” The full contempt package deployed: curled lip, eyes rolling skyward. “And after I went out of my way to warn you. Tsk tsk tsk.”

(Not knowing was perfectly normal, and she wasn’t remotely angry — this was simply the Yoon-a method: manufacture playful grievance, collapse the distance. Because if this boy really did end up with Taeyeon eonni, he’d be family — brother-in-law, functionally. And a brother-in-law with THIS caliber of pen? Priceless infrastructure. Someday, songs for Girls’ Generation… perhaps a couple of extra lines of part for a certain visual… Investment in futures was, after all, her specialty.)

Except he declined to follow the script.

“Does nuna know MY birthday?”

“Ani. We’ve met a handful of times — how would I know your birthday?”

“Then me not knowing yours is equally normal, no? Where exactly is the problem?”

“…There is, in fact… no problem.”

She raked back her hair, planted a hand on her hip, and discovered she was — for no reason she could articulate — deeply annoyed. She took a step toward him.

He took a step back and bowed, smooth as poured water.

“Nuna, I have a practice session. Bye!”

And he was gone.

“Hah. Unbelievable.”

She laughed at the empty rooftop, bit her lip, and filed the account. Ji-ho. I am REMEMBERING this.

The escapee, meanwhile, was replaying the encounter on the stairs — the off-shoulder ruffled denim top, the fine collarbones, the porcelain skin — and, being a connoisseur of old kung-fu films since his LA video-store childhood, found exactly one wuxia nickname surfacing from the fandom lexicon: the famously svelte young swordsman of legend. He chuckled to himself all the way down. People get names wrong all the time. Nicknames — never.
