Chapter One Hundred Thirty-Two

Thoughts on the MVs

Money in hand meant investment on the table.

Field Notes

The regressor’s investment survey, autumn 2012: the most obscene return in reach was Bitcoin — roughly ninety-fold at the coming peak. But the volatility was violent, and the infrastructure worse: no domestic exchange, no regulatory frame, technical and custodial risk at frontier levels. The wild-west stage — a hundred civilians enter, ninety-seven get skinned. Scale was impossible. Pass, for now.

The second opportunity had a date on it: the November election across the strait of water to the east. The man who would win it would fire his famous “three arrows,” and the yen’s slide would lock into resonance with a soaring stock market — the Nikkei 225 up roughly 80% between November 2012 and May 2013, currency swinging 15–20%, options leverage running to thirty-fold. The famous benchmark: Soros’s team, shorting the yen, clearing a billion dollars in three months.

Accessible from the peninsula, timable to the week, near-certain in direction. Verdict: wait for November — then short the yen, and go long the Nikkei.

With the recording wrapped, he turned to the MV slate.

Industry standard: full-budget MVs for the titles, cheap narrative clips for a few B-sides. Per the company plan, “If You” and “Eyes, Nose, Lips” would get top budgets and a first-rank directing team; “Moonlight,” “Spring Day,” “The First Snow,” and “As It Flows” would get modest story MVs. “If You” was settled: IU, as a favor — same company, guaranteed talk-value, merciful on the budget.

“As for ‘Eyes, Nose, Lips’—” In the meeting room, Ji-ho turned a page of the deck. “I want to invite Taeyeon.”

Park bujang-nim pushed up his glasses, a glint surfacing.

“Taeyeon? Girls’ Generation’s LEADER? The idea’s excellent — but SM…”

“Send the formal invitation first,” Ji-ho said evenly. “Then I’ll go to SM in person. Taeyeon and I have… some private history as colleagues. Direct communication will go smoother.”

“If we land her, the attention doubles by itself. The company supports the attempt — the invitation goes out today.”

Out in the corridor, he sent one short message.

Message · → Taeyeon 🐿

Nuna — the company’s sending a collaboration request to SM. Female lead, my new MV. “Eyes, Nose, Lips.” I want to make this one with you.

The song that had witnessed the two of them cross their line — it carried meaning for both of them. (And, ahem, borrowing one’s own girlfriend’s fandom was simply efficient resource use.) “Spring Day” would go to Kim Ji-won — a face made of spring. Two of the remaining slots he wanted for Wendy and Irene; trainees appearing in MVs was routine, though whether SM would allow the credits was another matter. The last slot could be the company’s problem.

Two days later, SM replied: interested; discuss details. Ji-ho scheduled the visit at once.

Two Days Later SM Entertainment — Main Building seven months, and a different door entirely

The black van eased to a stop before the landmark facade. Ji-ho stepped out and looked up at the building that had once been both longing and pressure — sunlight breaking off the glass curtain wall exactly as blindingly as the first day he’d walked in.

The lobby met him with the old familiar air: sweat and perfume, the faint thud of practice-room beats bleeding down the stairwells. A knot of trainees in company sweats did a collective double-take and fell to whispering.

“That’s Ji-ho sunbae, right? He’s even more handsome in person—”

“Three months of training here, straight into the debut team, terminated, went to LOEN — and EXPLODED.”

“‘Some’ lives in my phone. Three hundred loops minimum—”

“And the morning-glory one! Artsy AND devastating. Exactly my type.”

He crossed to reception without a flicker.

“Hello — I have an appointment with Taeyeon-ssi regarding a collaboration.”

The receptionist recognized him instantly; the professional smile carried one unprofessional gram of curiosity. Waiting, he surveyed the lobby wall — the generations in poster form, H.O.T. and S.E.S. through TVXQ and Girls’ Generation to EXO — and let his eyes rest one extra beat on a group poster where Taeyeon laughed with light in her eyes.

“Ji-ho-ssi — this way, please. Director Lee Soo-man would like to see you first.”

Of course he would.

In the elevator’s mirror wall, the staffer stole glances at the young man beside her: dark-grey tailored suit, plain white shirt, no tie — loose, and somehow more imposing for it. Nothing left of the stubborn-eyed trainee who had sweated through these floors half a year ago.

They passed a practice room; through the glass, a trainee spotted him and lost the beat, and the instructor’s voice cracked across the room:

“FOCUS! That man trained three months and debuted into an inferno — you’ve trained three YEARS and can’t match a rounding error of him!”

Flushed faces; no rebuttals; harder practice. He withdrew his eyes without a ripple. The industry’s oldest law: success is the only testimony that gets heard.

He knocked. The familiar voice: “Come in.”

Lee Soo-man’s office was wide and bright, walls hung with citations and photographs. The man himself sat behind the broad desk, lifted his glasses at the sight of him, and produced a smile with a sigh folded inside it.

“Ji-ho. It’s been a while. …Sit.”

“Lee Soo-man seonsaeng-nim.” A measured bow; a composed seat.

Coffee arrived; the door clicked; two people faced each other across a desk and a season of history.

“Honestly,” the old producer began, not hurrying, “‘Some’ outran a great many forecasts. Including mine.”

“Good luck.”

“Luck?” A short laugh; the cup went down. “I’ve heard the ‘Eyes, Nose, Lips’ demo. Songs like yours are not written by luck.”

A pause. The gaze went complicated.

“Truthfully — when I let you walk, I believed I wouldn’t regret it. A rookie of three months disputing his debut contract? However fine the seedling, it wasn’t worth breaking the company’s rules for. But now…” A sigh. “‘Some.’ The artistry AND the commerce of the morning-glory song. The top-tier endorsement you took alongside Yoon-a. You proved your price, Ji-ho. And this new ballad — another detonation waiting for a date. Your gift is worse than I estimated. Considerably worse.”

Ji-ho let the silence carry it, then answered, level:

“Thank you for saying so. But today I’m mainly here about the MV — to speak with Taeyeon nuna.”

Back to business — no grudge displayed, no triumph either. The maturity of it deepened the old man’s regret visibly: an artist with the talent AND the temperament — he should have been SM’s pride. But rules were rules precisely because they could not bend for the exceptional.

“…You and Taeyeon,” Lee Soo-man said at last, with care. “The relationship.”

“Seonsaeng-nim.” Ji-ho smiled. “Given our history, natural contact draws less attention than avoidance would.”

“True enough. As long as she’s willing, the company won’t interfere.” The businessman’s sharpness resurfaced. “But — since you’re here. Next year’s EXO comeback album. Beyond ‘Growl,’ I’d like you to keep writing for it. At your CURRENT market rate, naturally.”

The wager was already a lost cause on SM’s books — but if EXO ignited, production fees were pocket change, and “title track personally crafted by Ji-ho” was a marketing hook that would bill his own fandom for SM albums.

“Thank you for the offer — I’ll consider it seriously. But right now, my own album has all of me.”

No refusal. No interest. Lee Soo-man read the exact width of the distance and didn’t push.

“Of course. The album comes first. Then — may the collaboration with Taeyeon go well.”

“Thank you.”

Ji-ho rose — then stopped, and bowed once more, deeper.

“Thank you, seonsaeng-nim.”

“…For what?”

“For giving me the solo chance at all.”

“That chance was earned by your own ability — and surrendered by it too.” A small shake of the head. “You owe me no thanks.”

“You were willing to bet on a Korean-American kid with three months of training and no history in this country,” Ji-ho said, and meant it. “That trust is worth thanking. …Also — Wendy and Irene—”

“The company’s already agreed,” Lee Soo-man cut in. “If anything, thank YOU for handing them the opportunity.”

“Then I’ll take my leave. Be well, seonsaeng-nim.”

The door closed on the young man’s straight back, and Lee Soo-man shook his head slowly at the empty room.

“Truly… I let a very big fish swim away.”

Four months ago: one anonymous trainee among hundreds, grinding for a sliver of a chance. Today: a collaborator, walking these corridors with his own catalog under his arm, inviting the building’s top idol into HIS video. The envious trainee glances, the staff’s careful courtesy, the founder’s regret and recruitment — all of it said the same quiet sentence: leaving had been correct.

A staffer led him to the third floor. He pushed open the practice-room door — and Taeyeon was already on the sofa, two cups of warm water set out, white knit and jeans, hair soft on her shoulders, eyes going instantly and unprofessionally bright.

“You came.” — with heroic calm.

“Mm. I came.” He sat across from her and slid the demo and the concept deck gently over. “‘Eyes, Nose, Lips’ — the demo, and the first shooting treatment. Listen first.”

She put in the earbuds. The piano opened — and her eyes moved like weather.

She knew this melody in her bones. The camping night in Jeonju — the apology sung in a tent, the kisses that followed it, eyes, nose, lips — the night she became truly his. And now it would anchor his first album, and he had come here to put HER inside it. How was she supposed to stay composed?

The track ended. The staff had long since withdrawn. She took out the earbuds, stood, turned the lock, drew the curtain — and landed in his arms, on her toes, arms around his neck, kissing him with everything unsaid.

“You’re too good to me. I love you.”

A long while later she subsided against him, arms looped at his waist.

“When do you shoot?”

“Next Monday.” A beat. “The album has two titles. The other one — the company’s arranged IU.”

Her body stiffened one degree; her arms tightened two.

“…Understood.”

A silence. Then, looking up:

“Who’s directing?”

“Director Jang Hang-jun. He’s known for fine-grained emotion.”

She nodded — she’d worked with him; the reputation was earned — and flipped through the schedule. A variety offer sat on that date. Irrelevant. THIS song’s MV belonged to her, and no calendar on earth outranked it.

“I happen to be free that day.”

“Thank you, nuna.”

“Thanking me with just your MOUTH?”

“What should I thank you with, then?”

“Tonight. My place. I’ll be waiting.”

“And will you be having candy tonight?”

“—Yes, yes, FINE — and I’ll bite you to DEATH!”
