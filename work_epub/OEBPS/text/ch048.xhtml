Chapter Forty-Eight

Morning Light, and Youth at Full Volume

“Nuna. Morning.”

Irene wore a pale beige sweetheart-neck knit cardigan over washed-blue jeans today, hair gathered loose at the back of her head, a soft smile sitting easy at the corner of her mouth.

“Morning.”

He turned to Seung-wan — who was staring at him. Openly. Vacantly. Fully buffering.

“Yah, Wendy.” The laugh escaped him. “What’s with the trance?”

She snapped back online, ear-tips going faintly pink — and would sooner have died than admit the casual styling had briefly disconnected her brain stem. Spine straight. Righteousness deployed as cover.

“You’re TEN MINUTES late! Per the rules of the bet — breakfast for me AND eonni. Pay up!”

“When did I agree to any bet?”

“You didn’t REFUSE!” She blinked at him, logic assembling itself with majestic confidence. “It’s like when a girl says ‘no’ but means ‘yes’ — if a guy doesn’t refuse, that’s binding consent. Everyone knows this.”

The jurisprudence left him speechless. He shook his head, laughing.

“Fine. Breakfast’s on me. But—” his gaze slid to Joo-hyun, honestly curious, “—how did nuna end up joining?”

She clasped her hands behind her back and tilted her head, a glint of mischief surfacing.

“Why? Not welcome?”

“Not welcome? Nuna, with you walking next to me, every man on this street is about to hate me on sight. My vanity has never been better fed. I’m just curious, that’s all.”

“I just got back from Maple Country, and YOU’RE basically fresh off the plane from LA,” Seung-wan supplied, sidling up to Joo-hyun with credit-claiming energy, “so I begged eonni to come be our local guide!”

Ji-ho knew Dongdaemun like his own closet — a guide was the last thing he needed. But a beautiful nuna volunteering to spend the day walking beside him? There existed no version of him that refused. He swept an arm toward the street, courtly to the last degree.

“Ladies. After you.”

The three of them turned into the food alley, where the morning air already hung thick with competing delicious arguments — seafood pancakes, fish cake skewers, sundae, tteokbokki, stall after stall streaming steam into the light.

Seung-wan’s “breakfast” turned out to mean grazing the entire alley on foot — one portion of everything interesting, split three ways on toothpicks, a few bites each. With the evaluation days away, all three were guarding their weight like national secrets; tasting rights only.

Coming out the alley’s far end, Ji-ho drifted a few steps ahead. The girls lagged behind — Joo-hyun with a bag of nurungji-cheese potato cakes in hand, golden-crisp and trailing cheese pulls, exactly her weakness for sweet-savory things. She nibbled and her eyes narrowed to contented crescents.

“Eonni — after evaluations, we’re coming back, right? There’s SO much I didn’t dare eat…”

“Deal.” Though she carried her own casualty: the apple-pie shop two stalls back. Her one true love. She’d stared, wavered, weighed the evaluation — and walked on. Adulthood was a scam.

“Eonni~” Seung-wan’s eyes had locked onto the potato-cake bag, tongue touching the corner of her mouth. “Can I try one?”

“Here. Eat.” The bag handed over with doting indulgence.

“Thank you, eonni!”

And then Joo-hyun’s eye fell on the little tub of cheongyang chili rings and dipping sauce the stall-owner had thrown in free — and a small demon woke up somewhere behind her smile. Her voice dropped into coaxing registers.

“Seung-wan-ah… you know these taste even better with the chili rings and the sauce. Much better.”

“Really?!”

The little foodie’s eyes lit; sauce was applied; two chili rings were loaded aboard; the whole construction disappeared in one ambitious bite.

One second later:

“Mmgh— HAAH— HOT! Eonni — HOT—!!”

Her entire face went scarlet on contact. Tongue out, panting, both hands fanning frantically, eyes flooding.

“HAHAHAHA—” Joo-hyun folded at the waist, shoulders shaking.

“EONNI!!” The betrayal registered at last, at volume.

“Well? Better, right?” — wiping actual tears of laughter.

“It IS better— but it’s SO HOT— hss— HAH—” Stamping in place, and still — categorically — refusing to spit out the bite.

Cheongyang chilis ran hot even for spice-hardened locals — and Seung-wan’s poor tongue had just cleared Canadian customs. Face glowing like a ripe tomato, unable to avenge herself on an eonni, she cast her wrathful gaze up the alley… to where Ji-ho strolled ahead, oblivious and unbothered.

“Eonni—” inspiration struck; her voice dropped, “—Ji-ho’s from LA. Tex-Mex, hot sauce on everything — Americans like that can DEFINITELY handle spice, right?”

Joo-hyun caught the shape of the plan instantly, eyes gleaming.

“Quick. Feed him one.”

One shared conspiratorial look, two badly suppressed grins, and they scurried to catch up.

“Ji-ho—” Seung-wan arranged her face into what she sincerely believed was sincerity, “—want to try this potato cake? It’s SO good.”

He stopped and turned, gaze landing immediately on her flaming face.

“Seung-wan. Why is your face that red?”

“N-no reason! Weather’s hot…”

“It is warm, I guess. Almost summer.” No suspicion whatsoever; his hand went out for the cake.

And right on cue, gently:

“You have to have it with the cheongyang rings and the sauce. That’s the proper way.”

His hand paused above the sauce.

“I don’t really do chilis.”

Doesn’t do chilis??

“WAIT—” Seung-wan lunged back in, “—there’s a LOT of cheese in there! Without the heat it’s way too rich! You HAVE to add the spice — that’s just food science!”

He studied her at length, eyes narrowing.

“Seung-wan-ah. You wouldn’t happen to have burned your own mouth off and decided to drag me into the fire with you… would you?”

“How— how could you even—” Her eyes went everywhere except his face, volume decaying fast. “…I’m hot from the WEATHER… chingu-yah… would I lie to you?”

“If the chilis didn’t improve it,” Joo-hyun added, gravely, magnificently straight-faced, “why would the stall-owner give them out free?”

“Right! Exactly! Just TRY it, it’s amazing!”

The harder they sold, the more certain the verdict: fraud, conspiracy, two counts. His eye drifted to the remaining chili rings — and a scheme assembled itself with an almost audible click.

“Eating it plain is boring anyway.” The smile that surfaced had angles in it. “Let’s make it a bet.”

“A bet on what?” Joo-hyun, interest fully hooked.

He peered into the bag, counting.

“About ten rings left — five each. We each eat them on a potato cake. First one to reach for water loses and buys samgyetang for lunch.”

Samgyetang! It’s exactly the season for it!

The girls’ eyes met — and found the same gleaming verdict in each other. He’d said it himself: he didn’t do chilis. This was free soup.

“BET!” In perfect unison.

He paved a potato cake with five chili rings and held it out, eyebrows up.

“Which of you is going first?”

Joo-hyun glanced instinctively at Seung-wan — who deployed an instant, piteous, still-traumatized expression. Soft-hearted as ever, and privately certain of her own iron tolerance — he’s ALREADY lost, honestly — Joo-hyun took the cake herself.

“I’ll go.”

One deep breath, and the fully loaded potato cake went in.

Fire flooded her mouth instantly. She chewed with a perfectly composed face. Swallowed with a perfectly composed face.

“Sss… not spicy.” A pause of exactly one second. “Your turn — hss-hah—”

He looked at her — at the heroic, watering-eyed performance of total composure — and his face collapsed into theatrical despair.

“NOT spicy? Then I’ve already lost, haven’t I?” A great tragic wail: “I SURRENDER! Lunch is on me!”

And he turned and RAN — gone like weather, coat-tails and all.

Irene stood rooted, mouth on fire, brain empty.

Seung-wan needed two full seconds — then detonated.

“PFFT— HAHAHAHA! Eonni! He PLAYED you! He was never going to eat it!!”

It landed. He had never once intended to touch a chili — he’d simply conceded and fled, leaving her holding five rings’ worth of consequences.

“YAH!! SONG JI-HO!!” Cheeks blazing with heat and outrage in equal measure, she took off after him. “STOP RIGHT THERE!!”

“Eonni, wait for me— hahaha—” Seung-wan gave chase on her shorter stride, laughing too hard to run properly.

Down the Dongdaemun morning street they went, one figure ahead and two behind — morning light at the perfect angle, and youth at absolutely full volume.
