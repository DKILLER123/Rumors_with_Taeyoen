July 4, 2013. Cloudless.

Sulli woke first.

She blinked, and spent a full three seconds locating herself — strange ceiling, strange bed — no. Not strange. Mine now. Ours.

She turned her head with great care. He was still under, breath long and even — and asleep, with the effortless command switched off, he looked almost boyish. Across the firm line of his chest, the faint red tracks her own hands had left last night hadn’t fully faded.

Last night arrived back all at once — steam, warmth, and a joy so new it had burned going down. Her face ignited on schedule.

“Awake?”

The voice came out lazy; the arm tightened and reeled her in.

“Mm—”

She answered into his chest, declining all eye contact.

“Want to get up?”

“—No.”

She wrapped his waist and stayed exactly where the warmth was.

His hand slid under the blanket and settled warm at the small of her back. “What are you thinking about?”

“Oppa, I… I feel fine. Warm all over. Not — not like the internet said…” The admission arrived at half volume, pink around the edges.

“Haha.” His hand moved gently. “Sulli-yah. Every body is different.”

It was the Halo, quietly finishing its overnight work. Some facts could wait indefinitely.

She made a contented sound and narrowed her eyes like a cat being groomed — and then tipped her head up, teeth catching her lower lip, gaze climbing to his face.

“Oppa. Call me Peaches again.”

“Peaches. My honey peach~”

The rest of the morning ran two hours off the clock; when he next consulted the wall, eight had become nine forty.

✦   ✦   ✦

“Hungry?”

A head-shake. Then a nod. Past hunger and into static; but the throat was dry.

He rose; she peeked over the blanket’s edge at the retreating line of his back — the fresh scratch-marks vivid — and slammed her eyes shut, mortified by her own archive. Footsteps left, and returned; he sat on the bed’s edge with a glass of warm water.

“Drink.”

Sitting up turned out to be a negotiation her lower back declined to join. He held the glass to her lips and she drank most of it in small bird-sips off his hand.

“Sleep more?”

Head-shake. Nod. Not sleepy. Just — disassembled. Every component filing its objection.

He read the predicament, set the glass down, and lay back down around her. “Then we lie here.”

“What time is it?”

“Almost ten. Anything scheduled today?”

“A radio interview at two… and a theater surprise-visit run for the film.”

“Cancel both.” No hedging in it at all. The LOEN talks had broken out of their stall — SK softening week by week — and the confidence had a floor under it now. “Today you rest.”

“But—”

“No but.” His finger wound a loop of her hair. “The film detonated. You’ve earned one day.”

At the word film, her eyes came back on. “Right — the numbers—”

“Want to see?” He reached for the tablet.

While it woke, she twisted the blanket corner in both fists. Day one belonged to the fans. Day two was the actual verdict.

The screen lit. He refreshed the live tracker.

“Wah—”

It escaped her in a breath.

“The comment sections detonated too.”

He opened the NAVER movie board: rating holding at 9.7.

She read them one by one and her eyes went hot at the rims. No hired praise, no organized malice — just ordinary people, moved in the ordinary, irreplaceable way.

“Everyone… really seems to love it,” she said, very quietly.

“Because you acted it well.” He turned on his side to face her. “Sulli. You made Go Min-i a person. That’s why they feel it.”

She pressed her lips together. He was absolutely flattering her. She also happened to entirely agree with him.

“Oh — and this.” He swiped to another page. “Look.”

Twitter’s trending board: the film’s OST All-Kill tag sitting at number three. The top post, from a music blogger: “‘Love Me Like You Do’ and ‘Shallow’ trading the summit across six charts. A genuine double royal flush. Ji-ho’s production verified — again.”

She laughed out loud at the thread. “Everyone’s so SHOCKED that you can do everything.”

“Naturally.” He arched a brow and installed maximum smugness. “I’m a full-spectrum artist.”

“Narcissist.” She poked his cheek, grinning.

They lay there until her stomach filed a formal complaint, audibly. She clapped both hands over it.

“Hungry?” He sat up, laughing. “I’ll order in. What do you want?”

“Anything~ something simple.”

“Mm — let me go check which building number this is.”

“HAHAHA — mwo-ya, Oppa! You don’t even remember the BUILDING NUMBER?”

The manager had found the place; he’d toured it once and memorized the route, not the address. He retreated with dignity, in a t-shirt and track pants.

She watched the door close behind him and an unfamiliar steadiness settled over her whole chest.

A home. I have a home. And a man who loves me in it.

She worked herself upright against the headboard — still creaky, improving — and found her phone on the nightstand carrying a dozen unread messages: friends, colleagues, congratulations stacked on congratulations. “Sulli-yah! The movie destroyed me!” “Have you SEEN the numbers? Daebak! You’re buying dinner!” “Your acting leveled UP. So proud of you!”

Then the manager called. She hesitated one beat, and answered.

Voice Call · Manager eonni

“Yeoboseyo! Sulli-yah, have you seen the projections?! Daily champion AGAIN today! Hahaha!”

“Eonni, I saw~”

“This film is clearing FIVE MILLION admissions minimum! Sulli-yah, it’s wonderful!”

— a glance at the sheets; a private blush; a smile in the voice — “Yes. It’s wonderful.”

“Right — you haven’t forgotten this afternoon? I’ll come pick you—”

“Eonni. Today — I want to rest.”

— a beat of reflexive objection, dissolved by the numbers, and by a certain name she knew stood somewhere near her artist — “…Okay. Rest properly. I’ll tell the company you’re under the weather.”

“Thank you, eonni.”

“Thank ME for what. Sulli-yah — you know I’ve always stood on your side.”

“I know, eonni. I won’t forget it.”

She hung up and worked through the rest — Ji-yeon, Ji-eun, Ha-ra eonni, Victoria-eomma, Soo-jung — every reply congratulatory, every line of them faintly, deliciously envious. Her mouth stayed curled the entire time she typed.

Then the photo album. She adored taking pictures; there were over a hundred of the two of them now. She went through them one at a time until the door opened.

“I ordered your cream pasta.” A pause for effect. “And bought your mango. And the Häagen-Dazs. Vanilla.”

“WAH, DAEBAK!” Both arms shot up, fully restored. “Oppa. Hug.”

He returned to the bed and they went through the album together — “Oppa, remember this one? Gapado—” — until the food arrived downstairs, and he handled the door the careful way, cash left outside, retrieved after the courier’s footsteps faded.

“Peaches. Up. Food.”

She wobbled to the bedroom doorway hand-on-wall and stopped there, watching him lay out the delivery with his particular neat-freak precision — everything aligned, everything squared.

This exact minute was the whole prize: no stages, no lenses, two ordinary people and one over-organized table of takeout. She had never in her life felt this specific kind of safe.

“What are you staring at?” He looked back. “Eat.”

Cream-mushroom-bacon pasta; a small portion of grilled pork belly; pickled radish. She took one bite and her whole face closed in bliss.

“Delicious.”

They ate quietly, trading small nothings.

“Really nothing at all today?”

“Mm. Home only. Movies, books, sleep — whatever you want.”

She considered. “Then — a movie. Together.”

“Which one?”

“Don’t care. I just want to watch one WITH you.”

He paused on that, then smiled. “Flipped, then.”

“Okay~”

Dishes done together; mango cubed by him; phone scrolled by her. The tracker kept climbing — fifty thousand admissions before eleven a.m.

“Might break three hundred thousand today,” he said, delivering mango.

“Really?” Eyes at full glitter.

“Very possible. Day two after word-of-mouth ignition is usually the peak curve.”

He cast the film to the TV and pressed play. She burrowed into his side, claimed a cube of mango, fixed her eyes on the screen — and somewhere in the second act, her eyelids lost the argument.

By the credits she was fully asleep in his arms — last night’s expenditures and this morning’s collecting their interest. He switched off the TV, resettled her weight, and looked down at the sleeping face for a while.

He checked the tracker one last time.
