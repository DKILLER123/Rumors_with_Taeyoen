Chapter Sixty-Six

Strawberry Gummies

His face was innocence itself, eyes clear as spring water — and Taeyeon believed approximately half of it. She advanced half a step, eyes boring up into his.

“You’re REALLY not calling me short?”

“Really not!”

“Really?”

Her brow itched suddenly; her little finger rose and lightly traced the tail of one perfectly drawn eyebrow.

“Absolutely not!”

His gaze followed the motion — landing on that immaculately groomed pair of willow-leaf brows, precise in shape, soft in arc, clearly the product of serious morning effort. And into his head, unbidden, marched the entire internet folklore about Taeyeon’s eyebrows — the peach-fuzz “mustache” jokes, the whole affectionate catalog of nicknames.

He laughed.

Which was a catastrophe.

Because she had JUST been talked down — just finished privately scolding herself for oversensitivity — and now he was laughing. Worse: laughing while staring directly at her eyebrows.

Taeyeon had lived her whole life with exactly two untouchable subjects: the eyebrows, and the height. And this boy had just gone two for two in a single minute.

“WHY ARE YOU LAUGHING?!”

The slim pale arm swung; he dodged.

“Yah— nuna, have you lost your mind?!”

“You DARE dodge?” Teeth audibly gritted, she gave chase. “Call me short, will you — you are DEAD today—”

The green emergency-exit sign glowed conveniently beside them. He shouldered through the heavy fire door and went up the stairs three at a time, explaining over his shoulder:

“You’ve got it wrong! I was laughing at something ELSE!”

“Something ELSE? WHAT else?!” She barreled through after him, short legs churning up the steps. “Explain yourself CLEARLY!”

He reached the half-landing, opened his mouth — and closed it. If she finds out the ‘something else’ was her eyebrows and the mustache memes, this stairwell becomes my tomb.

The hesitation, from below, read as a full confession. He’s got NOTHING. It WAS about my height!

“Out of excuses?! YAH! Get DOWN here!”

Three floors of pursuit later, the tank ran dry. Years of dieting had left her stamina strictly decorative; she stopped, hands on hips, breathing hard, fine sweat at her temples. Then the eyes rolled — and the voice went silk.

“Okay, okay — I believe you now. Come down first! My legs are sore, I can’t chase anymore.”

He leaned over the railing and looked down at the upturned face — flushed cheeks, heaving breath, a paper doll out of battery.

“Nuna. Do I look that easy to fool?”

Exposed, she stamped in place, hating him with her whole tiny body — rested ten seconds — took a deep breath — and resumed the climb on pure spite.

Two steps up, inspiration struck. Her left foot “slipped” on the stair’s edge.

“Aya—!”

The short cry rang up the stairwell. She curled onto the step, clutching her right ankle, brows knotted, breath hissing in broken little pulls.

Above — the footsteps stopped dead. Then came back down, fast.

“Nuna?! What happened?!”

He was beside her in seconds, half-kneeling, hand closing gently around the slim ankle.

“Did you twist it? How bad is it?”

Face buried deep between her knees, shoulders trembling with what could plausibly pass for pain, she squeezed out a wet, quavering whimper:

“Hurts… can’t move it…”

Full panic now. He reached to lift her—

“Can you stand? Should I take you to a hospital? Or— sit somewhere first, ice it—”

The instant his hand touched her arm, her head snapped up and her fingers clamped his sleeve like a trap springing.

“CAUGHT you!”

“…You PLAYED me?” Two full seconds of processing — then equal parts outrage and helpless laughter.

She tilted her chin up in triumph, victory curling at her lips, free hand already reaching to twist his arm in disciplinary fashion — but he was faster: one turn of the wrist and he’d slipped her grip entirely, caught both her hands instead, and folded them in front of him.

She pulled, hard, backpedaling—

“Yah— let GO—”

He drew her back in — a little too well. She collided with his chest, and both of them went completely still.

Her struggle died mid-motion. Their eyes met, and somebody’s breath — possibly both — stopped.

He could see the fine down on her cheek at this distance; the run-flush spreading from cheekbone to ear-tip, cherry-ripe. Her lips parted around quick, shallow breaths, and the air between them carried warm sweetness — strawberry gummies, rich and clean.

Her lips were thin, pressed together, wet-glossed; the corners tilted faintly upward by birthright, so that even not-smiling she looked three parts sweet. And the eyes — those beautiful phoenix eyes — had gone perfectly round with alarm, his own reflection standing small and clear inside them.

The stairwell went silent as a held breath. Two heartbeats hammered — kung-kung, kung-kung — loud enough to be mistaken for each other.

Her head swam. Instinct said retreat; his grip on her wrists said otherwise.

“You… let go of me…”

It came out as barely a murmur, trembling at the edges.

His voice had dropped half a register, unreasonably clear in the sealed stairwell.

“Answer one question, and I’ll let go.”

“Wh… what question?”

Her gaze fled everywhere except his face. He leaned down — closer. The sweet candy scent tangled with her perfume and refused to disperse. Her face went from warm to scalding.

His eyes traveled, slow and deliberate, down to her lips — pink, full, glossed bright.

“Nuna. Your lips are very pink tonight. What brand is the lip balm?”

“…Mwo?” Her brain blue-screened at the swerve.

“I don’t think ‘Mwo’ is a brand.” The corner of his mouth curved. “Nuna — could you tell me with your own lips?”

Her mind had gone white static — no processing power left to catch the double meaning — and the answer fell out on pure reflex:

“…Okay…”

He released her wrists.

And before her relief finished arriving, those warm hands had settled instead around the soft curve of her waist. Skin registered heat through thin fabric — his palms burning — and the burning went straight through her composure and set fire to everything on the way down.
