Chapter Two Hundred Twenty-Five

Stand Back — Watch How It’s Done

August 1, 2013 · before noon LOEN Entertainment HQ, Seoul · lobby, corridors, and the grand conference room the artist walks back into his company as its owner — and two men forget how to stand up straight

The car pulled away from the SK tower and turned toward LOEN Entertainment.

In the back seat, Cho Young-chul sat in silence for a long stretch — then finally took out his phone and opened the morning’s entertainment feeds.

Overnight aggregation · trending

“Ambiguous” with multiple female artists? · Multi-boat suspicion? · “Anonymous LOEN staffer” cited as source

No photos. No names. No documents. Only the drumbeat of “insider” repeated until it sounded like evidence.

“—Absurd!”

His brows crushed together as he scrolled the lurid write-ups, and at the phrase LOEN INSIDER a nameless heat climbed his neck. Rubbish of this grade — and STILL no clarification posted? What was Park Sung-hoon doing? What was Yoo Jae-ha doing? Letting it ferment?!

The secretary in the front passenger seat turned, offering a tablet, tone careful.

“Sajang-nim. Based on what I’ve been able to establish — this scandal was PLANNED by Vice President Yoo Jae-ha, and executed through PR Director Park Sung-hoon’s channels.”

“MWO?!” His head snapped up, eyes going sharp. “Why?! Have they lost their MINDS?!”

“You’ve been at SK headquarters these past days, Sajang-nim. Inside the company… certain things happened.”

In tight, economical sentences, Lee Jae-gi laid the chain out link by link: Yoo Jae-ha’s PRIVATE OST request, refused; the public corridor collision; the settlement freeze routed through Finance Director Seo Dae-ha; Ji-ho’s raid on the twelfth floor. The car went dead quiet. Cho Young-chul closed his eyes against the seatback, ran the whole sequence twice — and opened them onto a much darker arithmetic.

“You’re telling me — Hoejang-nim accepted the OST commission from SBS itself, and refused ONLY Yoo Jae-ha’s?”

“Yes, Sajang-nim.” A beat. “Looking at it now — the Hoejang-nim was likely never acting on impulse. Choosing THAT moment, THAT method, reads as deliberate. He was… inviting the escalation. Manufacturing his own grounds for a housecleaning.”

Cho Young-chul nodded slowly, face heavy. The boy — the OWNER — had baited the hook, and his own two lieutenants had swallowed it to the gills. And the deeper logic was plain from here: a man doesn’t buy a company to collect dividends. He buys it to WIELD it. Which means HR and Finance — the two levers of actual control — get handed to people whose loyalty is beyond question. Yoo Jae-ha and Seo Dae-ha were HIS left and right arms, the core of the bench he’d built over years. Removing them wasn’t personnel adjustment; it was the amputation of Cho Young-chul’s influence, followed by a full reshuffle of the power structure.

“Sajang-nim. What… do you intend to do?”

He watched the streets run backward past the glass and weighed it honestly. He was fifty-two. Three years from retirement age. A man his age, at his grade, hunting an equivalent seat in THIS industry — near impossible. Founding something of his own — the risk was wrong and the stamina was gone. What he wanted was simple: to finish his run at LOEN, quietly, and collect the retirement he’d earned. Working for SK was working for wages. Working for Ji-ho would be — the same wages. And viewed coldly: SK remained second-largest shareholder, its resources still in reach; the new chairman was twenty and radiating exactly the kind of energy that produces GROWTH; a smooth transition, competently served, might even close his career on a high note. The boy couldn’t run daily operations personally — he’d been at LOEN one year; his bench of trusted managers was one deep at most. A steady hand who knew where everything was buried had real, provable value. For three years, at least.

So: how does one make “keep Cho Young-chul” feel like the obviously correct decision? His eyes narrowed, and the answer arrived with the old, cold fluency of thirty years in rooms where these things are decided. A pledge. Proof of allegiance, paid in advance. Yoo Jae-ha and Seo Dae-ha were his own men — he HAD raised them. But their little war had enraged the new owner, dragged the company toward a mud pit, and now stood squarely between the new chairman and control of his own house. On every axis — public, private — they had stopped being useful arms and started being liabilities. They were young enough; with LOEN executive lines on their résumés, they’d land somewhere. He, meanwhile, needed to demonstrate three things, fast: loyalty, efficiency, and value.

“Jae-gi.” His voice had leveled out entirely. “Send the notice: the new Hoejang-nim arrives at the company in thirty minutes. All executives, director level and above, to the grand conference room immediately. No absences.”

“Yes, Sajang-nim!” The phone was already out.

He lifted the tablet again — onto a photo of Ji-ho and Ji-eun, an old stage collaboration now being recirculated with scandal captions — considered a moment, and added:

“Notify Artist Development. IU attends as well.”

Lee Jae-gi blinked — then understood, and bowed his head to the phone.

“And one more. From Administration, to all staff: the CEO will be accompanying the new chairman on a tour of the building. Professional appearance, all posts manned, zero mistakes.”

“Yes! At once!”

✦ ✦ ✦

LOEN Entertainment. The grand conference room.

Ji-eun arrived puzzled and punctual — and felt the air change the moment she opened the door.

Both flanks of the long table were solid executive faces: Artist Management, Music Production, Digital Distribution, Physical Distribution, Business Partnerships, Legal & Compliance, PR, Human Resources, Administration, the Melon Operations Center, Overseas Business— Vice President Yoo Jae-ha. Executive Director Kim Jun-myeon. Finance Director Seo Dae-ha. Record Division Head Shin Min-hyuk. Production Center chief Kim Si-won. Artist Development office head Lee Ji-yeon— The full senate, packed in and silent. And exactly one artist in the room: her.

She bowed her way down the row of executives, careful and correct.

“IU. Over here.”

Executive Director Kim Jun-myeon lifted a hand toward the empty chair at his side.

“Ne, Director-nim.” She sat neatly, then leaned in, voice low. “Director… is something important happening? Everyone’s… here.”

He gave her one measuring look, then answered just as quietly.

“The sale of LOEN’s controlling stake is complete. CEO Cho is escorting the new chairman to the company right now. They’re minutes out. Wait and see.”

New chairman— Her pulse ticked. Without deciding to, she scanned the room one full circuit. Ji-ho’s seat: empty. A full-senate assembly, the company’s single most valuable artist — and he’s NOT HERE? Not notified in time? Or— Something with too many wings started up in her chest. She folded her hands and made herself sit still.

✦ ✦ ✦

Before the LOEN tower, the car came to rest.

Ji-ho stepped out. Sunlight broke over the black shoulders of the suit; he lifted his head and looked up the face of the building he had entered, one year ago, carrying a trainee’s bag and a stranger’s odds.

From today, it was his. Actually, legally, entirely — HIS. Something enormous and quiet turned over in his chest, like an engine the size of a district catching on the first crank.

Cho Young-chul hustled around the car’s nose and bent into a deep, deliberate bow.

“Hoejang-nim! I apologize! The disorder inside the company — the trouble it has caused you — is my failure of management!”

Ji-ho’s gaze flickered over the bowed, gray-shot head. Message received, in full: the man had picked his side, and picked it fast. He didn’t want to leave. He was offering submission — and partnership.

Ji-ho reached out and steadied the older man’s arm, bringing him upright, and smiled.

“Sajang-nim. I told you — LOEN’s foundations are sound, and that’s YOUR operation.”

“What’s past is past; what matters is how it gets resolved. Going forward, manage your people properly — make sure they understand exactly who owns this company. That will be enough.”

“Ne! Hoejang-nim! Understood — I will NOT disappoint you again!” He snapped straight, face set, and angled his body into the escort position. “Please.”

They walked for the doors side by side — the chairman half a step ahead — with Lee Myung-ha’s legal team and Lee Jae-gi trailing in formation.

The staff had been “manning their posts” in the lobby and main corridors since the notice dropped — which is to say, waiting and watching. And when they saw WHO walked in ahead of their bowing, escorting CEO, the entire ground floor stopped breathing. The young man in front — that’s SONG JI-HO? The company’s own top artist? The one drowning in scandal since midnight? Why is he with the CEO— wasn’t the CEO bringing the new CHAIRMAN— …where’s the chairman? Question marks detonated in a hundred heads at once.

He crossed the marble at an unhurried walk, tall, the black tailoring catching the lobby’s gold light, and the gaze behind the gold-rimmed glasses passed over them level and calm — and person after person felt, inexplicably, a flutter under the sternum and an urge to lower their eyes. BORN ARISTOCRAT spread through the hall like a change in pressure. Curiosity bent, degree by degree, into hush.

“What are you all standing frozen for?!” Lee Jae-gi’s voice cracked across the lobby. “This is the new chairman of LOEN Entertainment — Chairman SONG JI-HO! Greet him properly!”

A thunderclap, delivered indoors.

Ji-ho? CHAIRMAN?! The artist Ji-ho — BOUGHT LOEN? Is our OWNER?! The information hit too fast to process — but Korean spines process faster than Korean brains. Under the CEO’s sweeping glare, under Lee Jae-gi’s bark, and under that silent, enormous pressure rolling off the man himself—

“Hoejang-nim! Annyeonghashimnikka!!”

The greeting went off in overlapping volleys, bodies folding to ninety degrees in wave after wave across the lobby.

He didn’t stop walking. The level gaze passed once over the field of lowered heads, and he nodded, small and exact.

“Thank you for your work.”

No speech. No pause. In Cho Young-chul’s escort, through a corridor of astonishment and awe and bent backs, Ji-ho walked — without hurry, without doubt — toward the conference room where the executives waited. Ahead of him, down every hallway, the call of “Hoejang-nim!” propagated like a current: staff who’d gotten the notice and staff who hadn’t, freezing, gaping, then folding at the waist as the black suit passed.

Ninety degrees of respect, wall to wall, as far as the eye could see.

Walking the familiar corridor, feeling the temperature of every glance convert from confusion to shock to scrambling deference, Ji-ho burned steadily brighter inside. One year ago he’d entered this building as a trainee, bowing first and lowest to everyone, memorizing executives’ faces from below. Months ago he’d been the company’s indispensable asset — and still a line item, still subject to the Yoo Jae-has of the world. Now he walked the same carpet as its OWNER. The rule-writer. The man who would decide, this afternoon, which careers in that conference room continued to exist. The executives he’d once greeted were sitting in there right now, palms sweating, waiting for HIM. The ones who’d played their small dirty games were about to stand judgment. Power, he decided — savoring the voltage of it through every nerve — power was the one satisfaction no woman, no chart, no full house at CGV had ever quite matched. A man could be born, live, and die — but THIS feeling was the receipt that proved he’d been here at all.

✦ ✦ ✦

The conference room could have heard a pin land on carpet.

Phones had been silenced an hour ago. Every back was straight. The waiting had acquired physical weight.

Click.

The handle turned, and the room rose as one body.

Cho Young-chul entered first — face formal, and then, to the visible disorientation of everyone watching, he stepped IMMEDIATELY aside, bent at the waist toward the open door, and swept his arm inward.

“Hoejang-nim. Please.”

Here it comes—

“Annyeonghashimnikka, Hoejang-nim!”

Every person in the room bowed to ninety degrees toward the doorway, in unison — and held it, in the iron grammar of peninsula hierarchy, no one daring to straighten before permission, every crown of every head aimed at the floor.

Unhurried footsteps crossed the threshold.

Ji-ho’s gaze traveled across the dark field of bowed heads — the executives who had run his life by memo for a year — and settled, at the far end of the long table, on one small figure. Ji-eun.

Tak. Tak. Tak.

Leather soles on polished marble, each step landing somewhere in everyone’s chest. The air had stopped circulating. Ji-eun could hear her own pulse climbing. Holding her bow, eyes on the floor, she tracked the gleaming black shoes in her peripheral vision as they came down the room — toward her end of the table.

One step. Two. Three—

The footsteps stopped at her side.

And a low, familiar, WICKEDLY amused voice arrived just beside her ear:

“Heh. Ji-eun-ah. I told you I wasn’t lying.”

!!!!!

Her brain went to white noise. She straightened out of the bow without permission from anyone, head coming up — into a pair of deep eyes folded with laughter. Ji-ho— CHAIRMAN? The two words caromed around her skull and refused to dock. THREE DAYS, he’d said. YOU’LL BE GLAD, he’d said. AFTER TOMORROW YOU’LL UNDERSTAND—

“All right — everyone, enough formality. Up. We’re not strangers here.”

His voice lifted, easy, almost casual.

Release. The room unbent en masse — all but two — and the faces that came up were a gallery: shock, vertigo, disbelief in six shades. Record Division Head Shin Min-hyuk, Production Center chief Kim Si-won, Artist Development’s Lee Ji-yeon — the ones who’d worked with him closest — stood openly stunned, eyes ricocheting between Ji-ho, the CEO, and the two men still folded at the waist. Someone pinched his own thigh, hard, under the table. The pain reported back: REAL. All of it. Real.

Because two men had NOT straightened. Vice President Yoo Jae-ha and Finance Director Seo Dae-ha remained locked in their bows, and the trembling running through both frames was visible from across the room. Sweat slid from their hairlines, down pale temples, and dripped.

“How— how is this possible—”

Ji-eun heard herself whisper it, felt her knees soften, staggered half a step back— and a hand caught her arm, quick and sure, and eased her down into her chair.

“Sit a moment. Breathe.”

She sat, eyes still unfocused, the world quietly rearranging all of its furniture around her.

Cho Young-chul chose the moment and stepped forward, voice pitched for the record:

“Everyone. Mr. Ji-ho has successfully acquired fifty-five percent of LOEN Entertainment from SK Group — and with additionally acquired holdings, now controls sixty percent of total shares, making him largest shareholder and controlling owner. Effective immediately, Mr. Ji-ho assumes the office of Chairman of LOEN Entertainment.”

Not one sound. A room of drowning people, all politely holding still.

Ji-ho’s gaze returned, at leisure, to the two figures still bent double and shaking like sieves. He walked over — no hurry in it at all — and stopped first beside Finance Director Seo Dae-ha.

The nearer the footsteps came, the worse the shaking got. Seo Dae-ha had gamed out a connection between Ji-ho and the buyers — but who in HELL games out the artist BEING the buyer! Finished. Everything, finished. Shibal — Yoo Jae-ha, you’ve buried us BOTH!

“Director Seo.”

A hand landed lightly on his shoulder. The man jolted like a struck wire, snapping upright, face assembling a smile several structural grades worse than weeping, sweat rolling freely.

“H-Hoejang-nim!”

“You seem unwell, Director.” The tone was mild — solicitous, even. “Air conditioning this strong, and still sweating through your collar. No wonder — earlier — even simple sums were coming out wrong.”

“I’m— I’m sorry! Hoejang-nim! Truly, deeply sorry!”

Grammar had left him; apology was the only syntax remaining.

“Sorry? For what?” The hand withdrew. The voice stayed perfectly level, weatherless. “I should thank you, Director. The very same afternoon, my settlement was recalculated, corrected, and paid in full. Remarkable efficiency.”

The featherweight compliment went through Seo Dae-ha like a harpoon. His face achieved a shade of paper not found in nature.

Ji-ho had already turned to the last bowed man in the room.

“Vice President Yoo. Stand up.”

Yoo Jae-ha unbent by joints, like something rusted. His face was a ruin — ash-gray, hair pasted to his forehead with sweat, the eyes behind the gold rims scattered and hopeless.

“I’m told the Vice President came up the hard way,” Ji-ho said, and his tone carried something almost like reflection. “Full scholarship through Seoul National — no family behind you, nothing but ability. Genuinely difficult. Genuinely admirable.”

Yoo Jae-ha’s lips worked. Nothing emerged.

“So here’s what I actually want to know.” The voice didn’t sharpen; somehow that was worse. “Why is it that a man who climbed from the bottom — the moment he reached altitude — swung hardest at everyone still below him?”

The gaze rested, calm, on the sweat-slicked face.

“Is it that the respect you never received when you were poor — you’ve been billing for ever since, with interest, out of everyone weaker than you?”

A full-body flinch. The head dropped further. And no answer came — because every possible answer was a confession, and the man knew it. Defending himself now would only be a second humiliation, self-administered.

Ji-ho didn’t press. He patted the man’s shoulder once — the gentlest gesture in the room, and the heaviest — and walked to the head of the table.

He stood. Turned. Let his gaze cross every face.

Every spine in the room found two extra degrees of straight.

“I’ll skip the ceremony.” His voice was even, unhurried, carrying easily. “Yes — the company has left SK Group. No — nothing negative follows from that. Current operations continue unchanged for now. Personnel decisions and long-term strategy I will settle carefully, in consultation with CEO Cho Young-chul, and announce properly. Not before.”

A pause. The eyes went sharp.

“I’m not a small-hearted man. My goal is to take LOEN further and higher than it has ever stood. And I’ll make one promise in front of everyone: if you have ability, and you create value for this company — what you receive going forward will be MORE than what you received before. Substantially more.”

Short. Total. The stick shown once, the ladder lowered immediately after.

“That’s all. Dismissed.”

“We attend the Chairman’s leave!!”

The second bow was deeper, louder, and very nearly musical in its unison — a room that had spent thirty minutes learning the new anthem.

He nodded, and his gaze slid once more past Ji-eun — still glass-eyed in her chair — and the corner of his mouth lifted, one almost-invisible degree, before the face returned to state neutrality. He turned for the door, Cho Young-chul falling in behind him.

And at the threshold, he stopped. Looked back down the length of the room, directly at her.

“Ji-eun. My office. Now.”
