Chapter One Hundred Thirty-Four

IU: I Won Again

Shooting Days Jeju Island — “If You” MV Set working title: “The Undeveloped Hours”

Performance

THE TREATMENT · “If You” — a ballad of regret and remembrance; the story must hold the line of “what was ignored cannot be recalled.”

Concept: photography and time as one metaphor. He plays a professional photographer — a man who captures everyone else’s beautiful moments and never once truly SEES the person beside him. She plays the lover who waits quietly at the edge of frame, and fades from it.

Autumn night, Jeju. Machine-made rain slanted through the lights in soft gauze. Ji-ho stood below an apartment block, water tracking off his hair, drop by drop onto the camera against his chest — watching a slight white figure under a clear umbrella walk, inch by inch, out of his world. His throat worked. No sound permitted to come out.

“IU-ssi — two more steps, slower — YES, that fading rhythm, exactly that.”

The director’s voice through the radio snapped him back: not a real goodbye. A set.

“Again from one — the parting! Standby — ACTION!”

Performance

Slate down. He drops into the role like a stone into water.

Hands in coat pockets, camera on his chest, eyes locked through the rain on the white coat’s retreating line — slower now, hem lifting in the fan-wind, raindrops jumping and sliding on the clear umbrella: every frame a farewell that never finds its voice.

“Close-up — his HAND!”

The right hand leaves the pocket. Fingers half-curl in the air — hang there — and fall, folding back into the dark of the pocket. One complete, silent surrender.

“PERFECT. That exact state!” — the director’s fist closes on air. At the corner, she stops. Per script: one last look back. Across twenty meters of rain their eyes meet — hers grieving, unwilling — and then she turns and is gone without a second glance.

“CUT!”

The set came back to life — lights re-rigged, powder puffs at a run, warm towels and ginger tea in circulation.

“The emotional register is dead-on,” the director said, clapping his shoulder. “Your acting’s real, Ji-ho-ssi. With you two, we may actually finish early.”

“You direct in complete sentences. It makes it easy.”

“Ha! Let’s work together again sometime.”

“Gladly.”

The day ran hard, and by “that’s a wrap” the sky was full dark — a third of the MV already banked. Then Dong-hwan led in a procession of carrier bags, and the smell of fried chicken physically transformed the set’s morale.

“Late-night supper, courtesy of Ji-ho and IU — chicken, tteokbokki, seafood porridge, hot chocolate!”

A small cheer; folding tables bloomed.

“Everyone genuinely worked hard today,” IU said, out of costume and into a cream knit, voice gone soft. “Lighting spent HOURS layering that rain. Camera carried rigs all day. Thank you.”

Ji-ho pressed a hot cup into the nearest grip’s hands.

“Especially the rain crew — that long under cold water. Hot bath tonight, that’s an order.”

“No no— you two ACTED so well we forgot we were tired! We were watching the monitor like a drama!”

“Right?? Um— Ji-ho-ssi, could I get an autograph?” — a young lighting assistant, eyes shining.

“Of course.”

The set loosened into a warm mess of food and shop-talk. He and IU sat slightly apart, porridge steaming between them.

“This MV,” he said, stirring. “Thank you.”

“Thank me for what? You still owe ME a tour cameo.” She glanced up, a small curve at her mouth.

“…Right.” He HAD promised. “When’s the final show?”

“Last day of the month. Real Fantasy — the encore, Seoul.” A sip of porridge; then, too lightly: “By the way. ‘Eyes, Nose, Lips.’ I hear Taeyeon eonni is the lead.”

His spoon paused a half-beat.

“That’s right.”

“So today, when I played ‘the one who gets overlooked’…” the smile tilted, “…call it drawing on lived experience?”

Beautifully engineered — parked exactly between joke and confession.

“The one solo queen of the girl-group golden age who stands toe-to-toe with the top groups — IU. Who on this peninsula could overlook YOU?”

“Do you really not understand? Or are you—”

She didn’t finish it. She didn’t need to. Since learning how deep his catalog ran, she had grown visibly less patient — more moves, less concealment. He changed lanes, gently.

“Tomorrow’s sweet scenes — want to block them in advance?”

“No need.” She shook her head, and the smile acquired several extra layers. “Other roles I’d worry about. Playing ‘in love with you’? Complete confidence. After all—”

She stood, and left the sentence — and him — sitting there.

After all, not all of the “liking” would be acting. He ate his porridge in silence and ran the projections: she was moving boldly because she believed she and Taeyeon were at the SAME stage — both circling, both ambiguous. If she learned the truth — that he and Taeyeon were months deep and fully mutual — the friendly war could turn genuinely ugly. Feelings wouldn’t hold that line. But interest might. She was ambitious, and she was twenty — and ambition is a blade that cuts the mountain open, right up until it turns in the hand.

Day two moved to a lovingly distressed old apartment — the couple’s “first rented room”: peeling walls done by the art department, simple furniture, an aster on the windowsill about to bloom.

Performance

SCENE · KITCHEN. She stands at the stove in a checked apron, frying eggs, humming something shapeless. Script: he back-hugs her; chin to shoulder; watches her flip the eggs.

“ACTION.” He crosses, arms sliding around her waist, cheek at her neck, breath brushing her ear. One beat of stillness in her shoulders — then she recovers, shaping the egg with the spatula.

“Smells amazing,” he murmurs — not in the script. The director doesn’t call cut.

“Almost done,” she answers, perfectly natural, turning her face until they’re nearly nose to nose. “Oppa — plates.”

OPPA? She is a year older than him. The word short-circuits him for one honest, camera-visible second.

“What are you spacing out for?” Her shoulder bumps him lightly backward. “Plates. Food.”

“Because you’re too pretty,” he returns, easy as breath, releasing her for the cupboard — and behind the monitor the director leans forward, silent: THIS improvised temperature is exactly the commodity.

“CUT! Excellent — one more for the close-up! From here on, both of you: freestyle permitted!”

Performance

SCENE · BEDROOM. She’s in an oversized white men’s shirt over cotton shorts, hair styled damp, cross-legged on the bed toweling it — the freshly-showered domestic hour.

He enters, grins at the picture she makes, crosses to the bed, and tips her over backward into the mattress by the knees — her startled yelp entirely genuine — then props himself above her on both arms.

“Ani— let me DRY it first—” she protests, pushing at him. “Can’t wait,” he says, and goes for her ribs instead — and the two of them dissolve into the scripted play-fight, laughing—

—and then the laughing runs out, and the script does too. His hand is at her waist. Her breath is quick from the tussle. The camera creeps in on two pairs of eyes finding each other, and the air changing state.

(All performance, naturally. There are four cameras, a director, and seven staff packed into this bedroom, staring. Nothing kills ambiguity like an audience with clipboards.)

And then — off-script entirely — she tips her chin up and presses one short, clear, shampoo-scented kiss to his cheek.

He locks up completely. Behind the monitor the AD looks at the director; the director raises a hand: KEEP ROLLING. And an actor who isn’t released has to keep acting. His face finds a smile first; he looks down at her — smug, daring him, lips a fresh lychee-pink. He leans down by degrees, close enough to read the care in her skin — and stops, stranded: the director won’t cut, and he can’t actually—

She solves it. One yank of the duvet over both their heads — and under the sudden cotton dark, she kisses him. Properly.

The thin blanket turns the frame into suggestion — dim shapes, held breath, a hint of motion — everything the camera needs and nothing it can prove. Two seconds. Then she pushes him back, flips the duvet off, and sits up, cheeks pink, expression immaculately professional.

“CUT! …Excellent!”

“IU-ssi — that improvisation was superb,” the director enthused. “The sudden kiss reads as the overlooked partner’s subconscious grab for proof of existence. What made you think of it?”

The tip of her tongue touched her lip, unhurried.

“I just felt the scene wanted a kiss there. The characters are still in the honeymoon at this point — that kind of sudden closeness is instinctive. Possessive. And—” a pause; one pass of her eyes over Ji-ho; back to the director, “—isn’t the very next scene the turn, where the photographer starts disappearing into his work? Leave the SWEETEST kiss here, and the loss afterward cuts twice as deep. Contrast.”

Structured. Character-grounded. Airtight.

“As expected of an actress who drew a rookie-award nomination on her first project.” The director turned. “Ji-ho-ssi? Your startled reaction read very true.”

He was already off the bed, straightening his collar. He looked at her. Perfectly serene.

“It works,” he said. “The emotional bridge is smoother with it.”

“Then it stays! Next setup — Ji-ho-ssi, from here you begin ‘vanishing into your own world.’ Hold that drifting-away feeling!”

The shoot rolled on. And nobody saw, as she walked to her mark with the white shirt lifting in the fan-wind, the fingertip that traced her own lips once, absently — or heard the small clear voice underneath:

Taeyeon gets the other MV. But in THIS one — his first on-screen kiss belongs to me.

She took her place in the lights believing she’d beaten Taeyeon again, and her mood was excellent. By “action,” her face held nothing but the character’s fully-committed honeymoon smile — as if the improvised kiss had only ever served the story, and none of the undertow existed at all.

The rest was straightforward: the photographer chasing work across the city, the missed anniversaries and birthdays, the small domestic wars, the sweetness spent down to zero, the parting — earning for you, losing you by the earning. “If You” wrapped in regret, exactly as designed. IU vanished back into her schedule; Ji-ho rolled into the next production.

The B-side MVs were mercifully simple — one or two days each. “The First Snow,” with Wendy: he drove the sports car, took her along the coast road, walked a beach hand in hand for the cameras, hung a love-lock at N Seoul Tower, and stood with her under machine-made first snow for the closing embrace.

Functionally: a paid, filmed, fully-sanctioned day of playing hooky with the boy she was quietly crushing on. Wendy spent the entire shoot approximately one meter off the ground.
