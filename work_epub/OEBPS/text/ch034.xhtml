Chapter Thirty-Four

The Company Pays Its Debts

The Next Day SM Entertainment — CEO’s Office where futures get priced

“Sajang-nim. Good morning.” The bow hit ninety degrees exactly, voice clear and respectful.

Kim Young-min looked up from the file he’d been studying — Ji-ho’s file — took a moment’s unhurried measure of the young man, then set the papers down and gestured at the chair.

“You’re here. Sit.”

Ji-ho sat — spine straight, posture textbook, deference calibrated to the last degree.

“A month with us now.” The CEO’s smile was cordial, the opener soft. “How do you find it? Settling into life on the Peninsula all right?”

“Sajang-nim, SM’s reputation as the industry’s summit is entirely deserved. This month I’ve learned more than I can properly measure. As for life here — I’ve adapted well, thank you.”

“Good.” A nod — and then the gaze sharpened, pleasantries concluded. “I’ve studied your file carefully. The vocal ability is genuinely exceptional; that is your weapon. But your dancing and camera sense currently sit well below our debut standard. The gap is not small.”

“I understand completely, sajang-nim. I’ll close it — faster than expected.”

Kim Young-min nodded again, and behind the glasses the real ledger turned its pages. Sulli’s fanbase alone makes him a made man with the public. The netizens have canonized him. And a Korean-American hero — bilingual, LA-raised, made for the American market and every English-language headline in between — the affinity and topicality practically monetize themselves.

Add the rest: he now holds the absolute trust of Sulli’s circle — the f(x) members, Goo Hara, Lee Ji-eun. He has Girls’ Generation’s gratitude through Tiffany — the whole group’s, by extension. One offhand compliment from any of those seniors on a variety show is a firehose of free traffic. The affair walked him into every SM artist’s field of view wearing a halo. Nobody refuses friendship with the heroic colleague. The boy is an undeveloped gold mine — and the company has already drafted the perfect debut narrative: the Hero Trainee.

Inconveniently — EXO is mid-launch, so no new boy group for years. And SM has never once built a true male solo from scratch; BoA is the only solo success in the catalog, and Kangta only went solo after H.O.T. dissolved. How to maximize this asset… the directors are still chewing. Meanwhile every rival agency in Seoul has surely noticed him too. Which is why the CEO of SM Entertainment is personally pouring tea for a trainee this morning: the boy must be given reasons to stay.

He let the smile warm by several degrees.

“You handled the incident with exceptional judgment. SM rewards what it values — clearly, publicly. At month’s end, during evaluations, the company will hold a formal commendation ceremony: a Special Courage Award of fifty million won, with full press coverage arranged.”

Special Commendation · Terms
Courage Award₩50,000,000 (~$44,000)
CeremonyMonth-end evaluations · press invited

Ji-ho’s eyes kindled on the spot. Fifty million won — around forty-four thousand dollars. Enough to quit the bar shifts entirely and pour every waking hour into training. Enough to breathe.

“Thank you, sajang-nim — thank the company! I won’t waste this. I promise you that.”

“There’s more.” The CEO looked pleased with the reaction. “You’re aware Girls’ Generation is launching the sub-unit TaeTiSeo — their first album, ‘Twinkle,’ begins MV production shortly. The company has decided to cast you in it.”

Ji-ho’s breath caught. Ordinarily, only trainees on the runway to debut got MV appearances, brand shoots, drama cameos — the pre-launch fame machine. This assignment did double duty and everyone in the room knew it: it anchored Ji-ho to SM, and it hitched TaeTiSeo’s promotion to the hottest positive news story in the country. Two birds; one very well-aimed stone. And TaeTiSeo would go on to be one of the most successful sub-units ever assembled. Starring in their debut MV was a golden ticket by any arithmetic.

He thanked the CEO again, fervently.

“One more thing.” Kim Young-min held up a finger. “Given your vocal gift, the company has arranged an introduction — to a vocal teacher. Jung Soon-won. The One. Understand clearly: we are providing the opportunity. Whether you pass his bar and become his disciple is entirely on you.”

The One?! Ji-ho’s pulse jumped.

“Thank you, sajang-nim! The chance alone is more than enough — I’ll take it gladly.”

Field Notes

The One — Jung Soon-won. Among the most authoritative vocal instructors on the Peninsula; SM’s long-serving in-house music teacher, with over two hundred idols passed through his hands — TVXQ, Girls’ Generation, Super Junior among them.

But taught is not chosen. A disciple is someone The One deems worthy of everything he knows, held back nothing. He has said it publicly: of two hundred–plus students, only three ever made him willing to empty the vault — Kim Jaejoong, Kim Junsu, and Taeyeon.

And Taeyeon — who debuted as Girls’ Generation’s leader and rose on pure vocal power to the summit of female solo acts — is the disciple he prizes most. To be chosen is to inherit not just technique, but the man’s entire web of connections.

Money. Resources. Connections. All three laid on the table in one meeting. Poaching him now would take considerably more than a rival’s business card. Kim Young-min nodded, satisfied with his morning’s work.

“Go find him in the vocal studio. The company’s already spoken with him.”

“Thank you, sajang-nim!” The bow on the way out was even deeper than the one coming in.
