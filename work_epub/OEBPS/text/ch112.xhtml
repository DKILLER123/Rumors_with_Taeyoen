At the sink: cheeks full, skin lit from within, eyes carrying their own light — the whole face clean, even, taut. The Idol Aura’s daily gift: every morning a factory reset into radiance.

Manager Ryu Dong-hwan was already waiting. Ji-ho climbed into the van and accepted the offered breakfast.

“‘Some’ just lost the top spot.”

Ji-ho’s stomach dipped — he grabbed the tablet and scanned fast.

Melon. Mnet. Daum. Every major real-time chart, the same title in the #1 slot:

“Gangnam Style.”

And just like that, the tension drained out of him. Ah. That one. No shame in this loss whatsoever.

Field Notes

The regressor’s dossier on the incoming asteroid: “Gangnam Style” would own not just August but the year — and then the decade’s highlight reel. Two hundred million YouTube views in 66 days; the first video in history to cross one billion; YouTube’s most-played crown held for years on end. Number one on iTunes in 35 countries; the UK singles chart summit — the first Korean artist ever to take it; 9.7 million copies sold worldwide in 2012 alone.

The horse dance became the year’s global signature — imitated by office workers and idols, by Britney Spears, Justin Bieber, and Robin Williams on their own broadcasts, and, immortally, by UN Secretary-General Ban Ki-moon side by side with PSY himself. Before it, K-pop in the West lived inside diaspora fan circles. After it, the mainstream had turned its head — the paved road that BTS and BLACKPINK would one day drive down at speed.

A genuine, once-a-generation global monster. And the one detail the regressor’s archive had failed to store: the release date. Who, after all those years, remembered WHEN the meteor launched — only that it landed. Against this particular final boss, there was exactly one strategy: bow, and step off the tracks.

Starting today, “Gangnam Style” rules August. Nothing to be done — you don’t out-chart a comet.
