Ji-ho had not expected one confession to trigger so many rewards.

Stage Valor would strengthen his command in live performance. Sweet Persuasion was less revolutionary—gentle words and convincing eyes had never been his weakest tools—but even an incremental advantage could matter in the emotional weather surrounding him.

The Large Business Wish Sphere was too valuable to spend without a plan.

Known Under Heaven, however, could transform his international expansion. Ji-ho had grown up in Los Angeles, learned to move between American and Korean social codes, and spent his second life studying how artists crossed borders. The skill turned that hard-earned adaptability into instinct.

Morning Ji-ho’s villa · main bedroom shared warmth, trapped hair, and the intimacy of an ordinary toothbrush

Jiyeon woke because she was warm.

The bed did not have the lonely chill of the dormitory. Heat surrounded her, along with the sense that she had slept deeply enough to polish the world clean.

Then she remembered.

She was curled against Ji-ho with her head on his arm. A section of her hair had become trapped beneath his shoulder.

The realization that she had spent the night beside a man arrived several hours late and turned her face pink.

“You’re awake?”

His breath warmed her ear.

She tried to turn and winced.

“You’re lying on my hair.”

“Mianhae.”

Ji-ho shifted. Jiyeon freed the dark strands and turned to face him.

Morning light made his skin look almost unreal. The authority of Chairman Song was nowhere in sight; only the young man remained.

She stared too long.

Ji-ho lifted her chin lightly.

“What are you looking at?”

Too close.

Jiyeon’s composure failed. She sat up abruptly.

“I need the bathroom.”

She gathered her bag and fled behind the door, breathing hard against it.

After attending to her period and washing her face with cool water, she looked into the mirror.

She expected swollen eyes and exhaustion after the previous night’s crying. Instead, her complexion appeared unusually clear and rested.

“Why am I nervous?” she whispered at her reflection. “It’s only him.”

A gentle knock interrupted her.

“Everything all right?”

“Ne. I’m almost finished.”

When she opened the door, Ji-ho waited in the hall with sleep still softening his expression.

“My turn.”

He passed close enough for his clean scent to catch her again. Jiyeon stood motionless for two seconds, then hurried back to the bedroom before her face betrayed her.

A few minutes later, he emerged with a new toothbrush already prepared for her.

“Yours.”

“Thank you.”

“You don’t need to thank me for toothpaste.”

They brushed their teeth side by side. Their reflections—two people in sleep clothes, sharing a sink—felt more intimate than any luxury gift.

Jiyeon kept sneaking glances. Ji-ho eventually caught her.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

Back in the bedroom, Ji-ho lay down again and lifted one corner of the blanket.

“Come here.”

Jiyeon approached slowly and left a careful space between them.

“Why are you so far away?”

“I’m warm.”

“Are you?”

He drew her gently against him.

Her face settled over his heartbeat. His remained calm; hers refused all discipline.

“Your heart is racing.”

“It is not.”

“It is.”

He tightened his arms and allowed the room to grow quiet.

“Are you still tired?” she asked after a while.

“No.”

“Then why are we still in bed?”

Ji-ho kissed the top of her head.

“Because I want to hold you a little longer.”

Jiyeon went still, then softened completely. Her arm circled his waist.

“You knew I wasn’t feeling well last night, didn’t you?”

“You avoided the ice water at dinner. In Korea, that usually means something.”

She remembered the warm water waiting at the villa and felt affection rise with almost painful force.

“You notice ridiculous things.”

“Useful things.”

Jiyeon lifted herself enough to kiss him first.

Ji-ho answered without hurrying. The kiss remained tender, bounded by the care he had shown from the moment she entered the car.

When they separated, she stayed close, forehead resting against his.

“I meant what I said last night.”

“I know.”

“Good.”

Morning sunlight spread across the bed while they held each other, with no need to prove anything beyond remaining there.
