Chapter Seventy-Two

The Negotiation

On its surface, the speech was self-analysis. Underneath, it stated one fact with total clarity: SM was not his only option — and not the party holding absolute control. He carried a walkaway card in his pocket, and the card was insultingly cheap.

“Are you threatening me?”

The voice had gone hard. Ji-ho rose from his chair — and bowed, deeply and correctly.

“I wouldn’t dare. Seonsaeng-nim — you are someone I deeply respect. You built the trainee system. You gave people like me a door to knock on.”

He straightened, eyes steady.

“I’m not threatening you. Today is only this: a boy carrying a dream, sitting across from the godfather of this industry — telling him what the dream is.”

A pause. Then, unexpectedly, Lee Soo-man smiled — something wry and almost rueful moving through it.

“You negotiate well, Ji-ho-ssi. You’ve seen your own leverage clearly. HOWEVER—” the pivot came smooth and merciless, “—a solo debut is not won with talent and negotiating skill. The market you’d face is savage. A foreign-born male soloist succeeding in this country’s mainstream? Nearly vertical odds. Company investment requires projected returns. Your stage experience, your variety-show instincts, your long-term output stability — all unknowns. And the audience: why should fans embrace a solo rookie who appears from nowhere, instead of a favorite they raised up through a beloved group? Have you thought through ANY of this?”

Every question was a blade laid against a load-bearing joint. But he wasn’t demolishing — he was stress-testing.

“I have.” The answer came fast — clearly not improvised. “Stage experience accumulates. Variety instincts can be trained. But some things — the timbre, the writing, and the hunger for the stage, the need to say something my own way — those are factory-installed. I have the second kind. And I have the confidence to build the first kind.”

He slowed, and let the sincerity through.

“Seonsaeng-nim — I’m not asking for a top-tier resource bombardment out of the gate. Start me with a digital single. Let the work speak. If the market answers, escalate step by step. My own writing cuts the company’s production costs and risk from day one. My American background gives the company a native bridge into the English-language market. The Sulli incident gave me public goodwill here that money can’t buy. Weigh all of it against the obstacles you listed — and I believe the scale tips my way.”

Lee Soo-man listened in silence, one finger tapping the table without seeming to know it.

Clear-eyed past his years. Saw the wall — and arrived carrying both a ladder and a price list. In a building full of obedient trainees, this one is… rare. The talent he had already verified. The temperament, he was verifying now.

“Your singing.” A deliberate change of lane. “I’ve heard your evaluation recording, and practice clips besides. The technique still has rough grain — but the timbre and musicality stand out, and the emotional delivery in ‘A Boy’ was… startling. With The One training you, your vocal already clears the baseline for a solo artist. That much I acknowledge.”

A positive signal — deliberately placed.

“Thank you, seonsaeng-nim.”

Lee Soo-man was quiet for a long moment, gaze drifting to the window, weighing invisible ledgers. The room held its breath around the occasional soft tap of his finger.

Then he looked back.

“Ji-ho-ssi. I’ve heard you. It’s bold — and not without logic. Your talent, the company values highly, and has no intention of losing.” He rose — the universal signal of a closing meeting. “But a solo debut, at SM especially, is not decided in one room. It touches corporate strategy, resource allocation, market risk. I need time — and I need the other directors and production heads.”

He crossed to Ji-ho and — unexpectedly — clapped a hand on his shoulder.

“Keep your focus. Keep sharpening — the voice, the dancing, the writing. All of it.” The tone carried something close to encouragement now. “You’ll have my preliminary answer within days. Until then, this conversation does not leave this room.”

“Yes, seonsaeng-nim. Understood.”

He bowed and withdrew. The door clicked shut behind him, sealing away that invisible pressure, and in the bright corridor he let out a long breath, palms faintly damp. Facing Lee Soo-man without nerves would have been a lie — but everything he could say had been said; everything he could show had been shown.

Now: the waiting.

✦   ✦   ✦

Behind the closed door, Lee Soo-man returned to his seat, picked up the iPad, and pulled the full trainee file — then the demo data on both songs. His finger paused, briefly, over one field: Nationality: USA. He tapped play on “Some,” and that catchy, subtly aching melody filled the room again.

“Solo… American… a prodigy writer…”

The murmur trailed into the music. Behind the lenses, the gaze had gone fathoms deep.

“Perhaps the times really are changing. A new experiment… may not be unthinkable.”

He pressed the intercom.

“Schedule a meeting — day after tomorrow, afternoon. CEO Kim Young-min, Director Yoo Young-jin, and the artist-development team leads. The agenda? The future development path of one particular trainee. We need to talk.”

✦   ✦   ✦

Ji-ho didn’t go back to the practice room. He rode up to the rooftop terrace instead — gray rattan chairs, small potted greens, half-frosted glass railing; the quiet place where trainees and artists came to breathe over a coffee.

He stood at the rail and looked out over Seoul. The weather was cloudless — a perfect match for the inside of his chest. He gazed at the Han River, spread both arms wide to the sky, and let himself feel it, fully, for one unguarded minute.

Debut. It’s finally, actually coming.
