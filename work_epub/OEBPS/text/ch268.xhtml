Chapter Two Hundred Sixty-Eight

Even a Little Tangerine Is Cute

Apgujeong and Cheongdam · Seoul after dark a coveted script, a difficult role, and four months in which Yoon-a intends to prove she can remain only an actress

Seoul had only begun to wake for the night.

Apgujeong’s neon washed the sky in violet and rose. Perfume, alcohol, and smoke from expensive barbecue drifted through the streets. Behind Cheongdam’s glass façades, dinners continued beneath warm light.

A white Volkswagen Scirocco moved quietly through traffic.

Yoon-a drove with both hands steady on the wheel.

YOONA · PRIVATE DINNER OFF SCHEDULE

Top · sage-green satin utility shirt · sleeves rolled to the forearms

Denim · smoke-gray high-waisted jeans · clean tapered line

Shoes · simple white sneakers

Impression · understated enough to deny effort, precise enough to prove it

She had spent half an hour deciding whether to dress carefully.

Too polished, and Ji-ho might think she cared about his attention. Too casual, and she would appear disrespectful toward the script.

In the end, she chose the version of herself that looked effortless only because effort had been concealed well.

Traffic provided the excuse for arriving twelve minutes late.

The car entered a narrow Cheongdam lane and stopped before an understated restaurant. Yoon-a handed the key to the valet and followed a host through a deep garden toward a private hanok room.

She removed her sneakers at the threshold and stepped onto the warm wood floor.

“The person buying dinner is late.”

Yoon-a looked up.

Ji-ho wore a pale checked shirt and straight casual trousers. The styling was simple, clean, and unfairly effective.

That face explained at least half of Taeyeon and Tiffany’s poor judgment.

She placed her bag beside the low table and sat opposite him.

“Mianhae. Traffic.”

Every Seoul resident understood that excuse because it was usually true.

“You haven’t ordered, have you? The sinseollo is excellent here. So is the gujeolpan.”

“Choose what you like. I’m not picky.”

Yoon-a called the server and ordered.

Across the table, Ji-ho studied her with open interest.

Yoon-a’s real-life presence differed from the flattened perfection of photographs. Her features were soft rather than sharply sculpted; expression, proportion, and movement created the impact. Wide or low camera angles could exaggerate small imperfections, but in person the overall balance was immediate.

She finished ordering and caught him looking.

“Could you be less obvious?”

“Are you ill?”

“No. I know I’m beautiful. I’m warning you that staring too long may damage an important producer’s eyesight.”

“Then there is no danger.”

Yoon-a’s smile stiffened.

Ji-ho watched her cheeks warm and smiled.

“You turn the color of a little tangerine when you’re angry.”

“A tangerine?”

“A cute one.”

“Keep talking and I’ll put you in the sinseollo.”

He decided he had teased her enough and placed the script on the table.

Yoon-a’s expression transformed.

“Writer Park’s script?”

She accepted it with both hands.

The title page read My Love from the Star.

Alien. A promise crossing centuries. A vain top actress and a hero who had trained himself not to care.

By the end of the second episode, Yoon-a had forgotten the arriving dishes.

This would succeed.

She felt it with the particular certainty actors sometimes experienced before reason caught up.

This role could be the opportunity she waited years to receive.

Ji-ho waited until she finished.

“Well?”

Yoon-a returned the script reluctantly, eyes following it after it left her hands.

“It’s excellent.”

She reached for the bottle.

“Brother-in-law, allow me.”

Ji-ho covered his glass.

“You drove.”

“The valet can arrange a designated driver.”

“One glass with dinner. No more.”

She nodded. Neither intended to make a career decision while intoxicated.

Once the first dishes had been tasted, Ji-ho set down his chopsticks.

“It is a strong script. You should also know that your current acting cannot carry Cheon Song-yi.”

Yoon-a’s hand tightened around her glass.

“I know.”

The answer was quiet and immediate.

Her debut role in You Are My Destiny had earned praise years earlier. Since then, idol schedules had consumed the time that acting required. Offers still arrived because she was Im Yoon-a—not because directors believed she had mastered the craft.

She possessed enough self-awareness to understand the difference.

“Writer Park trusted me with the project,” Ji-ho said. “LOEN can give you an audition and serious preparation. It cannot hand you the role if you remain unready.”

Yoon-a searched his face.

“Then teach me.”

“I told you the problem before. Did you change it?”

She fell silent.

“Girls’ Generation comes first, so acting receives whatever time survives. You protect the idol image in every scene. You avoid looking foolish, ugly, desperate, or uncontrolled.”

“Cheon Song-yi cannot be played carefully. She has to be ridiculous without becoming shallow, arrogant without becoming hateful, wounded without asking the audience to pity her.”

Each point found a place Yoon-a had already criticized in herself.

“I’ve been an idol for years,” she said. “I know I hold the image too tightly. Wanting to release it and knowing how are different things.”

Her eyes steadied.

“That is why I need someone to teach me—and push when I retreat.”

Ji-ho watched her.

“Between now and late December, you have two tour dates and year-end work.”

“Everything else I can spare goes to acting. Character study, physical comedy, scene work, voice, expression. I’ll do it.”

He smiled slightly.

“You sound determined.”

“Will you teach me?”

Instead of answering, Ji-ho asked:

“Why do you think I offered?”

Yoon-a’s pulse shifted.

She knew attraction existed between the lines. Pretending otherwise would be childish. Turning the role into payment for affection would be worse.

“Because I’m Taeyeon eonni’s member. Because we’re friends. And because you think I might become good enough.”

“All true.”

“Not the entire truth.”

Ji-ho did not deny it.

“Then we establish a boundary. Training earns an audition. The audition decides the role. Dinner earns neither.”

Yoon-a released a breath she had not realized she was holding.

“Agreed.”

“And no reporting Taeyeon’s private life to me.”

She blinked.

“I was prepared to become your most loyal informant.”

“You may inform me of group schedules relevant to production. Nothing else.”

Yoon-a raised three fingers with mock solemnity.

“I swear to protect the drama schedule and betray no unnie.”

“I’ll remember that.”

“Is there a punishment if I fail?”

“Additional scene study.”

“Cruel.”

EvaluationFormal audition after training · writer, director, and production representatives present

No role guarantee. Continued casting depends on Yoon-a’s performance and the production team’s agreement.

Ji-ho spent the rest of dinner breaking down Cheon Song-yi’s contradictions. Yoon-a listened with the concentration she usually reserved for choreography corrections, asking questions whenever an emotional transition felt too convenient.

“Late December,” he said. “When you have time, contact my office. Sessions are scheduled and documented.”

“Your office?”

“Acting lessons.”

Yoon-a’s face warmed at what she had briefly imagined.

“I knew that.”

“Of course.”

By the end of dinner, the atmosphere had loosened. Yoon-a drank only the agreed glass; the restaurant arranged a designated driver for her Scirocco while Ji-ho’s driver waited outside.

The night air carried the first suggestion of autumn.

“I can take my own car,” Yoon-a said.

“The designated driver can follow. Ride with me. We need to confirm tomorrow’s first exercise.”

She entered the back seat beside him.

Neon moved across the windows.

“Do you honestly think I can play her?”

Ji-ho turned toward her.

“I cannot answer that yet.”

Her expression fell slightly.

“Only you can answer it. You can remain a beautiful idol who acts when schedules permit, or risk looking terrible until you become an actor.”

“I can open the room and teach you what I know. You still have to cross it.”

Warmth settled in Yoon-a’s chest.

“Thank you.”

The car continued through Seoul.

Yoon-a knew Ji-ho’s motives were not perfectly clean. She also knew the opportunity was real.

Four months of training did not require surrendering her boundaries. Accepting an audition did not make her anyone’s lover.

But she would have to face Taeyeon with the knowledge that she had accepted private help from the man already complicating the group’s heart.

Tiffany had opened that door first. Taeyeon’s forgiveness—partial, painful, and rooted in years of love—had shown everyone that crossing the line did not necessarily mean losing everything.

That precedent was dangerous.

Yoon-a watched the city pass and tightened her resolve.

Training. Audition. Boundaries.

Four months.

Surely she could manage that.

Surely.
