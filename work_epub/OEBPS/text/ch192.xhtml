Chapter One Hundred Ninety-Two

A Rose in Full Riot

May 21, 2013 LOEN studio → SM practice room → one very small elevator the “Shallow” session, a coffee delivery into the lion’s den, and a conversation in falling metal

Two days of drilling vanished into the calendar. Under his patient instruction, Taeyeon had finally banked the full pronunciation map of “Shallow” — some syllables still smuggled in a Korean accent when she wasn’t watching them, but the song ran end to end now.

May 21, morning. The LOEN studio, console lights on: he and Taeyeon stood at their mics on opposite sides of the glass, piano intro in both sets of headphones.

recording session · “Shallow” · duet — take after take

Her opening line comes through the monitors clear with one deliberate grain of sand in it; his answer rides in underneath, and the two voices climb the pre-chorus together.

Two first-class instruments, two people who share a pillow — by rights it should be easy. It is not easy. He is chasing something past accuracy.

“Stop.” The talkback clicks. “Nuna — ‘shallow,’ the word itself. The feeling’s wrong. Not sadness — DESPERATION. The cat stranded on the flat: it tries the climb, and fails, and tries, and fails.”

She nods behind the glass, breathes once from the floor of herself, and begins again.

And so it went: line by line, section by section. Re-record, adjust the bite of a consonant, the placement of a breath, the temperature of a phrase. Every line sung four and five times; whole passages halted mid-flight to argue an approach. It was the expensive way to record — brutal on the clock and worse on the throat. Three hours in, Taeyeon had emptied three cups of warm water and her voice was starting to close.

“Okay — that’s the morning.” He checked the time: eleven-thirty. “Any more and we damage the instrument.”

She came out of the booth deflated. “Is my English really that bad?”

“Not even slightly.” He ruffled her hair, smiling. “You’re doing beautifully. This song just has a high bar. Tiffany nuna takes the afternoon for the theme — you rest the throat.”

✦   ✦   ✦

He drove her back to SM, detouring for ten iced Americanos in two carriers, and pushed open the practice-room door into a wall of bass.

Eight women mid-choreography — everyday practice wear, denim shorts and bare legs to the horizon — sweat shining at temples and throats, the air a compound of faint perfume and hard work.

The famous thigh armada, at full sail.

The music cut. Heads turned.

“AH! Taeyeon eonni’s back!”

“And Ji-ho!”

Tiffany clapped twice, cheeks pink from the run-through. “Okay — everyone, take five.”

Fans joked that Tiffany was “the only girl in Girls’ Generation,” but on the clock she was the discipline officer — at least until the maknae bloc derailed her, at which point, being unable to defeat them or outrun them, she historically joined them.

“Annyeonghaseyo, nuna-deul.” He raised the carriers. “Hydration.”

“Aigoo — THAT ‘nuna’ I dare not accept.” Choi Soo-young’s eyes did one full mischievous rotation and she arrived at a trot, relieving him of the coffee with both hands. “BROTHER-IN-LAW. I’ll take those. Thank you, brother-in-law~ I’ll enjoy them thoroughly~”

Taeyeon’s face went off like a struck match. Several nights’ absence from the dorm had long since made the relationship the team’s worst-kept secret.

“Brother-in-law. Annyeonghaseyo.”

Seohyun stepped up and delivered a full ninety-degree bow, expression grave as a notary. The others wielded “brother-in-law” as a toy; Seohyun alone appeared to have legally registered him as one — whether from true rigidity or a black belt in deadpan, no one had ever established.

“Nuna—” he half-lifted a hand, “—let’s each keep our own reckoning, please.”

“Obviously we keep our own reckoning. Or did you actually plan on being MY brother-in-law?” Yoon-a lifted a coffee, one eyebrow up. “Yah. Say ‘nuna.’ Let’s hear it.”

He grinned. “Everyone else, separate reckonings. YOU, Yoon-a — you specifically must call me brother-in-law.”

“MWO?!” Her eyes went wide. “Yah, you little—”

“Enough.” Jessica’s hand closed on Yoon-a’s wrist out of nowhere, tone gone formal. “How can you call Ji-ho ‘you little—’? Where are your manners?”

She turned to him and inclined her head, perfectly civil. “Ji-ho. Thank you for the coffee. I’ll enjoy it.”

“Ani, EONNI?!” Yoon-a stared at her, betrayed. “Why are you being POLITE to him? He’s four years younger than me! FIVE younger than you!”

“Shigeuro.” One sideways cut of the eyes. “Quiet. Is the coffee not enough to plug your mouth?”

Yoon-a subsided instantly — outwardly. Inwardly the alarm bells were deafening. What HAPPENED to Sica eonni? Since when is she Ji-ho’s honor guard??

Yuri sipped her coffee, eyes ping-ponging between him and Taeyeon, and produced a smile of frank insinuation. “Taeyeon-ah. These past few days your — complexion. It’s excellent.”

“Eh?”

“That specific — watered-by-love luminosity.” Yuri leaned in, volume dropping to a stage whisper. “Not sleeping much, are we?”

“YAH!” Comprehension and combustion arrived together.

“Aigoo, our Taeyeon’s shy.” Soo-young entered the fray. “But honestly — Ji-ho — no, brother-in-law. You must be GENTLE with our Taeyeon. She’s small. Fragile. Cannot withstand vigorous—”

“SOO-YOUNG-AH!” Taeyeon stamped her foot.

He stood at the edge of it, opening his mouth periodically and finding no safe entry point. These women, once rolling, escalated without a ceiling.

Sunny cradled her cup, smiling like a well-fed hamster. “Though Taeyeon HAS gotten taller lately. Could it be that — certain exercises stimulate growth-hormone secretion? Is that real, Ji-ho?”

One second of total silence. Then the room detonated.

“YAH! LEE SOON-KYU!”

“Call me SUNNY!”

“And I meant STRETCHING! Stretching exercises!” Maximum innocence. “Taeyeon-ah, what were YOU thinking of?”

“You did NOT mean stretching!”

Hyo-yeon was slapping her own thigh, wheezing. “But Taeyeon-ah, seriously — hold on tight. A man like this has QUEUES. That IU, for one — no?”

The air shifted a half-degree at the name. Tiffany, quietly nursing her coffee at the edge of the group, felt her fingers close harder around the cup. Jessica’s glance flicked to her — then away.

“Ji-ho isn’t that kind of man,” Jessica said, evenly. “And — Taeyeon is more than enough.”

Yoon-a stared at her. AGAIN? What is going ON today?

The jokes compounded — from “nights away from the dorm” to “that glow,” from “growth spurts” to “the recommended frequency of certain exercises” — until Taeyeon stood the color of boiled shrimp, hands on hips, deploying the last weapon in the leader’s arsenal.

“ENOUGH! One more word and I — I’ll—”

“You’ll WHAT?” Soo-young leaned in, fearless. “Hit me? Here. Free shot. Come on~”

“I—”

Nothing came. Against these particular eight, her threats had never once completed loading. Yuri and Sunny bracketed her in a two-sided hug — “Taeyeon-ah, don’t be shy~” “Right, right, we’re all adults here~” — while she struggled, outnumbered, and shot him a look across the room that read, in capital letters, SAVE ME.

He cleared his throat. “That — the time. Tiffany nuna and I have the afternoon session.”

He looked at Tiffany. “Nuna. We should go.”

“Right — recording.” She nodded, setting down her cup. “Taeyeon, rest your throat properly.”

“Ah — leaving ALREADY?” Soo-young looked genuinely bereaved. “I had so much more gossip queued.”

“Whatever gossip you want, you can ask me directly.” He smiled, mild as milk. “Though personally I think — Jung Kyung—”

“YAH! I’ve lost interest in gossip!” Soo-young’s face executed a full weather change. “Let’s keep practicing! Aigoo, we’ve rested FAR too long!”

He laughed, crossed to Taeyeon, set an arm lightly around her shoulders, and dropped his voice to her ear alone. “Pick you up tonight?”

“Mm—” She nodded, still pink.

“Then I’m off. Nuna-deul — good practice.”

He collected Tiffany and made an orderly withdrawal. The door swung shut on the laughter.

✦   ✦   ✦

The corridor went quiet around them.

“Just now—” he let out a long breath, “—the nunas are terrifying.”

“They just love to tease. There’s no malice in it.”

They walked a few paces. The silence acquired texture. Then, without preamble:

“Ji-ho. You and Taeyeon — it’s real, isn’t it? You’re serious about her.”

His step checked. He turned. She wasn’t looking at him; her voice stayed low.

“I’m not interfering. It’s only — Taeyeon is my best friend. I won’t watch her get hurt.”

“I won’t let her be hurt.”

Flat. Certain.

“Then—” She raised her head; something layered and bright moved through her eyes. “—never mind. It’s nothing.”

The silence came back, heavier.

He looked at her — at the sadness she was holding just behind the smile — and after a long moment began, quietly: “Tiffany nuna. What we—”

“Don’t.” She suddenly didn’t want the rest of the sentence to exist. “Let’s go.”

She lengthened her stride toward the elevators. He followed, watching her back, and understood with total clarity: something was slipping its leash. A song, once begun, resists stopping mid-phrase.

And he was conducting several orchestras at once — each playing a different score. His one job was keeping the melodies from bleeding into each other and turning to noise.

The elevator opened. She stepped in and pressed for the lobby. He stood beside her. The car began its descent, humming faintly in its shaft.

In the small sealed space, her perfume unfolded — not fresh florals but something riper and more deliberate: tuberose over amber and sandalwood, warmth with a sweet, adult edge to it.

His gaze moved over her — unhurried, unhidden.

Today: a loose white cotton shirt, the top two buttons undone over fine collarbones and a modest triangle of fair skin; the hem tucked casually into slim light-blue jeans. She knew her own architecture cold. Broad-enough shoulders that could carry fabric; a shortish waist she’d learned to blur rather than fight; a leg line most of the industry would sign contracts for; long arms, fine wrists. So: the open V-neck lifting the eye to the neck and shoulders, the relaxed cut erasing the waist question entirely, the skinny denim putting the leg line on the record, and nude strap heels running the whole proportion up another honest ten centimeters. She stood there like a page out of an American editorial — indolent, warm, faintly Californian.

“What are you looking at?” Her voice landed clear in the metal quiet.

He didn’t bother retreating. “Nuna’s styling today is very good.”

“Is it?” She turned, set her back against the rail, tilted her head. “Better than Taeyeon’s?”

A question with a blade in it.

He met it straight. “Different genres. Taeyeon is — fine porcelain. Exquisite, breakable, to be handled with both hands. Nuna is — a rose in full riot. Self-illuminating.”

“And Sulli? The one who shot the whole film with you.” Her eyes didn’t blink. “What’s Sulli?”

“Sulli is—” a beat, “—a peach at dawn, still carrying the dew. One bite and the sweetness goes straight through you.”

“Quite the sommelier.” She laughed, low. “Then me. Besides the rose — anything else?”

Ding. The doors opened on the lobby.

He didn’t answer. He made a small after-you gesture instead.

Her heels struck the marble in clean rhythm. They crossed toward the parking structure side by side.

“Nuna.” His voice finally came, pitched low. “You’re a coffee with whiskey in it. The first sip is bitter. The second is fragrant. The third—” a pause, “—is a habit you don’t quit.”

Her step faltered — one beat.

She turned. Afternoon light fell through the structure’s skylight and cut her face into bright and shadow. Her eyes in that light ran a strange color — not plain brown but shot through with amber gold.

“Ji-ho,” she said, very calm. “You’re dangerous.”

“I know.”

“You’re going to break a lot of hearts.”

“I know that too.”

“Then why do you keep doing it?”

He stopped walking and gave her the answer with his eyes level. “Because some people, knowing the danger completely, still can’t stop themselves from stepping closer. Moths know exactly what fire is. They go anyway. That’s what light does.”

She was silent for a long time.

A philanderer — and an honest one. Telling her to her face: I want you, and I will not release Taeyeon. Both things. At once.

She said nothing more. She opened the passenger door and got in.

He rounded the hood, started the engine, and eased them out into the afternoon river of the city.

“This afternoon’s session.” Seatbelt clicked; the voice re-entered work mode. “Be strict with me. I want this song sung — perfectly.”

“I will be. Your voice was built for this one. You’ll deliver it.”

Not only because it fits, she thought, watching the streets pour backward. Because you wrote it.

Some sentences never need saying. The song had already said them.

Love me like you do — how? In the dangerous way? In the addictive way?

Perhaps there was no standard answer.

Or perhaps the answer was very, very simple.
