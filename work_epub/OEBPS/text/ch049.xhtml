Chapter Forty-Nine

Cliché, But It Works

Ji-ho’s legs ran to the horizon; Irene’s and Wendy’s did not. The chase was never a contest — but as the street funneled into the crowded clothing district, he eased up on his own, unwilling to bowl over pedestrians, and let himself be caught.

“Yah! Hah… hah… CAUGHT you!”

She arrived winded, fist closing on the hem of his shirt, bent double and gulping air. Seung-wan — short stride, modest cardio — had been dropped so far back she’d left visual range entirely.

“Nuna, I surrendered already.” He turned, the picture of persecuted innocence. “Lunch is on me. What more can a man give?”

“YAH!” She straightened, cheeks flushed with running and rage and residual capsaicin. “Who PERMITTED you to surrender?! Surrender is NOT PERMITTED!”

Her lovely eyes had gone perfectly round, competitive fire roaring behind them at full draw. Irene did not lose. Irene especially did not get swindled and then lose.

“Eat it! NOW!” The threat register, deployed: “If you don’t eat it, I am NEVER letting this go!”

He shook his head, smiling, thoroughly unafraid.

She bit her lip — then snatched up the last potato cake, paved it with every remaining cheongyang ring, rose onto her toes, and drove it toward his mouth by main force.

“Yah — OPEN!”

His hand closed gently around her slim wrist, immovable.

“Since when can’t a man concede a bet?”

She pulled. Nothing. She let go of his shirt hem, balled that hand into a small fist, and swung for his shoulder — and he caught that wrist too.

Both wrists pinned, she twisted once, twice — solid as architecture.

“Let GO of me!”

“Accept my surrender and I’ll let go.”

“NO! You’re not ALLOWED to surrender!”

Deadlock, in the middle of the street. One second. Two—

Neither of them registered the motorcycle engine screaming around the alley corner.

“COMING THROUGH! MOVE—!!”

It came too fast for thought. Joo-hyun’s whole body locked — brain still buffering — and then the grip on her wrists simply vanished.

“LOOK OUT—”

An arm wrapped her waist and hauled; the world spun once, hard; her cheek landed against something solid and warm. He’d pulled her clean into his chest and turned — putting his own back squarely to the oncoming bike.

It passed close enough to comb the wind through her hair. Close enough that the draft off it shoved them both half a step.

The engine howl dopplered away down the street. Somewhere behind them, pedestrians shouted after the rider; the sounds arrived blurred, background static — because what Irene’s entire sensorium had locked onto was this:

His arm was still around her waist.

Her cheek still rested against his chest — and through the thin fabric his heartbeat came through with total clarity, thud, thud, thud, steady and strong and somehow louder than her own considerably less organized percussion.

She stood absolutely still in the circle of him, not daring to move a millimeter. She could feel the rise and fall of his breathing. The tension still wired through his arm. The warmth of his exhale ghosting across the top of her hair.

“Are you okay?” He looked down, and the fear was still audibly in him — the after-kind, the real kind. “Did anything hit you?”

She tipped her face up slowly and met his eyes, and found them full — completely, undisguisedly full — of worry for her.

Heat climbed her face. Her heart was attempting to exit through her ribs. Her mouth opened; her throat had gone to sand; nothing came out. In the end she managed the smallest shake of her head, and a voice like a mosquito’s:

“I’m… I’m fine.”

The hero saves the beauty. The oldest, cheapest, most shameless page in the entire book — and it worked every single time.

He released her slowly, staying close. The truth was, the move had been pure spinal reflex — and now, replaying it, his own aftershock arrived: if it had actually hit her—

She looked down. The potato cake was still crushed in her hand — the chili rings had scattered across the pavement somewhere in the chaos. And she discovered, examining the wreckage, that even if they hadn’t, she no longer had the slightest interest in force-feeding him anything.

“Eonni! Ji-ho! Yah— wait for me…”

Seung-wan arrived at last, hands braced on knees, dragging air. She looked up between them, blinking.

“Eonni — well?? Did he eat it?”

Joo-hyun didn’t answer. She quietly ate the last bite of potato cake herself, turned, and set off into the clothing district.

“Come on. Let’s buy things before the crowds hit.”

“Eh?” Seung-wan tilted her head at the retreating back. Weird. Why are eonni’s EARS red? But the thought didn’t survive contact with a street full of shops. “Eonni, wait for meee!”

Ji-ho followed, and his gaze kept finding its way — without instructions — to Irene’s back: the hem of the cardigan swaying with her steps, the ghost of her waist still printed on his palm. Soft. Impossibly slender. A single handspan of warmth.

✦   ✦   ✦

The clothing district swallowed them for hours.

His own mission was surgical: the Hip-Hop Kid loadout — oversized tee, denim jacket, high-waist loose jeans, sneakers, ball cap, shades. Inside an hour he’d secured everything but the right cap.

Which converted the rest of the day into pure escort duty. And never — never — underestimate the shopping stamina of women. Joo-hyun and Seung-wan worked the street end to end, entered every promising door, tried on everything with potential, and cross-examined each other’s opinions with the seriousness of appellate judges.

“Eonni, this shirt! Look!”

“The color suits you — the cut’s good too. Try it on?”

“Okay!”

Ji-ho held the growing constellation of shopping bags and leaned against walls, waiting. And — perhaps without noticing it himself — his eyes spent most of that waiting on one person: the careful way she straightened Seung-wan’s collar; the small frown of genuine deliberation when she held a piece to the light; the shy, lip-pressed smile when a shop clerk complimented her.

She noticed. Every time their eyes caught, hers fled first — and something she couldn’t name made her movements gentler after each escape, her voice softer, her posture more aware of itself.

“Ji-ho!” A head popped around a rack. “What about this one?”

“Good. Brightens your skin.”

“Then it’s THIS one!”

Shirt purchased, the two of them decided — against all known physics — to walk the entire street again from the far end. He surveyed the bags multiplying in his hands, laughed helplessly at nothing, and followed anyway.
