Chapter Twenty-Nine

First Meeting: Lee Soo-man

Ji-ho pocketed the phone, then drew out a wad of napkins and wrapped the drugged glass with surgical care — no fingerprints of his own joining the evidence — and stowed it safely.

He surveyed the room. On the floor, the tattooed man was still keening softly, a junkyard dog with a stepped-on paw. Choi Jae was gone from the world entirely; by the look of that sleep, he’d be lucky to surface by tomorrow.

Sulli sat collapsed where the fight had left her, shoulders quaking, tears striking the floorboards without a sound. Nineteen years old, and tonight had gone through her like an artillery shell.

Hyo-yeon slumped against the sofa, half her face ballooning, the handprint rising in it like a brand — that blow had been no joke.

Tiffany braced against the table’s edge, forehead beaded with cold sweat, face bloodless, right leg held carefully bent — and not one sound of complaint out of her. Nobody survived to the top of this industry without being ruthless with their own pain.

Nichkhun stood silent in his corner. He knew — everyone in the room knew — that whatever might have begun tonight was already over.

“Come on. Let’s find somewhere quiet to talk.”

Tiffany pushed herself up — and her knee reported in with a spike of white pain.

“Sss—!”

Her eyes flicked to the handprint on Hyo-yeon’s cheek, worry crossing her face; then to Ji-ho, voice pitched with a weakness she’d have died before showing a camera.

“Could you… give me a hand up?”

He steadied her arm. She rose against the leverage — and ended up mostly against him, a drift of jasmine arriving with her. Objectively pleasant. He filed it away and said nothing.

“Sulli. Up we get.” She freed one hand and drew the girl to her feet; Sulli came up like a marionette, boneless and obedient. A pat on the shoulder. “Hyo-yeon. You functional?”

Hyo-yeon was nursing a coal of pure fury — this face would take two weeks to deflate, and what exactly was she supposed to tell the company? She fixed the tattooed man with a glare, walked over, and drove a kick into his gut.

“AGH!”

He curled up like a boiled shrimp. She added two more, found it still insufficient but acceptable, and limped back to Tiffany’s side.

They relocated to an empty booth down the corridor. Lights on; door shut; the noise of the world cut off.

Ji-ho leaned against the door and ran his private accounting. The plan had been surgical: show her the video, let her see the man’s true face, walk away — a quiet correction to a tragedy, no strings attached. Instead she’d sprinted off with the evidence and detonated a confrontation, and now he stood hip-deep in the whole affair.

Done is done. A man doesn’t take back a move once his hand leaves the piece. The question now is the endgame — walk out of this mess with something to show, and with the risk sanded down to nothing.

He considered a moment longer, then unlocked Choi Jae’s phone, opened the gallery, and set it down on the table with a flat clack.

“See for yourselves.”

Sulli reached it first — and one glance had her hand clamped over her mouth, tears breaking loose all over again. The gallery was an atrocity: image after image of unconscious women, and a dozen-plus videos. Her thumb, shaking, touched one. Ugly sounds spilled from the speaker; Choi Jae’s contorted face filled the frame, busy over a girl too drunk to know she existed.

“Ough—”

Her stomach heaved; her face went gray-white. If he hadn’t come. If that man hadn’t kicked the door in, that would have been— tomorrow that would have been ME. How would I have— how does anyone live after—

Tiffany took the phone from her, and she and Hyo-yeon bent over it together. With every swipe their faces darkened a shade further.

“That ANIMAL!” Tiffany’s breath went short; a cold sweat crawled down her spine. I clinked glasses with that thing tonight. I sat one meter from the gates of hell and ordered a drink.

The shaking got worse the longer she looked. She slammed her palm on the table, yelped at her own leg, and shoved the phone away.

Nichkhun picked it up. His brows drew down further with every file. There were group videos in there — things that would detonate on contact with daylight. And the math assembled itself coldly in his head: this scandal was already a bomb, and tonight’s guest list tied f(x), Girls’ Generation, and 2PM to the blast radius. If this broke wrong, it would be a tsunami through three countries’ trending charts.

“We can’t just call the police!” His head snapped up. “The companies first — they have to manage this. They HAVE to be in front of it.”

“Agreed.” Hyo-yeon spoke around the pain in her cheek. “This is too big. The company carries it, or it buries us.”

Tiffany pulled a long breath in, forcing the shake out of her hands, and looked to the girl still crying silently beside her.

“Sulli. Your call too?”

Sulli’s mind was white noise. She nodded because nodding was easier than existing.

Tiffany levered herself and Sulli upright — and her leg buckled; she caught the table. “Move fast. Call the managers. We go back to the company.” Then, turning: “Can I ask you to come with us?”

“Of course. I’m SM anyway.”

“Mwo?!”

Four voices, one syllable. Even Sulli forgot to cry.

“You’re— an SM trainee?!”

“Ne, sunbae-nim.”

“YAH! You little—!” Delight and outrage collided in Tiffany’s face. Then she simply grabbed him, spun him, and draped herself over his back, chin hooking his shoulder. “Meeting a sunbae and not greeting her properly?! Your punishment: carry me.”

His back reported a warm, soft, entirely unfair weight. Tiffany’s figure was considerably more generous than cameras let on; through two thin layers of fabric he could feel the heat of her and every curve of the arrangement. He threaded his arms under her knees and boosted her secure, fingertips accidentally grazing smooth skin — and felt the tiny shiver run through her.

Caps and masks went on; appearances were reassembled; he begged off the rest of his shift. Then, with a Girls’ Generation member on his back and half of tonight’s trending topics limping behind him, Ji-ho walked out of the whole flaming mess.

01:00 · March 29, 2012 SM Entertainment — Conference Room nobody in this room is happy to be awake

Lee Soo-man. Kim Young-min. Park Jin-young. Flanking them, the legal, PR, and artist-management heads of both SM and JYP — every face carved from the same grim stone as two phones passed hand to hand around the table.

Lee Soo-man had arrived annoyed at being summoned in the dead of night. Then he watched the storeroom video, and then the gallery — and the annoyance graduated to open fury. His palm hit the table like a gunshot.

“Animal!”

Park Jin-young was no calmer — tie dragged loose, face rigid. Sulli wasn’t his artist, but this particular species of crime sat at the exact center of everything he despised.

Kim Young-min pushed his glasses up, expression fathoms deep, already running the only calculus a CEO runs at 1 a.m.: how does SM take minimum damage — and is there, anywhere in this catastrophe, something SM can gain?

Lee Soo-man closed his eyes, breathed, and opened them, level again. His gaze crossed the four figures along the wall: Sulli hiccupping through the aftershocks. Hyo-yeon and Nichkhun studying the carpet. And the trainee — perfectly, strangely calm.

“Ji-ho. You shot the video. Walk us through tonight — complete, in order, and do not skip a single detail.”

“Ne, Lee Soo-man seonsaeng-nim.” A step forward, voice steady. “Tonight I was working my part-time residency at Whisper Bar. After my first set, I went to the storeroom behind the kitchen to make a private call. As I was finishing, I heard two men outside the door — Choi Jae, and a man he called Yun Seong-sik…”
