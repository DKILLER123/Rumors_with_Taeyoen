Chapter Eighty-Two

Loving Someone, or Loving Yourself

IU wiped the fine sweat from her forehead — and then noticed that the wrestling match had left Ji-yeon’s already-loose pajamas in a state of open structural failure, one whole fair shoulder bared to the room, and beneath the fabric… a conspicuous absence of any second layer.

“Ji-yeon-ah. Are you not wearing ANYTHING under that?”

Ji-yeon glanced down, yanked the collar shut, and went on the offensive.

“What are YOU staring at, little pervert? Like YOU wear one after a bath! No woman does — it’s miserable! And there’s no MAN here, so who cares—”

IU had already turned back to reading a wine label.

“…Except there is, though? Ji-ho’s literally in the living room. Go put something on.”

Ji-ho.

The name landed like a dropped glass. Right. RIGHT. How did I forget— the FOOD? It was the food’s fault. The table was RIGHT there—

And then the evening’s footage began replaying itself with merciless clarity: the introduction. The handshake. The small polite bow she’d made — at HER height, against HIS height, wearing THIS neckline—

Her face ignited.

If he didn’t see — fine. But if he DID—

Shame, fury, embarrassment, and mortification arrived simultaneously and formed a committee. She stamped her foot.

“…You’re right. You pick the bottles. I need my room for a second.”

“Mm. Go.”

She speed-walked to the bedroom, kicked off her slippers, and dove face-first into the bed, legs kicking at the mattress.

“AAAAAAGH! INFURIATING!”

Two full rotations across the bedspread later, she sat up, raked her tangled hair into order, and began the necessary psychological repairs.

It’s fine. Jiyeon, it’s FINE. Maybe he didn’t see anything. And even if he did… well. He’s ridiculously handsome, so honestly? You didn’t exactly LOSE on the exchange. Right. Correct. No losses recorded.

Construction complete, she climbed off the bed, retrieved proper underthings from her overnight bag, changed — paused briefly to consider the mirror, and the thought of exactly what might have been visible, and combusted all over again — then raided IU’s closet for the most conservatively necklined pajamas in the drawer, and only then trudged back toward the liquor room.

Delicious food is a TRAP. Never again. NEVER again.

IU had the bottles picked, took in the borrowed pajamas without a flicker of surprise — their closets had been communal property for years — and nodded at the haul.

“This should do it. Hara eonni and the peach can go as hard as they want. You and me — pleasantly buzzed and NO further.”

They rejoined the living room, and the drinking resumed — until a few rounds in, IU brought up the house.

Hara confirmed it cheerfully: she’d bought a standalone home in Cheongdam-dong.

Two Houses · A Tale of Two Ledgers
Hara — Cheongdam-dong, standalone (built 1975)₩1.165 billion
IU — Bangbae-dong villa (built 2010)₩840 million
Distance from Hara’s house to Cube Entertainment~200 m

Prime location — but a thirty-seven-year-old building, hard to resell, limited upside. IU had counseled patience once already. The deal had closed anyway.

“Eonni.” IU’s voice was careful. “You chose that spot because Cube’s right there, didn’t you. So it’s easier to see Jun-hyung oppa.”

Hara turned her glass slowly, and the smile that surfaced was tender and faintly bruised at once.

“Mm… it makes meeting so much easier. You know how it is — we’re both buried. Our whole dating life happens inside parked cars. There isn’t one private room in the world that’s OURS. This half-year in Japan, I’ve seen him twice. Maybe three times. This trip home — we won’t manage even once.”

A pause. Quieter:

“If it goes on like this… I keep being afraid that one day we just — end. Out of nowhere.”

IU set her glass down.

“But eonni — don’t you think you’re making yourself too small in this? That house is every won you’ve ever saved. The FIRST question should have been what works for you — location for your life, value for your future. A 1975 build is hard to exit and slow to appreciate. And if—”

She stopped herself. The worry in her eyes finished the sentence anyway.

Hara was silent a moment. When she spoke, her voice had gone somewhere far away.

“Ji-eun-ah. You and I aren’t the same.”

She looked up, and old shadows moved behind her eyes. A childhood short exactly one commodity: the feeling of being loved. The terror of losing love once found. The reflex, whenever anyone offered a single measure of sincerity, to seize it with both hands and repay it ten and a hundredfold.

“Loving someone — isn’t that supposed to be with everything you have? Accommodate him. Bend for him. As long as… as long as he keeps loving me.” The smile wavered. “Isn’t that how it works?”

IU shook her head slowly.

“Eonni. Before loving anyone else — a person has to learn to love herself. The world is FULL of men, and no one can promise you the one in front of you is the right one. The only real protection we have is becoming strong and whole on our own — so we never have to shrink inside a relationship, and never have to fear choosing wrong. Because even in the worst case… we can catch ourselves.”

She reached across and closed her hand gently around Hara’s cool fingers.

“I’m not saying Jun-hyung oppa is bad. I’m saying — when you decide things, leave a little room in the plan for YOU.”

Hara gripped back, a little too tight.

“I know you mean well. But for me… love IS holding nothing back. The renovation’s already started — three months. And after that…” the smile steadied itself by force, “…we’ll see each other all the time. Probably.”

Two gazes met across the table — one lucid and cool, one ardent and unbudging. IU didn’t argue further. People couldn’t even change their own parents; what chance did anyone have with a friend’s heart? A friend could counsel once, twice — beyond that, words were just weather.

They looked at each other in silence. Then, softly, they touched glasses.

Two philosophies of love, grown from two very different childhoods — no right answer between them. Only choices.

Ji-ho and Sulli had both gone quiet, each folded into private arithmetic. At this table of five, the happiest childhood belonged to Jiyeon, with IU’s a distant second. The other three carried their own versions of the same original wound.

And Ji-yeon herself sat chin-in-hand, watching them all, finding the taste of her drink suddenly complicated — then glanced down at her borrowed, resolutely modest pajamas, remembered the entire humiliating campaign of psychological reconstruction upstairs, and arrived at a burning need for one specific answer.

Her gaze swung to Ji-ho — and narrowed with purpose.

Time to get him drunk.
