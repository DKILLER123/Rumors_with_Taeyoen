Chapter Two Hundred Nine

Opening Day

June 6 – July 2, 2013 Press junkets · Running Man · stage greetings nationwide · one new apartment a month-long promotion sprint — and at midnight, the lights go down

Next morning, first light.

Sulli woke slowly, warm all the way through — the poached-in-a-perfect-hot-spring warmth that had no business existing on a weekday. The usual morning heaviness simply wasn’t there; in its place, an absurd clear-headed lightness.

The Halo, doing its quiet night work — though she had no name for it.

She blinked, registered the pleasant weight around her: his arms banded around her waist, chin resting on her hair, breath long and even. Still deep asleep.

She tilted her head up with great care. From this angle: the sleeping face at rest — brows smoothed, mouth soft, the whole architecture of him quiet.

Thump. Thu-thump. The feeling arrived with a clarity that made her hold her breath.

She watched him for a long while. Then, moving by millimeters, she eased back just far enough — and leaned in, and touched her lips to his, quick as a sparrow at a feeder.

Soft. Warm. That clean scent of his.

She pressed her lips together over the smile. He stirred faintly and did not wake.

Bolder now. The second kiss lingered — slow, wandering, small increments of theft. His mouth was warmer than she’d budgeted for. Her heart sprinted; her cheeks heated; her eyes had gone heavy-lidded with unhidden greed.

She drew back, admired the still-sleeping face with proprietary satisfaction — and dove back in anyway: the tip of his nose. Each closed eyelid. And back to his mouth, kiss after small kiss, an appetite with no visible bottom.

“Mm—”

The sustained harassment finally dragged him up out of sleep. His eyes opened on fog — and focused on a flushed, luminous, entirely unrepentant face two inches away.

Caught mid-theft, she didn’t retreat an inch. She smiled — the full sweet crescent — and delivered, in her softest morning voice:

“Oppa. Good morning~”

He hauled her back into the circle of his arms and rubbed his chin over her hair. “Morning… what time is it?”

“No idea~ still early, whatever it is.”

She nestled into his chest, listened to the steady heart under her ear, and her eyes made one sly rotation. Then the face tipped up, glinting.

“Oppa. How does holding ME compare to holding sister-in-law Taeyeon?”

One second of total silence, during which his sleep evaporated without residue.

He looked down at the small fox smiling in his arms and exhaled through his nose. Then he simply tightened his hold, breathed in slowly, and said, low, against her ear:

“Like this. This is the entire feeling of holding you.”

Her eyes curved. “And?”

“That’s all of it.”

“You haven’t said what holding Taeyeon eonni feels like! And whose hug is more comfortable!”

“Mm~ can’t say. At present I’m holding you. No basis for comparison.”

“…Slippery.”

She jabbed a fingertip at his lips. “Then kissing. Is sister-in-law’s mouth softer, or mine? No dodging this time.”

He was silent exactly three seconds. Then he rolled — and she was under him, a startled squeak escaping as his weight settled, her face going peony-pink.

“That question—” his nose brushed hers, voice dropping as he closed toward her mouth, “—requires careful comparative study.”

The study was conducted gently, and at length.

When he finally drew back, her eyes had gone glossy and her lips were kiss-swollen, and he laughed low. “There. Data’s in.”

“Mm?”

“Your lips—” his thumb traced her lower lip once, “—are like cherry-blossom petals just opened. The kind you want to keep tasting.”

A beat. His eyes went honest.

“And most importantly — right now, this moment, they’re the only lips I want to be kissing.”

Full-face detonation. She buried herself in his chest, voice muffled and delighted: “Oppa is SO slippery.” — “You’re the one asking impossible questions.” A laugh, an adjustment, one satisfied cat re-settled into his arms — and the morning gave them another hour.

✦   ✦   ✦

June 6, 2013. Lotte Cinema, Myeong-dong. Press screening and full media day.

Outside the hall stood the towering key art: GG-company rookie Seo Yeon-woo and the mysterious Go-i, laughing at each other in the sunlight of Gapado.

Backstage, Kwak Jae-yong issued final orders: “Remember — nothing beyond the trailer. No plot.”

At the mirror, Sulli adjusted her makeup. Twenty years old, a New Star award from the SBS Drama Awards already on the shelf for To the Beautiful You — but a first film lead was a first film lead.

“Nervous?” He’d drifted to her shoulder.

She turned, cat-eyes blinking. “I’ve been acting since I was small. What’s to be nervous about?” A beat; the grin arrived. “Worry about YOURSELF, Oppa. The questions are going to come down like rain.”

They did.

“Ji-ho-ssi — first film lead, playing a character older than yourself. Biggest challenge?”

“Age has never been an obstacle to love — in the film, even ten years of separation can’t stop Yeon-woo from recognizing his Go-i.” He glanced sideways, smiling. “Honestly, the hardest part was that Sulli and I know each other TOO well. The strangers-meeting scenes took real work.”

Warm laughter from the floor.

“Sulli-ssi — the cat mannerisms: the fear of water, the light drop from heights, the fascination with yarn. Directed, or yours?”

She tilted her head — shutters intensified measurably — and answered. “The director gave me enormous room. He said, ‘You ARE Go-i; Go-i is you.’” Her gaze softened. “Actually, before shooting I adopted the cat from our production. I kept her with me and just — watched. A lot of what’s on screen is borrowed directly from her.”

“The film’s tone shifts sharply in the second half — from sweetness into something surreal and sorrowful. Any hints about Go-i’s secret?”

Kwak took that one. “Only this: the film is about more than love. It’s about memory, repayment, and sacrifice. As the trailer says — some meetings are reunions across forms.”

Ninety minutes; then the photo wall. More than one sharp-eyed reporter noticed that whenever the two leads stood close, Sulli listed — very slightly, very naturally — toward him. Like a cat locating warmth.

Headlines wrote themselves in real time: “Rescue Siblings No More — the Rescue Couple?”

✦   ✦   ✦

June 7, the NAVER Movie interview room — beige sofa, cat plushies on the table.

“Most unforgettable moment of the shoot?”

The two leads exchanged a look of shared, complicated silence.

The kiss scenes. Obviously. All ten takes of them.

The host lit up like a slot machine. “WHAT. What was that look? Something happened?”

“The most unforgettable would be—” Sulli’s ear-tips went pink, “—my kiss scene with Oppa.”

“A KISS scene?!” The host was on her feet. “You two — there’s a kiss scene?!”

“It’s a ROMANCE film. Of course there’s a kiss scene.” Sulli fanned her burning face with one hand. “But because Oppa and I are practically real siblings, I kept breaking out of character. So I NG’d — ten whole times.”

“TEN?” The host’s eyes ping-ponged between them — and landed on open envy. “Ahh, I’m so jealous of Sulli-ssi. I also want to kiss Ji-ho ten times.”

“YAH, eonni!!!” Sulli collapsed into scandalized laughter — then physically interposed herself. “Andwae. Not during the theatrical run. Go Min-i does not permit others to kiss Seo Yeon-woo.”

The set dissolved. Recovering, the host faced the lens: “Viewers — The Girl in the Sun, July 2. Please shower it with love. There’s a kiss scene~”

Twin bows: “Please watch over us. Gamsahamnida~”

The clip broke a million views in two hours.

The online drumbeat continued. In the small hours of June 8, her Cyworld updated: “Watched today’s interview replay with ‘Yeon-woo sunbae’… now the whole country knows I adopted the cat ㅠㅠ” — attached, a photo of the two of them teasing Lucky.

His own post went up on Instagram: “As the writer, and as the actor — the greatest happiness is when an audience meets a character and recognizes something. I hope Seo Yeon-woo reminds you of first love: that clumsy, sincere kind of liking, the kind willing to believe in every impossible thing.” Below it, a set photo: Sulli leaning on his shoulder, the two of them on the shoreline rocks, watching the sun go down.

On Twitter, the hashtags for the film and the couple climbed the trends side by side, and the amateur detectives went to work on every frame: the look exchanged at the press conference, the natural contact in the interviews, the mirrored posts. The consensus solidified nightly: this isn’t acting.

✦   ✦   ✦

June 15, the Running Man set.

Both of them counted as family on this show by now — but this taping was the film’s promotion special. The entrance: him, Sulli, Lee Kwang-soo, Lee Min-ki, four abreast.

“Today we welcome the three leads of the upcoming film The Girl in the Sun—” Yoo Jae-suk announced warmly, “—Ji-ho-ssi, Sulli-ssi, and Min-ki-ssi!”

“Welcome, welcome!”

Lee Kwang-soo stared at the host who had just erased him. “Jae-suk hyung. I am ALSO in this film!”

“Kwang-soo-yah.” Yoo Jae-suk examined him with open distaste. “Look at your face. Standing next to these three — is it appropriate?”

“And anyway, aren’t you a comedian?”

“Hyung, what are you SAYING? I’m an ACTOR!”

The set went down in flames of laughter.

The games were built off the film: an individual race, eleven players, four rounds of summer-food missions yielding clues, and a final dash to identify the “lovers’ sanctuary.”

He and Sulli devoted the entire middle section of the race to eating. Clues acquired: zero. They lost with total magnificence — the “winning couple” title going, in the day’s best punchline, to Lee Kwang-soo and Yoo Jae-suk. The episode was scheduled to air opening week.

✦   ✦   ✦

June 18–25: the nationwide stage-greeting tour.

Seoul, first stop — a CGV hall, five hundred seats, and a wall of sound when the two of them walked out. On screen behind them: the middle-school Yeon-woo stepping in front of the bullied Go-i; ten years later, the long look across the convenience-store bench.

“Hello — we’re Seo Yeon-woo and Go Min-i of The Girl in the Sun.”

They bowed. Rising, Sulli spotted the first RESCUE COUPLE support signs in the crowd — and something in her chest went quietly, privately sweet.

A young woman won the question mic: “If it were you — knowing you could only have a limited time with the person you love — would you still begin?”

“Go-i’s choice already answers that.” Sulli went first. “Even if it’s only ten years — even knowing she’ll vanish at the end — she goes to Yeon-woo without hesitating.” She turned her head, and her eyes found his, wet-bright. “Because real love isn’t measured in time.”

“And Yeon-woo, knowing the truth, still chooses to walk her the rest of the way,” he picked up. “That’s the film’s whole argument: rather than refusing to hold something because you’re afraid to lose it — treasure every hour you’re given.”

Busan — her hometown crowd. Asked about the hardest scene:

“The farewell. Go Min-i knows she’s leaving, and still has to act normal — making Yeon-woo’s last breakfast. Smiling while crying. I NG’d a dozen times.” A perfectly timed beat. “The director said I was crying too hard — that it looked less like a cat repaying a debt and more like someone dodging one.”

The whole Busan hall roared.

✦   ✦   ✦

June 27, MBC FM4U, the late-night booth of a starlight radio hour. Eleven p.m.

“The song playing now is Go-i’s favorite in the film,” Sulli’s voice went out soft over the air. “She’s always saying — ‘wouldn’t it be nice if it could stay like this forever.’”

“If — just if — Sulli-ssi were given one do-over like Go-i, what moment would you return to?”

A brief, warm silence down the line. Then, with an audible smile:

“Right now is already good. Busy, tiring sometimes — but making something like this with people like these, with this many people waiting for it… this is the time I’d choose.”

And privately, where no microphone reached: any time with Oppa in it. That’s the happiest time.

July 1, the eve — CJ’s special presale fan event, five hundred seats sold out in ten minutes. Every guest received a postcard, a cat keyring, and one blank card.

“Please write something to yourself, ten years from now,” Sulli explained. “In the film, Go Min-i spends ten years arranging one meeting. So — ten years from tonight: where will you be? Who will be beside you?”

A twenty-something wrote: “In ten years, I hope I can still cry at a pure love story like I did today.” Her boyfriend, beside her: “In ten years, I’ll rewatch this film with the person next to me.”

He watched Sulli write hers: “Ten years from now: with the person I love, living the life I wished for.”

He thought a moment, and wrote: “Ten years from now: everyone who loves me, and everyone I love — happy, and whole.”

The final group photo: five hundred raised cards, white as a field of petals.

✦   ✦   ✦

After, in the car, the city sliding past.

“Tomorrow it opens,” she said.

“Nervous?” — the same question, a month later.

“Ani.” The smile she turned on him was clear all the way down. “This film already gave me love. I don’t want to be too greedy.”

“Sulli-yah. Oppa is a greedy man. You’re allowed some too.”

She tilted her head, blinking. “Okay then — let’s hope admissions pass five million?”

“Five? You can be greedier.”

“Seven million? Is that too greedy?”

“No. I’d call that exactly right~”

“Hehe. Oppa — tonight I want to sleep holding you.”

“Oh? You’ve done that a dozen times this month without asking. Why so polite today, our Peaches?”

“Mm~ the weather’s getting so warm. Tonight I might not wear pajamas~”

He looked at her steadily. She bit her lip and held the look, cheeks burning, unblinking — brave all the way through it. A month of shared pillows and packed schedules had kept them at the edge and never over it.

He reached over and ruffled her hair, voice gentling.

“Sulli-yah. I’ve prepared a present. Give me two more days — okay?”

She ran the private arithmetic she’d been quietly studying for weeks — the calendar, the timing — and found it agreeable.

“Okay~ I’ll listen to Oppa~”

✦   ✦   ✦

July 2, midnight. The Girl in the Sun opened nationwide — 690 screens, 3,510 first-day showings, the schedule wide open before it.

The first frames rolled. And the film’s first detonation arrived early: Son Ye-jin’s cameo — half the hall gasped aloud at that face — and from there the story simply took the room and held it.

When the credits rose, nobody stood. They waited — through the crawl, to the post-credits scene.

Midnight premiere · The Girl in the Sun · post-credits

Cherry-blossom season. Seo Yeon-woo on a bench under the falling petals. A small white cat appears at his feet — pauses — looks back at him once, and leads.

He follows. And at the end of the petal-strewn path: a girl, turning.

Dedicated to everyone with the courage to love completely — knowing full well what will be lost.

fade to black · end

Muffled crying in the dark. And then applause like weather.

By the time the first audiences reached the street, the first review was already pinned to the NAVER movie front page:

NAVER MOVIES · first review · July 2, 2013

This is not a fantasy about a cat becoming a girl. It is a fable about repayment, about sacrifice — about the purest love a human being can reach. Ji-ho and Sulli did not perform a romance. For two hours, on that screen, they made love itself briefly visible.
