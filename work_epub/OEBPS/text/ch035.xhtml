Chapter Thirty-Five

The First Disciple and the New Kid

In the SM vocal studio, Taeyeon and Seohyun sat in deep focus under Jung Soon-won’s instruction — with TaeTiSeo’s debut bearing down, this was exactly the moment for fine-tuning.

Real
Seo Ju-hyun
Lead Vocalist · Maknae
Girls’ Generation · Seohyun
Born: June 28, 1991

The principled youngest member of Girls’ Generation, disciplined to the point of intimidation and sincere in everything she chooses.

Knock knock.

“Come in.”

Ji-ho eased the door open — and blinked at the sight of Taeyeon and Seohyun inside. He recovered fast, attention going to the burly man at the mixing console: glasses, narrow appraising eyes, an expression that had clearly never wasted a smile in its life.

“The One seonsaeng-nim. Hello — I’m Ji-ho.”

“You’re here. Sit over there and wait.”

“Ne.” Then, with a polite dip toward the two women: “Taeyeon nuna. Seohyun nuna. Hello.”

Nods came back; he found a chair and made himself invisible.

Field Notes

Master class in session:

“Taeyeon — ‘Twinkle’ carries several F#5 passages. Live, with an open mic, take them on high-placement head voice blended into mask resonance. Focused, penetrating, no shrillness — and no strain or cracking at the top. I’ve taught you this a hundred times; just keep it in mind.”

“Seohyun — that top line is a genuine challenge for you. If it runs heavy in live performance, change the approach: near C5, release the cord tension, support from steadier airflow, borrow a light head voice to take the load off. Don’t let it turn tight and sharp.”

“You’ve both been debuted five years. I have full confidence — just mind the details. Off you go; I have business.”

“Understood, seonsaeng-nim.” Taeyeon — the favorite, comfortable enough to be curious — smiled instead of leaving. “Is the business… him?”

“Company says he’s a promising seedling. Wants me to take him as a disciple.”

“A FORMAL disciple?” Real surprise crossed her face.

“If he’s actually the caliber they claim. If he doesn’t clear my bar, I don’t take him. You know that.”

The finer the teacher, the harsher the filter — supreme technique poured into a talentless vessel was wasted twice over. The One took disciples the way collectors took masterpieces: rarely, and only when certain.

“Seonsaeng-nim — I was on his entrance panel. I’d like to stay and watch.”

“Suit yourself.” He beckoned. “Ji-ho. Come here.”

Ji-ho was on his feet instantly.

“What’s your strongest genre?”

“Ballads.”

“Ballads. Sing me a stretch of ‘Like Being Shot.’”

“Ne, The One seonsaeng-nim.”

Inside, gears turned fast. Baek Ji-young’s signature piece — a merciless audit of a singer’s entire toolkit: range, technique, and above all the emotional ledger. Original key A-flat; verses coiled low and patient, storing charge; the chorus a minefield where one careless step meant cracking in front of the most important ears in the building.

The tactical solve came quickly: drop a half-step to G, anchor the working range G2 to C5 — power without shrieking, depth without losing the detonation.

He closed his eyes. One slow breath in; one slower breath out. Open.

Performance

— verse: G2, chest resonance dark and restrained, grief told through clenched teeth — Like being shot — the soul goes out of me — and I just smile. Like this. I just keep smiling… The line climbs to D4 — pre-chorus, breath level as a held glass, every note laying track for the explosion. A heart shot through — our memories bleeding out — I try to hold on tight. I try to stop the wound… Chorus: E4 in full mixed voice, weight without force — then C5 taken on a light head extension, nothing squeezed, nothing torn. Even if my heart stopped beating, I don’t think it could hurt like this — teach me what to do. Teach me how to heal… The bridge — the summit. C5 attacked soft and finished strong: breath-tone entry, mixed-voice landing, agony given architecture. It really is like being shot. It hurts — this much, THIS much — and still I’m somehow alive. Isn’t that strange? How am I supposed to forget you? I don’t know. I don’t know…

Seohyun’s eyes had gone perfectly round. A trainee one month into the building — out-singing her, and she was honest enough with herself to know it, by a distance she could measure.

Taeyeon had stopped pretending to observe professionally somewhere around the pre-chorus; her small hand kept time against her knee, face open and lost in it. He’s genuinely — if I sang this myself, that’s… about where I’d land. About.

The One listened with eyes narrowed to blades and a face that disclosed nothing.

Ji-ho finished the climax and stopped, chest heaving, a fine sweat at his hairline. Full-commitment singing billed the body like sprinting.

“Wow. Daebak.”

Clap-clap-clap-clap!

Seohyun and Taeyeon burst into applause — and, rarest of sights, The One was smiling and joining them.

“You trained at another agency before this. Yes? Nobody sings at this level without accumulated years.”

“No agency.” A shake of the head. “But I sang bars for two years — residency work. Taught myself everything I could get my hands on in between.”

Self-taught. Two years. The One rubbed his chin. The boy’s working span ran G2 to C5 — fifteen degrees, sitting perfectly inside the male comfort zone while preserving the song’s full emotional architecture. That exact solve was what seasoned balladeers reached for — usually after a decade of stagecraft. And the color of the voice: warm, dense, clean straight through. Where Baek Ji-young’s version bled — that rasp, those torn-open top notes, grief flung outward at the world — this boy had rendered the male translation: grief folded inward, pressurized, dignified. Mature loss, sung from inside the wound.

Flaws? Plenty, to his ear: chest resonance underexploited, the falsetto-to-mix handoff still seamed, graduated breath-tones barely explored. Enormous headroom. Which was precisely the point — headroom on top of THIS baseline.

He had walked in today intending a polite refusal. After Taeyeon, lightning didn’t strike the same studio twice; he’d agreed to this audition as a favor to the company, nothing more. He sat there now, revising. I want to see it. What this becomes with everything I know poured into it — I want to SEE it.

Decision made, the examination continued — foundations now: pitch control, dynamic control, register transitions, edge-handling from the middle voice up, cord compression at the top. His method was simple and merciless: improvised piano, follow me if you can.

The studio piano stood waiting. His fingers hovered over the keys. One minute to prepare.

“Hum along. Follow me.”

A bright run in D major leapt off his fingertips — an improvised ballad fragment, verse gentle, chorus vaulting suddenly upward with a key-change trap buried in the seam. Ji-ho drew breath and rode it, mid-low register clear and steady on the melody’s back.

The One’s eye-corner twitched upward a millimeter. The tempo snapped faster; two transition notes vanished from the pre-chorus, deliberately, leaving raw silence in the line. A normal student would stumble into the gap. Ji-ho’s ear bridged it on instinct — a feather of breath-tone laid across the void, a soft slide tucked into the tail — and the broken phrase came out sounding composed.

“Not bad.” The words came out low, head never lifting.

The hands turned; the music plunged into F, low register flooding the room thick and heavy, the whiplash total. This was Ji-ho’s soft flank — his cords tightened; he grabbed for chest resonance to shore up the bottom, and it wasn’t enough. The note cracked, drifting off pitch.

A frown. Then the fingers detonated upward — straight into the high register, an F#5 stabbing bright enough to press on the eardrums. Ji-ho declined the frontal assault; he took the note on a whisper of head voice instead, touching it rather than seizing it.

The keys halted on a modulating tail-note.

“Mm. The training time really is short. This is where you fall off.”

“I’m sorry, The One seonsaeng-nim.” Ji-ho caught his breath, chagrined.

“For a first session?” A shake of the head — and the tone flipped to something almost warm. “That was more than good enough.”

“Wednesdays and Saturdays. Come find me. Every week.”

Ji-ho froze, not quite daring to parse it. Taeyeon laughed and smacked his shoulder.

“What are you standing there for? He’s taking you. Greet your teacher properly!”

“Seonsaeng-nim! Thank you — thank you!”

“Enough. Out, all of you.” But the smile stayed.

The three of them spilled into the corridor. Seohyun’s envy was open and entirely without malice.

“Congratulations, Ji-ho. Really.” She, by her own honest reckoning, was one of the two hundred The One taught but would never choose.

“Thank you, Seohyun nuna.” Then, turning, barely containing himself: “Sajae-nim — is there some formal ceremony for entering the discipleship?”

“Sa-jae?” Taeyeon blinked at the title — senior disciple — turned it over, and found she liked the taste of it considerably. “Seonsaeng-nim hates ritual and fuss. So, no. No ceremony.”

“None from him. What about from you?”

“From ME?” She pointed at herself, lost. “Why would I have a ceremony?”

“I grew up on old kung-fu movies back in LA,” he said, absolutely straight-faced. “The rules are ironclad. When the master’s busy, the first senior disciple trains the newest one. You’re his most treasured student — that makes you First Disciple. I’m the baby of the lineage. When I get stuck and seonsaeng-nim’s unavailable, tradition says you HAVE to guide me. It’s the law of the mountain.”

Taeyeon and Seohyun exchanged a look of complete cultural bewilderment.

“…Is that actually how it works?”

“Absolutely. And the First Disciple is always the strongest — second only to the master. Like you, sajae-nim. Reigning number-one main vocal among active girl groups. Obviously.”

“Hahahaha — WHAT number one, don’t talk nonsense!” Her mouth denied it; the corners of her mouth, climbing without permission, voted otherwise. …the new kid has taste, though.

And, well — if she was First Disciple, then guiding a struggling junior was simply her lineage duty. Besides, he’d saved Sulli and Fany both. A person worth knowing.

“You have my Kakao. If you get stuck, message me. If we’re both in the building, just come find me.”

“Yes, sajae-nim.” The grin threatened to leave his face permanently damaged.

There’d been a rumor, once — years from now, in a future that no longer had to happen — that Taeyeon’s type ran younger. Ji-ho filed the thought away with scholarly detachment. Purely for research purposes. The woman would hold top-tier status for nearly two more decades; whatever traits kept a person at that altitude that long, any one of them would supercharge a career. And the fact that she happened to be exactly, precisely his aesthetic — that was simply data.

Human hearts were fickle instruments. When the career had been hopeless, the career had been everything. Now — with the first real light on the road — older appetites stretched, yawned, and checked the schedule. And conveniently, the excuse had already written itself: it’s for the career.

Money. Resources. Connections. And a master with room for one more disciple.
