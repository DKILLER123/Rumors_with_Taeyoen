Chapter Eight

Irene

“Ji-ho? Why are you still here?”

A familiar voice, from behind. He spun — Kim Mi-yeon. The bow was instant.

“Seonsaeng-nim, good evening.” A beat, then the explanation: “My dance foundation is my weakest link, so I wanted to put in extra hours. Then the lights just… died on me.”

“Knowing your own gaps and working to close them — good.” Open approval in her eyes; then, patiently: “It’s cost-cutting. Basement levels one and two lose power at ten every night.”

He stole another glance at the tracker. Longest training time: 10 h 13 m. The phantom was still going.

“Seonsaeng-nim, honestly — I’m still not confident about the evaluation. I’d like to keep practicing tonight. Is there anywhere I still can?” No performance in the asking; the hunger in his eyes was real.

“Basement three.” She pointed toward the stairwell. “Official trainees’ floor. The power stays on.”

Official trainees. So that was his invisible rival — someone from the floor below.

She read his hesitation — wrongly — and smiled.

“It’s fine. After ten, provisional trainees may use the third floor too. The company has always encouraged extra practice. Believe me — they encourage it.”

“Understood. Thank you, seonsaeng-nim.”

“Fighting.” A smiling nod, and she turned for the stairs — after a very long day, finally off the clock.

“Good night, seonsaeng-nim.”

He watched her go, then took the stairs down to basement three — and clicked his tongue at what he found. It wasn’t one maniac burning the midnight oil. It was all of them. Room after room of official trainees still going at half past ten — including, he noted with betrayal, a provisional trainee from his own practice room who he’d assumed had gone home.

So that’s how it is. Fine. I’ve got a system that repairs my body overnight — let’s see who can out-suffer whom.

Time dissolved into the work. Eleven. Midnight. Half past. One a.m.

NOW everyone has to be gone. He stopped, grabbed his bottle, and drank in long gulps — his throat felt like a house fire.

Unbelievable. UNBELIEVABLE. Someone is still going! Genuine awe was starting to creep in under the frustration. He set the bottle down. Enough — he was going to walk every room on this floor and put a face to this monster.

Room after empty room. Then, the last one: inside, a slender figure was dancing flat out, sweat flying, completely surrendered to the music.

So it’s you.

He leaned quietly against the doorframe, eyes fixed on the reigning champion of the grind.

The girl chose that exact moment to turn — and their eyes collided, dead center.

Both of them froze.

Recognition hit Ji-ho like a dropped chord. The face before him belonged to Irene — the girl the future would crown one of the legendary visuals of her era.

A flawless oval face, its lines flowing as if drawn in one unbroken stroke. A long, graceful neck. Proportions in almost mathematical harmony, features so fine they looked sculpted — eyes bright and deep as winter stars, a high nose bridge lending the whole face dimension, full lips with an exquisite curve, the corners tilted faintly upward so that her smile, when it came, landed like spring.

Even now, still a trainee, she was already half a legend inside these walls.

For her part, Irene studied the unfamiliar face in the doorway with open puzzlement. A new kid?

In his twelve years on the Peninsula, Ji-ho had crossed paths with plenty of celebrities; some had become real friends. But standing in front of the teenage edition of a future superstar — that was a first, and for a moment his brain simply buffered.

She didn’t know him. But they were both company trainees, so she gave the polite nod the situation called for, then walked over to her water bottle, ready for a break.

He returned the nod with a small courteous bow of his own — and retreated to his practice room.

Irene, of all people, is the final boss of the grind. He hadn’t tried to talk to her; interrupting a senior’s practice at one in the morning was a spectacular way to make a terrible first impression. There would be time. There would be years.

As a veteran of the bar circuit, 1 a.m. was when his old life used to start — drowsiness wasn’t the problem. The reward was the problem. To claim it, he had to outlast Irene. So he squared up to the mirror, alone, and went back to sanding down his weakest edges, one eight-count at a time.

Fully absorbed, time betrayed him again. When he next looked up, the wall clock said 2:30 a.m.

Sweat stood out on his forehead in fat beads; his hair was plastered flat. The hoodie had been peeled off and flung to the floorboards ages ago, and his tee clung soaked to his back. He gulped air, toweled his face, and sipped water in small careful mouthfuls, the way the lounge veterans had taught him to protect a working throat.

2:33. He checked the system.

Quest Tracker
Longest training time today13 h 20 m
Your training time13 h 10 m
Streak0 days

Ten minutes. Ten minutes behind! Did the queen of the grind finally go home? Thank you, God. Thank you, Buddha. Thank you, everyone.

He had to confirm it with his own eyes. Down went the water bottle; off he went at speed toward the last practice room, praying the whole way — Irene, Irene, I am begging you. Please, PLEASE be done for tonight.

The light was still on. His heart dropped through the floor like a stone into cold water. He crept to the doorway, edged his head around the frame—

Empty. Gloriously, beautifully empty!

YES! It’s over! It’s finally over!

From eight in the morning to half past two the next day, he had been locked in silent, single-combat with Irene — and now, at last, dawn was breaking on his side of the field. A strange, swelling emotion rose in his chest, as if he’d just completed some monumental, historic labor.

“Um… excuse me—”

“HOLY—!!”

The voice materialized directly behind his ear, and Ji-ho’s entire soul briefly exited his body.
