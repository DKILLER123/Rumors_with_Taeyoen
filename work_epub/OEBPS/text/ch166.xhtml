Chapter One Hundred Sixty-Six

The Secret of the Milk

Late January 2013 · Night Banpo — Taeyeon’s apartment one boyfriend, four members, one marked cup

He was deep in the kiss when instinct made him open his eyes — and register, with a small jolt, that the audience had grown to three extremely attentive light bulbs.

Taeyeon surfaced a beat later, followed his gaze, and combusted on the spot — cheeks going the exact shade of an overripe peach. She rallied by force of will, gripped his hand tighter, and went on the offensive.

“What are you all LOOKING at? If you’re jealous, go find your own!”

It was 2013, after all. The company had loosened its grip on dating — stay out of the headlines, and eyes stayed conveniently closed.

“Aigoo — my ramyeon!” Yoon-a evaporated into the kitchen with suspicious urgency.

“We meet again, Ji-ho.” Sunny’s greeting arrived wrapped in a smile with several layers to it.

“Nuna. Good evening.”

Jessica still hadn’t rebooted. Her eyes stayed fixed on the joined hands.

“Wait — so you two weren’t just a RUMOR?”

“Wah, daebak. Since WHEN?”

“Ani — Sunny, Yoon-a — you both KNEW?”

Taeyeon towed him over and performed the honors.

“Sica, formal introduction: my boyfriend, Ji-ho.” She turned to him, sparkling. “And Ji-ho, this is my—”

“Girlfriend. Jung Soo-yeon.”

“Eh??”

Jessica went scarlet from cheek to ear. He blinked, equally blindsided.

“Hahaha—! Before YOU came along, that’s what the fans always called us.” Taeyeon was doubled over at her own joke.

“Jessica nuna. Hello — Ji-ho.” He offered a hand, warm and easy.

“Ne… hello.” The blush hadn’t finished leaving; the handshake was brief and pale-fingered.

“Ya, Ji-ho — come carry the ramyeon!” The summons from the kitchen was not, by any standard, a request.

“Carry it yourself.”

“MWO? You little—” Yoon-a emerged with both hands on her hips, wrath incarnate. “You’re the maknae here now! Maknae work is MAKNAE work!”

“Terribly sorry, Yoon-a.” His eyebrow arched to maximum smugness. “I’m your brother-in-law now. Honorifics, please. Thank you.”

“Mwo?!”

Her eyes went from him to Taeyeon — nestled birdlike at his side — and the internal wail was almost audible: he really IS the brother-in-law. Infuriating!!

“Yoon-a-ssi. Say ‘hyeongbu.’”

Grinding her teeth, she executed a full tactical pivot instead — attaching to Taeyeon’s arm and deploying aegyo at six-year-old caliber.

“Eonni~ eonni, LOOK at him. He’s bullying your cutest, most precious dongsaeng. Eonni~~ ten years of sisterhood, eonni! You HAVE to take my side~”

A twenty-four-year-old woman, whining like a kindergartner. From anyone else on earth it would have been unbearable. From Yoon-a, the nation had voted, it was a national resource.

“Okay, okay — stop teasing her.” Taeyeon herself was buckling.

“Understood. Nuna has spoken; I obey.”

He headed for the kitchen, and Yoon-a trailed him at gloating range.

“SEE that? In eonni’s heart, I outrank you. Hmph. Behave from now on. Say ‘brother-in-law’ to me again and I’ll hit you.”

He glanced back, confirmed Taeyeon had been pulled aside by Jessica for interrogation, pulled on the oven mitts, lifted the ramyeon pot, and dropped his voice to a register built for exactly one listener.

“Keep gloating, and I tell nuna that you like me — and that in Europe you made the first move.”

He strode off with the pot.

“SONG JI-HO! You—!”

She stamped her foot hard enough to rattle the cabinets. Eight hundred explanations, and the misunderstanding only compounded interest. That absolute MENACE. She gathered the cutlery and followed, fuming.

They ringed the table around the ramyeon pot while Jessica ran the full best-friend questionnaire — when did it start, who confessed first, all the classified material — and Taeyeon narrated with shining eyes while Yoon-a and Sunny, who had heard it all before, listened like it was breaking news anyway.

The pot emptied fast. Then came the dishes.

Yoon-a ran the assessment: Taeyeon — a kitchen hazard who could not reliably boil ramyeon. Jessica — professionally lazy, famously so. Sunny — one look confirmed: not someone to conscript. And the brother-in-law — jokes aside, one did not actually make the hyeongbu wash up. The task fell, by unanimous silence, exactly where it always fell.

“Unbelievable. By AGE I’m not even the maknae anymore,” she informed the sink, scrubbing.

Meanwhile, he rose and murmured:

“Nuna, come with me a second — I need you for something.”

“Oh.” Taeyeon followed, obedient as a duckling.

Behind them, Sunny’s eyes narrowed to slits.

At the study door he stopped.

“Wait inside — I’ll grab something.”

She wandered the study, hands clasped behind her, revisiting the fan gifts on the shelves — until he returned carrying a glass of milk.

“Where’d you get milk?”

“Bought it on the way over.”

She had long since stopped believing the cover story, not that she’d ever said so. Every glass he handed her tasted subtly unlike any milk on the market. The members had at one point stuffed the dorm fridge with Yonsei bottles and drunk themselves sick of it — and not one of them had grown a millimeter. Only she changed. And he was still growing too; four centimeters of her own progress hadn’t closed the gap between them at all. Where it came from didn’t matter. What mattered was that something this precious existed — and he chose to share it with her. That was the whole answer.

She took the glass and drank it straight down, then ran the tip of her tongue around her lips, impish.

“See? All gone.”

“Well done, Taeyeon.” He ruffled her hair like a proud parent.

“Ya! I’m your NUNA!” The small fist came up, fierce as a kitten.

He set the glass on the desk, drew her in, and kissed her slow — strawberry sweetness and warm quiet — until she melted against his chest.

“Ji-ho-ya. Thank you.”

“That’s sudden. For what?”

“Just… for all of it. Everything.”

Knock knock knock.

“Taeyeon? Ji-ho? You two done talking?”

Taeyeon sprang out of his arms.

“Done!”

“Coming in, then~”

Sunny pushed the door open and took inventory: one flushed leader — bullied again, ugh, honeymoon couples — and, on the desk, one glass wearing a thin white film down its inside. Milk residue. There it was. Whatever Taeyeon had been drinking, it was NOT the Yonsei anything. Science, sorcery, or something in between — irrelevant. What mattered was that the secret existed, was findable, and might contain centimeters with HER name on them. Ji-ho, ah, Ji-ho. How exactly do I get that miracle out of your hands?
