Chapter One Hundred Eighty-Nine

What Jessica Found

May 19, 2013 LOEN Entertainment · the main recording studio two demos, one corridor standoff — and a bank notification that changes everything

Around the sofa they went — Jessica in pursuit, Tiffany dodging, Krystal curled into the corner cushions praying for neutrality.

“YAH! Tiffany, STAND STILL!”

“Sica, this is YOUR bad sleeping habit! You sleep naked and blame ME!”

“YAH — SHUT UP!!”

“Do you have any idea how comfortable it— wait, have YOU never—”

“CLOSE. YOUR. MOUTH.”

A cushion flew; Tiffany dodged it laughing. On the sofa, Krystal wore the expression of someone receiving premium gossip at home delivery. Eonni sleeps naked?? And WAIT — if I’d gone to wake her, that scene would’ve been MINE. Tiffany eonni STOLE my—

As Tiffany rounded the sofa, Krystal’s arm shot out and clamped her in place.

“Eonni, quick! I’ve got her!”

“Soo-jung-ah — EXCELLENT work!”

Jessica pounced, and the household dissolved into one shrieking, laughing pile.

✦   ✦   ✦

It was past ten when the two of them finally reached the LOEN studio — nearly an hour behind schedule.

Through the door: he and Taeyeon were already inside. She sat on a high stool at the console, score in hand, head tilted toward him; he leaned in to listen, and the window light fell across the pair of them like a graded shot from someone’s better film.

Tiffany’s breath caught for a beat. She cleared her throat. “Sorry — we’re late.”

Taeyeon looked up, and her whole face bent into crescents. “It’s fine, we just got here too.” She hopped down and trotted over to claim Tiffany’s arm. “Fany-yah — how are you two arriving TOGETHER?”

“You know how Sica is with mornings~” Tiffany said, smiling, deftly omitting the crime scene.

He turned and inclined his head to both. “Tiffany nuna. Sica nuna. Annyeonghaseyo.”

“Ne, annyeonghaseyo.”

The returned greeting came in stereo, and carried one thin filament of awkwardness.

“Sit, please.” He gestured at the sofa across from the console. “Something to drink? Coffee? Tea?”

“Iced Americano.”

“Same.”

Taeyeon headed for the pantry — she knew where it was now, a small domestic fact she wore with visible pride — while he handed each guest a bound packet.

“Today’s mainly a walkthrough. Tiffany nuna — this is the full demo, lyrics, and the emotional brief for ‘Love Me Like You Do.’ Familiarize at your pace.”

“Okay.”

“Taeyeon nuna’s side comes after the theme’s recorded — hers is in English too, so the lyric memorization and pronunciation work need their own runway. Time stays comfortable that way.”

No objections. Standard OST cadence: two days to internalize a demo, ten to sixteen booth hours, roughly three days end to end — four, if the producer was chasing that last few percent of fusion between feeling and melody.

Tiffany bent over the lyric sheets; Jessica tilted in, reading over her shoulder.

“Nuna—” he asked, as Taeyeon returned balancing two iced Americanos, “—how do the next few days look for you two?”

“These two days are rest days.” She set the glasses down. “Then it’s world-tour prep — choreography, mostly, with variety and commercial slots scattered through.”

“Fany’s schedule is about the same as mine.”

He weighed it. “Tight. The director’s waiting on the songs for the edit.”

Tiffany looked up from the packet with a proposal already assembled. “Give me two days for lyrics and guide-track practice. Then we split into half-day blocks — say, I record mornings while Taeyeon rehearses at the company; she records afternoons while I rehearse. Six hours a day each, throat gets real rest, and the tour choreography survives one absent member per session.”

Elegant. Both trains kept running — and, privately, one more benefit she declined to say aloud: no full days spent watching Taeyeon and Ji-ho be luminous at each other across a mixing desk.

What she’d missed, of course, was the arithmetic’s remainder: subtract Taeyeon from the room, and what’s left is her, and him, alone. Possibly more dangerous, not less.

“That’s a great idea!” Taeyeon lit up. “Two consecutive full days of recording genuinely wrecks a throat. Singing properly is expensive.” She turned. “Ji-ho-ya, what do you think?”

He thought: not facing the girlfriend and the unresolved situation simultaneously — pressure halved. He said: “It works well. We’ll run it that way.”

“Then let’s hear the full ‘Love Me Like You Do’ demo now, and I’ll brief both songs — feel, and watch-points.”

He crossed to the console and pressed play.

DEMO · “Love Me Like You Do” · Ji-ho · 2013 — theme, The Girl in the Sun

The intro entered on solo piano — slow eighth-notes, light but weighted, the romantic-lazy atmosphere sealing the room within four bars. Through the verses the piano stayed the floor, simple chord movement underneath, and then the strings began arriving in strata: a low cello murmur under verse one, violins easing in at the pre-chorus, the full section unfurling at the chorus, cresting at the bridge — orchestration and voice heating in lockstep. The final section shed layers one by one, back down to the single piano line: end answering beginning, the afterglow doing the last of the work.

No heavy ballad drums anywhere. The rhythm ran on soft electronic kick and snare at an unhurried pulse, bass walking the low line beneath — support without intrusion, drift without drag. And the true design lived in the architecture: every entrance and exit of an instrument, every swell and retreat, mapped exactly onto the song’s emotional line — tentative warmth, rising heat, full surrender, gentle landing. The listener’s pulse was being conducted, and would never notice.

The last note thinned into silence. The studio held still for several seconds.

Then three women applauded at once.

“Wah — DAEBAK!” Taeyeon had gone pink with excitement, and the look she turned on him was pride with the density of syrup. “Ji-ho-ya — you — it’s INCREDIBLE! How are you like this?! It’s too good!”

Some songs only need one hearing to own you. This was one — a song with its chart position already written into it.

Jessica sat conquered. The fragment in the van had been impressive; the full build was a different order of thing entirely.

This is genuinely a great song. This person is genuinely — formidable. No wonder Taeyeon fell when he was still a trainee.

Tiffany’s hands had tightened on the score, nails printing small crescents into the paper.

Love me like you do — love me like YOU do. Ji-ho. Why are you this talented? Why did you save me? Why did you reach for me at all? Why — why are you TAEYEON’S? If you belonged to anyone else on this earth, I could fight for you. Anyone else. Why her?

“It’s — really beautiful.” Her voice came out slightly too taut. “Your talent still astonishes me.”

“It came from feeling. That’s most of it.” He deflected the weight with a small smile. “Next — the OST.”

He switched files.

DEMO · “Shallow” · Ji-ho · 2013 — OST, The Girl in the Sun · duet

The verses ran low and conversational, simple stepwise melody — two people talking quietly in the dark. The pre-chorus carried a wordless climbing refrain, mid-ranged and simple enough for anyone to hum, engineered to be the song’s most contagious cell. Then the chorus lifted hard, feeling breaking loose from restraint, the contrast doing exactly what contrast does.

The instrumentation stayed monastic: acoustic guitar, piano, light strings — no drums to speak of, no electronic ornament. Guitar laid the early ground; piano and strings arrived late and low; the voice stood at absolute center the whole way. The minimalism kept every ear pinned to the words and the feeling in them.

Shallow. A shallow place in the water — exactly where a small cat had once been stranded, in a film about being saved.

This wasn’t a single wearing a movie’s name. It was load-bearing narrative — melody, lyric and vocal line built flush against the story’s spine, the film’s designated sound-mark. Watch the film, be moved, hear the song, and the emotion would re-arrive on cue, image and music bound in one memory.

The last note dissolved. The studio went quiet again — differently this time.

Shallow water. As if Girls’ Generation had never run aground.

The Black Ocean years had nearly crushed them — stranded exactly like that cat, desperate and unhelped. The bewilderment at reality, the refusal of loneliness, the hunger for one honest feeling: anyone who had lived any of it would hear themselves in this song.

Taeyeon had carried the leader’s share of that era — pressure so deep she’d sleepwalked through the dorm shouting, “I’m Taeyeon, leader of Girls’ Generation — please give our team your support, thank you!” She’d run herself to the rim of empty keeping the group’s name alive.

Now her eyes had gone red at the edges.

“Sorry—” Her voice caught. “Restroom. One minute.”

“DaeDae — wait for me.” Tiffany was up in the same second, following her out.

“Nuna, I’ll walk you—”

“No need — I know the way. Corner, straight to the end, right.” A hand waved him back. “Go in. Don’t leave Sica sitting alone.”

“Okay.”

He watched them go, then turned back into the studio — where Jessica was watching him come through the door with an expression stacked several layers deep.

How is this man DOING this? Songs that reach straight through the sternum—

“Sica nuna,” he began. “About that time—”

“That time is over.” She cut across it, even-voiced. “We both had wrongs in it. You’re Taeyeon’s boyfriend; I’m Taeyeon’s sister. Shall we turn the page?”

A beat of surprise; a nod. “Done.”

“These two songs are truly well made.” She let the sentiment out with genuine weight. “The company saw your value back then too — that’s why they debuted you in three months. A pity they couldn’t keep you in the end.”

“Successful companies run on their own successful rules.” He smiled slightly. “And rules, by definition, can do some things and cannot do others. SM and I simply weren’t fated.”

She half-understood it, decided it was profound, and was still turning it over when—

He checked the screen and dipped his head apologetically. “Sorry — one call.”

“Go ahead.”

He stepped out into the corridor. The voice on the line arrived hesitant.

Voice Call · Sulli

“Yeoboseyo — Oppa. The car. Was it you?”

“Mm. It arrived?”

“It arrived… Oppa, I can’t accept this—”

“You’re not allowed to not accept it. A birthday present — an oppa buying his little sister a car, what of it? Not compensation. Not a debt.” A beat. “Sulli-yah. We’re people who went through life and death together. Let’s not do arithmetic over this. Okay?”

Silence held on the line a moment. A BMW in this market was punishingly expensive — import duties saw to that — and she hadn’t chosen him for any of this. But he wasn’t wrong either: between the two of them, counting money was the one thing that would cheapen it. If SHE ever gave HIM a gift, the price tag would be the last thing she’d weigh.

Voice Call · continued

“Color and model — do you like them? I had a feeling you’d want the red one.”

“…I like it.” — the surrender audible, and the smile inside it too.

“Then that’s everything. I’ve got things here — next time we meet, you drive it. Bye.”

He hung up before hesitation could reload, went back in, and dipped his head to Jessica again. “Nuna, apologies — two more minutes.”

“It’s fine. Go ahead.”

He sat and started thumbing out a message to Sulli — break-in mileage, first service, insurance registration, the practical liturgy of a new car.

✦   ✦   ✦

Meanwhile: the far end of the corridor.

Taeyeon and Tiffany, returning from the restroom, turned the corner — directly into Ji-eun and her manager.

The air crystallized on contact.

Taeyeon? At LOEN? Not even hiding anymore — are they going public?

Ji-eun’s face did nothing. Taeyeon’s and Tiffany’s faces also did nothing, at some cost. This particular hoobae had a documented history of provocation — and, if certain instincts were right, a documented interest in a certain producer.

“Annyeonghaseyo, Taeyeon sunbae-nim. Tiffany sunbae-nim.”

Flawless courtesy. Nothing to cite.

“Ne, annyeonghaseyo, Ji-eun-ssi.”

Standard-issue smile, returned. Ji-eun turned to her manager. “Oppa — go in first? I need a minute.”

He glanced at the three of them, developed an urgent interest in being elsewhere, and left.

“What brings Taeyeon sunbae to LOEN?” The tone was light; the eyes were not. “Girls’ Generation must be so busy right now.”

Taeyeon’s smile brightened by several unnecessary watts. “Ji-ho’s film. He wrote a song specially and invited me to sing it. A male-female duet, actually~”

Ji-ho. Ji-eun’s mouth twitched; her fist closed at her side. Ji-ho, you — one day it’s HER song, the next it’s — are you running a COURTSHIP DISTRIBUTION SERVICE—

Watching that expression curdle, Taeyeon’s stomach dipped — this woman really is circling my Ji-ho — and then, promptly, the pettier and far more satisfying thought arrived on its heels: Stole one of my songs, did you? He wrote me ANOTHER. Hmph.

Ji-eun rebuilt her face in a half-second and smiled. “Congratulations, sunbae. His duets do tend to work — ‘Some,’ the morning-glory song. Yours should do well too. After two hits in the format, the public trusts his pen completely.”

Taeyeon’s smile fixed in place.

Translation: he wrote ME a duet too, sunbae-nim. Two, actually. Which is one more than you.

This shameless woman! Stole my song and BRAGS about it!

Tiffany felt the barometric pressure drop, squeezed Taeyeon’s hand once in warning, and deployed her own smile straight at Ji-eun. “Oh — I keep seeing articles saying you and Ji-ho have some kind of conflict. No coffee truck for his shoot, no cross-promotion at all. You didn’t fight, did you?”

One frame of stiffness — then seamless recovery. “Sunbae jests. Ji-ho-ssi and I are strictly professional. As for support and promotion—” a small, unreadable pause, “—perhaps the timing simply hasn’t been right.”

Three women stood in the corridor with the air pulled wire-tight between them.

✦   ✦   ✦

In the studio, a chill walked up the back of his neck. He went still, listening. Ji-eun’s voice. And Taeyeon’s. And Tiffany’s.

No. NO. They’ve MET.

He dropped the phone and ran. The phone skidded off the console edge and landed at Jessica’s feet.

“Mwo-ya!”

She bent to retrieve it. The screen was still lit — an open chat with Sulli. She did not intend to read it. The eyes, unfortunately, are faster than intention: “Did the car key arrive?” — “Remember to register the plates.”

Sulli? A CAR? Sulli and — Ji-ho??

And then the phone buzzed in her hand, and a bank notification unfolded across the top of the screen. She swore afterward, to herself, that she had not meant to look. But that many zeros generates its own gravity. Her thumb moved before policy could veto it.

[KB Kookmin Bank]  On May 19, 10:23, your account received a transfer of ₩460,000,000,000 from [DREAM LIFE] Fund. Available balance: ₩461,504,010,453.39

She blinked. Counted the zeros. Counted them again, one at a time, moving her lips slightly.

Ones. Tens. Hundreds. Thousands… hundred million… ten billion… hundred billion—

FOUR HUNDRED SIXTY BILLION WON?? Balance: 461.5 BILLION???

She pinched her own thigh, hard.

“Ah! Appa!”

The pain reported: not a dream.

Ji-ho has — 461.5 billion won? A fund? WHAT fund? Investments?

Her mind whited out at the edges. She had money — real money, years of it, top-of-industry money. She had seen wealth. But 461.5 billion was not a number from her world: all nine of them, all these years, all combined, did not sum to it.

Her hands had begun to tremble. She backed out of the notification like a burglar leaving through the window she’d come in by, set the phone exactly where it had fallen — no. On the console. Natural. Casual. — and snapped her eyes back to the lyric book, seeing nothing.

Where does that kind of money COME from? And why is he buying SULLI a car—?

Her heart hammered like something trying to get out and file a report.

✦   ✦   ✦

In the corridor, he arrived mid-standoff at a walk he hoped read as casual, and inserted himself into the geometry with surgical placement — between all three of them at once.

“Ji-eun. Drama’s wrapped?”

She looked at him, eyes running several simultaneous calculations. “Not yet. I’m in today about the new single.”

“Ah.” He nodded — then, with clean, deliberate ease, set his arm around Taeyeon’s shoulders. “Taeyeon nuna’s recording the film’s OST. Tiffany nuna has the theme; Taeyeon nuna has the duet.”

The openness of it left nothing to grip. Ji-eun’s jaw set behind a courteous face.

“Then — I won’t intrude on your work.”

One last look at Taeyeon, unreadable to anyone who hadn’t watched her practice it — and she turned and disappeared into the practice room.

The door had barely shut before Taeyeon pinched his side. “You’re that FAMILIAR with her? ‘Ji-eun’??”

“Work relationship. Purely work.” Immediate, practiced. “Nuna — let’s go record. Sica nuna’s sitting in there alone.”

✦   ✦   ✦

The three of them returned to a studio in perfect order. Jessica had reassembled the calm of a lifetime’s practice and held the phone out to him. “You dropped this.”

“Thanks, nuna.” He pocketed it without a glance.

And so he failed to notice that the way she looked at him had — quietly, completely — changed.

It was no longer the look one gives “Taeyeon’s boyfriend,” nor even “the gifted producer.” It was the look reserved for a man with 461.5 billion won who hands out sports cars the way other people hand out coffee coupons.

Her fingers curled in her lap without instructions.

If all of that came from his own investing—

She thought of Hyo-yeon’s investment banker — the impressive one, the connected one — and almost laughed. Compared to THIS? What exactly is that banker supposed to be?

Across the room, Tiffany had stepped up to the mic to trial the chorus of “Love Me Like You Do,” her voice warm and magnetic, wrapping the melody’s want in something silked and true. Taeyeon sat at the console in monitor headphones, listening with her whole face; sunlight lay across her shoulders, the onyx blossom at her throat burning its small dark fire. He stood behind her, bending now and then to murmur a note into her ear.

The picture was, as ever, warm.

But Jessica sat with her melting iced Americano, took one slow sip, and understood that something in the room’s physics had permanently changed. The cold went down her throat and did nothing at all to the small fire that had started somewhere behind her sternum.

461.5 billion. A BMW, sent like a text message. And talent on top of all of it—

She lowered her eyes.

If he can invest like that — if he has capital like that — then the investor my brand actually needs… might be sitting right here.
