Chapter Forty-Six

Heartbeats and Ambiguity

That technique. That impossible combination of elements.

And then Taeyeon read the lyrics.

Performance

— the rewritten verses of “A Boy,” now carrying Ji-ho’s own life —

A kid who landed at eighteen and never once stopped running. Confidence too big for his circumstances, wielded as his sharpest weapon. Every climb promising a descent on the far side.

Boy — never forget the road that brought you here.

Looking back at the younger self with the burning eyes; the dream too hot to hold and too precious to drop.

Boy — take that remarkable voice and shout it at the world. Shine like the brightest star.

Every line was a dreamer’s molten sincerity poured straight onto the page — the whole vagabond stubbornness of trainee life, the reckless solitary courage of a boy chasing something enormous. That unbroken, untamable arrogance of youth practically burned through the paper and scalded the eyes.

She lowered the draft slowly, and her gaze went — entirely without permission — to the profile of the boy at the console.

Ji-ho was steady by temperament, but when he sank fully into work, a keep-out field of pure concentration settled around him — that particular magnetism of a person wholly inside his own world, competence and responsibility and focus fused into something that had nothing to do with the shape of a face and everything to do with why hearts complicate themselves.

Though the face, she conceded, was not hurting his case. Sword-straight brows. Clear eyes. A clean, high-bridged profile.

Her thoughts drifted backward on their own: the first meeting, that evaluation room, the voice that had genuinely startled her. Then the night he saved Sulli — the goodness of it, the nerve of it. Younger than her. Sang like that. Looked like that. Kind and brave on top of it. Professional-grade piano. And now — wrote his own songs.

Every item on the list landed square on the soft parts of Taeyeon’s heart, and a thought surfaced whole: Ji-ho is a buried treasure vault disguised as a person. Every single meeting, another drawer slides open. How is anyone supposed to NOT want to keep digging?

Her heart, uninstructed, began to sprint — hammering hard enough that she could hear it in her own ears, loud enough that it seemed one more beat would leap clean out of her chest and land in front of him.

She wrenched her eyes to the arrangement window on the monitor and ordered her pulse to behave. Her pulse declined. Her thoughts, for good measure, tangled themselves into yarn.

Ji-ho noticed none of it — brow faintly creased, lips pressed thin, fingers rattling across the keyboard in dense bursts while the tracks on screen shifted and stacked and turned, under his hands, into music.

The sunset came through the glass and lit him up at the console, and Taeyeon’s heart rate problem got measurably worse. She pressed a hand quietly over her sternum. Please. Just — slower. Please.

She picked up the spare monitoring headphones, settled them on, and closed her eyes to listen properly.

The acoustic snare laid the whole song’s rhythmic foundation — pure, insistent pulse. The hats snapped the groove into three dimensions, and her eyes flew open, openly shaken. You can DO that? But the ambush was the bagpipes: that strange, wind-scoured timbre threading a vein of desolate vastness through what should have been a straightforward pop-rap track — and in one stroke the melody stepped clean out of every cliché available to it and became a thing that existed nowhere else.

This is a masterclass. Every bar of this is a masterclass.

She fell in completely. Whatever girlish flutter had been occupying her chest was bought out, wholesale, by the older love — the music itself — and she sank into the world he was building and stopped keeping track of time.

At last the keyboard clatter stopped. He exhaled long, kneading the bridge of his nose.

“Done. Want to hear it through?”

She pulled the headphones off. Her cheeks were flushed, and her eyes, fixed on him, were doing something between sparkling and detonating.

“DAEBAK! Ji-ho — where did you even get the idea? Scottish bagpipes and classical piano, inside an electronic rap track?!”

“The haze and the fire together — it tracks the lyrics exactly, lost to determined! It’s BRILLIANT!”

He scratched the back of his head, looking genuinely — and a little guiltily — embarrassed.

“It’s okay, I guess. Pulled an all-nighter on it. The inspiration came in hot.”

“An ALL-NIGHTER?!” The delight flipped instantly to a frown of real concern. “You maniac. How is your body supposed to survive you?”

He didn’t answer that. He stood and headed for the recording booth instead.

“Let me run a scratch take. Tell me how the whole thing sits.”

The booth door clicked shut. Headphones on, mic height adjusted, an OK sign through the glass.

Performance

— the track opens: steady drums first, then that unmistakable voice sliding in over them — The early verses ride gentle: soft drum work under hazy electronic color, the sound of a boy remembering himself. The rap diction lands crisp and clean, edged with a young man’s unbent pride. As the lyric turns toward loneliness and the weight of carrying it alone, the pulse thickens — drums crowding in, the electronics straining brighter— — and at the summit, the line about shouting that voice at the world, the whole arrangement detonates at once: rhythm as pure release, the kind of drop that empties a listener’s chest for them.

At the console, Taeyeon sat fully submerged, hand keeping time without her.

“…Well?”

His voice through the talkback snapped her upright. The thumbs-up came up on pure reflex.

“DAEBAK! It’s a genuinely great song — yah, Ji-ho, what is your head MADE of? The arrangement instincts, the rhythm-emotion mapping — it’s stunning. Genuinely stunning.”

He came out of the booth wearing a mild, warm smile.

“Ease up on me — I know exactly how many flaws are still in it. One night’s work; the lyrics and the arrangement both have polish left to take. You’re the professional here. Give me your real notes?”

The praise-mode powered down; the professional powered up. She reached for the lyric draft and tapped two passages.

“These rhymes land stiff — they’ll snag the tongue at tempo. And the hook melody — it burns, but it’s flat-line burning. Give it more contour and the emotional climb will actually read.”

He nodded, dragged a chair over beside hers, and leaned in.

“You’re right — that’s exactly the spot that bothered me. What if it moved like… this?”

He talked as he wrote, pen scratching across the draft — and his breath, entirely incidentally, brushed warm across her ear.

Color bloomed instantly up her cheek. She leaned in anyway — composure worn like a costume — until she was all but resting against his side, eyes locked on the page with tremendous, unconvincing professionalism.

“And on the falsetto passage in the hook — I’ll thin the backing layers there, something soft under it so the falsetto’s texture carries. Nuna, what do you think?”

He turned his head. She looked up.

Two faces, suddenly, impossibly close — close enough to share breath, close enough to count the fine down along a cheek, close enough that skin registered the warmth of every exhale.

Both of them went perfectly still. Time, obligingly, stopped.

And into the silence, something unnamed and shimmering filled the air between them.
