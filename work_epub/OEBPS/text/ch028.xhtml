Chapter Twenty-Eight

Rule One of Bar Life: Know How to Fight

Tiffany moved before anyone — one leg shot out and Choi Jae went down over it, crashing across the floor. She grabbed Sulli’s arm in the same motion.

“Sulli, RUN — call the police when you’re safe!”

“Seong-hun hyung!”

The shout came from the floor — and the tattooed man detonated into motion. One stride, and his hand clamped Tiffany’s wrist and wrenched backward. Both women went down hard; Tiffany’s leg cracked against the table leg and the scream ripped out of her before she could bite it back. She curled around the leg, white-faced.

“Eonni — EONNI! Are you okay?!” Sulli was over her in an instant, hands fluttering, tears starting again.

Tiffany sucked air through her teeth. And Kim Hyo-yeon — dance-machine Kim Hyo-yeon, deceptively strong and done being polite — spat a curse and threw a punch.

“Ssibal son of a—!”

The tattooed man never saw it coming — not from a woman — and it landed flush, snapping his head sideways, staggering him two steps. The power on that— the accuracy— that came from a GIRL?

He spat blood, and whatever passed for restraint in him burned off all at once. The backhand caught Hyo-yeon full across the face.

CRACK!

She hit the floor, ears ringing, the room tilting, limbs refusing orders.

“Yah — NICHKHUN! What are you STANDING there for?!”

Tiffany’s shout jolted him loose. He roared and charged — and learned, in approximately four seconds, the difference between idol muscles and street muscles.

SLAP!

An open hand spun him half around, vision sparking. He gathered himself to swing back — and the tattooed man smashed a bottle on the table’s edge and leveled the glittering stump at his face.

“Squat in the corner, pretty boy — or I carve that face off and you can wave your idol career goodbye!”

Carve— the FACE?

Nichkhun’s entire livelihood flashed before his eyes. Heroics had a time and place — for a girlfriend, sure, he’d bleed. But Tiffany was a maybe, a might-be, one drink deep — and the face was a career. Gamble and win, get the girl; gamble and lose, lose everything. The math finished computing, and he put his hands on his head and squatted.

Tiffany stared at him from the floor, and something curdled permanently. The “Beast Idols.” THIS is the beast? She understood the calculation — everyone in this industry ran it — but understanding wasn’t respect. A man who wouldn’t stand up now would vanish the day a dating rumor broke. Whatever this evening had been auditioning for, the audition was over.

Choi Jae picked himself up off the floor and started toward Sulli, unhurried now. The drugged glass was in his own stomach — but the pills needed time, and he’d be long gone before they dragged him under. All that mattered was the phone. Destroy the phone, and tonight became her word against his. Shame about the girl, though. Such prime goods, wasted. No luck at all.

“Stay away from me!” Sulli scrambled backward across the floor, phone clutched to her chest.

“Give me the phone, you little—” He closed in, crouched, grabbed.

“HELP! Somebody—!”

Someone. Anyone. Please—

The booths were beautifully soundproofed. He knew it, and his lip curled.

“Scream. Go on — scream your throat out, princess. Nobody’s coming. Now GIVE me the—”

He was prying her fingers back one by one, her thin wrists no match for him, the phone sliding out of her grip—

“SOMEBODY — HELP — ME—!”

BANG!

The door blew open.

Ji-ho took in the room in one photographic instant — Tiffany down, Hyo-yeon down, Nichkhun squatting, Sulli pinned — and was already moving, two running steps and every kilo of him behind the kick.

“AGH—!”

Choi Jae left the ground, bounced twice, and fetched up against the table base in a heap.

Hands on hips, chest heaving, Ji-ho glared down at the girl on the floor.

“You — are you missing the part of the brain that does risk assessment?! You can call the police from ANYWHERE! Who runs BACK into the—”

She looked up at him through swimming eyes — and from down there, backlit by the doorway, he seemed to be quietly giving off light. Something in her chest unclenched for the first time all night. Safe. The word arrived from nowhere. Safe.

“I’m sorry— he— I just— hic—” It dissolved into sobbing.

The tattooed man hauled Choi Jae upright. Choi Jae wiped his mouth and glared murder across the room.

“Yah. Ssibal. And who the hell are YOU?”

“Weren’t you looking for my phone?” Ji-ho helped Sulli up, lifted the phone from her hands, and tucked her behind him. Then he crooked two fingers at the men, and his smile had teeth in it. “I shot the video, too. Come and get it.”

“SSIBAL!”

Choi Jae snatched an unopened bottle off the table and charged.

“Too slow.”

Ji-ho slid off the line of the swing and drove a kick into his gut that folded him in half and launched him backward. He rolled twice, met the floor with his face — and, the sleeping pills in his bloodstream choosing that exact moment to file their report, went magnificently, bonelessly unconscious.

“YAAAH!!”

The tattooed man came on swinging the broken bottle, glass edge strobing under the booth lights, fast and mean and experienced.

But tonight he’d drawn Ji-ho.

Ji-ho flowed around the first slash, the second, reading the rhythm — then the opening arrived and he took it, one vicious rising snap-kick, dead on target.

THUD.

“AAAAAGH—!!!”

The bottle hit the carpet. The man folded around his own center of gravity, knees clamping, face cycling through several previously undiscovered colors, breath whistling in and out in tiny sips.

In his corner, Nichkhun felt a phantom chill pass through his entire lower body. …I felt that from HERE.

“Ssi… bal…”

Impossibly, the man raised the bottle again, knees still pressed together, shuffling forward like a penguin with a grudge — and Ji-ho’s long leg swept up in a flying arc and cracked across his neck. He dropped where he stood and commenced sleeping.

Ji-ho dusted his palms off, unimpressed.

“That’s it?”

In bar life, a weak liver was forgivable. Weak fists were not. He’d been marinating in nightlife since sixteen — fourteen straight years of it. Truth be told, the singing had always been the side hustle; the real trade he’d mastered was this one. He’d only gone respectable the last couple of years.

From the floor, Tiffany gazed up at him through her lashes, pain momentarily forgotten, eyes gone glittery. So THIS is the hero-saves-the-beauty thing. Oh no. Oh no, he’s so cool—

Ji-ho crossed to the snoring Choi Jae and fished out his phone. Slide-to-unlock — no passcode, standard for the era. He opened the photo gallery, looked for exactly one second, and clicked his tongue.

Yeah. That’s a prison sentence. That’s Jung Joon-young numbers — six years minimum, and that’s with a good lawyer.
