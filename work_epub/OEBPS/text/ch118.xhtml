Chapter One Hundred Eighteen

Running Man — The X-Man Special (I)

August 13, 2012 Incheon — Garden Stage Behind a Department Store a flower gate, a lift platform, and twenty-odd cameras

A handsome little raised stage had been built in the garden — an archway wound solid with fresh flowers — and behind it, on the lift platform, Ji-ho and IU waited for their cue.

Out front, the members were running the cold open. After a round of banter, Lee Kwang-soo studied the flower stage with theatrical gravity.

“Staging on this level… we’ve only ever done this when Ha Ji-won nuna came.”

“True, true,” the others chorused.

“And when Ji-sung came,” Haha added.

“Right, right — Park Ji-sung tier,” Ji Suk-jin clapped, illuminated.

Expectant faces all around. Yoo Jae-suk eyed the flower gate.

“So who IS today’s guest? Please — come out!”

The platform hummed to life. IU — who had chosen tall stiletto heels specifically so the opening two-shot next to Ji-ho wouldn’t read as a height chart — was in the middle of sneaking a look at him: grey minimalist tee, black wide-leg trousers, white sneakers, 183 centimeters making casualwear look like a campaign. The sudden lurch of the lift caught her mid-peek; she staggered back two steps with a yelp.

“Eomma—!”

His arm was around her before thought. She landed against his chest.

“WOW!”

“MWO-YA!”

The members produced enormous cartoon shock — and not all of it was performed. Everyone had met that morning; the surprise was scripted. Ji-ho and IU ascending into frame in a full embrace was not.

Kwang-soo clocked the stilettos, processed at champion speed, and deployed his most disreputable grin.

“Ooooh~ SOME?”

“Wow~ SOME!” The pile-on was instant — atmosphere, talking point, and free song promotion in one motion.

IU, a Running Man regular since the June episode with Park Ji-sung, stepped out of his arms, laughing, waving it down.

“Aniya! My heels are just too tall today! The lift moved and I lost my footing — Ji-ho saved me.”

Not a small thing, either — if IU had actually fallen, her fandom would have arrived at the production office with torches by nightfall.

Haha wheeled on the PDs in performative fury.

“YAH! Who did that?!”

“WHO pushed the button?! You’re DEAD today!” Kwang-soo charged after him.

“Aigoo, Haha oppa—” IU covered her face, laughing under the burden. “I’m FINE. Don’t overdo it.”

“Aigoo — Ji-eun-ah, Ji-ho-ssi, just come down first,” Yoo Jae-suk called, right on time.

“Ne.”

He helped her down the flower steps, one careful stair at a time. Song Ji-hyo watched him descend with an unconcealed, dreamy grin — until Gary’s face soured beside her.

“Ji-hyo-yah. Don’t forget what day it is. It’s MONDAY.”

She burst out laughing despite herself.

Introductions done, Yoo Jae-suk arranged Ji-ho and IU center, planted himself beside Ji-ho, looked at the guest, looked at himself, and sighed from the soul.

“Ji-ho-ssi is… a COMPLETE flower boy. Look at this. LOOK at this. The skin is genuinely glowing.”

“Shining. Actually shining.”

“And this muscle line? Wah. Unacceptable.”

“Truly too handsome.”

Variety grammar: everything oversized — words, faces, gestures. Every member was now beholding him like a man staring into headlights. And in front of him: twenty-plus camera positions. His first big outdoor variety, and no idea which lens to feed. Optimal strategy: smile at all of them.

Song Ji-hyo’s eyes sparkled; she covered her mouth.

“Daebak. He’s even MORE handsome when he smiles!”

Gary clutched his chest and toppled backward, mortally wounded. Kwang-soo caught him in a panic and went for his philtrum with a rescue-pinch; Gary recovered with miraculous speed.

Oversized, yes — but outdoor variety is an economy of screen time, and this entire exchange was now un-cuttable. Everyone had done their job.

And then Yoo Jae-suk earned his title. Having registered the flicker of lens-lostness on the rookie’s face, the nation’s MC smoothly rotated the spotlight.

“Ji-eun-ah, life’s been good lately, hm? Not just the nation’s little sister anymore — now it’s DIGITAL QUEEN.”

“Aniyo, Jae-suk oppa exaggerates~” — shy, and precisely one degree smug — “actually, today I’m mostly here to introduce a very important music partner.”

She stepped aside and ceded him the center.

“Exactly!” Jae-suk lifted his voice to the cameras. “The man who swept every chart in the country with one song and dragged the entire nation into ambiguity — SONG JI-HO!”

“WAAAH!!!” The members delivered maximum-courtesy pandemonium.

“‘Some,’ right?” Kim Jong-kook stroked his chin, sizing Ji-ho up. “It’s been on loop in my car. Genuinely — the mood improves the second the intro starts.”

“What, does it remind you of Eun-hye?” Jae-suk, with the oldest knife in the drawer.

The Eun-hye card. Jong-kook broke on impact, tipping his head back at the sky with a long-suffering exhale while the members howled. Every time. The Capable One never survives that name.

Jokes settled, Jae-suk resumed captaincy.

“I hear Ji-ho-ssi doesn’t just sing — words, music, arrangement, the whole craft. A genuine all-round musician. ‘Some’ is entirely his own writing, and with Ji-eun the chemistry turned out to be explosive. Isn’t that right?”

An encouraging look. Ji-ho took the handoff with a small bow.

“Ne — thank you, sunbae-nims. Working with Ji-eun nuna on ‘Some’ was an honor. In the booth, she handed me more inspiration than I brought.”

“SEE? See?!” Haha leapt out, jabbing a finger. “Handsome, talented, AND humble! Yah — how are the rest of us supposed to LIVE?!”

“Ji-ho-ssi, you’re the trend of trends lately — ‘Some’ playing on every street. Must be exhausting?” Jae-suk asked, all seniorly warmth.

“Ne — much busier than before. I’m grateful for all of it.”

“First time on our Running Man — nervous? Our Ji-eun’s a veteran here. Did she hand down any secret tips before you came?”

Ji-ho’s eyes curved, recalling something. He glanced at IU — who was pressing her lips together suspiciously — then turned back to Jae-suk with a tone pitched exactly between earnest and deadpan.

“Ji-eun nuna did tell me quite a lot—”

“Oh? Such as?” Jae-suk leaned in. Every ear on the lawn rotated.

“She said—” a pause, calibrated, until the whole clearing hung on it, “—whatever you do, build a good relationship with Jae-suk sunbae-nim.”

“HAHAHA!” Jae-suk threw his head back, delighted, pounding Ji-ho’s shoulder. “SEE! Our Ji-eun has EYES! Correct! Befriend ME!”

“Yah — why only Jae-suk hyung?!” Jong-kook flexed an arm in protest. “Befriend ME and you WIN the games.”

“Exactly! We matter too!” Gary and Haha filed their objections.

“Ji-eun-ah…” Kwang-soo assumed his most bereaved face. “Was I not good to you? Why only Jae-suk hyung?”

Chaos, laughter, everyone talking at once — and through it, Jae-suk, still glowing, pressed the follow-up:

“And did she say WHY you should befriend me specifically?”

In the middle of the din, Ji-ho kept his face immaculate and detonated the payload with the calm of a man reading a weather report.

“Ji-eun nuna said — because Jae-suk sunbae-nim is very generous. To the hoobaes he likes… he buys CARS.”

One second of frozen air.

Then—

“PUHAHAHA!!!”

“Aigoo!! My STOMACH!”

“A CAR?! YAH! Beautifully done, Ji-eun-ah!!”

Total collapse. Jae-suk stood slack-jawed, then tipped his head back to the heavens in existential surrender, wheezing — then leveled a finger at IU, who had taken refuge behind Ji-ho, laughing so hard her eyes had vanished. Ji Suk-jin, Haha, and Gary staggered into each other; Kwang-soo went down entirely, beating the grass.

The famous car-gift bit from IU’s episode with Park Ji-sung — resurrected through the mouth of a debut-fresh rookie, delivered with a straight face and perfect timing. The cameras drank in every collapsing face, and then found Ji-ho’s expression: one faintly arched eyebrow, sly and somehow still wholesome.

The opening was fully lit. On his first outdoor variety, Ji-ho had caught the nation’s MC’s serve, returned it with topspin, and manufactured his own first highlight — a made member of the family inside fifteen minutes.
