Chapter Two

Dream Life: The Simulation

The light thinned and faded. Ji-ho lowered his hand and slowly opened his eyes.

A crosswalk stretched out before him — familiar and foreign at once. Pedestrians streamed past. Traffic hummed.

“…Where am I?”

He stood there, dazed, half-convinced he was dreaming. Then the phone in his hand started to ring, and he answered it on pure reflex.

Incoming call · Unknown number

“Hello?”

“Hello — is this Ji-ho?” The voice on the other end spoke careful, accented English.

“Speaking.”

“Hi, Ji-ho. I’m calling from SM — trainee development. We have your audition scheduled for today, so I wanted to confirm. Have you arrived?”

Ji-ho pinched his own thigh. Hard.

“Ssss—!”

Pain knifed through him, sharp enough to make him suck in a breath. It was vivid. Unmistakably, gloriously real. Then this isn’t a dream?

“Ji-ho? Are you all right?” The voice came again, gently concerned.

“Fine — I’m fine.” He wrestled the storm in his chest down by main force, steadied his breathing, kept his voice level. “I’m in Seoul now. Barring accidents, I’ll be at the SM building within the hour.”

“Great. I’ll have everything arranged on this end. See you soon.”

The call ended. Ji-ho stood rooted to the pavement, gaze drifting unfocused across the streetscape — familiar, foreign, both. This was the day of my last SM audition. The final one, before I gave up.

Back then, every company had turned him away for the same tangle of reasons. Eighteen was already late for a trainee. His dancing had been embarrassingly loose — a handful of moves copied off videos, held together with nothing. His singing had leaned entirely on a God-given throat, with no systematic training behind it; everything he knew, he’d scraped together himself in smoky bars. An overseas kid with no family, no backers, no safety net. Company after company had weighed it all and quietly passed.

If not for a sharply cut, handsome face and a hundred and eighty-two centimeters of height, he wouldn’t have made it past the door even once.

If time had sent him back to fourteen instead — with an age advantage like that, any company would have signed him on the spot, polished him for a few years, and debuted him as a matter of course. But eighteen, in the brutally competitive world of idol trainees, was already “elderly.” The difficulty curve went vertical.

For five years he had watched a character with his own life story blaze across a virtual stage, living out the dream chapter he’d never gotten to write. Of course he’d dreamed of starting over — everyone does, at three in the morning. But it had never felt like this. Not like something he could reach out and touch.

Around him, passersby chattered in Korean, the city’s particular melody threading through it. A soft spring breeze carried the scent of cherry blossoms — that faint, distinctive fragrance like crushed almonds folded into something green and new — and it curled around him and refused to leave.

Real. It was all devastatingly real.

Ding— “Game initialized. We wish the player a pleasant experience.”

The synthetic chime went off inside his skull like a thunderclap. Ji-ho flinched — then understood. Of course. The game. That impossible, unexplainable game had carried him back to the exact fork in the road.

He glanced around, found no one watching, and slipped into a quiet side alley. There, he drew a breath and recited solemnly in his mind: Dream Life: The Simulation.

A pane of soft light unfolded before his eyes — the interface he knew better than his own face. At its center stood a lifelike three-dimensional model of himself, dressed in a plain tank top and shorts, radiating a kind of scrappy, unpolished youth.

Player Status

Player: Song Ji-ho

Charm: 44 (Looks 8 · Physique 5 · Voice 7 · Presence 5 · Experience 6 · Social 5 · Confidence 3 · Learning 5 · Wealth 0 · Fame 0)

Identity: Dreamer

Height: 182 cm  ·  Weight: 63 kg

Constitution: 65  ·  Cognition: 63

— Professional Skills —

Vocal: 54  ·  Dance: 32  ·  Acting: 41  ·  Composition: 12

Instruments: Guitar, Grade 6

Wardrobe: None  ·  Partner: 0/1

Fans: 116  ·  Style Index: 30

Ji-ho knew this screen the way monks know scripture.

Charm was the crown stat: push it past certain thresholds and the game unlocked rewards — precious ones, the kind that bent a career’s whole trajectory. As for the professional skills, a 50 was respectable by civilian standards, but in this industry it meant exactly one thing: you had barely found the door.

Five years had blurred plenty of details, but two things he remembered with crystal clarity — the newbie gift pack, and the main quest line.

The interface itself was clean and spare. As the quests advanced, more modules would unlock — Wardrobe, the Shop, and the rest — each one another rung on the ladder.

Quest Log

[Main Quest] Become a trainee. Officially begin your journey toward the dream.

Reward: one professional-grade instrument skill, chosen at random.

In the Inventory, a lone newbie gift pack sat in an otherwise empty grid — quietly waiting, holding all the possibility in the world.

Ji-ho stared at it and drew a slow breath. He couldn’t recall exactly what was inside anymore. He only knew, bone-deep, that for a beginner standing at the bottom of the mountain, this pack could rewrite everything.

“Open the newbie pack.”

At his silent command the pack shivered, as if a current had passed through it — then bloomed open.

Rewards Acquired

[God of Learning · Lv.1] — Each day, from 20:00 to 21:00, anything you study is engraved into memory as if branded there, unfading for twenty years. Note: this skill can be leveled up.
