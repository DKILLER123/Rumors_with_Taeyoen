Chapter One Hundred Thirty-Three

As Long as the Face Holds, the Kingdom Holds

That Evening Taegi’s Shellfish Hot Pot — Private Room two trainees, one verdict about a face

Irene and Wendy had arrived early to the reserved room.

“Eonni, Ji-ho says order first — he’s almost here.” Seung-wan slid the menu over and stood. “Bathroom run.”

Joo-hyun nodded, eyes on the menu and mind elsewhere. The MV invitation had come through the company two days ago — opinions solicited, permission unstated. Tonight’s dinner presumably carried the verdict. For a trainee, a slot in a hit artist’s MV was gold: precious exposure, and serious weight in evaluations. She wanted it. And facing the hoobae who had debuted into a bonfire while she still trained in the dark — the wanting came tangled with things she couldn’t name.

In the bathroom mirror, Seung-wan inspected her new lip gloss — a soft rose that flattered her skin — took a breath, and acknowledged the obvious: somewhere along the line, she had started caring, very much, how she looked in front of Ji-ho.

Back in the room, he’d arrived — jacket off, white shirt, easy.

“Sorry — traffic.” He took the seat across, natural as ever.

“It’s fine! We just got here too.” Seung-wan, instantly, cheeks warming — and she caught his glance resting on her face for exactly one beat.

Orders in; broth bubbling; the seafood smell unfurling.

“The MV,” he opened. “I spoke with Lee Soo-man seonsaeng-nim directly. It’s approved — you’re both in.”

“REALLY?! That’s amazing!” Seung-wan lit up like a signboard.

“Really. Production meetings and shooting dates — I’ll send everything ahead.”

She beamed, arranging his utensils with conspicuous devotion.

“Ji-ho. You’re genuinely the best. This is such a huge chance for us.”

And she meant every syllable. SM’s debut race was a meat grinder; a credit in a mega-hit MV could be the coin-flip difference between making the new girl-group project and not. From same-age company friends, to practice partners, to THIS — the ledger of small kindnesses in her chest kept getting longer.

“Thank you for the opportunity,” Joo-hyun added — warmer than usual, and still three sizes more restrained than Seung-wan.

“We’re chingu, aren’t we?” He spread his hands, collecting his due shamelessly. “I’ve scraped together a little standing — of course I think of you two. And when I open my company later, don’t forget to come rescue me. We DID bet on it.”

“Aigoo, relax.” Seung-wan patted her chest. “You’re this loyal — bet or no bet, when I’m a superstar I’ll come carry you.”

“I’ll hold you to it.” He smiled — and his gaze touched her glossed lips a moment. “The lip color today suits you, Seung-wan. Really.”

Her face went scarlet on a switch; her fingers flew to her mouth; sweetness flooded in behind the embarrassment.

“R-really? I— it’s a new shade, I just bought it—”

“Mm. It flatters your skin.” Then, evenly, to the other side of the table: “Joo-hyun nuna’s skin is looking great too.”

A pale smile; no comment.

The pot boiled; the talk flowed — Seung-wan carrying it with practice-room stories, him volleying back, Joo-hyun quiet, occasionally serving Seung-wan vegetables.

Halfway through, Joo-hyun’s chopsticks slipped to the floor. She bent — he bent in the same instant — and under the table their hands met: her fingers cool, his fingertips warm. A half-second of contact, and both of them paused on it. She looked up into his eyes at close range under the warm light — and Dongdaemun came back without permission: being pulled into his chest, the temperature of him, the arm, the heartbeat, that specific unreasonable safety.

“Did you find them?” Seung-wan’s voice broke the circuit.

“Mm. Found them.” Joo-hyun surfaced fast, sat straight, ears faintly warm. He resettled too, expression administratively normal.

✦   ✦   ✦

After the meal, Seung-wan — freshly reminded of her praised lip gloss — rose to touch up her makeup, and the private room went quiet around two people and a dead flame under the pot.

“Seung-wan says you’ve been training even harder these two months,” he said, breaking it.

She dabbed her mouth with a napkin, and took her time.

“Because effort is all I have.”

“I don’t have your kind of talent. Same late start — nineteen, both of us. You trained three months and had a debut project. The contract was unfair, so you WALKED, joined a new company, and detonated. I’m not jealous, I just—”

The sentence ran out of road. Envy, refusal to yield, self-doubt, and one unclassifiable thread of closeness — she couldn’t untangle it herself.

“Forget it.” A self-mocking half-smile. “Call it jealousy of your talent. Since I can’t DO what you do, all that’s left is to work harder.”

He looked at her: the faint fatigue under the light, and above it those big stubborn shining eyes.

“Nuna. Everyone has their own weapon. And training like THIS will wreck your body long before it fixes anything.”

“Then tell me.” A rare crack of vulnerability showed through. “What IS my weapon? What am I actually good at?”

The question jammed him for a second. She worked like a machine; her dancing was solid; her singing balanced. But honestly? In a company like SM she faced hard math — petite frame, proportions the trainers nitpicked, the unkind nicknames. Among the future lineup: Seul-gi danced, Seung-wan sang, Soo-young was built like an argument. And Irene’s single overwhelming weapon really was… that face. People said “as long as the face holds, the kingdom holds” — and wasn’t that saying also a kind of cage?

He scratched his head, and chose honesty.

“Nuna’s weapon is this face. It is genuinely, unfairly beautiful.”

“…The face.” Her expression froze — an answer that flattered and wounded in the same stroke.

“Right,” he pushed on, trying for lightness. “Debut, and the face alone carries you. As they say — as long as the face holds, the kingdom holds.”

He heard how it landed and opened his mouth to patch it — and she laughed first: a small sound, bitter at the front, strangely unburdened at the back.

“Thank you for being honest. At least I know I still have a face.”

And then his voice changed register entirely.

“But nuna — the face is only the ticket. How far you go runs on other fuel: your stubbornness. Your work. How much you love the stage. I’ve watched all of it.”

She went still.

“SM has no shortage of beautiful people,” he said. “But people who know their own weaknesses EXACTLY and grind at them anyway, without an ounce of self-pity — those are rare. THAT’S the most valuable thing you own.”

The door opened; Seung-wan returned, refreshed.

“What are you two talking about? The air in here is SO serious.”

“Nothing much.” Joo-hyun recovered first — and turned on him a smile with nothing held back in it. “Effort and talent. That’s all.”

That smile — first thaw, first bloom. No, he thought. He hadn’t misspoken at all. That face was, in fact, heaven’s best gift to Irene.

✦   ✦   ✦

The night’s business concluded: Seung-wan and Joo-hyun confirmed; Kim Ji-won’s agency had accepted the formal invitation without a blink — a lightly-known rookie’s company doesn’t refuse a chart-conqueror’s MV.

Logistics notes accumulating in the back of his head: buy a second car — this one was getting recognized; keep the old one as a decoy commuter. Upgrade the apartment to a proper secure building. All of which meant the personal manager and assistant question was now officially overdue.

He dropped the two trainees back at SM, and pointed the car at Banpo-dong. Taeyeon wasn’t home yet; he opened the laptop and went back to futures mechanics and currency-volatility theory, the God of Learning quietly branding every page in place. Memorization wasn’t mastery — but a full theoretical arsenal would be more than enough for one November trade.

Dusk turned to lamplight without his noticing.

Taeyeon came home to a lit apartment — and the specific, particular warmth of lights that meant someone waiting.

“Ji-ho-ya — I’m home.”

Silence. She set down her bag, stepped out of her heels, padded through the rooms — bedroom neat, kitchen empty — and found the study door ajar with a blade of light under it. Inside: Ji-ho in noise-canceling headphones, eyes locked to a screen where a white-haired old lecturer walked through red-and-green candlestick charts she couldn’t read and didn’t need to.

She crept up and laid a hand on his shoulder.

“Ji-ho-ya.”

“Hm?” Headphones off; face lighting. “Nuna’s home.”

One arm around her waist, one easy pull, and she was sideways across his lap, arms going around his neck for a long soft kiss.

“What’s got you concentrating that hard?”

“Investment lectures.” His fingers moved through her hair. “Radio recording ran long? You look wrung out.”

“Mmm~” A boneless sound; she melted into him. “The guest wouldn’t stop TALKING, and then they added an extra interview— I’m dead.”

“Hard worker.” His palm traveled her back, warm. “Want a massage?”

“YOU know massage?” Open suspicion.

“Underestimating me again?” He arched a brow. “It’s my best event.”

✦   ✦   ✦

What followed was, despite her heckling, legitimate technique: calves worked slowly along the muscle line, then hamstrings, the tight band at her lower back, the thin blades of her shoulders — ten minutes under his hands and the day’s whole inventory of tension dissolved. She lay flattened into the mattress like warmed butter, producing, at one point, a small melodic hum with a main-vocal’s chest resonance in it — then heard herself, and buried her burning face in the pillow while his mouth curved and his hands kept working.

“Nuna. The jeans are in the way — let me—”

“ANDWAE!” She catapulted across the bed like a startled rabbit and sat up clutching a pillow. “Thank you!! I feel MUCH better!!”

She fled with a bundle of sleepwear, leaving a trail of flustered footsteps. He laughed at the empty doorway.

In the bathroom she leaned against the door, exhaled at length, and waited for her pulse to acknowledge management. The hot water ran; steam fogged the mirror; and just as the warmth had smoothed her out entirely — the lock turned, quietly.

The rest of the evening was not recorded. The moon, for its part, went behind a cloud and stayed there — and in one small warm room, everything that was tired, pressed, and distant dissolved into water-sound and lamplight.
