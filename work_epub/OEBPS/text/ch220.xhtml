Chapter Two Hundred Twenty

A Rooster for the Monkeys

July 29, 2013 LOEN Entertainment HQ, Seoul · SHIN & KIM, top floor ₩310 billion on the table, a vice president looking for a wall to run into — and a corridor full of witnesses

Gentle. Slow. A small waist swaying like a metronome set to largo. Ordinarily, Choi Sulli didn’t much care for Ji-ho at his most careful — too polite, not enough weather. But tonight was different. Maybe it was the day she’d had, but the long, unhurried tenderness of it landed as something else entirely: like being a rare treasure unwrapped a centimeter at a time, examined, and found priceless.

The night thickened; the house settled into even breathing.

When Ji-ho came back from rinsing off, Tiffany was already deep asleep in the main bedroom, chestnut hair fanned across the pillow like seaweed, her sleeping face serene except for the small, stubborn crease between her brows — as if even her dreams refused to sit still.

He lifted the corner of the duvet and eased in, gathering the warm weight of her against him. She made a small sound without waking, arm finding his waist on autopilot, cheek nuzzling to its usual spot over his chest.

“Sweetheart… where’d you go?”

“A walk. Took a work call.” His hand moved slowly over her hair. “Mianhae. Didn’t mean to wake you.”

“Gwaenchana…”

She mumbled it and sank straight back under.

✦ ✦ ✦

July 29, 2013. Seoul.

The floor-to-ceiling glass of Ji-ho’s personal studio in the LOEN building took in the whole skyline. He stood at the window, back straight, a tall solitary line against the afternoon light.

In the week just past, his name had swept the media at a pace that had stopped being news and become weather:

THE WEEK IN NUMBERS · SONG JI-HO · LATE JULY 2013

“Love Me Like You Do” — Billboard Hot 100#13 peak · 3 weeks on chart

“Shallow” — Billboard Hot 100#15 peak · 3 weeks on chart

The Girl in the Sun — cumulative admissions11,970,000

7All-time box office (passing Haeundae and Taegukgi)and still climbing

A pure love story at number seven in peninsula film history. Song Ji-ho: the undisputed phenomenon of the summer.

And as he stood watching the city, the familiar chime arrived on schedule.

DREAM LIFE — MILESTONE REWARD

Ding~ Core fanbase has passed 500,000. Rewards issuing—

AcquiredPREMIUM ALBUM SALES BOOST CARD — designated album sales +50%

AcquiredADVANCED FILM EDITING — frame rate, transitions, scoring, color grading at fingertip command; cuts land seamless

AcquiredWARDROBE · BORN ARISTOCRAT — black tailored suit, black leather shoes, black tie, white shirt, gold-rimmed glasses, haute mechanical watch

Special effectPresence suppression: an untouchable, keep-your-distance air in any room. Staff loyalty sharply up. Negotiation success rate significantly up. (Scales with player wealth.)

Next reward tier unlocks at: 1,000,000 core fans.

Half a million core fans. Not the froth of social-media follower counts — the real thing: people who would spend, show up, and hold the line no matter what he released next. Albums, concerts, endorsements, merchandise… fifty myeon of bedrock. Something in him settled another centimeter into the ground.

Knock, knock.

“Come in.”

Ryu Dong-hwan entered — and the usual crisp competence on his face had a hesitation folded into it today.

“Ji-ho. The album songs… coming along?”

“Got some sparks. Sit, hyung.” He waved him to the sofa and took the seat opposite, posture easy. “Say what you came to say.”

Dong-hwan rubbed his hands together, then committed.

“Ji-ho. Any chance you have time to write… two OSTs?”

One eyebrow ticked up. Dong-hwan knew perfectly well he was heads-down on the album. If he was raising OSTs now, someone was pressing on him — and the pressure was aimed at this office.

“OSTs?” The word came back with a faint, entertained curl. “Go on.”

“First one — Bon Factory production, the Hong sisters writing, Jin Hyuk directing. So Ji-sub and Gong Hyo-jin starring. The Master’s Sun. Airs in August. Second — SBS, directed by Kang Shin-hyo, written by Kim Eun-sook, Lee Min-ho and Park Shin-hye starring. The Heirs. October broadcast.”

“Heh. Interesting.” A soft laugh. “Everyone wants a lungful of my wind. The Master’s Sun airs in AUGUST — the theme song should’ve been locked ages ago. Swapping composers this late — aren’t they worried about burning the original one?”

Dong-hwan’s voice dropped.

“Word is the production company didn’t like what the original composer turned in. The Bon Factory boss and our Vice President Yoo Jae-ha are childhood friends — so it came down through Vice President Yoo. As a favor.”

“Vice President Yoo—” His fingertip tapped once on the sofa arm.

“Right.” Dong-hwan nodded, and added the warning label. “He runs publicity, licensing, AND human resources. That’s a lot of building. We… can’t afford to cross him.”

“We,” the manager said. Noted.

“Can’t afford to?” Something cold surfaced briefly in Ji-ho’s eyes. “So this is a company ORDER?”

“It’s… a request,” Dong-hwan amended, visibly uncomfortable.

“Hahā—” He laughed outright, shaking his head. “A man asking a favor who won’t show his own face? Curious way to ask. And The Heirs? Also Vice President Yoo’s ‘request’?”

“That one, no. SBS came direct, and writer Kim Eun-sook personally passed along her hopes. That’s a favor worth selling.”

Ji-ho was quiet a moment. Then, mildly:

“Hyung. Reading between the lines — our Vice President Yoo does a fair amount of business on the company’s letterhead and his own behalf, doesn’t he.”

Dong-hwan’s face flickered.

“There’s… talk, sure. But he’s capable — the CEO paid heavy money to bring him in four years ago. When he asks for something, the smart play is to just… do it.”

“Is that so?” The pleasant, unbothered expression came back down over Ji-ho’s face like a shopfront shutter. “Then decline The Master’s Sun. The Heirs OST — I’ll take it.”

“What?” Dong-hwan’s head snapped up, certain he’d misheard. “You’re serious?”

“My Korean’s not that bad, hyung.”

“Ani, that’s not what I—” He was flailing now. “If you refused BOTH, we could spin a reason! But taking one and rejecting one — rejecting the one the vice president asked for HIMSELF — that’s not declining, that’s slapping him across the face in public! There’s no version of this where crossing him helps us! Think it over—”

“Nothing to think over.” Ji-ho rose and moved behind his desk. “Send exactly that answer. I know what I’m doing.”

Dong-hwan looked at the calm, closed profile, recognized a door with no handle on his side, and left trailing one long, heartfelt sigh of dread.

Only when the door clicked shut did the cold rise fully into Ji-ho’s eyes. Capable? A trusted senior officer? Holding publicity, licensing, and HR in one fist? Precisely the kind of man you could NOT leave standing after the takeover. What he needed was a pretext — a target with enough rank to make the lesson carry, without making the new owner look like he was purging rivals for sport. Kill one rooster, properly chosen, and every monkey in the building learns the new rules in a single afternoon. Yoo Jae-ha. Don’t disappoint me.

✦ ✦ ✦

SHIN & KIM, top floor.

Park Sung-jun ushered him into Lee Myung-ha’s wide corner office, and the peninsula’s top M&A lawyer rose from behind the desk with a smile that, for once, had nothing professional about it.

“Mr. Song — please, sit!” A wave of the hand. “Min-ju, forget the coffee. Bring my good tea.”

The secretary bowed out. The door closed on three people, and Lee Myung-ha’s smile banked down into something brighter-eyed as he slid a thick file across the desk.

“Mr. Song — mission accomplished. SK Group has agreed in principle to sell its fifty-five percent of LOEN Entertainment for two hundred eighty billion won.”

His heart kicked hard. His face stayed level. He took the file and ran the key terms fast.

“In parallel, we quietly reached several of the minority holders. They’re willing to package out roughly five point two percent of their combined shares.”

PROJECT DREAMLAND · DEAL STRUCTURE — JULY 29, 2013

SK Group block — 55.0%₩280,000,000,000 · agreed in principle

Minority holders package — ≈5.2%terms settled · confidential

Total consideration — ≈60.2% control≈₩310,000,000,000

Core agreement signingAugust 1 (proposed)

Full completion, est.September — inside the FTC divestment deadline

“Outstanding.” He set the file down, satisfaction plain in his eyes. “The peninsula’s best firm, living up to the billing. You’ve worked hard, Lee-byeonhosa-nim.”

“Hahaha — all in the job.” Then the lawyer’s smile took on a delicately apologetic angle. “Only… Mr. Song, this brings total consideration to roughly three hundred ten billion won. A touch past the original three-hundred-billion estimate.”

“That’s nothing.” Ji-ho waved it off with genuine largesse.

This piece of the board changed everything. With LOEN in hand, he stopped being a stone other hands placed — and became the hand. Money, beauty, fame, power: the real campaign was about to open. Ten billion extra bought speed and certainty. Cheap at the price.

“The overage still counts toward the firm’s bonus at the agreed ratio. You and your team have earned every won of it.”

Something gleamed in Lee Myung-ha’s eyes, and his smile achieved a new grade of sincerity. For him, this was the deal of a career: a reigning superstar reverse-acquiring a conglomerate’s entertainment subsidiary. The kind of trophy that goes first on the firm’s history page.

“Mr. Song’s breadth truly is something. Then — assuming all goes smoothly, we sign the core agreement August first. Rest assured: SHIN & KIM will run every remaining step immaculately. Full completion around September.”

Which landed squarely inside the Fair Trade Commission’s deadline for SK’s non-core divestments. Clean.

“I have complete confidence in the firm.”

They shook on it, hard, both sets of eyes bright.

At the door, Ji-ho paused, and asked with studied idleness:

“Lee-byeonhosa-nim — in the course of acquisition due diligence, getting a detailed picture of the target company’s executives… their backgrounds, their, let’s say, USABILITY… that’s straightforward for a firm like yours, I’d imagine?”

Lee Myung-ha was far too sharp to miss the string under the words. He blinked once, and produced the smile of a man reading the same page.

“A comprehensive assessment of the management team is a necessary — and fully compliant — component of due diligence. SHIN & KIM holds strictly to professional standards. That said—” He glanced at his protégé. “Sung-jun-ah. Mr. Song, as prospective acquirer, would like a deeper reference view of LOEN’s management STABILITY. Follow up. And remember — every piece of information through public, lawful channels only, as reasonable basis for the acquisition decision.”

“Understood, seonsaeng-nim. I’ll handle it properly.”

“Much obliged.”

A small nod, and he was gone.

✦ ✦ ✦

Dusk had reached the LOEN building by the time he returned.

He had just turned into the corridor toward his studio when he met, head-on, a storm front: a middle-aged man of middling height, gold-rimmed glasses, face the color of bad weather — Vice President Yoo Jae-ha, trailing a wake of subordinates fresh out of some meeting.

The man had clearly received Dong-hwan’s answer. At the sight of Ji-ho his face went from overcast to iron, and he didn’t bother hiding a gram of it.

“Ji-ho!”

Full name, at corridor volume. Passing staff glanced over, registered WHO, and found urgent reasons to study the carpet and accelerate.

“Is there a company left in those eyes of yours? Any concept of rank? Of seniority?”

Ji-ho stopped, and regarded him with level calm — no retreat, no flutter.

“Vice President Yoo. I’m afraid I don’t follow.”

“Don’t FOLLOW?” A snort. Yoo Jae-ha closed a step, finger jabbing at Ji-ho’s chest. “The Master’s Sun OST — I asked you PERSONALLY. That was me honoring you! Giving you face! And you turned around and threw it back at me? What — wings dried out now? The company makes you famous and suddenly anything goes?”

“Let me tell you something, Ji-ho — I don’t care how big an artist gets: in LOEN, you follow LOEN’s rules! If I say write, YOU WRITE!”

He didn’t so much as glance at the growing constellation of sidelong eyes down the corridor — a man addressing a subordinate performer he considered infinitely disposable, contempt worn like a lapel pin. Ji-ho felt the cold smile assemble itself internally. Of course. Peninsula entertainment executives, top floor to basement, held artists in exactly this regard. Kim Jong-kook at the peak of his fame had gotten his ribs broken by his own company’s boss — then been blacklisted until he crawled back to apologize and re-sign. But I’m not one of your caged singers. And as for LOEN having MADE me…

Ji-ho’s face stayed pleasant. The curve at the corner of his mouth acquired an edge.

“Vice President Yoo. Writing takes inspiration, and inspiration takes scheduling. My focus at the moment is my own album; I can’t split it. As for rules—” a beat, voice carrying clean down the corridor, “—I don’t believe the company handbook contains a clause compelling artists to accept work that isn’t an official company assignment. Particularly work of a PRIVATE-favor nature.”

“And on the subject of the company making me famous — a question, since you run publicity. What work, exactly, did YOUR department put in to make me? And how much money, exactly, have I made for this company?”

“You—!”

Yoo Jae-ha’s face cycled red, then white, then red again. The calm was worse than defiance; the logic was worse than the calm.

“Fine! FINE! Don’t regret this, Ji-ho!” The pointing finger trembled with rage before he snapped his sleeve around. “We’ll see about you!”

He stormed off, fury radiating, subordinates scurrying after him in petrified silence.

The corridor held its breath. Everyone who had witnessed it stood recalibrating — some awed at the nerve, some already drafting the eulogy for his career, some simply enjoying the show. Ji-ho smoothed the front of his shirt as if nothing whatsoever had occurred, and walked on toward his office.

And in the corner by the pillar, a small figure stood rooted, shock and incomprehension written all over her face. Ji-eun — just back from her schedule, having planned a casual “coincidental” run-in to start repairing things — had walked straight into the whole scene instead. She could not understand it. Why would he DO that — publicly cross the company’s most powerful vice president? And underneath the bafflement, the old complicated tide rose again: her best friend Choi Sulli, off the back of one Ji-ho film, was now a ten-million-admissions leading actress — future wide open, one hand already on Chungmuro’s door.
