Chapter One Hundred Fourteen

Hunter and Prey

The van merged into traffic. IU propped her chin on her hand and stared out at nothing.

The window glass held a faint ghost of her face — makeup half-gone, the real skin underneath: white, fine-pored, flawless. Ji-eun studied her own reflection, and the stage-sweetness in her eyes slowly condensed into something else. The look of a creature that has sighted quarry.

Taeyeon.

She turned the name over slowly, a playful curl at her mouth. Every detail of the day replayed in high definition: the questions dressed as professionalism with needles sewn into the lining; the effortless proximity to Ji-ho’s side; and the closing number — that hug that might as well have been a title deed.

“Like my little brother.”

Ha. Women reading women are never wrong. That is not how anyone looks at a brother. That is an owner inspecting her property — and scanning the horizon for raiders.

And in her eyes, I am the raider.

Her phone chimed — the newsfeed announcing the tenth first-place trophy. All-Kill. Triple Crown. Five consecutive music-show wins. A glorious ledger.

And everything Taeyeon had done today whispered a single sentence over it: this song was supposed to be mine.

The realization sent a strange bright current through her. Guilt? Apology? No — something closer to the exhilaration of a successful raid. Things taken from someone else taste sweeter. Like the last slice of strawberry cake fought over with a cousin — the fridge was full of other desserts, but THAT slice, because she wanted it too, tasted double.

Ji-ho… is he like that too?

She tilted her head and looked at the man beside her, resting with his eyes closed.

The focused profile while he listened to her read a song. The patience of a man re-cutting one harmony forty times. The furnace-warm palm during the staged hand-hold. Today’s terrifyingly precise anatomy of four different voices. And next: a first album, reportedly all self-written. If it’s all at the level of “Some” — then this man is excessive. Deep-set features, ideal bone structure, instantly recognizable. Ruthlessly punctual, always over-prepared. Reverent about music, brilliant without arrogance. Looks you in the eye when he speaks; flawless manners; flawless sense of distance. Wide shoulders, narrow waist, legs beyond reason. And over all of it — that steady, self-possessed gravity that talent gives a man.

Assets like that make a man a scarce commodity. And scarce commodities get fought over. Basic economics.

She settled back and closed her eyes, and Taeyeon’s micro-expressions replayed against the dark.

She’s planting flags. “We have secrets you don’t know.” “He is inside my territory.” …How interesting. The harder she guards him, the higher the appraisal. Nobody stands sentry over an ordinary man. Only the genuinely precious get fought for — and only what gets fought for is genuinely precious. I’ve understood that since childhood. In this industry, “value” has exactly one definition: to be needed, contested, desired. The unwanted are worthless at any quality. The fought-over turn to gold at any quality.

And there’s the other thing. Her fingertips brushed her own lips, where the stage color still clung. Her provocation woke something with teeth. We were always rivals — same generation, same charts, the comparison nobody says aloud. Now the rivalry has annexed a second territory. The song: you lost the opening move, and I made it famous. The man: you’ve marked him yours — so now I have to try the lock. Not because I love him. Not yet, anyway. But because Taeyeon treasures him — and watching a treasured thing walk away from its owner… the anticipation of that expression sent a frankly wicked warmth through her.

Besides. The game is worth playing on its own merits. Circling a man like that — testing, pulling, watching for hairline cracks in all that composure, watching him balance two women, watching who conquers whom in the end. CONQUEST. The word alone raised her pulse. Men are mountains; the climb is the point. Anything easily had is missing the main ingredient. You ascend, you switchback, you turn refusal into tolerance into welcome into surrender — and the summit feeling beats any trophy ever minted.

The dressing room: when I leaned for the water bottle, his eyes moved away. Which means his eyes had to move. He saw. He minded. Good. That’s called an opening move. And Tiffany’s sudden arm — Taeyeon’s face in that moment deserves to be framed. Three women. One man. The oldest play in the repertoire, and it never closes.

She glanced sideways. He was resting exactly as she was pretending to.

What are you thinking about, Ji-ho? Her possessiveness? Tiffany’s warmth? …Or possibly — me?

“Ji-ho.” Her gentlest register.

“Hm?” His eyes opened.

“Thank you for today’s analysis. ‘A storyteller’s tone’ — that’s my favorite review I’ve ever gotten. Next collaboration… maybe we try something with even more story in it.”

Nothing overt. Nothing forward. Just bait on the water — a musical invitation, the safest lure in existence, the one that lowers every guard.

And sure enough, he smiled, warm as ever.

“Nuna’s voice really is built for narrative songs. Let’s talk it through when there’s time.”

See? It begins. Men never refuse the little-white-flower type — purity is the most lethal charm on the market. Taeyeon: can you hold the wall? With your “nuna” seniority, your sunbae posture, your private little passwords? Feelings don’t honor first-come-first-served, and they don’t check rank. It’s open war, and the weapons are charm, brains, patience, timing. And I, Ji-eun, am EXCELLENT at war. They called me a chubby little nobody, and I shut every mouth with three high notes. They said I could only sing cute — I proved them wrong on the next album. I started alone, and now I run both charts and screens. Every road I’ve walked proves one theorem: what I want, I eventually hold. Ji-ho will not be the exception.

The game is on. Taeyeon — look sharp. And Ji-ho… ready to be fought over?

The van pulled up at LOEN’s entrance. He stepped down first; she gathered her bag and followed, unhurried, eyes carrying that locked-on light.

Taken tastes sweeter. Contested means precious. This three-player game — I’m not just playing. I’m winning. Beautifully.

She waved.

“Ji-ho — no more schedules today, right?”

“Mm.”

They walked in together. First-month arithmetic: promotions, variety tapings, a showcase, pictorials, interviews — plus vocal and dance intensives and stage postmortems. LOEN had deliberately kept his variety load light; they weren’t building an idol. With promotions over, what remained was the important programming, fan meetings, guesting on IU’s tour — and the album. Her own calendar was all tour.

Which left the evenings conspicuously open. Her big bright eyes blinked up at him — then made one leisurely pass over the shoulders-to-waist ratio.

“Ji-ho. I almost never see you in the company gym. So why is your body like THAT?”

“I train at home, mostly.”

“Is that so~” The eyes went glossy. “Then you must be very GOOD at training.”

He nodded. Fatally.

“Perfect!” Delight — then a fetching shadow of worry as she extended one slim white arm for inspection. “I’ve gotten a little fat lately. And the tour’s coming. Teach me?”

He stopped and looked — genuinely looked — at the arm in question. Porcelain. Slender. Fat located: none.

The pitiful, blameless face beneath it was a protective-instinct delivery system. He laughed.

“Sure.”

“Thank you! I owe you another meal.” Taeyeon: the war is ON.

9:00 PM LOEN — Company Gym, Deserted warm light, empty racks, loaded intentions

The machines stood in silent ranks under soft amber light — a room composed like a scene waiting for actors.

In the locker room, Ji-eun made her final inspection: pale lotus-pink sports top, trim waist and flat stomach on display; deep-grey high-waisted leggings doing exact justice to the line of hip and leg without trying too hard; a translucent white mesh cover floating over shoulders and arms. Perfume at the ear and inner wrist — not the stage florals, but a private woody neutral, cedar and amber in the base, legible only at close range. Hair up in a high bun, a few strands staged along her neck. Makeup scrubbed to zero, lips balmed: bare-faced, she read years younger, and years more innocent.

“Perfect.”

She told the mirror so, and the mirror agreed.

He was already there when she pushed the door open — back to her, adjusting a treadmill, black training vest and grey joggers, the lamplight doing free advertising for the architecture of his back.

“Nuna. You made it.”

His glance was polite, one second, and gone.

“Warm up first? Twenty minutes easy.”

She’d caught it, though — the single frame of stillness when he first saw her. One frame is data.

She mounted the neighboring treadmill with unhurried grace, the mesh hem drifting to expose a pale line of waist, and turned to him with her face tipped up into the overhead light, eyes doe-wide.

“Is this speed okay?”

“Fine. Start at six.”

They ran side by side to the hum of belts and breath. Her peripheral vision kept his profile on file: the jawline, the nose.

“Hoo— so what’s YOUR usual split?”

“Strength work, mostly core and legs. Cardio as garnish.” Brief, businesslike. “What does nuna want to focus on?”

“Waist and glutes.” Perfectly natural. “Tour costumes are unforgiving.”

Warm-up done, she stepped down and stretched — arms high, back arched, the full line presented to the room. His gaze crossed her for a moment, then went to the equipment.

“Planks first. Wake the core up.”

He demonstrated — spine like a level line. She assumed the position with her hips a strategic few degrees high.

“Like this?”

He crouched close — close enough for cologne and breath to register.

“Core tight. Don’t let the waist sag — imagine the navel pulling toward the spine.”

His hand hovered, professionally hesitant. She adjusted — imperfectly, on purpose.

“Like THIS?”

A one-second pause; then a light, corrective touch at her side — warm through the thin mesh, restrained, contact and release.

There. That microsecond of stiffness in his fingertips. Noted and filed.

“Got it.” She beamed up at him, eyes going crescent. “Thank you.”

Glute bridges next; the amber light, the quiet, his brief steadying touch at her calf — heel drives the floor — every unit of contact logged by both parties under rigorously maintained cover stories. She sat up afterward, flushed with effort, and watched him drink — the working line of his throat.

“Your discipline is unfair. I can never resist eating.” She rose, drifted over, and picked up his bottle with total naturalness. “What’s this one?”

Distance: collapsed. Her woody perfume, warmed by exercise, occupied the space between them.

“Electrolyte water.” He leaned back half a degree.

She declined to notice the retreat — advanced instead, tipped her head back against the wall, set her lips to the bottle’s mouth, and drank, long and unhurried, a stray line of water escaping down the white curve of her throat. Half the bottle, gone.

“Sorry. I was thirsty.”

Eyes wide. Expression: unimpeachable.

He regarded the faint balm-print on the bottle’s rim for two silent seconds.

Koreans share food easily enough — but THAT, in an empty gym at 9 PM, is not sharing. That’s semaphore. …So I’m the prey, am I? Interesting. Side note: acquiring LOEN with IU’s wholehearted support attached — doesn’t that simplify the math considerably? Plus one money tree, pre-bound. Fine, then. Let’s find out which of us is actually holding the net.

“It’s fine,” he said, with a face like still water.

“What’s next?”

“Squats. Empty bar — form first.”

He demonstrated; she racked in; he coached from her flank — feet shoulder-width, knees out, hips back — and when the first rep wandered, his hands settled lightly at her sides, professional to the millimeter, while his voice arrived low at her ear and his breath raised goosebumps she declined to acknowledge. Her own breathing missed a step; she catalogued his shampoo, his cologne, and the clean aggressive baseline underneath, and executed the next rep with a small escaped sound engineered to carry exactly as far as him.

“Right. Just like that.” He released, stepped back. “Feel the drive yourself.”

The remaining sets she did honestly — sweat darkening the mesh, cheeks flushed, hair damp at her neck, the room slowly filling with heat and hormones and mutual, meticulously deniable awareness. His professional gaze, she noted, was lingering measurably longer than at session start. Instinct doesn’t lie, whatever the face does.

“Last set. Then stretching, and we’re done.”

For stretching she selected, naturally, the assisted kind — seated forward fold, one leg extended.

“Hamstring’s tight. Press for me?”

A hesitation — then he knelt behind her and pressed, gently, exactly where asked. His breathing is faster than baseline. Logged. She folded forward, spine curving, and felt his fingertips graze the sensitive back of her calf and flee each time as if scalded.

You feel scalded too? Good. So do I. …Off-script, that last part.

“Done.” He stood. “Other side.”

When it was all over, both of them wore a sheen of sweat and the room’s air had gone humid and complicated.

“Thank you. Genuinely professional coaching — it felt completely different.” She toweled off, mesh collar slipping off one shoulder unremarked. “So it’s settled: you teach me. And in return — dinner. Refusal not accepted.”

He looked down at her. Bare-faced, bright-eyed, undefended — with something banked and burning far at the back of the eyes.

“…Alright.”

“Then — next session.” She turned for the locker room, light-footed, and at the door glanced back exactly once.

He was still by the squat rack, looking her way. At the instant of eye contact his gaze slid off, entirely natural, back to the equipment.

But I saw it. He wavered. Fractionally. Under control. But a waver is a beginning.

The locker-room mirror showed her flushed, glittering, humming with post-exercise vitality and something adjacent to a successful hunt.

Taeyeon — are you watching? Your man spent the evening coaching MY squats. Palm on my waist. Breath at my ear. The war’s first shot is fired — and the opening engagement is mine.

She hummed the “Some” hook and stepped into the shower, the water sluicing away sweat and doing nothing whatsoever about the appetite underneath.

Ji-ho. This is only the overture. Slowly, now.

Out in the gym, Ji-ho watched the door she’d vanished through, and smiled — small, unhurried.

IU-yah. You’re weaving a net. So am I. Slowly, now.
