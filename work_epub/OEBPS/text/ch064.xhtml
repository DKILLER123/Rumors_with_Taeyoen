Chapter Sixty-Four

Weak Stomach — Doctor’s Orders: Soft Rice Only

Watching him visibly question his life’s work, she panicked at her own handiwork and scrambled to patch it.

“Ahem— my standards are just very high. From me, ‘okay’ is a rave. The song will hit. It WILL hit.”

He’d charted this bug weeks ago: Taeyeon, in front of people she was close to, ran a hard mouth over a soft everything-else. The body did the caring; the mouth denied all involvement; and when cornered, out came the house specialties — Be quiet! Stop talking! — cuteness deployed as legal defense.

They walked back out to the console. With a contrarian, he’d learned, you didn’t probe and you didn’t push — you brought patience and plain words, and cut through the self-tangling with directness. So he skipped the games entirely.

“Nuna. Sing this song with me.”

“With you?” Head tilt; the beautifully drawn brows knitting. “Meaning what, exactly?”

He stopped the transport and looked at her straight.

“Meaning exactly what it sounds like. I want to release this song as a duet. With you.”

Her expression flickered through surprise into something sharper. The brows drew deeper. She searched the amber eyes.

“…You’re planning to debut SOLO.”

“Yes.” No decoration. “I’ll upload this to the company library — and then I’m going to talk to the company about debuting.”

She saw it in his face: this wasn’t a whim, it was a war plan. She looked away and actually thought.

Field Notes

Why solo is the hard road — the industry math:

The Peninsula built its cultural-export machine on one invention: the idol group, mass-produced through the trainee system. Groups spread risk — vocal, dance, visual, variety roles each catching different fans, “group stans plus solo stans” compounding into sales and votes; member chemistry generating free variety-show content; the whole engine running “members feed the group, the group feeds the members.” Big-house groups years in the making almost never miss.

But an assembly line produces products — adequate, interchangeable. Pull one member out alone and the seams show: no group dynamic to sparkle against, lopsided skills exposed on a bare stage, no song strong enough, no individual gravity. Solo success out of a group belonged to the born-distinctive — Lee Hyori, Kim Jong-kook, G-Dragon, Taeyeon herself, HyunA, Park Chae-young. Everyone else’s solo made one small splash and sank.

And debuting solo from NOTHING — no group, no fandom? BoA. IU. A bare handful of others in the industry’s whole history. That was the road Ji-ho was proposing to walk.

Deliberation complete, her verdict came out wrapped in open worry.

“Soso. Solo is not a road that walks easy — and you’d be walking it as a foreigner, which is harder still. With your ability, you could be the absolute core of the next boy group. The company will almost certainly say no to this.”

“Nuna.” He understood the worry and held his ground. “The company launched EXO last month. The next boy group is four years out — prep starting around 2014 at the earliest. I can’t wait that long… and the company knows it can’t make me. Actually—” a sudden grin, “—the company is in more of a hurry than I am. My termination penalty is embarrassingly cheap.”

She blinked — and then the arithmetic assembled itself across her face. Not three months trained. Penalty pegged at triple the training costs — call it a few million won a month, so the whole buyout ran barely thirty-some million. His bravery bonus alone had been fifty. And if any rival company ever heard the two songs this boy had already written, his phone would melt from agencies fighting to pay it for him.

…He’s right. The company IS the anxious one.

The unrealness of it washed over her — three months of training, and possibly a solo debut out of SM — followed immediately by the correction: if he weren’t exactly this absurd, would I have fallen— would I have NOTICED him in the first place? Honestly, this comes down to my excellent eye. I found him first.

She started grinning at nothing.

“Nuna. What’s the dopey smile?” A hand waved before her eyes. “You haven’t answered. If I actually get the solo debut — will you sing it with me?”

Who ELSE would sing it?! In her head the matter had been settled somewhere around the second chorus: this was the song of the two of them; it was HERS. What her mouth said, naturally, was:

“Hmm. I’ll have to consider. I’m very expensive, you know. And there’s the company’s arrangements to think of.”

Grinning like that while playing hard to get. That mouth is a national monument. He rolled his eyes and declined to dignify it.

She let the tsundere glow fade — and then a genuine question surfaced. Why THIS song for a debut? “A Boy” is the obvious debut track… unless “Some” means something. Unless it’s because this one is about us— Her pulse ticked up a notch. She asked with maximum engineered carelessness:

“But why choose this one and not ‘A Boy’? That one’s practically BUILT to be a solo debut song.”

“If I were an idol a few years into a career, with a fanbase — ‘A Boy’ every time.” His hands spread. “But I’m a pure rookie. I have to lead with the song I believe hits hardest.”

“…Just because of that?” The disappointment leaked straight through her voice.

“Of course not.” He shook his head. “There’s a much more important reason.”

The disappointed heart re-inflated instantly, hoisted itself up onto tiptoe:

“The more important reason… being?”

His smile went almost bashful.

“I’ve had a weak stomach since I was a kid. Doctor’s orders — I should really only eat soft rice.”

“…Soft rice?” Total blankness. “What does that mean?”

The idiom hadn’t crossed the ocean yet — in Koreatown back home, everyone knew “eating soft rice”: living off a woman. On the Peninsula they had their own term for it, but the flavor was different. He was, he reflected, wasting an excellent bilingual joke on the one person present without the dictionary. He translated:

“It means — I’m a nobody with no name value. So my plan is to find a beautiful, rich, extremely famous nuna… and have her carry me.”

Beautiful. Rich. Extremely famous. A nuna. That is— that is literally, item for item, ME.

And the projection completed itself unbidden: debuting together on this song. Then — after — actually being together. The sheer ROMANCE of the sequence—

The verdict came down like a gavel:

“Eat! MY bowl of soft rice — you’re eating EVERY grain of it!”
