Chapter Fifty

Sealed With a High-Five

Arm looped cozily through Seung-wan’s, Irene’s eye snagged on a hat boutique across the way — and something clicked. Ji-ho still needs the cap. Her feet had stopped before the thought finished.

“Eonni? What is it?”

“Hat shop. Ji-ho said he still needed a ball cap, didn’t he? Let’s look.”

Seung-wan’s puzzlement deepened by one full degree. Her radar had been pinging all morning: eonni’s attitude toward Ji-ho had shifted somewhere — subtly, definitely — into a register of particular attention.

Oblivious to the surveillance, Joo-hyun turned and waved down the street.

“Yah, Ji-ho! How does a grown man dawdle this much? MOVE!”

Most men’s enthusiasm for shopping had a four-hour battery; his had been in the red for some time. He sighed and picked up the pace.

“Two certified beauties escorting you around the district — men would PAY for this — and look at that reluctant face,” Seung-wan said, with maximal contempt.

“Two beauties?” He arrived wearing total bafflement, gaze sweeping the street in genuine search. “I count one — nuna. Where’s the other one hiding?”

“YAH! Losing your eyesight at YOUR age?” She planted herself directly in front of him, inflating. “An enormous beauty is standing RIGHT here and you can’t SEE her?”

He looked her up and down — slowly, twice — then shook his head and sighed. He said nothing. He said, thereby, everything.

“SONG JI-HO!” Fists clenched, fury total. “Today you DIE!”

“Enough, enough—” Joo-hyun caught her mid-launch. “Buy the hat, then samgyetang. We’ll bankrupt him at lunch instead.”

The word samgyetang defused Seung-wan instantly. One last withering glare, and she towed Joo-hyun into the boutique.

A hat shop? He hurried after them.

Inside: three tidy ranks of shelving stacked with berets, ball caps, cowboy hats, fedoras — every style, season, and function in the catalog. They made straight for the ball-cap section, where Seung-wan snatched up a red one and began flipping it over like a customs inspector.

“Ji-ho, try this one!” She thrust it at him. “Soft-crown fit — hugs the head shape. Makes your face look SO much smaller.”

He batted her hand away with theatrical outrage.

“And what is THAT supposed to mean? Are you calling my face big?”

“Heehee — I never said it. You said it~” Tongue out, entirely unrepentant.

While the two of them sparred, Joo-hyun had gone quiet beside the second shelf — caught by a pure white ball cap. The brim curved at exactly the right angle; a small, precise star logo sat embroidered on the front; the whole design ran minimal and quietly distinctive at once.

One look, and her mind had already put it on him. It suits him. That clean, fresh thing he has — it suits him exactly.

“This one looks like Ji-ho,” she murmured to no one — and crouched to take it from the shelf.

She lifted the white cap by the brim and turned it in her hands, more satisfied by the second. Then, still crouched, she tipped her head back and raised it high toward him.

“Ji-ho, look at this one!”

He glanced down at the sound—

—and his eyes never made it to the hat.

The soft knit of her cardigan had shifted with the crouch, the sweetheart neckline slipping loose — and the track lighting overhead laid a fine, luminous wash across fair skin, a glimpse of something soft and snowy that was over in half a second and stopped his breath cold anyway.

He looked away instantly — ceiling, fascinating ceiling — and then, being human, his gaze wavered back once, briefly.

“…Very white. Uh — very nice.”

The first look away was respect. The brief second glance was simply what happened when a person stood in front of something beautiful — the same helpless extra beat everyone gives a good painting.

She noticed nothing, read it as endorsement of her taste, and popped upright, glowing with vindication.

“I KNEW it! White is absolutely your color!”

He recalibrated his eyeline with great professionalism and accepted the cap. And credit where due — her eye was genuinely excellent: a Converse canvas soft-crown, vintage-washed, the classic star logo stitched small; light, breathable, and built to sit perfectly on top of denim or a hoodie. Street styling in one object. He tried it on.

It fit like it had been commissioned.

“This one.”

“You don’t want to try any others?” — mildly startled.

He looked at her, and let the pause carry a little more weight than hat shopping strictly required.

“When I’ve seen the thing I want — I don’t hesitate.”

Something fluttered, once, low in her chest. It was probably her imagination. It was probably about the hat.

“Done choosing? PAY! And then — samgyetang~!” Seung-wan had him by the sleeve, towing.

They left the clothing district for the morning’s food street, now packed to the gutters with the lunch crowd. The three of them drew close by necessity — Joo-hyun’s arm through Seung-wan’s, Ji-ho at Joo-hyun’s other shoulder, running quiet interference with his height whenever the crowd surged.

She kept up her end of Seung-wan’s chatter the whole way. Most of her attention, however, was tracking something else entirely: the small constant adjustments of the tall presence beside her, the way space kept being made for her without a word. Being sheltered like this, she discovered, was a feeling a person could get greedy about.

Seung-wan was captivated by Dongdaemun. The other two were captivated by considerably nearer subjects.

They found a samgyetang house doing roaring trade — the Peninsula’s classic answer to the muggy exhaustion of early summer, hot ginseng-chicken soup to burn the fatigue out. Seung-wan, loudly committed to bankrupting Ji-ho and quietly aware of a trainee’s finances, ordered the cheapest set for the table.

Over the soup, talk turned — inevitably — to the evaluation.

“So, Ji-ho.” The rookie began her intelligence-gathering, eyes bright over her spoon. “What song did you prepare?”

He set his spoon down, dabbed his mouth, savored the twin stares of anticipation — and delivered:

“Secret~”

“YAH!” The whiplash of denied expectation nearly took Seung-wan’s breath; her fist trembled with the pure desire to plant itself in that infuriating face.

Beside her, Joo-hyun’s breathing had audibly deepened. Her own small fist had closed.

“But—” unhurried, insufferable, “—I will say I’m confident about making A-rank.”

Both girls froze mid-motion. A rookie. Promoting to A-rank. At his FIRST monthly evaluation?

“Pfft. Big talk.” Seung-wan recovered first, dripping skepticism.

“Want to bet on it?”

“Bet WHAT?”

The voice came from Joo-hyun — sudden, sharp, leaning in. She had not forgotten the chili-ring swindle. That account was still open, and today it would be settled with interest.

He met her eyes and laid it out, entirely serious.

“If I make A-rank — you and Seung-wan each owe me one unconditional favor. Anything I ask.”

“And if I DON’T make A-rank — I owe each of you one. Unconditional. Anything you ask.”

The girls looked at each other and conducted an entire conference in one glance. Ji-ho, your singing is scary, fine — but we have SEEN your dancing. A-rank? At the first evaluation?

Two fair hands shot out in perfect unison, palms up, confidence absolute:

“BET!”

Clap!

Clap!

Two high-fives, sealed and witnessed. The wager stood.
