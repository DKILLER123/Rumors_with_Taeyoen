Chapter One Hundred Forty-Six

What the Ace Endorser Brings Home

Late November 2012 · 4:00 a.m. Seoul → Busan promotion-week velocity

Four in the morning, and Seoul still hung in that half-dreaming dark between night and day.

“Ji-ho, the car’s here!” Dong-hwan came hustling over with a coat and a thermos. “Busan first today — fan-signing this morning.”

“Then the fan meeting and the showcase in the afternoon.”

“Music radio tonight.”

He took the thermos. Warm water down the throat took some of the rasp off the early hour. He nodded and said nothing — a week straight of four-hour nights. Busy did not begin to cover it.

He folded himself into the van and dropped into the seat. Dong-hwan drew the curtains and dimmed the lights with the practiced economy of a man converting a vehicle into a mobile sleep pod.

“Sleep. I’ll wake you when we’re there.”

He closed his eyes.

The comeback titles were still sitting on top of every chart, and the price of that was the schedule from hell — music shows, variety, pictorials, fan meetings, stacked edge to edge. Thankfully, there was the Idol Aura. The van rocked gently; his consciousness blurred; and over the two-hour ride, LV3 deep-sleep repair did what eight honest hours could not.

✦   ✦   ✦

He sat before the dressing-room mirror while the stylist worked on his hair. The man in the glass was precise-featured without being soft, and the skin showed no flaw a camera could find.

“Ji-ho — the L’Oréal Men contract is confirmed.” Dong-hwan came in fast, a stack of documents in hand and excitement he was visibly failing to suppress. “Eight million dollars a year. GLOBAL ambassador!”

A ripple of sharp breaths crossed the dressing room. Two assistants exchanged a look of open shock.

“Terms?” He barely raised his eyes.

“Two global campaign shoots a year, four brand-event appearances.” Dong-hwan flipped pages. “One new clause — they want at least two social-media appearances a month. Because, well—” He lowered his voice. “Your skin is honestly too good to be scientific.”

Unscientific? Of course it was unscientific. That was the LV3 Idol Aura at work — hydration, luster, elasticity, all quietly serviced every night while he slept.

“Is the shoot schedule sorted?”

“Next month in Paris — four days apart from the Gucci shoot.” Dong-hwan pulled out a second file. “And speaking of Gucci: details finalized. Twelve million dollars a year. Full-line ambassador.”

This time even the stylist’s hands stopped for half a second.

“They want you at the opening show of Milan Fashion Week next year, and they’re placing your outdoor campaigns in twenty flagship shopping districts worldwide.” His voice had picked up a faint tremor. “And the biggest thing — they want to launch a dedicated capsule collection under your name.”

A dedicated collection. The words hung in the dressing-room air. In fashion, that phrase means the house has decided your face is part of its iconography — not a billboard, a partner.

He closed his eyes briefly and pulled up the interface. In the titles column, one entry was glowing.

DREAM LIFE — TITLE ACTIVE

TITLEACE ENDORSER

EFFECTOne top-luxury brand collaboration invitation per year

CURRENTConsumer purchase intent for endorsed products +40%

PROGRESSTop-luxury houses endorsed: 2 / 5

NOTE: For every 5 top-luxury endorsements held, gain one additional annual invitation.

The title never handed anything over directly. It engineered opportunity inside reality — a Gucci market-research team that “happened” to notice his new album, and the sales curves his school-uniform and outdoor-wear campaigns had bent upward. L’Oréal, meanwhile, was the Global Top-Tier Endorsement Card from the shop, cashing itself out on schedule.

ENDORSEMENT LEDGER · NOVEMBER UPDATE
L’Oréal Men — global ambassador · 2 campaigns + 4 events/yr$8,000,000 / yr
Gucci — full-line ambassador · Milan FW opening · 20-district outdoor · capsule collection$12,000,000 / yr
Combined, before year-end album settlement$20,000,000 / yr

“Ji-ho-ssi, time to change.” The stylist’s soft reminder.

“Hyung,” he said, shrugging into the shoot suit, “re-cut next month’s schedule for me. The Paris week — nothing but the L’Oréal and Gucci shoots. No other work.”

“Obviously. Already done.”

Two top-tier contracts, plus next month’s settlement share — and the number would finally be big enough. Ignite the Wealth Robot, catch the Japan trade before the window shut. Every piece was sliding into place.

✦   ✦   ✦
November 30, 2012 · 6:00 a.m. Incheon International Airport — VIP wing departure day: MAMA, Hong Kong

The van rolled into the VIP lane at Incheon while he rested behind closed eyes.

“Ji-ho hyung. We’re here.” The assistant’s voice was soft.

He opened his eyes already looking rested — one of the aura’s quieter miracles.

In the VIP lounge, a familiar figure stood at the floor-to-ceiling window, back turned.

“Ji-eun.”

IU turned around. Ivory knit, plain jeans, long hair loose over her shoulders — dressed down to nothing and still unmistakable.

“Ji-ho.” She smiled, and her eyes curved into crescents. “Long time no see.”

It had been long. Since that blurred-boundary midnight in Changsha, they had each been swallowed whole by their own schedules — his album campaign, her calendar. Apart from the one livestream she’d crashed with coffee, they had barely breathed the same air.

He clapped her shoulder lightly, natural as anything.

“Let’s go.”

✦   ✦   ✦

First class. The attendant drew the partition curtain, and the small space held exactly two people.

IU leaned against the window, eyes resting on his face.

He had changed too much in too little time — and not only the luxury contracts and the detonation of his popularity. There was something settled in him now, a gravity that made looking away feel like effort. The system called it I Am A Superstar; she had no name for it at all. She adjusted her position, the better to study him.

“Can’t sleep?” His eyes were still closed when he said it.

“Did I disturb you?” Very softly.

His eyes opened; his head turned.

“You were watching me.”

Not a question. A statement of record.

Her face warmed, but she didn’t look away.

“I was thinking about how the you sitting here is different from the you of three months ago.”

“Different how?”

“Brighter.” She said it honestly. “It’s like — every time I see you again after a while, there’s more of you to be pulled toward.”

He was quiet a moment. Then he reached over and drew the slipped blanket back up over her.

“My grandmother used to say the face follows the heart — and circumstances follow the face. When your situation rises, it gives your character room to grow. And your character, in turn, changes what people see when they look at you.”

“Rest a while. Today’s going to be long.”

She nodded, none the wiser and obedient anyway, closing her eyes with an inward sigh.

Some conversations simply could not happen at thirty thousand feet, in a space anyone might walk into. She knew which conversation. So did he.

✦   ✦   ✦
2:00 p.m. · Hong Kong A luxury beauty studio — the dressing suite final styling before the carpet

IU sat before the mirror as the stylist laid down the final stroke.

“Perfect.” The stylist stepped back two paces, sincerely admiring. “Ji-eun-ssi, this gown might as well have been sewn onto you.”

She rose, and the sheer white skirt lifted and settled like breath. Thin beaded straps making the line of her shoulders look impossibly delicate; semi-sheer white gauze embroidered with leaf-vein patterns and seeded with small pearls; a cinched waist; a hem that moved like weather. Long black hair, barely waved, down her back. Soft pink lips, luminous skin.

She turned toward the neighboring station — where he had just been finished. Black bespoke suit. Wide shoulders. Narrow waist.

Four eyes met.

For one full second she forgot to breathe. She had always known he was handsome. But this — the deep-set eyes under finished styling, the immaculate hair, the tailoring wrapped around that frame — had left “handsome” behind somewhere and arrived at something that made the heart stutter. He was looking at her too: the soft wave of black hair, the fragile line of shoulder and collarbone, the waist a hand could circle — and then back up to her eyes. What passed between them was pure, unhidden appreciation, running both directions at once.

“Ahem.” A manager’s voice broke the quiet. “You two — if styling’s done, you can rest for half an hour.”

“Then there’s a simple rehearsal. You have the opening performance.”

The makeup artists and assistants filed out with professional tact. Her manager took a call and hurried off; his was summoned by the brand team to confirm run-of-show.

The door clicked softly shut.

The world went suddenly quiet.

IU pinched a fold of her skirt. Released it. Half a month of unsaid things crowded up her throat — and then she looked at his calm profile, lit and unlit by the glow of his phone, and swallowed them back down.

“Ji-ho.” She finally spoke.

He looked up.

“I—” The word caught.

The room was empty, but anyone could walk in at any second. Not here. Not like this.

She stood abruptly.

“My zipper’s sitting strangely. I’m going to fix it.”

She walked into the changing room and shut the door.

Back against the panel, she took several slow breaths. The girl in the mirror was flushed at the cheekbones, bright-eyed — nerves, and resolve, in equal parts. No more stalling.

She eased the hidden side zipper down, stopped it halfway. Then she opened the door a hand’s width, put out one wrist wearing its thin chain bracelet, and beckoned.

“Ji-ho.” Her voice was deliberately light, with a carefully measured note of helplessness. “The zipper’s stuck. Could you come help me?”

He set down the phone and crossed the room.

The changing room was narrow, hung with backup gowns. She stood with her back to him, the bare skin of her spine holding the light like porcelain. The zipper had indeed caught at the waist, pinching a small pleat of gauze.

“Here?” His fingers found the pull, and drew it up for her.

The next second, she spun.

Fast — startlingly fast — both palms planting against the wall on either side of him, penning him between her arms. Up on her toes. And her lips, tasting faintly of sweet fruit, pressed home.

Her arms wound around his neck and pulled him down into it.

He froze for exactly one breath.

Then his arms circled her waist — so slight it felt breakable — and he turned them both, reversing the geometry, and now she was the one pressed gently to the wall, and the kiss stopped being hers and became theirs: deeper, hungrier, entirely unapologetic. Fruit gloss and mint. His hand traced the bare line of her back and felt the tremor answer it. In that sealed sliver of space, every sense ran hot.

When they finally parted, both of them had lost the rhythm of their breathing. Foreheads touching. Air shared.

“I missed you so much.”

The words came out on the ragged edge of the kiss.

His thumb crossed her flushed cheek.

“I know.”

He bent and left one more kiss on her lips, brief as punctuation.

She looked up at him — enormous wet eyes, aggrieved and luminous at once — and bit her lower lip, the old habit turned suddenly lethal.

“I missed you so, so much,” she repeated. “But I knew you were buried in the album, so I didn’t let myself distract you. Not even once.”

A pause. The transparent angling for praise arrived right on schedule.

“Wasn’t I good?”

He studied her. The nation’s little sister, incandescent on every stage in the country — standing in a closet saying things that would melt broadcast standards.

He took her face in both hands, thumbs tracing the fine line of her jaw.

“This face,” he said slowly, “was better than good.”

She blinked once — then laughed, pressing her lips together, and the laugh spread up through her whole face like light through water.

Like the first cherry blossom of spring, he thought. And then she folded the laugh away and looked at him with total seriousness.

“After the ceremony tonight,” she said, each word set down separately, “I need an answer.”

Footsteps and voices drifted down the corridor outside, far and then nearer.

“You know what I want. Don’t you?”

He looked at his own reflection in her eyes — at the hope and the fear flickering there together.

It was time to settle this. And now — finally — he had the standing to.

A knock at the outer door.

“IU-ssi? Ji-ho-ssi? Time to head out.”

“I know.” He nodded.

And before she could say another word, he bent his head and took her soft, sweet mouth once more.

“Ji-eun, Ji-eun — I’m bringing the car around. Come down quickly.”

The manager’s voice, right outside. The knife-edge thrill of nearly-caught ran through her like current — breath quickening, pulse loud, the moment carving itself somewhere permanent.

“Time to go.”

He released her, and his voice had already found its usual level ground.

She drew a long breath, put her skirt and hair to rights. The mirror showed lips faintly swollen, eyes still wet and shining. She produced a lipstick from her clutch and repaired the evidence in four practiced strokes.

At the changing-room door she looked back at him once.

The look said: I’m waiting.

He inclined his head, a single small nod.

Then the door opened, managers and stylists flooded in, noise filled the suite to its corners — and IU walked out into the middle of it already transformed, once again the top idol who had never once, in her entire life, kissed anyone in a closet.
