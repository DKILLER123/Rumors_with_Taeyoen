Chapter Forty-Four

Main Quest: One Song to Stun Them All

The last light phrase settled — hers, feather-soft on the landing — and she looked up, faintly breathless, directly into his eyes.

The studio went abruptly, totally silent. Two sets of slightly quickened breathing, and nothing else.

Her palm still rested on his chest. Under it: his heartbeat, strong and evenly timed. In the dark of his eyes she could see, with perfect clarity, her own small reflection looking back.

Something strange and warm moved through her — the distinct, physically implausible sensation of two heartbeats negotiating toward the same tempo.

One second. Three. Five. Seven…

The air thickened into something neither of them had scheduled. An invisible current made a lazy circuit between them.

She surfaced first — eyes breaking away, body angling half a turn, hand rising involuntarily to the back of her neck.

“So— that’s the feeling. Mixed voice. You’ve got it memorized now?”

Watching her fluster, Ji-ho banked a small, private win. Effective. Extremely effective.

“Ne, nuna. Memorized.”

“…Why are you smiling like that?”

“Like what? Nothing.” The smile folded away into earnest gratitude. “Thank you, nuna. Really.”

She nodded — and then neither of them had anything to say, and the silence came back with interest.

Her ringtone shattered it — a soft ballad snippet blooming from her pocket. She dove for the phone.

“Hello?”

“…Beer?”

Her voice jumped an octave — and her eyes darted to Ji-ho with unmistakable guilt before she hunched a few degrees away and dropped to a murmur.

“…Got it. Yeah.”

She hung up, raked a flustered hand through the hair at her ear, and reassembled her composure by visible effort.

“It’s getting late. I’m heading out.”

“Me too, nuna. Walk out together?”

“Mm. Come on.”

He killed the studio lights and pulled the door shut, and they walked the corridor side by side. His stride, at full length, would have left her a corridor behind; he shortened it without a word, matching her rhythm so naturally it looked like coincidence.

The empty hallway held one sound: her mid-heel Mary Janes on polished marble — tak… tak… — echoing down the space.

And for no reason she could name, Taeyeon noticed her heartbeat settling into step with it — and a small, unscheduled happiness bubbling up under her ribs the whole way down.

April 8, 2012 The Peninsula — Music Industry twelve boys, one earthquake

EXO formally debuted with their first mini-album, “MAMA.”

From conception, the group had been aimed square at the colossal Chinese market: the album released in a Korean version for EXO-K and a Chinese version for EXO-M, and first-day sales came in blazing. EXO-M — carrying Chinese members including Zhang Yixing — closed the distance with Chinese fans instantly, popularity compounding by the day, while SM worked the phones and landed them a spot on Happy Camp, China’s most beloved variety juggernaut. On home soil, “MAMA” announced EXO-K with authority. The group SM had bet the treasury on had done the rarest thing in the industry: exploded on arrival.

And the aftershock reached one particular practice room: the company had now seen, in hard first-week numbers, exactly what a bridge into a foreign market was worth — and how much torrential attention a well-positioned international artist could command. Somewhere upstairs, quietly, the executive gaze on a certain Korean-American trainee grew several degrees warmer.

Ji-ho, for his part, changed nothing. Sweat, drills, repeat.

That night, after training, ab board, and a long shower, he sat at his desk and slit open a courier envelope: three paper certificates from the Korea Copyright Commission. The songs were his — legally, permanently, provably his. Classics that would outlive every trend between now and their release dates, sleeping safely under his name.

Main Quest · Triggered
Ding — First monthly evaluation approaching. Main quest issued. Report to the Quest Hall.

[Main Quest] One Song to Stun Them All — Current rating: B-rank trainee. Achieve promotion to A-rank at the month-end evaluation. No reward for holding rank or demotion.

Rewards: Advanced Songwriting  ·  Squat Rack  ·  Random professional instrument mastery ×1

[Advanced Songwriting] — Full command of core-four arrangement and mainstream section design; independently deliver commercial works across lyrics, melody, and arrangement.

[Squat Rack] — Want to reach the bottom of things? Squat to the bottom first! Major lower-body strength gains, sculpted legs, elevated testosterone, improved daily vitality.

A main quest! His fist clenched on pure reflex. And the headline reward was the exact missing piece: Advanced Songwriting, the key that would unlock his entire memorized catalog — stack it on the rap-writing mastery he already held, and the hits of the next decade became producible. As for the squat rack: no man alive had ever refused one.

The verdict was instant: month-end, A-rank, non-negotiable.

But SM’s evaluation grid was merciless — vocals, dance, attitude, character, teamwork, all weighed together. Singing, attitude, character: fortress-solid. Dance and teamwork: mortal. Holding B was safe. Jumping to A was a coin flip.

So how does one stun a room full of professional evaluators…?

His eyes drifted to the system wardrobe — to the Hip-Hop Kid outfit — then to the Advanced Rap Mastery sitting in his skill list, and the circuit closed with an audible click.

An original rap. Written by me, performed by me, in THAT outfit, with THIS skill. Main-vocal-grade singing, elite rap, self-written material — that stopped being an A-rank argument entirely. That was a pre-debut team argument.
