Chapter Twenty-Seven

Black Into White

Sulli surged to her feet without warning, scrubbed the tears off her face with both hands, and something new burned where the despair had been. Hate — clean and hot. Ji-ho’s phone still locked in her grip, she bolted from the break room like a horse throwing its rider.

“Yah — Sulli!”

He lunged after her — and collided full-length with a drink server just outside the door. They went down in a tangle of limbs.

“AGH!” The server clutched his forehead. “Yah, are your eyes decorative?!”

“Sorry — I’m so sorry—” Ji-ho was already scrambling up, pain filed under later, sprinting the way she’d gone.

He scanned as he ran — there, above: she was already up the stairs to the second floor. Since when do idols run like track athletes?! He took the steps three at a time — but by the time he hit the landing she’d vanished past the corner, and the corridor stretched out long and door-lined and silent.

Worry chewed at him. His video had lit this fuse; she was as unstable as a person could be, and if she did something irreversible tonight, that would live with him permanently. Out of options, he started on the private rooms, one by one.

The first door: a young couple, mid-cuddle, staring at the intruder in mutual horror.

“Wrong room — sorry!”

Door closed. Next.

✦   ✦   ✦

Sulli, meanwhile, had stopped outside her own booth. One deep breath. She wiped the last tear-tracks away with the back of her wrist, set her face — and slammed the door open.

“Sulli-yah! Where’d you run off to?” He was on his feet at once, smile stacked on smile, waving her over. “Gone so long — I called and you didn’t pick up, I was about to come looking! Here, sit, sit. I switched you to a sweet fruit wine — barely any alcohol. You’ll love it, I promise.”

Her eyes fixed on the pale pink glass waiting at her seat. The laugh that came out of her was scraped hollow.

“Heh. Hahaha.”

“Sunbae. I don’t like fruit wine. But we shouldn’t waste it, should we? So — that glass. Drink it for me.”

“I ordered it specially for YOU, though.” The smile actually gentled, tenderness laid on with a trowel. “You’ve always loved sweet things, haven’t you? Try it. It’s honestly so sweet — just like you.”

Once, a line like that would have made her glow for an hour. Now it crawled on her skin. Her fists clenched at her sides, and the shout tore out of her:

“Do you not understand Korean?! I said — DRINK IT FOR ME!”

The booth went pin-drop silent. Every head turned.

“Sulli-yah, I…” The smile hung on his face like a picture knocked crooked.

“What’s wrong? Scared to?” She advanced on him one step at a time, eyes honed to knife-edges. “Why would that be? Could it be — because of the sleeping pills you put in it?”

“Sleeping pills?!”

Nichkhun, Tiffany, and Hyo-yeon went rigid in the same instant — three pairs of eyes dropping involuntarily to their own glasses.

Something lurched behind Choi Jae’s face; then the dry laugh, the patting-down-the-tablecloth calm.

“Sulli-yah. You’re drunk, hm? That’s not a funny joke — not even a little. Come on, you’re done for tonight, let’s call it—”

“A JOKE? Hahaha — oh, a joke!”

Her whole body uncoiled into the swing. The slap detonated across his face — CRACK — hard enough to stagger him sideways, ears ringing like struck bells.

Nichkhun, Tiffany, and Hyo-yeon sat frozen solid, statues with drinks. Even the tattooed man at Choi Jae’s flank was too stunned to move.

Choi Jae shook his head once, cheek flaming, brain sloshing — and the costume finally came all the way off.

“YAH! You ssibal crazy woman — have you lost your MIND?!”

“You son of a dog!” Her hand found a bottle on the table and hurled it. “Going to ‘have me’ tonight, were you?! Going to film your trophy?!”

He ducked; the bottle grazed his scalp and burst against the wall in a spray of glass and liquor.

And through the ringing in his ears, the words finally landed. She heard. She HEARD — but how? The storeroom? Nobody ever goes back there— He’d used that room himself, for things nobody walked in on. It made no sense. Doesn’t matter. Don’t panic. Control it.

He watched Nichkhun, Tiffany, and Hyo-yeon rising, faces changed, closing in — and made his play. His voice cracked into wounded passion, the performance of a lifetime:

“Sulli! I LOVE you — genuinely! You don’t accept my feelings — fine! Reject me, fine! But to smear me with something like THIS? Why?!”

“This glass — the one I supposedly drugged?” He seized it, threw his head back, and drained it to the dregs, slamming it down like a gavel. “There. NOW what do you have to say? I am so — so disappointed in you. I must have been blind!”

He snatched up his jacket, wounded dignity in motion, already turning for the door.

Sulli was shaking — actually vibrating — with rage, teeth grinding audibly. Black into white. Right in front of everyone. This is what he does. This is what he’s always been doing.

She raised Ji-ho’s phone. No lock screen — mercy of the era. She found the video, dragged the slider with a steady finger, and hit play at volume:

“Choi hyung, if it’s really Sulli, I can’t hand this over. She’s not some nobody, not some fresh college kid. Anyone who’s climbed to her level of fame is sharp as they come. This isn’t a girl you smooth over after the fact with an envelope and some sweet talk.”

Choi Jae’s face went white as quicklime. The finger he leveled at her shook with fury.

“You — you were THERE?! You dared record me?! You vicious, scheming little—”

Even now. Even NOW he dares. Trembling head to foot, she pulled out her own phone and her voice came out cold enough to frost the glass on the table.

“Whatever’s left to say — say it to the police.”

The recording rolled on, merciless:

“And what would you know about it? We’re not even dating and she’s already in love with me. Trusts me like a child. Tomorrow morning I pet her hair, tell her it’s all fine — and I save myself the confession AND the gifts—”

The police. The word snapped something. He lunged at her, roaring:

“You WENCH — the police?!”
