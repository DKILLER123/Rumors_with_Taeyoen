Chapter One Hundred Forty-Two

Detective Sunny

The next morning Banpo, Seoul — the apartment breakfast, milk, and a premeditated tape measure

Taeyeon lay curled deep in the duvet, breathing in the soft rhythm of someone still far out at sea in a dream.

In the dream she was, in fact, at sea — a small boat in storm weather, lifted and dropped by wave after wave, water coming over the side until she was soaked through.

…Soaked?

She woke all at once and patted the sheets in alarm.

Whew. Fine. They’d changed the bedding last night before actually sleeping.

The other side of the bed was empty — long since familiar. He always woke first.

She stretched, long and luxurious, and the duvet slid from her shoulders.

Why did today feel THIS good? Rested down to the bone. The world looked scrubbed and bright, like a window someone had finally cleaned. Warm everywhere. Absurdly, indefensibly happy.

She hopped down, light on her feet, and took a long hot shower. Then she wiped the fog from the mirror with one small pale hand—

—and startled at her own reflection.

Her skin had gone white to the edge of translucence, luminous as a fresh-peeled lychee. The shower’s heat had left a pink bloom across her cheeks that only made it look clearer. She pinched one experimentally: full, springy, unreasonably smooth.

“Mm. Skin’s in insane condition today. And my whole body feels light.”

“…Is it because Ji-ho’s back? Is this what a good mood does?”

She pressed her lips together over a smile. So it was true after all: a girl is a flower — and this particular flower evidently bloomed best when watered with a boyfriend’s love.

Hair dried, bun tied, something simple thrown on — devastating regardless — and she padded out of the bedroom humming a certain debut-era earworm under her breath.

Yoon-a and Sunny were technically awake. They were draped across the sofa like two coats no one had bothered to hang up.

“Good morning, Yoon-a! Sunny!” Beaming, she scanned the apartment for a boyfriend and found none.

“Morning, eonni.”

“Morning, Taeyeon.”

Both greetings arrived flat, sanded free of all vitality.

“What’s the matter with you two?” she asked brightly. “Couldn’t sleep in a strange bed?”

Yoon-a fixed her with a long, profoundly wronged stare.

“Eonni. Why we slept badly. You truly don’t know?”

“Exactly.” Sunny sat up and leveled an accusing finger. “We thought you’d adopted a cat.”

“YA! Quiet!” Taeyeon’s face achieved full crimson in record time, and she grabbed for a new topic like a fire extinguisher. “Where’s Ji-ho? Where did he go?”

“‘Ji-ho-ya.’ ‘Ji-ho-ya.’ Listen how sweetly she calls him.”

“Truly. Gets a boyfriend, forgets the sisters who bled beside her for years.”

“What can you do? Her boyfriend’s handsome AND built.”

“Aigoo. The envy. The genuine envy.”

They volleyed it back and forth over her head, ignoring her entirely — two commentators calling a match she wasn’t permitted to enter.

Taeyeon — whose competitive spirit had always run at least three sizes larger than her frame — stalked over, reached out, and captured Yoon-a’s face by the cheek.

“Yoon-a. I see your skin has gotten itchy.”

“Ah— ahh— eonni, I was wrong, I was WRONG!” Piteous wailing. “I haven’t seen him since I got up! I don’t know where he went either!”

Released, Yoon-a retreated to safety. Taeyeon pivoted to Sunny and secured the second cheek.

“I call my own boyfriend something sweet. And? Is there a problem?”

But Sunny was not Yoon-a. Sunny counterattacked — both hands closing over Taeyeon’s face and kneading it like proofing dough.

“No problem. None whatsoever. It’s just—”

She stopped mid-sentence.

“…Hm?”

“Wait. Taeyeon. This feels AMAZING. Your skin is ridiculous.”

She kept kneading, visibly developing a dependency — the springy apple cheeks, the impossible slip of it under the fingers. Clean brows, full luminous skin, that bright unguarded look—

Like being teleported straight back to eighteen-year-old Taeyeon.

Yoon-a leaned in as well, extended one scallion-pale finger, and poked Taeyeon’s cheek. Soft. Elastic. Confirmed.

“Wow. Daebak.” It came out reverent. “This texture— can dating genuinely make a woman this pretty?”

There was a thread of envy woven through it that she hadn’t noticed in herself. The finish on that skin was better than her own — and hers was the finish other agencies cited in pitch meetings. This was seventeen-year-old skin. It defied accounting.

Taeyeon privately found it miraculous too. Since being with him, the good news simply had not stopped: first the height that had tormented her for years, then this brand-new lightness of body and mood, as if someone had exchanged her batteries for a better chemistry altogether. — Ji-ho really is my destiny. The thought grew clearer and firmer by the day.

Three heads were still bent together over the phenomenon of Taeyeon’s face when the entryway keypad sang.

The door opened on Ji-ho in full civilian armor — cap, mask — carrying paper bags stamped with the logo of a famous bakery.

“Ji-ho-ya!” Taeyeon bounced up to meet him and relieved him of the bags. “You went out just for breakfast?”

“Mm.” He pulled off the mask and cap, and the face beneath was fresh, clean-lined, faintly wind-cooled.

He’d woken with too much energy to lie still, given up on a second sleep, and gone for a slow run instead — collecting breakfast on the return leg. With Utterly Unremarkable running, recognition wasn’t even a line item.

“Nuna, take these over and set up — I’ll handle the milk.” He handed her most of the bags, keeping back a single bottle printed with the Yonsei label.

Taeyeon glanced at it. The very brand he’d named the night before.

In the open kitchen he washed four glasses, opened the perfectly ordinary bottle of Yonsei milk, and poured two.

Then — casually, incidentally — he glanced back at the kitchen doorway. In the living room, three women were laying out cutlery and laughing at something in low voices. No eyes on him.

⟨ SYSTEM ⟩  Inventory — Height-Reforging Milk · month three of the course · two servings withdrawn

A flick of intent, and the bottle from the system vault was in his hand. Two quick, steady pours — one into his own glass, one into Taeyeon’s. Milk-white folded into milk-white and vanished. Four identical glasses.

He carried them out on a tray. The table was already set: croissants — the no-oil, low-sugar kind — berries, cherry tomatoes, boiled eggs.

“Wah, that looks SO good!” Yoon-a’s eyes lit with a reflexive, broadcast-grade sparkle.

Variety-show instincts, carved permanently into the soul.

Four people sat. Sunny’s gaze locked onto the glass before her like a targeting system acquiring signal. She lifted it, examined it against the light, sniffed it, and looked up at him with shining eyes.

“Ji-ho. This is it? The milk you give Taeyeon?”

“Yes, Sunny nuna. Yonsei’s special-supply line — it’s not easy to find in ordinary stores.” Not a muscle out of place; the tone was sincerity itself.

Sunny hesitated no longer. She took a long, devout swallow and considered it with a sommelier’s focus.

“Mmm— the milk flavor really is rich. Very smooth going down.”

She finished the glass in a few more swallows — drinking, apparently, not milk but the elixir at the gates of the Kingdom of the Tall.

Breakfast unrolled at an easy pace. Taeyeon tore her croissant into small pieces and dabbed them in tomato juice. Yoon-a worked through the berries in tiny bites, eyes flicking now and then to the wordless coordination running across the table. Ji-ho — having registered that Taeyeon’s glance lingered on the berries exactly twice — transferred a share of his own onto her plate without breaking stride in the conversation.

Sunny ate fast. Her mind was audibly elsewhere.

She finished the last bite of egg, patted her mouth, and deployed her opening move with studied carelessness.

“Oh, right — Taeyeon. What did you say you measure now? 161?”

“Mm. Measured just a few days ago.” A nod, entirely unsuspecting.

“Let’s see, then.” Sunny rose and crossed to a blank stretch of living-room wall. “Come here, come here — I’ll confirm it for you. Yoon-a, you too. Let’s all compare.”

Her tone was bouncy — a game invented on the spot, to all appearances.

Taeyeon trotted over, laughing, and stood flush against the wall.

From somewhere about her person, Sunny produced a small tape measure — an object whose spontaneity had clearly been in preparation for some time. She leveled it over Taeyeon’s head.

“161. Confirmed.”

Then she pulled in a cheerfully cooperative Yoon-a and repeated the operation.

“Yoon-a, 168. Correct.”

Sunny nodded, then stepped up to the wall herself. “Me too — it’s been ages.”

Taeyeon read the tape for her.

The number resolved, and the smile froze solid on Sunny’s face.

“…155—?”

She said it very quietly. Her eyes widened by degrees — then snapped to Taeyeon as grief and outrage arrived hand in hand.

“HA?! I’m a full SIX centimeters shorter than Taeyeon now?!”

Six centimeters. SIX. The gap had never been that big — never!

She drew a long breath, forced the seismic activity back beneath the crust, and turned toward the dining table, where Ji-ho was unhurriedly finishing his milk. Her eyes acquired a missionary gleam.

“Ji-ho! You too. Over here.”

He blinked — then read the set of Sunny’s jaw, which communicated with total clarity that no one in this household was having a peaceful morning until he had been measured, and rose with a laugh to stand at the wall.

He didn’t even have to try. Standing there in house clothes, spine easy, the proportions conducted their own argument.

Sunny went up on her toes and, with visible engineering difficulty, leveled the tape across the top of his head. She read it carefully. Then she read it again.

“184!!!” Her voice cracked clean out of its register, and the look she turned on him was undiluted disbelief. “Shibal— that’s TWENTY-NINE centimeters taller than me!!”

And beneath the comedy, the detective’s engine was already turning at full speed.

She was almost certain now: whatever height Ji-ho had walked into SM with, it had NOT been this. Boys develop late, granted — but a visible surge after nineteen? Vanishingly rare.

Now she held his current number. Next step: pull the intake records at the company and compare. If the difference was significant, “a lucky second growth phase” would not stretch far enough to cover it.

Meanwhile — she had drunk the so-called “same milk” today. If her own height failed to move a single millimeter in the weeks ahead… then the milk was not the mechanism. And if the milk was not the mechanism, Taeyeon’s growth had another cause. Taeyeon would never lie to her — which meant the anomaly lived on HIS side of the ledger.

He was hiding something. About the milk. About the height. Possibly about a great deal more.

Sunny’s eyes narrowed a fraction, tracking him as he laughed at something Taeyeon whispered — perfectly at ease, perfectly unbothered — and a purely detective species of excitement kindled in her chest.

Ji-ho, ah, Ji-ho. Pray I never catch you holding anything… interesting.

Because if you are sitting on a way to grow taller — you are about to learn precisely how terrifying a woman who wants ten more centimeters can be.
