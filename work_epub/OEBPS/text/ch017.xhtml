Chapter Seventeen

The Ab Board

Housewarming delivered, Ji-ho returned to his room — and only then remembered the [Win the Fans] rewards still sitting unclaimed in the vault.

A thought, and the softly glowing panel unfolded before his eyes. In the Quest Hall, the numbers refreshed at once. He hadn’t uploaded a thing all week, yet the practice-room clip of “Yesterday Once More” had kept quietly working the algorithm on his behalf: fans up to 2,300, total subscribers at 5,900.

“Claim quest rewards.”

Rewards Claimed
Ding — Second social-platform fan slot unlocked. Bind at will. Ding — Ab Trainer Board deposited to inventory. Withdraw anytime. Ding — Fan milestone detected. Achievement quest [Win the Fans · Stage Two] triggered.
Achievement Quest · Win the Fans — Stage Two

Objective: Dual-platform accumulation unlocked. Reach a combined total of 10,000 fans across any two bound platforms.

Reward: Professional-grade instrument skill (random)  ·  Skill Upgrade Card ×1  ·  Directed Opportunity Guidance ×1 (scope roughly specifiable)

Current total fans2,300 / 10,000

His eyes ran down the quest text. [Win the Fans] had no ceiling — he remembered that much from the game, where he’d once cleared a stage requiring fifty million. How long would that take in the real world? He shook the fantasy off. Pre-debut, fan growth moved like ants hauling rice. Towers rose from level ground; the only work that mattered right now was sharpening the blade.

His attention swung back to the physical prize.

The ab board… The icon sat crisp in the inventory, and beside it, the product slogan — a line of pure, weapons-grade audacity:

“Every rep you do on me is one more you’ll do on her.”

The corner of his mouth curled into the particular smile all men recognize. Whoever wrote that deserves a raise.

Ji-ho had a long-held theory: a man’s appeal changed carriers as he aged. As a student, it was sweat flying on a basketball court, straight shoulders, a clean face, good grades or quick jokes. Fresh into the working world, it became hustle, competence, upside. And past the gates of thirty, the metrics collapsed brutally down to two: power and money.

But one invisible wellspring of charm ran beneath every stage of a man’s life — strong legs and a strong core.

The virtues of powerful legs and a forged midsection required no elaboration. A flat stomach with visible definition banished the middle-aged grease years before they arrived; it made the cheapest T-shirt hang like tailoring; it upgraded an average face by two whole points on pure silhouette. And here was the beautiful part — unlike a face, which genetics assigned, or power and wealth, which fate rationed, legs and core answered to nothing but consistency. Show up, and the results showed up.

So: legs and core. A man trains both, always. Non-negotiable.

“Withdraw: Ab Trainer Board.”

Light flickered over the empty patch of living-room floor — and there it stood, materialized out of nowhere: matte black head to toe, a long ergonomic curve of a bench, a stout T-shaped base, an adjustable incline bracket. Professional in every line of it.

He knew this class of machine intimately. Shirt off, he took stock in the window’s reflection: stomach flat, no spare flesh — and no visible muscle either. The classic skinny but shapeless starting point.

He lay back on the board, hooked his feet under the anchors, toes up, knees soft, hands loose at his ears, spine flush to the pad. Deep breath. Core locked. He curled up slow to forty-five degrees, held the squeeze at the top for a deliberate second — abs burning against each other — then lowered with the same control.

“Exhale… inhale…”

Rhythmic breathing filled the quiet apartment. Twenty sit-ups a set; he gritted through three sets. Fine sweat beaded his forehead; heat and swell bloomed through his abdomen — that long-lost, weirdly delicious ache.

An untrained eighteen-year-old body should be cooked after three sets… He read the feedback and understood. System hardware. It’s offloading my lower back so everything hits the abs directly. Ridiculous. Wonderful.

Strength remaining meant strength to spend. Three sets of crunches next, for the upper abs. Then three sets of reverse crunches, carpet-bombing the lower.

Training was tedium and pain, never more so than at the start. So against his body’s mutiny, Ji-ho deployed the ancient technique of psychological warfare — grinding out reps while chanting internally, mainlining pure spiritual pre-workout:

“One — for my future girlfriend’s happiness!”

“Two — for every admiring glance I have not yet earned!”

“Three — for the spine-straight confidence of a self-made man!”

“Four — for being able to ride at full gallop at sixty!”

“Ahh~ feels great — FEELS GREAT—”

Great? Great, his foot.

When the final rep died, Ji-ho collapsed onto the cool floorboards like a man dragged out of a river, gasping like a landed fish. His abs had gone so sour they’d nearly seceded from his body; even smiling pulled something that hurt.

Training, he reflected from the floor, shared its fundamental physics with certain other strenuous joys of human life: agony at the start, always — but grit through the adaptation phase, and the payoff was a euphoria nothing else touched. The early pain was simply the toll. Whine at the first soreness, quit at the first burn, and every marvelous thing on the far side stayed forever out of reach.

Which was exactly what the chanting was for — anesthetize the present with the future, lie to yourself at full volume, shout this feels amazing until the day it finally did. Believe in the far-side euphoria hard enough, and you could survive any beginning.
