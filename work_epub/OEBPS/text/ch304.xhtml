What a Lecher!

Friday · October 4, 2013 · Singapore · late that night Mount Elizabeth Novena Hospital · Room 308 one answer at last · an audience of one too many · a most suspicious application of physics

How could a man be this awful?

He already had so many people beside him. Yet he had come after her too, and now he stood there speaking with such earnestness, looking at her as though there were nothing frivolous about any of it.

And she had fallen in love with him.

Love. Im Yoon-a had no use for the smaller word anymore.

A few minutes earlier · the doorway that settled it

The moment she had seen him inside this room, she had known she was finished. She could pretend to need very little, but when she was miserable, she needed someone beside her. Understanding why he could not come would never have made that need disappear.

A call from Seoul would have been reasonable. She would have understood his work. If he had asked whether he should fly over, she might even have stopped him herself.

You have an album to finish. Don’t be ridiculous. I’ll be all right.

She could almost hear herself saying it: the considerate girl, volunteering to be left alone before anyone had to ask.

Instead, he had come. By the time he called, he was downstairs. He had not rung ahead to negotiate the journey, or sent a message giving her time to talk him out of it.

She was so good at being understanding that people could mistake it for needing nothing.

It was a thoroughly unreasonable thing for a chairman to do. Work was waiting. Taeyeon eonni was here in Singapore; so were Fany eonni and Sica eonni. Any one of them could have walked into this room. All three of them could have.

He had come anyway.

That was what had undone her. Not a better explanation. The man himself, where she had never quite dared to expect him.

He was still waiting for her answer.

For once, Yoon-a did not look away or find a joke to stand behind. She met his eyes.

“I’m willing.”

Im Yoon-a · the decision, without consultation

What she knowsShe loves him. Not because the complications have disappeared, and not on the condition that he finds a tidier life tomorrow.

What he must faceHer love, exactly as he asked for it. She is not promising to become convenient.

Everyone elseTheir objections are theirs. If they cannot bear her being here, they know where the door is.

And why was that her problem? If they couldn’t stand it, they could go. Were they hoping to stay and share a table with her?

Song Ji-ho stared at her for a second.

Then delight broke across his face so completely that she barely had time to recognize it before her feet left the floor. He lifted her and turned with her in his arms.

“Put me down! I’m still a patient!”

She struck his shoulder, laughing and mortified. He set her down at once, although he seemed to consider letting go a separate request, one she had not made.

Within reach on the cabinet lay the thermometer she had abandoned when he came in. She retrieved it, suddenly remembering Seohyun’s instructions, and slipped it back under her arm. One sensible thing. She could manage one sensible thing.

Ji-ho rested his forehead against hers.

A very brief attempt at responsibility

“Andwae.”

He stopped against the palm she had placed over his mouth.

His breath warmed her hand. It tickled; she was trying hard not to smile.

“Your album’s coming out soon. I can’t give you my cold.”

“I’m not afraid of it.”

He kissed her palm, softly. While she was still registering that small, ridiculous tenderness, he eased her hand aside.

Then he kissed her, and the sensible thing ceased to matter.

She closed her eyes and rose onto her toes. Tomorrow, his other women, whatever anyone might say: she let all of it wait. Just once in her life, she wanted to stop standing at a safe distance from the thing she wanted.

Her hand found his shoulder.

Clink.

Something slipped from inside her gown, struck the floor, and rolled twice.

They both looked down.

The thermometer lay intact on the tile, having completed a more successful escape than either of them had managed.

Yoon-a looked up at him. His mouth twitched. They started laughing together.

One glass thermometer, unbroken. One reading that had never had a chance. The call button still waiting for a result worth reporting.

Ji-ho retrieved the thermometer, checked it and cleaned it, then shook the column down with a practiced flick of his wrist.

“What kind of nonsense is that?”

She gave him a reproachful stare. Then, instead of reaching for the thermometer, she turned her face aside and lifted her arm for him. The tips of her ears had gone red.

He eased the collar out of the way. A glimpse of pink beneath the gown caught his eye, and she noticed the pause immediately.

“Yah! What are you looking at? Put it in already.”

He obeyed. The cool glass made her draw a small breath.

“There.”

He straightened the fabric, pulled the blanket over her, and sat beside the bed. When he took her hand, her fingers were cool. He enclosed them between his palms and warmed them slowly.

“How did you get this ill?”

Yoon-a was quiet.

He would blame himself if she told him. She knew that. But she had just agreed to be with him; she did not want to begin by keeping the difficult part to herself.

“My Love from the Star.”

His hands stilled around hers.

That left him with nothing he could reasonably insist on. He lifted her hand and kissed her fingers.

She watched his lowered eyes and felt a quiet warmth spread through her. Missing this drama hurt. It would not be the last script in the world. Not with him beside her.

He let out a breath.

THE MANHe had also wanted the time with her. There was no point disguising that motive now that she had agreed to stay beside him.

She was giving up the chance to pursue the role, not surrendering a part that had already been guaranteed to her.

After all his arrangements, she was still going to miss it.

Yet the selfish wish beneath those arrangements had come true. She had chosen him. He could hardly pretend he had been concerned only with the casting.

Girls’ Generation was her youth. Faced with that choice, she would choose the group. He understood, and he would respect it.

An intention he keeps to himself · no borrowed trophies

A Baeksang for her television work. A Blue Dragon for a film. He could imagine both, but imagining an award was not the same as earning it.

He wanted to walk that road with her: the right scripts, the work, one credible performance after another. Not a woman in her thirties still being described as a future best actress, with the future endlessly pushed ahead of her.

This drama would go on without her. There would have to be other chances, honestly made.

They stayed like that, holding hands, talking about nothing long enough for it to become something precious.
