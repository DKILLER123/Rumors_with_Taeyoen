Chapter Two Hundred One

Single Yoon-a Takes Critical Damage

June 4, 2013 · late afternoon The hillside villa six rolls of toilet paper, one pink slipper interrogation, and a kitchen scene nobody warned her about

The scene at the door left him briefly outside his own body.

Six girls. Six bales of toilet paper.

“Congratulations on the new home — live comfortably here~”

Taeyeon led off, smiling, pressing the first roll into his arms. Milk-apricot cotton tee today, setting off skin gone luminous; when she tipped her head back to look up at him, her eyes bent into crescents and the sweetness was frankly unfair.

“Congratulations on the move. May only good things happen in this house.”

Yoon-a followed — considerably less gently — slapping her bale down onto the growing stack. Plain white tee, denim shorts, legs from here to the property line; the smile was certified harmless, and at the bottom of the doe eyes, one clean glint of frost.

“Congrats on the new place! Live comfortably~”

Sunny, Yuri, Jessica, Tiffany — one after another, near-identical blessings, identical motion, bale after bale onto the tower in his arms.

He hugged the rising column of good fortune, still processing — and got body-checked half a step sideways.

“Yah, move over. You’re blocking the door.”

Yoon-a shoved past, slipped out of her canvas shoes, and padded onto the polished marble in white ankle socks. Two steps into the entryway she stopped dead.

“Eonni — isn’t this your FIRST time here?” Her voice rang off the entryway stone. “So why are there brand-new women’s slippers? PINK ones? Could it be—”

She hoisted the fluffy pink slippers he’d staged for Taeyeon and looked back, gaze traveling a slow, meaningful circuit between girlfriend and boyfriend.

“Yah! Yoon-a! Put those DOWN!”

His arms were fully committed to toilet paper; volume was the only tool left.

“Mwo? What did you just call me?”

One beautiful eyebrow went up. She turned, and the white sock-soles moved fast and silent across the marble — one blink and she was in front of him, small fists raining on his arm and shoulder.

“Aish! I am FOUR years older than you! Ji-ho! You DARE first-name me? You’re dead, you brat!”

The force was real. This was not banter-force. This was grievance-force.

You forgot my BIRTHDAY! Hammer! Hammer! HAMMER-HAMMER-HAMMER!

“Sss— ah, YAH! Yoon-a! Striking your brother-in-law? You’re FINISHED!”

He parried one-armed, the tower tilted, and the whole cargo of blessings avalanched across the floor. He gave up, let it fall, and grabbed for her — and she twisted at the waist like a fish through fingers, escaped in one squeaking sock-slide, and installed herself behind Taeyeon, who had just donned the pink slippers and was pressing down a smirk. A face was made at him from behind the leader’s shoulder.

The slippers fit perfectly, of course. The sweetness in Taeyeon’s chest compounded another notch: he’d PREPARED for her.

Yuri hooked Taeyeon’s arm and towed her toward the wall of glass. “DAEBAK! This view! This interior!” She swept the living room. “Ji-ho is genuinely rich-rich. To RENT a place like this—”

Sunny drifted behind them like a detective on retainer, eyes doing a slow pan of every surface. “His copyright income from that one full album could fund a retirement. Taeyeon-ah — you struck actual treasure.”

“Aigoo, genuinely jealous. Handsome, talented, AND earning — Taeyeon, your luck is criminal.”

Taeyeon fought her mouth’s corners for dignity and lost comprehensively. Boyfriend envied by the entire sisterhood: vanity nourishment of the highest grade. Even today’s sunlight seemed personally flattering.

By the entry, he crouched over the scattered bales with a sigh. Tiffany and Jessica knelt to help. Tiffany wore a pale yellow knit, soft and clean — she gathered rolls silently into her arms, saying nothing, looking at him not at all. Jessica, cool as standing water, light-gold hair slipping off one shoulder and tucked back with a flick, retrieved two boxes.

“Thank you, nunas.” He eyed the identical bales. “But — why is it ALL toilet paper? And the same greeting six times?”

Jessica lifted her eyes and administered a brief look, then explained, tone unusually patient. “Taeyeon said we were coming for a housewarming. You’re American-raised — you might not know. On the peninsula, housewarmings usually mean practical gifts, and toilet paper is the classic: it runs out fast, which means every problem unspools and resolves smoothly. New life unrolling clean, luck rolling bigger and bigger.” A beat. “Six people giving the same gift supposedly multiplies the blessing.”

Clear. Organized. Weirdly patient. Beside her, Tiffany’s sidelong glance said what her mouth didn’t: since WHEN does Sica explain anything to anyone this nicely?

“Still — did it have to be ONLY toilet paper?” He laughed despite himself. “Fruit exists. Rice cakes exist.”

“Because toilet paper is CHEAP, obviously.”

Yoon-a had circled back and leaned against the entry cabinet, arms crossed, voice at refrigerator temperature.

“Hmph. Some people are stingy in their own conduct and still expect expensive gifts? Tsk tsk. The thickness of some faces.”

She sniffed and strolled off, leaving him blinking.

Offended her? WHEN?

He turned the question toward Tiffany with his eyes. She pressed her lips together, dodged his gaze, and said it low and fast: “Her birthday was the 30th of last month. You — forgot, didn’t you.”

He went still.

May 30th. The law firm. The war room. The LOI. — Completely. Utterly. Forgotten. And Yoon-a had sent HIM a birthday gift this year, hand-picked, from Japan. …Ah. THAT’s the private-grievance force.

The touring party returned from the living room and open study. Yoon-a’s small nose tested the air like a suspicious woodland creature. “What’s that scent? It’s actually nice.”

“Persian pomegranate-blossom diffuser,” Taeyeon supplied instantly, eyes curving. “My favorite lately. Ji-ho prepared it specially for me.” Her gaze went over to him, soft as syrup. “He’s always this thoughtful.”

“Euu-reohh~~”

Sunny, Yuri and Yoon-a hugged their own arms in synchronized full-body cringe.

Taeyeon’s lip curled. Single dogs. Beneath acknowledgment.

The party settled around the sofa cluster; the toilet-paper mountain was quietly warehoused.

“What’ll everyone drink? Tea? Coffee?”

“COFFEE!” — six voices, one syllable.

Truly, on this peninsula, coffee was not a beverage. It was a blood type.

“Give me a few minutes.” Seven pour-overs. Nontrivial workload.

“I’ll help!” Taeyeon was at his side in three steps, arm through his.

“Perfect. I need the labor.”

The two of them headed for the island counter, arm in arm.

Tiffany watched the linked arms recede — face flat, voice off. The sour foam rose slowly and sat behind her sternum, dense and airless. So THIS is what it feels like: watching the man you love and your closest friend be effortless in front of you, while your own position permits exactly nothing but silence.

“Fany — what’s wrong? Your face just went somewhere.” Yuri bumped her arm.

She snapped the crescent smile back up; the wattage didn’t quite arrive. “Ani. Just — suddenly worried about tour rehearsal—”

“Yah, no WORK during rest hours!” Yuri slung an arm around her shoulders. “How many years have we been doing stages? What’s left to fear!”

“Right, right. Today we decompress.” Sunny leaned in on the other side.

She smiled, nodded, and hauled her attention back into the chatter by hand.

✦   ✦   ✦

At the island, beans rattled into the grinder.

“Ji-ho-ya, mianhae—” Taeyeon leaned close, voice small. “I just mentioned it in passing. I didn’t think they’d ALL follow.”

“What’s there to apologize for?” He leveled the grounds, easy. “Besides being — mm, my girlfriend’s in-laws — they’re all my friends too.”

“Friends—” She repeated it lightly, and then, with studied carelessness: “You’re on… pretty good terms with all of them? Everyone was very enthusiastic today.”

He pressed the extraction button; coffee began its slow drip; the smell unfolded. He tilted his head at her, smiled. “Tiffany nuna and I got close through the whole Sulli thing. As for the others—” a beat. “Oh. Right. Yoon-a nuna is apparently furious with me.”

“Yoon-a? What did you do?”

“She sent me a birthday present. Her birthday — I was buried and forgot it completely.”

“MWORAGO?!” Her hand flew to her mouth — and then the understanding landed, followed immediately by unconcealed schadenfreude. “No WONDER! I thought she was weirdly proactive today — SHE’S the one who campaigned for everyone to bring identical toilet paper, going on about how six-six means double luck and has special meaning for—” she caught herself, “—for people raised abroad.” An elbow to his ribs. “Yoon-a holds grudges PROFESSIONALLY. Watch yourself tonight.”

He shrugged, handed her the first finished cup. “Standing by whenever.”

Who ends up regretting tonight remains statistically open.

He set the next filter. “Sunny nuna, meanwhile, remains obsessed with how you and I got taller. Reconnaissance mission, I’d guess.”

“Mwo?! Then—”

“Relax.” He patted her hand, voice dropping. “I haven’t had any special dreams lately. She can search all she wants — there’s nothing to find.”

She exhaled. So THAT’S why Soon-kyu shamelessly tagged along on every date lately. One hundred fifty-five centimeters: the one obsession of a woman otherwise fully satisfied by video games.

Run the ledger, then: Yuri came for alcohol and spectacle. Yoon-a came for revenge. Sunny came hunting the secret. Fany came because they’re close and just wrapped a collaboration. Every name accounted for—

—except one. Jessica.

Her brow creased by a millimeter. Sica’s shift around Ji-ho lately was too subtle and too total. Before: professionally indifferent, one acid comment per conversation. Now: drifting INTO conversations about him. Volunteering details about his work that even his girlfriend didn’t know. Like — like a FAN. But they’d barely interacted, apart from the OST session. Could one song do that? “Love Me Like You Do” turning HER into a music-stan? Or was it—

Her eyes settled on the profile beside her — the nose line, the jaw, the mouth pressed in concentration.

Sica is, famously, terminally, a face-fanatic.

An unnameable prickle of danger wound up her spine. She stepped in and wrapped her arms around him from behind, cheek flat against his back.

He paused mid-scoop. “What is it, nuna?”

“Ji-ho-ya—” The words came muffled, small, oddly fragile. “I don’t know why. But holding you like this — everything heavy feels survivable. Is that… am I being pathetic?”

Warmth flooded through him. He shut off the machine, turned in her arms, and took her small face in both hands. Her eyes were clear and bright and holding his reflection.

“Pathetic? No. That’s the best true thing anyone’s ever said to me.”

He bent his forehead to hers, breath mingling.

“Because holding you — that’s MY charger. Nuna. You’re my antidote too.”

Her heart performed a full compression sequence. Ji-ho. You are genuinely going to be the death of me.

She rose on her toes and kissed him. He answered instantly — one arm at her waist, the other lifting, and she was up on the clean island counter, his hand cradling the back of her neck, tenderly thorough.

A soft sound escaped her; her arms circled his neck, fingers threading into black hair, answering in kind.

“The coffee needs—”

Yoon-a wandered into the kitchen — and received, at point-blank range, the full unabridged scene.

“…Sorry.”

She backed out with her face on fire.

Taeyeon surfaced, went scarlet in one frame, and hammered a fist against his chest. “That was YOUR fault!”

You kissed ME, he did not say, wisely. “Nuna. Let’s plate the coffee.”

Yoon-a returned to the living room with her ear-tips glowing.

“Yah — weren’t you helping carry coffee? Where’s the coffee?” Sunny squinted at her empty hands.

“The coffee—” Yoon-a fanned herself, eyes flickering, voice dropping to gossip-band. “Taeyeon eon— Ji-ho oppa. The kitchen. Mm. That.”

She performed a two-hand gesture terminating in pressed lips.

Yuri rolled her eyes, unmoved. “Tch. A couple kissing in their own kitchen. Groundbreaking.”

Which — fine, was fair. Except the frame Yoon-a had actually walked into was: Taeyeon eonni seated ON the counter, two people fused at the mouth, visibly unwilling to ever file taxes separately again. Full French-cinema grade. For single-population viewers, the impact rating was simply too high.
