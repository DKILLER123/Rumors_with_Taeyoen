Chapter Seventy

The Prodigy Writer

Choi Sung-woo and Park In-jun reached the practice room mid-drill — and spotted their target instantly: the tall, straight-backed figure at the mirror wall, with Wendy and Joy resting nearby.

Before they could open the door, hurried footsteps sounded from the other end of the corridor.

“Sung-woo hyung. What a coincidence.”

The voice smiled; the feet did not slow. Sung-woo turned, and his brow twitched a millimeter. Yoo Ji-hoon — senior producer, Team 3, an assistant in tow. Team 3 ran TVXQ and Super Junior, but lately they’d been shopping for songs for certain collaboration projects.

“Ji-hoon hyung.” A nod — and an inward curse. News travels FAST in this building.

Ji-hoon strolled up to the practice-room door, gaze already locked on Ji-ho, and murmured sideways, tone light, eyes anything but:

“Looks like we’re here for the same thing.”

The instructor noticed the doorway delegation and paused the class; the trainees turned as one. Two producers plus staff, unannounced — this was not a common sight.

“Apologies, seonsaengnim — we need a quick word with Ji-ho. Bit urgent.”

A glance at her watch; a clap.

“Everyone — ten minutes.”

“Ji-ho-ssi. Sorry to interrupt — there’s something we’d like to discuss.”

“Producer-nim. Hello.” He bowed, correct as always, and came over.

Seung-wan’s eyes had gone round; she rose halfway without meaning to, staring across the room.

“Yoo Ji-hoon, Production Team 3.” Card presented, zero preamble. “That song you uploaded to the company library — ‘Some.’ We’ve heard it. It’s outstanding. We’re planning a cross-team collaboration project, and the song fits it perfectly.”

“Production Team 1 — we handle BoA, Girls’ Generation, and others.” Sung-woo’s card arrived half a second behind. “The duet format is genuinely inventive — the conversational interplay in the melody and arrangement especially. A rare piece of work.”

Two senior producers, presenting cards to a three-month rookie, over one song. A ripple of sharply drawn breath crossed the practice room; every eye converged.

Ji-ho accepted both cards with a calm that visibly wasn’t the nerves-suppressed kind, and inclined his head.

“Thank you both for the recognition.”

Sung-woo pressed first.

“BoA’s seventh full album is in final assembly — we’re short one high-caliber sub-title track. We believe ‘Some’ fits her voice and her musical standards precisely. Authorize it, and the company pays top-tier rookie composition rates — and frankly, BoA’s interpretation would give the song its definitive form.”

A named placement on the queen’s album — the offer most young writers would crawl across glass for. Ji-hoon countered without missing a beat.

“Ji-ho-ssi — our project pairs two extremely popular vocalists, male and female; the lineup under negotiation is worth real excitement. Placed there as the TITLE track, the promotion and exposure would be top-class. And—” a weighted pause, “—the collaboration format means you could join the project directly, as a featured artist or co-producer. For a rookie, there is no better introduction to the public.”

A diva’s album versus a marquee collab — with a production credit dangled on top, the one door rookies almost never got opened. The air between the two offers all but crackled.

A few meters away, Seung-wan’s heart hammered against her ribs. BoA! A headline project! One song did THIS?! She stared at Ji-ho’s back with something rapidly leaving the neighborhood of admiration for somewhere warmer — and clenched both fists over how he’d choose.

Under the room’s collected gaze, Ji-ho let a beat of silence pass. Then he bowed, slightly.

“I’m sincerely grateful — for the regard, and for terms this generous. But regarding ‘Some’… I have my own plans for it. For now, I’m unable to license it to either team.”

“What?” In-jun’s whisper escaped him.

Both producers froze outright. They had gamed out a bidding war; they had gamed out a rookie holding out for price. A flat, polite refusal had appeared in neither simulation.

“Ji-ho-ssi. Meaning…?” Ji-hoon’s smile folded away; his tone turned careful.

“I’m only a trainee, and my writing is still finding its own way forward.” He lifted his head; the amber eyes were perfectly clear. “That this song earned your ears means a great deal to me. But I want my work to enter the world at the right moment, in the exact shape I most want for it. That moment isn’t yet. Or maybe — I’m simply not ready to let it leave home like this.”

Not one word of the actual plan disclosed — and yet the message underneath was unmistakable: this writer treasured his work, and this writer would keep total authority over it. In a company as strictly tiered as SM, where producers held the whip hand, a rookie trainee with THIS much spine was practically a unicorn.

Sung-woo studied him for a long moment — thinking of “A Boy,” that other stunning, unmistakably personal song — and revised his estimate of the boy upward by a full category. More vision than we assumed. And considerably more ambition.

“Understood.” No anger; if anything, a new grain of respect. “Respecting the creator’s intent is our principle. Then — we look forward to your future work. And when something suits our team’s artists… I hope Team 1 gets the first listen.”

“Exactly right — let’s keep in touch.” Ji-hoon’s smile was back at full wattage. “Team 3’s door is always open. Next demo, send it straight to me.”

Both men were far too seasoned to force what couldn’t be forced; goodwill banked now was leverage later. A few more courtesies, and they swept out with their assistants — as if the skirmish had never happened.

The practice room, however, had permanently changed pressure.

The door clicked shut, and the murmurs boiled over.

“Did you HEAR that? BoA sunbae-nim’s album! Team 3’s collab project!”

“And he REFUSED? Is he insane?”

“That was Choi Sung-woo AND Yoo Ji-hoon. In person.”

“‘Some’? A NEW song? He wrote ANOTHER one after ‘A Boy’??”

Seung-wan reached him first, seizing his arm, eyes enormous, voice pitched low and failing utterly to contain itself:

“You— you really wrote a song THAT good? And turned them DOWN? WHY?!”

He looked at her flushed, incandescent face, and smiled, and gave her all four words he intended to give anyone:

“I have other plans.”

“Other plans…” she echoed faintly — staring at him like a stranger for one second, and like a shrine the next. “Whatever they are — you’re COMPLETELY ridiculous, you know that?! Those were two of the most famous producers in this whole company!”
