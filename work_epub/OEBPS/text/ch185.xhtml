Chapter One Hundred Eighty-Five

The Meeting That Never Happens

May 2013 · evening Gimpo International Airport → the van → home nine girls come home from Japan; one demo escapes one pair of earphones

May in Seoul had already picked up the first warmth of early summer.

The international arrivals hall at Gimpo was packed solid with pink light-sticks, and when nine figures appeared at the mouth of the corridor, the sound came down like surf.

“GIRLS’ GENERATION! GIRLS’ GENERATION!”

“Taeyeon eonni! Over HERE!”

“Yoon-a-yaaa—!”

Four months of touring Japan, closed out with the final show — Osaka, Nagoya, Fukuoka, seven cities, twenty performances, more than two hundred thousand tickets: the largest Japanese arena run any Korean girl group had ever posted, numbers that outshone most of the boy groups too. As an idol group, Girls’ Generation stood, at this moment, on the summit.

And a summit means a contract horizon drawing closer, and futures that need thinking about. None of that reached their faces. They smiled, waved, and let the security wedge carry them through the crowd.

Jessica drew eyes even by the group’s standards today: a sharply cut ivory trench over a champagne silk shirt, black skinny jeans into heeled ankle boots, the coat swinging open as she walked. Chin faintly lifted, sunglasses covering half her face and hiding none of the cool. Someone screamed “JESSICA!” — she turned toward it and gave one small wave, and the scream section detonated.

It’s time, she thought, and the idea she’d been incubating for a year hardened another degree. The personal lane: fashion.

✦   ✦   ✦

The van door shut and guillotined the noise.

Kwon Yuri poured herself across a seat with zero regard for image and produced a long, luxurious groan. “Ahhh~ HOME. Three days off. I’m diving in Jeju. Who’s in?”

Silence, wall to wall.

Taeyeon peeled her mask off a small, tired, still-precise face. “Diving? Pass. I will be asleep. In my own bed. For the entire duration.”

“Same,” Choi Soo-young said. “Three days horizontal. Minimum.”

“You’re all no fun.” Yuri scanned for allies. “Seriously — nobody?”

Yoon-a glanced up from her phone, smiling. “Eonni. You KNOW how I feel about swimwear.”

Flat-and-proud Yoon-a: sworn enemy of any garment requiring a figure to carry it.

Tiffany sat against the window, wired earphones in, somewhere else entirely.

“Eonni, diving carries real risk,” Seohyun said, with complete sincerity. “Please prioritize safety protocols.”

“Yes, yes, noted.” Yuri waved it off and pivoted. “Sica? You love trying new things—”

Jessica had the new Korean Vogue open and did not look up. “I have things to organize. No time.”

Kim Hyo-yeon, in the next seat, tapped her shoulder. “Sica. Plans tonight?”

“Mm. Dinner with Soo-jung.”

The industry’s most famous doting older sister: first evening back always belonged to Krystal. Standing appointment; natural law.

“Then tomorrow night?” Hyo-yeon pressed. “Jun-hyung has a good friend he wants to introduce to you.”

That got the head up. Jessica looked at her over the sunglasses. “Introduce someone? To ME?”

“Mm.” Hyo-yeon nodded, serious. “You’ve been wanting to build something of your own, right? This guy’s the real thing — ex-investment banker, New York, network for days. He could genuinely help.”

She felt the pull immediately. High-end connections were exactly the scaffolding the brand plan needed—

And then a hook of melody filled the van.

DEMO (excerpt) · untitled theme · Ji-ho · 2013 — for the film The Girl in the Sun

Hypnotic electronic texture under a lazy, magnetic female guide vocal; a cold, fractured beat set against warm R&B phrasing; a chorus engineered to colonize the inner ear on first contact. Two minutes of demo, and the whole van had turned toward it.

The source: Tiffany’s phone. The earphone plug had slipped its jack.

Seohyun leaned over. “Eonni, what song is this? I’ve never — is it new?”

Tiffany’s face did something complicated. She pulled out the remaining earbud. “It’s the theme song demo. Ji-ho wrote it. For the film.”

“Mwo?” Yuri materialized over the seatback. “THE song? The one he asked YOU to sing? The English one?”

“Ne.”

“Wah. As expected of that man.” Yuri shook her head slowly. “Has he ever written a song that DIDN’T hit? Fany-yah, congratulations — that thing reeks of a smash from one verse.”

Tiffany smiled. The smile carried weight it didn’t advertise. The song was, objectively, superb — hook after hook, immaculate production. But it was HIS song, written FOR her, and everything between them was a drawer that wouldn’t close — so the pride came braided with something more complicated.

Jessica had gone still, listening. American-raised, both of them — English-language pop hit her ear at native resolution. She set the magazine down, crossed the van, and held out a hand.

“Fany. Is that the full version?”

“No — just a section. He sent it so I could get familiar.”

“Let me listen.”

One earbud each. The melody rose again, and Jessica’s eyes drifted shut — and the professional half of her brain began taking dictation. A-flat major, ninety-six beats per minute — pop-forward but never noisy. The icy, fractured percussion against the warmth of the vocal line: first-class contrast, first-class production. The kind of record that would chart in any hemisphere.

And underneath the analysis, unbidden, a complicated tide.

✦   ✦   ✦

Across the van, Sunny nudged Taeyeon’s shoulder and dropped to a murmur. “Your little boyfriend invited YOU too, right?”

The mouth curved before its owner authorized anything. “Mm. A duet. Male-female.”

Sunny took in the smug little glow and pursed her lips. “Aigoo. Must be NICE. Treatment like that — I should find a producer boyfriend of my own.”

A best friend’s envy: premium-grade nutrition for any woman.

Taeyeon basked — and her thoughts, having received clearance, departed immediately for the vicinity of one specific person. Four months apart. Missing him had grown like a vine given a whole wall: his kiss; the gentle-overbearing hands; the arms; the — ahem — general vigor and stamina of—

She pressed her knees together and heard absolutely nothing Sunny said next.

“Taeyeon? Yah, Taeyeon-ah?”

She surfaced with a start, cheeks running a suspicious temperature. “Mwo? What did you say?”

Sunny took one look at the flush and diagnosed the entire condition. Fully captured. Certified relationship-brain. Tsk tsk tsk — another woman down the love pit.

“I SAID — when do you record?”

“Tomorrow.” Taeyeon reassembled herself. “The film’s wrapped; post-production needs the theme and the OST. Fany and I have to get them recorded fast — we can’t be the bottleneck.”

A blink. “Can I come to the recording?”

“Eh?” Instant, visible perimeter alert. “Sunny-yah. Why is it that every time I go see Ji-ho, you need to come along?”

Ridiculous vinegar-pot. That guard-dog face — as if I’d steal him. Sunny’s conscience ran a quick self-audit and conceded the surveillance had NOT been subtle lately — that height-secret was simply too valuable. And if I visit him PRIVATELY and you find out, you’ll combust entirely.

She recalibrated the face to maximum charm and leaned into Taeyeon’s ear. “Obviously as your family representative I have to vet him~ how else can I hand you over with confidence?”

The sparse eyebrows knitted. “Eh??”

“KIDDING.” Sunny straightened, all business. “I’m going home to see my sisters these few days. I was teasing.”

✦   ✦   ✦

Jessica finished the demo, returned the earbud, and wore, briefly, the expression of someone who’d misplaced something she couldn’t name.

She didn’t LIKE Ji-ho — to be precise, she didn’t like that scalpel of a mouth or the eyes that always seemed to have finished reading you a page early. But the talent was not up for debate. Two minutes of demo already showed complete architecture — a song built to seize ears and keep them.

And—

The restaurant argument replayed. Afterward, she and Yoon-a had talked it all the way through, and Yoon-a herself had said it: the words were brutal, and they were also the words of someone genuinely in her corner — the kind only a real friend bothers saying. True things simply arrived with blades attached.

“Sica? …Sica?”

“Hm?”

“Tomorrow I go record,” Tiffany said. “Come with me?”

A beat of genuine surprise. “You’re inviting ME?”

“Mm.” Tiffany nodded, and the smile had a small plea folded into it. “You clearly love the song. Keep me company?”

She WAS tempted — the full version was a real lure. But Ji-ho, the person…

Tiffany, for her part, had run her own quiet math. Alone in a studio with Ji-ho AND Taeyeon — the couple, plus her, plus everything unresolved she was carrying — was a triangle with no comfortable seat. A third party changed the whole geometry. And Jessica was the perfect third party: loved the song, had a legitimate reason, and got along with him badly enough to add zero new complications.

Seeing the hesitation, she swung Jessica’s arm. “Eonni~ Sica eonni~ come with me! I’ll go shopping with you after~”

Shopping. Direct strike, center mass.

Jessica’s eyes lit. And anyway — the person was the person; the SONG was the song. And she’d be there for Fany, not for him.

“Fine. I’ll come.”

Hyo-yeon leaned back in. “Sica — then Jun-hyung’s friend—”

“A few days from now. My treat.” A pause; a very Jessica addendum: “You said he’s impressive. Meeting someone like that unprepared would be rude to both of us. I’ll organize my materials first.”

“Fair. I’ll pass it on — he’s genuinely looking forward to meeting you.”

“Thanks.”

She reopened the magazine and discovered she was reading nothing. The melody had colonized the space where the text should land.

Love me like you do— like WHO does? Like HOW?

She shook the thought off, annoyed at its persistence.

✦   ✦   ✦

The van shed members stop by stop. Soo-young home; Yoon-a home — at this altitude of career, the dorm only filled when the schedule demanded it. Taeyeon was the last drop. She stood at the foot of her building, looking up at her own windows, when the phone buzzed.

Messages · Ji-ho ♥

“Landed? Home safe?”

“Just reached the lobby.”

“You made it back. Come up quickly, my main-vocal-nim.”

“I bought your favorite pork cutlet.”

My main-vocal-nim.

The flush arrived on schedule. She bit her lip against the grin, lost comprehensively, and then she was running for the elevator — four months of missing him suddenly unbearable for even one more minute.

✦   ✦   ✦

Tiffany, back at the dorm, sat turning her phone over in her hands. The chat log glowed: the demo file, most recent. Above it, months old, the conversations that had never resolved into anything nameable. She scrolled. Stopped. Sighed — and put the screen to sleep.

Across town, Jessica pushed through the door of the villa she shared with her sister and was immediately hit at running speed.

“EONNI!”

“Soo-jung-ah~” The airport frost melted on contact, total and instantaneous. “Did you miss me?”

“OBVIOUSLY! You were gone FOREVER—”

They folded onto the sofa, and Krystal unloaded months of accumulated news at high baud while her sister listened, smiling, inserting commentary at intervals. And intermittently — annoyingly — Jessica’s mind slid sideways: to tomorrow; to a recording booth; to an unfinished melody.

Eventually, reunion warmth achieved and banked, Jessica poured herself along the sofa and deposited both slender legs across her sister’s lap with the entitlement of a house cat.

Krystal stared at the legs. Rage was drafted, reviewed, and shelved as impractical. She smiled, sweetly. “Eonni. Feet sore? I’ll massage them.”

Deeply satisfied with the state of her household, Jessica opened her carry-notebook — pages dense with brand sketches, positioning notes, potential partners. She turned to a fresh page and wrote tomorrow’s date.

Then paused. And under it, in English, wrote: Love me like you do.

She looked at the line for a long time.

Then crossed it out, neatly, and shut the notebook — which did nothing whatsoever about the melody still playing where her thoughts should be.

Maybe the answer arrives someday. Maybe it never does. That’s life: unknown all the way down — which is the same thing as possible all the way down.

At some point Krystal had evaporated — the AUDACITY, skipping out on assigned massage duty; noted in the ledger, little one — and Jessica retired to her room and worked, properly and seriously, on the materials: the brand. Modern minimalism. Eyewear, ready-to-wear, denim, cosmetics. The Girls’ Generation platform; the Jessica name; the differentiated edge. Blueprint after blueprint, drawn with real care, for the meeting Hyo-yeon had arranged — because if the man was as impressive as advertised, she intended to arrive prepared.

The Jessica preparing so carefully for that meeting could not have known:

The days immediately ahead held something that would knock her clean past it — and after that, the meeting would never happen at all.
