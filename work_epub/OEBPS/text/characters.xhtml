Profile

Recruited into the publicity machine of a nine-member group eleven days before it collapsed, she said nothing for fourteen months and is known to the public, if at all, as the girl who replaced the girl who was said to be bullied. In Chapter 276 Ryu Dong-hwan and Lee Hyun-woo find her in a fourth-floor flat in Sinchon with the curtains drawn, and she hands them a taped-over phone containing thirteen messages sent to her the day before the broadcast that ruined eight other people. She kept them at her own risk and showed them to nobody. The Chairman’s office covers her hospital fees, takes custody of the device under a dated log, and asks for one interview on her doctor’s schedule — not his. Everything that happens to T-ara, to Core Contents Media and to the price of the company from this point forward is happening because a frightened nineteen-year-old decided to keep a record instead of deleting it. Portrait is a commissioned artist impression; her circumstances and statements are the novel’s invention.

Profile

Raised in Los Angeles’ Koreatown, Song Ji-ho grew up between two languages and one dream. His father left when he was eight; his mother’s last phone call reached him in a middle-school cafeteria before her number went dark. He sang in bars underage for tip money, learned Korean from his grandmother, and flew to Seoul at eighteen with less than four thousand dollars — and a second lifetime of memories no one else could see. Regressed from 2025 to 2012 with the “Dream Life” system, he has transformed remembered songs into a Korean entertainment empire: a charting solo career, a 12.84-million-admission film, and control of LOEN Entertainment. In Chapter 282 he spends two hours and eleven minutes getting one signature that costs the fourth floor its set, runs the last day of the “7 Years” video to a wrap at twenty-nine minutes past three, and offers the girls his own apartment for the night rather than a car back to the dormitory, in the voice of a man doing a job he loves on an hour of sleep. In Chapter 283 he stops denying the shape of a night he organised himself, and in Chapter 284 he explains to a driver of twenty that money buys a person nothing so much as the permission to be honest about wanting things; in Chapter 285 he wakes with an arm pinned on either side of him and regrets precisely one thing, loudly and with a straight face. In Chapter 287 he sleeps in for the first time in nine months, is fed a second breakfast by his secretary, marks Son Seung-wan in writing as the probable main vocal, and holds a night tutorial that is only technically about vowels; in Chapter 288, over an Opus One, he refuses a gift offered on the giver’s terms — and answers the fury that follows with one flat sentence about Sica.

In Chapter 290 he spends Saturday being photographed for ECHO’s three physical editions — the base, the deluxe, and the five hundred — dictates a private club into existence from the back seat (nine rooms, ten to twenty guests, personal account, no photographs, ever), and then teaches Yoon-a the short course: biography, crying in gears, the memory bank, the news-anchor problem, and one diagnosis she repeats to herself on the drive home. In Chapter 293, while the whole peninsula argues about a silhouette and thirty-two pages, her phone rings in a practice-room corner — and she is out the door before anyone can read her face, In Chapter 294, on a cruiser top deck, she answers “Are you my lover?” with a “not yet” that both of them file, names the two times he has moved her heart (the Alps piggyback, and her own curiosity), takes his reading of her without flinching — and is set a final examination before Busan: one confession, eyes up, one take. In Chapter 295 she takes it — after a six-study final lesson on a boat, a confession built out of her own observation ledger (“I kept every entry. But I’m not playing”), a fireworks kiss that mutes the world, and his answer at the rail: I think I have liked you for a long time too. In Chapter 296 she answers his “go out with me” with a “not yet” that owes one person first — then pays the apology into the wrong bed, holding Taeyeon through the night with a saranghae that gets adjourned to tomorrow.

In Chapter 295, at the rail under borrowed fireworks, he sets the final examination, teaches the waitress minute and the repeat exercise first — and when she says it was just a scene, replies that he acted it with real feeling, and signs the forehead kiss with the answer she was waiting on.

Distinguishing marks: warm amber eyes; a producer’s ear; a dangerous habit of being sincere with everyone at once.

In Chapter 303 he leaves LOEN after learning of Yoon-a’s collapse, entrusts the divided rehearsal groups to Soo-bin, and flies to Singapore. He gets her room number by prompting Jessica to correct a false one, waits for the hospital to clear his visit, and reaches Yoon-a less than ten hours after she fell. Faced with her demand to know whether he can bear her love, he refuses an easy assurance and asks her to discover the answer beside him.

In Chapter 304 Yoon-a accepts his invitation to be with him. He offers to discuss moving the drama’s schedule, then respects her decision to put Girls’ Generation first. Her acting ambitions remain something he wants to support through future work, not a role or an award he can simply bestow. When Seohyun returns, he hides in the bathroom; afterward, his gratitude gives way to a shamelessly implausible explanation for a wandering thermometer.

In Chapter 305 he stays beside Yoon-a through a renewed fever and wagers a conditional place in the Dream Family Fund against her overnight recovery. His confidence rests on his existing Boyfriend Halo and Idol Aura, not on the sham medical theories he uses while teasing her. When she wakes feeling restored, his requested favor is her best concert performance. Fund admission remains unresolved as their renewed intimacy is interrupted by Taeyeon and Tiffany outside the door.

In Chapter 306 he refuses to hide when Taeyeon and Tiffany arrive at Room 308. He acknowledges his relationship with Yoon-a, apologizes for concealing it rather than for choosing her, and tells Taeyeon he intends to keep the women he cares for beside him. When Taeyeon runs out crying, he orders Tiffany to remain and goes after her. His confidence that he can calm Taeyeon has not yet been justified.

In Chapter 307 he fails to catch Taeyeon at the elevators and returns to Room 308 alone. After Tiffany reaches her on speakerphone, he agrees to let Yoon-a attempt a private conversation first. He recruits Tiffany to help propose a surprise concert duet and promises her another song. His second idea involves the two pieces of Papaya of Fuller Grace already in his inventory; neither is given or consumed, and neither production approval nor forgiveness is secured.

His labelmate, duet partner, and the sharpest mind in any room she smiles in. Their chemistry sold a nation on “Some”; what the nation doesn’t know is that the chemistry was never acting. In the aftermath, Ji-eun admits how deeply the hierarchy hurts her, accepts responsibility for the fight, and returns her attention to the album and acting career Ji-ho has planned—without pretending the rivalry is resolved.
