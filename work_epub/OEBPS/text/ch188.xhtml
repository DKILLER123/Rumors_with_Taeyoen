Chapter One Hundred Eighty-Eight

Jessica’s Sleeping Habit

Sunday, May 19, 2013 · morning His bedroom · the Jung sisters’ villa a world gone strangely sharp — and one catastrophic wake-up call

Sunday dawn came up pale over Seoul, thin gold light slipping through the curtain gap. The forecast promised clouds burning off to sun, thirteen to twenty-three degrees, the exact weather engineered for listening to “Spring Day.”

He wasn’t releasing music these days — and it didn’t matter. His catalog ran on the calendar now: “First Snow” at winter’s door, “Miracle in December” in the deep cold, “Spring Day” in spring, “Moonlight” at the first heat of summer. “Some” for the almost-stage, “Strawberry Gummies” for the honeymoon, “If You” and “Eyes, Nose, Lips” for the wreckage. A full year, a full relationship — there was a Ji-ho track fitted to every station of it, and so the charts never quite let his name fall out of the top ten.

Rrrrring~

The alarm broke the quiet. A pale, slender arm emerged from the blankets, patted its way to the nightstand, and silenced the phone with the ease of long practice.

“Mmmn~ so comfortable—”

Taeyeon stretched until her joints crackled softly, opened her eyes—

—and felt, immediately, that the world was different this morning.

Not the light; the curtains hung as always. Not her condition; she’d slept like something recharged in a cradle, body weightless, mood full.

It was the RESOLUTION.

She blinked. Blinked again. Sat up slowly, blankets sliding to her waist.

“Strange…”

She rubbed her eyes. No contacts — she’d taken them out last night; the case sat right there on the nightstand. And yet the world stood in front of her, impossibly crisp.

She turned to audit the room. The succulent on the windowsill: every fat leaf individually legible. The small print on the CD spines across the room: still soft, but no longer the ten-year-familiar smear of a world without lenses.

Her moderate myopia was — receding. Her eyesight was coming back.

The understanding landed, and her hand came up over her mouth, and her eyes went hot in the space of one breath. Only someone who has lived nearsighted knows the size of it: no more groping for glasses as the first act of every morning; no more showering inside a fog; no more gritty, stinging lenses; no more bedtime ritual of putting the world away.

Her family ran an optician’s shop. She knew the science better than most idols knew their fan cafés: axial elongation, refractive power of cornea and lens — structural changes, permanent ones. Adolescent myopia deepens with growth and stabilizes in adulthood. It does not reverse itself.

And yet. She looked at her own two hands, at the dust motes burning in the light, at a world resolving line by line — and the disbelief and the joy arrived together, too big for the bed.

“What’s wrong?”

The voice came from behind her — low, magnetic, still furred with sleep. She turned. He’d woken, lying on his side, head propped on one hand, watching her with open enjoyment.

And for the first time in her life, she saw his face — bare-eyed, at full resolution.

Straight, dense brows that stopped short of severity. Inner-lidded phoenix eyes with that faint upward tilt at the tails, irises deep amber in the morning light. The high, clean line of the nose; the precise mouth; the jaw drawn in one stroke. A face balanced exactly on the border between boy and man.

Her heart skipped a full beat.

“Ji-ho-ya.” Her voice shook with it. “I can SEE you.”

An eyebrow rose, puzzled. “I’m right here. What’s hard to see?”

“Ani—” She jabbed a finger at the untouched lens case. “I’m not WEARING anything! My eyes — my myopia — it’s HEALING!”

Healing? He froze — and then the tumblers clicked. The Boyfriend Halo, level three. “Repair of hidden bodily damage.” Did nearsightedness count as hidden damage? Years of eye strain and abuse — apparently the system thought so.

What am I at this point? A walking elixir?

While he was still processing, she’d already pounced, landing across his chest, eyes blazing at point-blank range.

“It’s you, isn’t it?” she demanded. “The height. Now the eyes. And this — this feeling every single morning, like I’ve been reset. It’s YOU.”

He hesitated one moment longer — and nodded. There was no hiding this from the woman who shared his pillow, least of all this one, whose fine-grained attention missed nothing that mattered. The problem was that he had no explanation to offer.

Confirmation only escalated her. “HOW? How are you doing it?”

“Uh—”

Her hand slid to his waist and delivered a precision pinch to the muscle there. “YAH! You think I’m easy to fool, is that it?!”

He performed a wounded hiss — and then, fatally, nodded again, on reflex, because in certain narrow domains Taeyeon was in fact magnificently easy to fool, a homebody with a trusting operating system and no patience for fine print.

“MWO! YAH!”

The second pinch was real. He countered the only way available: he rolled her under him, blankets avalanching off the bed, morning light landing unobstructed across her — the round shoulders, the fine collarbones, the fair skin — her breath quickening, cheeks flushing, eyes bare of lenses and twice as liquid for it.

And it struck her that this was also a first in reverse: the first time she’d ever seen HIM this clearly. She’d always known the body was good — arms had reported the chest, fingers had walked the abdominal lines — but so often she’d been effectively blind at close range. Now the full resolution arrived at once: the shoulders’ span, the chest’s weight, the tidy machinery of the waist — and above it, that face.

She’d always known she was a face-fanatic. People said it about Tiffany and Jessica — Jessica the nominal one, Tiffany the true believer with impossible standards. Few people knew Taeyeon ran just as deep; she’d carried a grudge about the “ugly groom” episode of We Got Married for YEARS. And now, with the man himself an inch away in high definition—

My Ji-ho is genuinely, unlawfully good-looking.

He kissed her.

And she — kissed him back with only half of herself, because the other half had gone somewhere cold.

Young. Beautiful. Talented. And these miracles — height, eyesight, this glow every morning. What did SHE bring to the table? A somewhat famous girlfriend. Short. Nearsighted — well. Formerly. A twisted, oversensitive temperament. A career he matched and exceeded on every axis: singing, writing, acting, producing. And these gifts of his — they wouldn’t only work on HER, would they? If other women knew… The industry never runs short of beautiful, hungry, twenty-year-old ambition. They’d come like bees to spilled honey. And even without the miracles — that face, that catalog. Could she hold it? Could HE hold, against all of that, a heart that loved only her?

She had no answer. The thought wrapped her chest like wire.

He felt the absence in the kiss. His mouth traveled down the line of her neck to her collarbone — and where she would normally have already wound around him, she lay still, fingers knotted in the sheet.

He stopped. Propped himself up. “Nuna. What’s wrong?”

She shook her head and manufactured a smile. “Nothing… just drifted.”

“Thinking about what?”

She opened her mouth — and the insecurity jammed in her throat, unspeakable. What was the question, exactly? Will someone steal you? Will you only love me? Too small. Too abject. She was Taeyeon — leader of Girls’ Generation, main vocal of the nation’s first girl group. Since when did she do this trembling arithmetic?

But that’s love. The deeper it goes, the more it fears.

He studied her flickering eyes — and seemed to understand something. He didn’t press. He lowered his head instead, and let everything he had answer for him: attention, want, entirety — hers, only hers, at least in this hour, in this light.

And somewhere in the warm blur of it, she made a decision and let the wire loosen. Whatever. Right now he’s mine. Right now, everything he gives belongs to me alone. The future is long. The move isn’t to cling and dread — it’s to become MORE. Good enough to match him. Good enough that his eyes run out of room for anyone else.

Outside, Seoul woke — traffic, birdsong, a far-off construction site assembling the city’s morning score. Inside the small bright room there was only tangled breath, and two heartbeats negotiating themselves into one tempo.

After, she surfaced from the covers — hair wrecked, eye-corners pink, lips glossed with use — looked at him, and abruptly laughed, all the weather cleared out of her face.

“Ji-ho-ya.”

“Mm?”

“I’m going to work hard — and become better.”

He blinked, then caught her meaning, and pulled her in, lips to her forehead. “Pabo. You’re already more than enough.”

“Not enough.” Small, stubborn. “I want MORE than enough.”

A pause. Then, with the prosecutorial instinct she reserved for him alone: “Also — earlier. Why didn’t you kiss me back properly? Are you disgusted with yourself? Or with ME?”

He stiffened. Even a tiger doesn’t— no. There was no winning sentence here.

“Why aren’t you ANSWERING?”

Answer what, exactly? He flipped her small frame over instead.

Very shortly thereafter, the pursuit of answers was abandoned by all parties.

✦   ✦   ✦

The Girls’ Generation dorm. Tiffany woke on schedule — recording day.

She ran the morning like a checklist: at the vanity, makeup applied in fine, deliberate strokes, the standard of a woman intending to be seen. From the closet, a daisy-print wrap tea dress — the V of it cut precisely enough to show the fine collarbones and imply the rest, feminine and quietly lethal.

Bag in hand, she paused at the door. Taeyeon hadn’t come home last night. No mystery there: she was, at this hour, almost certainly folded into Ji-ho’s world, sugar-deep.

Tiffany exhaled once, softly, and drove to Jessica’s.

✦   ✦   ✦

The doorbell chimed; the door opened on Jung Soo-jung’s young, faintly amused face.

“Eonni. Good morning.”

“Morning, Krystal.” Tiffany leaned to peer past her. “Is Sica up?”

“Aniyo.” Krystal stepped aside to let her in.

“Soo-jung-ah~ go wake her for me?”

“Shireo!” The refusal was instant, the head-shake vigorous. “Eonni can do it herself. If I wake her, I get beaten. It’s science.”

Tiffany laughed helplessly. Jessica’s wake-up wrath was a documented natural hazard; nobody provoked the sleeping bear by choice. But the clock was running and the studio was waiting. She squared her shoulders and went in.

Jessica’s room was a full princess build — pink walls, canopied bed, a standing army of plush toys. And in the center of the soft kingdom, its monarch: sprawled bonelessly across the bed, profoundly asleep.

Tiffany crept to the bedside. “Sica~” Nothing. “Sica-yah~” Nothing.

She gritted her teeth, seized the blanket, and hauled it back in one great sweep. “SICA! The sun is on your—”

“AAAH~~!”

The scream was Tiffany’s.

Jessica shot awake mid-scream — and the situation resolved itself in both directions at once.

“YAH — why are you sleeping NAKED?!”

Tiffany stood pointing, scandalized, while her brain unhelpfully catalogued what the morning light was making no secret of: skin like white jade, a waist drawn with a fine brush, legs that went on past reason, proportions of frank, unfair perfection. Jessica — wrath instantly displaced by panic — snatched the blanket back and cocooned herself to the chin, cheeks flooding ripe-peach pink, equal parts fury and mortification.

“YAH! Tiffany! Who enters WITHOUT KNOCKING?!”

“I’m sorry, I’m sorry — we’re LATE, I panicked—”

“You are DEAD today.” A huff; a glare with no real ammunition left in it. “Why are you still STANDING there? OUT. I’m getting dressed.”

Tiffany retreated at speed and shut the door.

Thank god we’re past the debut years, she reflected, hand over her galloping heart. Back then, Sica would have actually killed me. …Also, fine, I sleep that way too when I’m alone. It IS the most comfortable. But still—

She thought of what she’d just seen, and sighed with the deep, wounded honesty of a fellow professional.

That figure is just… genuinely enviable.
