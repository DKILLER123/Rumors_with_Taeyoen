Chapter Two Hundred Twenty-Eight

The Tangled Knot of T-ara

August 2, 2013 A Core Contents practice room · the Girls’ Generation dorm · a private dining room, Seoul a group in the wreckage reads one headline — and its youngest member finally makes the call

The practice room lights burned bright, the mirror wall doubling six slender figures.

Jiyeon had just finished a high-intensity sequence, the fine hairs at her forehead soaked flat, black practice gear tracing the long clean lines of her as her breath came fast and shallow.

“Everyone take a break.”

Park So-yeon clapped her hands, voice warm and worn thin at the same time. She settled onto a floor cushion in the corner, sweat beading along her pale neck.

Since the bullying scandal had detonated, her smiles had grown scarce, and no concealer fully covered the bruised half-moons under her eyes.

“Eonni, let’s go shopping this afternoon, yes?”

Ji-yeon pattered over to Park Hyo-min like an affectionate cat, arms winding around her waist, cheek dropping onto her shoulder, eyes bright with a trace of coaxing.

Hyo-min laughed helplessly and ruffled her damp hair.

“Sure. It’s not like there’s anything else on.”

The words were light. The air in the room still stiffened for a beat.

Everyone knew the weight buried in “nothing else on.” The scandal had collapsed T-ara’s home market entirely — a career at full altitude, cut mid-flight. The company had pivoted them toward Japan, coasting on accumulated goodwill, and Japan hadn’t boycotted the way the peninsula had — but the shadow followed them across the water anyway. Album sales and endorsements had fallen off a cliff. In the members’ own private phrasing: scraping by, not quite starving. Which was why they had so very much free time.

And heavier than the stalled career was the fracture inside the room itself. T-ara’s six-way relationship was, by common industry assessment, the most complicated and most torn of any second-generation group. A lineup stitched together late — two original members leaving before debut, three replacements parachuted in with barely any trainee time. A rotating-captain system that guaranteed no fixed center of gravity. Years under a company whose management style was pressure and threat: perform, or be replaced. Small circles hardened early — Ji-yeon and Hyo-min, the original public faces, closest of all; So-yeon and Eun-jung holding the vocal and leadership core; Bo-ram and Q-ri orbiting the edges. Then the new member came, friction became faction, and when the scandal finally blew, the six of them were pushed out to face a nationwide siege — with their trust in each other already in pieces. Ji-yeon and Hyo-min were fed to the storm first. On camera they still performed unity. Off camera the temperature was near zero.

So the practice room’s response to a shopping invitation was what it always was now: So-yeon straightening her sock hems with her head down, Eun-jung drinking water against the mirror wall, Bo-ram and Q-ri murmuring at the margin, eyes occasionally drifting toward the little duo that still had each other.

Ji-yeon folded down cross-legged, sipping from her bottle, thumb idling up her phone.

Let’s see what today’s news is—

And a headline froze her scrolling finger mid-swipe.

Portal push · entertainment desk

BOMBSHELL: Ji-ho formally takes control of LOEN Entertainment!

“Kh— khack—!”

The water went down the wrong way; she coughed until her pale face flushed scarlet.

“Ji-yeon-ah! Are you okay?”

Hyo-min patted her back quickly, worried.

“F-fine—”

Her hand waved; her eyes never left the screen. That name. Ji-ho. The man who had kissed her out of nowhere after too much wine. The man who’d stolen another kiss at Ji-eun’s front gate and teased her shameless the whole time. The man with — by every rumor — an entire fleet of boats. Had acquired. LOEN. ENTERTAINMENT. Her thumb was unsteady as she opened the article: absolute controlling stake; new chairman; owner of Melon, of IU, of one of the industry’s great platforms. And a paragraph further down: Apink’s smash comeback “NoNoNo” — five music-show wins and counting, the song that had single-handedly reversed the group’s decline — written and produced by the same Ji-ho.

She stared at the phone, expression gone somewhere else entirely. Everything he’d said, that night, half-teasing over the rim of a glass — I have a way to help you. To help T-ara. She’d filed it under charming nonsense. And now— NoNoNo was a five-win comeback. LOEN was his. It had all been TRUE.

The recalibration happened somewhere below thought, the way it does: the liberties he’d taken, the crowded romantic ledger — set against the words LOEN CHAIRMAN, they suddenly read… differently. Understandable, even. Of course a man without extraordinary force couldn’t have drawn women like Taeyeon sunbae and Sulli into orbit. She caught herself thinking it was almost natural for a man like that to be surrounded — caught herself UNDERSTANDING Sulli’s choice, and Taeyeon’s — and didn’t fight it. Because more important than any of that: he had said he could help her. And whether T-ara could be pulled out of the fire was the single most important question in Jiyeon’s life.

She needed the “bully” label burned off. She needed the world to know she’d done nothing wrong. NOTHING. If the six of them had just stood together and exposed the truth first— but the team hadn’t held, and only she and Hyo-min eonni had stepped into the line of fire. Why should SHE carry this? She refused. She would show every last one of them they’d been wrong.

Her fingers had closed hard around the phone, nails printing crescents into her palm. She lifted her head, and something set and bright had come into her eyes.

“Eonni — I just remembered something I need to handle.” She stood, voice not quite steady. “This afternoon… I might not make it after all.”

Hyo-min blinked, then smiled, gentle as ever.

“Arasseo. What is it? Need help?”

“I’m going to meet someone.” She pressed her lips together. “Someone who… might actually be able to help us.”

“Who?”

“You’ll know soon, eonni.”

She grabbed her bag and was gone at a near-run.

At the corridor’s end was a quiet fire stairwell. She pushed through, leaned against the cold wall, and breathed — deep, deliberate, several times. Then she pulled out her phone and found the number she’d saved long ago and never once dialed. Her fingertip hovered over the call key, trembling faintly. Finally she bit down on her lip, and pressed.

Ring. Ring. No answer. Once. Twice.

She had nearly surrendered when the line clicked open.

Voice Call · outgoing — from a fire stairwell

“Yeah, who’s this.” — lazy, faintly impatient, a soft rustling somewhere behind the voice.

“Ji-ho. It’s me.” — softer than she’d planned.

“…Jiyeon?” — surprise, then a distinct warming.

“Mm. Can you talk right now?”

Two seconds of silence on the line. On the far end of it, Ji-ho glanced down at a suspicious ridge in the blankets, gave it a settling pat, and let it carry on.

Voice Call · continued

“I can talk.”

“Tonight— do you have time tonight? I’d… I’d like to buy you dinner.” — teeth catching her lip between the words.

“I’m an extremely busy man these days,” — amusement running under it — “but your invitation, I won’t refuse. Time and place are mine to pick, though.”

“Of course you pick — it has to fit YOUR schedule.” — and the warmth that bloomed in her chest at his yes made no sense at all, and she let that go too. “Thank you, Ji-ho.”

“SSS—!” — a sharp intake of breath.

“What happened??”

“N-nothing. I keep a cat. It just bit me. …Talk tonight.” — the voice suspiciously uneven.

“Okay~”

She lowered the warm phone, and a faint color climbed her cheeks in the cold stairwell light.

✦ ✦ ✦

Same moment. Ji-eun’s villa, the master bedroom.

Ji-ho tossed the phone aside and reached down to catch the “cat” by the scruff.

Ji-eun surfaced from the blankets wearing a smile of maximum provocation and zero remorse. She had, indeed, just bitten him.

“Disobedient kitten. That earns a punishment.”

“YA—!”

The rest of the morning’s discipline went unrecorded.

A long while later he released her and headed for the bathroom, and she lay limp across the mattress watching his back, muttering to the ceiling.

“Is he actually human…”

“Shibal. I’m DEAD.”

Then something surfaced through the fog, and she propped herself up on both arms.

“YA! Ji-ho! Have you set your sights on JI-YEON now?!”

Water started running. His voice floated back, glacially indifferent.

“Not your department, Ji-eun.”

“I— you—” Speechless, she dropped back into the pillows and swore at the ceiling fan. “Bastard. Absolute scoundrel.”

She’d made her peace with following this man — but HONESTLY. Taeyeon was one thing. Now he was working through her own address book, one entry at a time. Peaches: captured. And now Jiyeon had apparently delivered herself to his doorstep. At this rate, what exactly was her friend group becoming — a girls’ circle, or his harem’s waiting room?

✦ ✦ ✦

The Girls’ Generation dorm.

Taeyeon was working through a bowl of cereal at an unhurried pace, morning sun on her bare face making her look like a high schooler who’d wandered into the wrong apartment.

Yoon-a screamed.

“AH!!”

Jessica, directly beside her, nearly left her skin.

“Yoon-a-yah! WHAT!”

“Eonni! Everyone! The news! Ji-ho— he— he—”

Her tongue had tied itself; she settled for shoving the phone screen at the room.

Sunny leaned in and read aloud.

“LOEN Entertainment’s largest shareholder and chairman… Ji-ho? YA! Same name, different person, right?!”

“There’s a photo! THERE’S A PHOTO!”

Yuri piled in. And when the familiar face came up — rendered newly unfamiliar by the suit, the glasses, and a chairman’s gravity that pressed through the screen — the dorm went silent for a full three seconds.

“Eomeo—” Taeyeon snatched the phone, eyes going perfectly round. “He— he was SERIOUS?”

Something moved in Yoon-a’s chest.

“Eonni. He TOLD you?”

Taeyeon stared at the screen for a long beat, then nodded slowly.

“Mm… he called yesterday. Said he’d acquired LOEN. I thought he was joking, so I… hung up on him.”

The dorm regarded her with nine varieties of disbelief.

“Taeyeon-ah — is Ji-ho secretly a chaebol heir?”

Yuri’s question was the whole room’s question.

“Ani.” Taeyeon shook her head. “Ji-ho’s from an ordinary family. When he landed in Korea he had about three million won to his name. Total.”

“Wah… daebak.”

“That’s not possible.”

“How does a person DO that?”

“Acquiring LOEN — what does that even COST? What’s his net worth now?” Yuri ran numbers on her fingers and inhaled sharply. “Doesn’t that make him… almost two Lee Soo-man-nims?”

“MORE than that!” Jessica’s eyes had gone bright. “It’s a different SPECIES of thing! Seonsaeng-nim built and manages a company — this is direct capital acquisition, outright OWNERSHIP! And he’s twenty! TWENTY!”

As the one member who knew about the ₩461.5 billion — courtesy of one accidentally glimpsed bank message — Jessica had been braced for Ji-ho to make a large move. But not THIS large. Not prying LOEN out of SK Group’s hands, with Melon and the distribution channels and the whole apparatus attached.

It was into this tableau that Tiffany emerged from her room, took in the huddle of stunned faces, and let her eye-smile curl.

“Oh? You saw the news?”

“Fany! You KNEW?!”

Yuri seized the salient point instantly.

Tiffany flicked her hair back with a small, luxurious portion of smugness.

“Mmhm~ I heard a LITTLE, beforehand. Impressive, right? Our chingu.”

She weighted “chingu” just enough — and watching the members’ faces climb one more rung of astonishment, her heart quietly sang. Holding the exclusive, and releasing it THIS casually: delicious.

“YA! Tiffany! And you didn’t SAY?!”

Yuri lunged; Tiffany dodged, laughing.

Taeyeon watched her with a complicated face. So Fany had known first. …Of course. With that pride of hers — no wonder she’d been willing to follow him, even knowing about the years between him and me. Her Ji-ho really was too exceptional for anyone’s peace of mind.

Sunny watched Tiffany too, and finally, fully understood the surrender. Twenty. TWENTY. She admired her uncle enormously — and this boy, at twenty, had a fortune that would take nearly two of Lee Soo-man to match.

Yoon-a’s face did something complicated as well. She had no interest in being anyone’s mistress — none. But at this precise moment she understood Tiffany’s choice from the inside. Twenty years old. That face. That frame. That sense of humor. A fortune in the hundreds of billions. Writes songs the way other people write texts — songs that had carried Fany onto Billboard and buried her in endorsements. And now an entire company under him, pointed at the future. What woman’s pulse wouldn’t move? If he were single, she’d have considered chasing him herself. Pity. Taeyeon eonni’s hands had simply been faster.

And so it went, exactly as he’d predicted: when the title LOEN CHAIRMAN came down, every carefully seeded rumor and slander converted instantly to irrelevant static. The public’s attention, the industry’s focus, the media’s every lens — locked onto the drama of the acquisition. And around him, quietly, the private calculus of every woman who knew him shifted another degree: the gravity of what he WAS now outpulled the flaw in what he did.

✦ ✦ ✦

Eight in the evening. A private-kitchen dining room, Seoul.

Jiyeon sat by the window with both hands around a water glass she didn’t need to be holding.

She’d dressed with intent — a pale blue dress against snow-fair skin, long hair in soft waves over her shoulders, the makeup precise. She heard the door and her spine straightened by itself.

Ji-ho came in — plain black shirt, suit trousers, unhurried — and smiled when he saw her.

“Waited long?”

“No— I just got here too.”

She stood too quickly. Her pulse had opinions of its own.

He took the seat across from her; the courses began arriving — refined hanjeongsik, dish after quiet dish — and neither of them ate much of anything.

“You said you could help T-ara.” She got it out at last, eyes fixed on him, direct. “Was it true?”

He poured her a glass, unhurried, and slid it across.

“Answer me one thing first. What is it you actually want? T-ara cleared and active again — or your own success?”

She froze. Her mouth opened. Nothing came out.

“I—”

“No rush.” He raised his glass. “Take your time with it. But know this much: both of those things — I can do.”

He set the glass down, and his eyes when they came up were deep and sure and just faintly dangerous.

“But my help isn’t free, Jiyeon. I’m a businessman. I need to see value — and return.”

She looked at him — this man who made her furious and made her pulse race in the same breath — and felt the floor tilt, gently, under the evening. Because she understood, with total clarity: she was walking toward a crossroads with no way back. And the lamp lighting the road was him.
