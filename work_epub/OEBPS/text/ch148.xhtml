Chapter One Hundred Forty-Eight

Best New Male Artist

The same evening HKCEC — the arena floor greetings, alliances, and one gold envelope

“Wah — Ji-eun, you look SO beautiful today!!” Sulli arrived at a small run, inserting herself between Ji-eun and Ji-ho with surgical precision disguised as enthusiasm.

Ji-eun opened her arms and folded her in.

“Peaches~ you look beautiful too.”

“Really?” From inside the hug, Sulli turned her face up toward him, expectant. “Oppa. What do you think of my styling tonight?”

He gave it a serious top-to-bottom appraisal: the short hair, the hundred-seventy centimeters, the all-black ensemble making her look taller and finer-boned than ever.

“It looks great.”

Her whole face lit.

“But I don’t think it suits you. You should be in something with more life in it.”

The light dimmed; the small mouth pushed into a pout.

“Oh~ noted.”

Ji-eun shot him a glare and moved to console.

“Sulli, don’t listen to him. What does a MAN know about styling?”

“No — I think oppa’s right.” The betrayal was instant and total. “Honestly, I don’t even like black. I like apricot. And red.”

Ji-eun stood there, stunned. Excuse me? I was DEFENDING you. Am I the clown here?

The others were on their feet now for the greeting round. He caught the visibly-not-fine set of Taeyeon’s expression, made a rapid calculation, and walked straight to her — pulling her into a hug and pitching his voice for an audience of exactly one:

“I can smell the vinegar from across the room.”

She went blank for a beat — he DARED, in public? — and then the flush came up, and the knot of sulk in her chest dissolved without her permission.

“AAAAAAH—!!”

A wall of screaming rolled down from the stands: the audience had spotted the hug.

Somewhere in the EXO seats, Byun Baek-hyun turned his head, watched his goddess being held snugly in Ji-ho’s arms, and felt something inside him quietly break into small pieces.

Ji-ho released Taeyeon with perfect naturalness — then hugged Tiffany in turn. Mm. Fragrant. Soft. Quick release, one line left behind:

“Nuna, you look beautiful tonight.”

Then a brief, shallow hug for Seohyun.

To any observer: an SM-raised hoobae, overcome at seeing his sunbaes. Textbook.

✦   ✦   ✦

Then he walked toward the T-ara block.

There was empty space around their seats. Everyone was keeping their distance — the organizers had placed them far back and off-center to begin with. So far, only Girls’ Generation and f(x), plus a handful of second-generation names like Kim Hee-chul and Eunhyuk, had crossed the invisible line to greet them.

Seeing him coming, the members shot to their feet.

“Annyeonghaseyo, sunbae-nims.”

“Ne, Ji-ho-ssi. Annyeonghaseyo.”

Park So-yeon had not expected the hottest artist of the season to walk over — the surprise landed somewhere soft, and she filed the debt away where she kept the permanent ones.

“Your album is incredible,” she said, smiling for real. “We’ve all been listening.”

“Thank you.” His grin came easy. “I love your songs too — my ringtone is ‘Falling U.’”

“Really?” Park Hyo-min looked genuinely startled. She’d bought his album herself.

“Of course. If you don’t believe me, sunbae, call my phone right now and check.”

She waved both hands.

“Aigoo, no no — of course I believe you.”

His shoulders dropped in theatrical defeat.

“Ah. So the phone-number bid fails just like that.”

“…Eh?”

It was hard to reconcile: a woman with a fox’s face, wearing an expression that blank and that adorable at the same time.

He straightened — Taeyeon and Ji-eun were unquestionably watching — and put the sincerity back in his voice.

“The truth is, Ji-yeon and I are good friends.” He glanced at Jiyeon; she went very still. “I believe in you all. If the chance comes later, I’d like us to work together.”

“So — trading numbers would make that easier, wouldn’t it?”

“Much easier.” Hyo-min had her phone out already, openly delighted.

Because here was the calculus: Ji-ho was the hottest name in the industry, and every producer on the peninsula acknowledged the pen. If he ever wrote for T-ara — it might be the rope that pulled the whole group out of the mud.

He exchanged numbers with the rest of them too. The famous visual team earned the title honestly; every one of them was beautiful.

Last, he stopped in front of Jiyeon. Hesitated. Gave her a small nod. Turned to go.

She bit her lip, the call-out rising all the way to her teeth — and swallowed it. And that one anomalous beat did not escape her members.

“What exactly is going on between you and Ji-ho?” Hahm Eun-jung had her by the arm, voice low. “Is there—?”

“Aniya, eonni.” A shake of the head. “He and I… we’ve just had drinks at Ji-eun’s place twice. That’s all.”

“Mm-hm.” Jeon Boram stroked her chin. “Just two rounds of drinks — and he risks THIS to come greet us in public? And trades numbers with the whole team? You’re hiding something.”

The memory of that one accident flared hot under Ji-yeon’s ribs.

“Hiding what! You drink together twice, you’re friends. Obviously. Right, Hyo-min eonni?”

“Ji-yeon’s right,” Hyo-min nodded with total gravity. “One round of drinks doesn’t make a friend.”

“Two rounds means you trust how they hold their liquor. THAT’S a friend.”

Eun-jung wrapped an arm around Ji-yeon.

“Either way — a friend who shows up tonight is a friend worth keeping. Ji-yeon-ah, anyone willing to stand with us in public right now is real.”

“…Mm. I’ll remember, eonni.”

And it was no exaggeration: the gesture had moved all six of them. Right now they were as close to universally condemned as an idol group could be. It was easy to add flowers to brocade — and very, very hard to bring charcoal through the snow. His support tonight would still be remembered ten years on. As for Ji-yeon herself — he had said it out loud: he’d come because SHE was his friend. That part went somewhere deeper.

✦   ✦   ✦

He returned to his seat. Ji-eun leaned in, tone light as a feather and twice as pointed.

“You exchanged numbers with Hyo-min eonni?”

One row over, a pair of small ears rotated into position.

“Mm.” His volume was calibrated to reach exactly two listeners. “Watching them right now… it reminds me of the scandal, back when it was me and Taeyeon nuna.”

“The whole internet swinging at once. Extremists showing up at the door.”

“So — some of it’s sympathy.” A breath. “And besides. T-ara and Girls’ Generation are close, and Ji-yeon is your best friend. Anyone who can be a good friend to the two of you isn’t going to be a bad person.”

Ji-eun smiled to herself. He’d just mentioned the Taeyeon scandal to her face — flat, unbothered, nothing to hide. A guilty man would never. So the Taeyeon side of the ledger was one-way traffic after all: an eonni’s wishful thinking, nothing more. Case closed.

Taeyeon, meanwhile, ran the same sentence through entirely different arithmetic. First: guilt — that scandal had genuinely hurt him, and she owed him a proper apology, and yes, fine, she would buy THAT lingerie set when she got home, the one he would definitely— ahem. Second: joy. “Anyone who can be a good friend to the two of you isn’t going to be a bad person.” He trusted her judgment of people. He’d said so in public, sideways, where only she could hear the real sentence underneath. It was ridiculous how happy that made her.

He exhaled, long and slow. Stabilized. For now. After the ceremony he’d have to find a way in through the IU side and defuse this thing for good — because a bomb with two timers does not stay polite forever.

✦   ✦   ✦

At 18:00, Song Joong-ki opened the ceremony with a tribute performance honoring the late Leslie Cheung, and the hall rose to its feet.

Ji-ho and Ji-eun were already backstage.

“Three, two, one — go.”

They walked the tunnel and rose into the light, and the arena’s roar crashed over them from every side.

MAMA 2012 · opening stage · “Some”

The intro settles the arena to silence. They have done this so many times the timing lives in their bodies now — the circling verses, the almost-but-not-quite choreography of two people negotiating the exact border of more-than-friends.

She reaches out, unscripted, and takes his hand. He glances — one row of seats, one professional smile from a certain leader — and sweats through his own chorus without dropping a note.

Somewhere in Seoul, a certain trainee is watching this hand-hold in 1080p. Joo-hyun-ah. It’s work. It’s all work.

The sweetest love song in the country opens the ceremony, and the hall sings the last phrase back at them in ten thousand voices.

✦   ✦   ✦

The first award: Best New Female Artist — Ailee, by a landslide, on the strength of two runaway hits.

Song of the Year — no suspense whatsoever: Psy, “Gangnam Style.”

And then: Best New Male Artist.

He was nominated for “Some.” The math said it was safe. The heart did not care what the math said — every other award would come around again next year. The rookie award existed exactly once in a lifetime.

“Please welcome the presenter for Best New Male Artist — Han Ga-in-ssi.”

She crossed the stage in an off-shoulder white chiffon blouse over a long black A-line skirt, elegance itself.

“Hello, I’m Han Ga-in. It’s an honor to present this award. Let’s meet tonight’s nominees for Best New Male Artist.”

The screens ran the nominee reel. Cameras swept the floor, splitting the big screen into live reaction boxes: B.A.P grinning gamely; John Park pressing his lips together, boyish; Busker Busker’s frontman closing one fist. And Ji-ho — who, the instant the lens found him, broke into a full-wattage smile that set off a chain reaction of screaming down the fan block.

On stage, Han Ga-in opened the gold envelope. Glanced down. Let the pause stretch, elegant and merciless, while the spotlights prowled between four faces.

“The 2012 Mnet Asian Music Award for Best New Male Artist goes to—”

His breathing stopped somewhere around his collarbone. The arena noise pulled back like a tide, leaving only the heavy, distinct percussion of his own heart.

“—Ji-ho, ‘Some’! Congratulations!”

BEST NEW MALE ARTIST — SONG JI-HO

“Some” (with IU) · presented by Han Ga-in

A rookie award comes once. Exactly once.

The hall went up in one motion.

Applause, cheering, the fan zone erupting in a single sustained shriek. For one blank instant his mind held nothing at all; his body stood up on its own.

Ji-eun turned, radiant, and gave him a hug with actual force in it, words rushed against his ear:

“Congratulations!”

Then Taeyeon and Tiffany were up and hugging him in turn, and the aisle opened in front of him.

Walking to the stage, Absolute Focus and Star Presence hit some strange resonance — the black suit seemed to drink every stray photon in the arena and return it as something quieter and his own. Ten thousand pairs of eyes tracked one man up a staircase.

He took the strange-angled, satisfying weight of the trophy from Han Ga-in’s hands.

“Congratulations, Ji-ho-ssi.” Warmth in it, and open appraisal.

“Thank you, sunbae-nim.”

He bowed, turned to the microphone, and faced the sea of light.

Below: Ji-eun, face tilted up, clapping hard, eyes shining. Taeyeon, smiling wider than he was. Sulli, beaming. Tiffany, applauding with her whole chest.

“Mm—” The sound system carried the first syllable out dry; the rest arrived steady. “Honestly, standing here, holding this… it feels—” A pause, searching. “It feels slightly unreal.”

Kind laughter, warm applause.

“Thank you to Mnet, and to every fan who voted, who streamed, who showed up. Without you there is no ‘Some’ — and there is no version of me standing here.”

“Thank you to my company, and to everyone who worked on the song. And especially to my partner — IU-ssi.” He found the white figure in the dark. “Working beside someone that talented, who works that hard, is a rare kind of luck.”

She nodded hard, hand pressed over her mouth.

“And thank you to my producing seonsaeng-nim — for the vocal craft, and for everything this song taught me.”

One row down, a certain leader’s smile went brighter by two full stops.

“A rookie award comes once. Exactly once. I’m grateful for it, and a little afraid of it — because this isn’t a finish line. It’s a starting block.” He lifted his eyes into the main camera. “From here, better music. I won’t waste this luck — or the eyes of anyone watching.”

He raised the trophy and bowed, deep, to the floor and to every lens in the building.

“Thank you!”

The wave that came back nearly took him off his feet.

✦   ✦   ✦

The ceremony rolled on — and soon arrived at Best Collaboration. He and IU were nominated for “Some.”

Against one genuinely dangerous opponent: HyunA and Jang Hyun-seung’s “Trouble Maker.” Both songs were monsters. “Some” owned the charts; “Trouble Maker” owned the stage, and had released earlier. The votes were said to be nearly dead even — and all four nominees sat very straight in their seats.

“The 2012 Mnet Asian Music Award for Best Collaboration—”

“—Ji-ho and IU, ‘Some’! Congratulations!”

2012 Mnet Asian Music Awards · Hong Kong

❦ ❦ ❦

BEST COLLABORATION — SONG JI-HO × IU

“Some” · over “Trouble Maker” (HyunA & Jang Hyun-seung)

Two trophies, one night — and the evening’s arithmetic not yet done.

The fan zone detonated all over again.

In the end, the bigger combined fandom carried the audience vote, and the chart dominance carried the rest. HyunA and Jang Hyun-seung wore their disappointment for exactly one camera frame, then packed it away with professional grace and crossed the aisle to congratulate them.

“Trouble Maker” was ferocious. It had simply met “Some” — the way “Some” itself, in the Song of the Year bracket, had met “Gangnam Style.” There are years when losing is its own kind of honor.
