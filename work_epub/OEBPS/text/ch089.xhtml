Chapter Eighty-Nine

Tension, and Thrill

Ji-ho caught it — that one-beat silence, the micro-shift in Taeyeon’s expression.

Something moved in his chest. He kept his smile warm, faintly bashful, and answered Tiffany’s praise first.

“Tiffany nuna’s being too generous. Honestly, a lot of these ideas came out of our conversations — you inspired half of it.”

A compliment returned — and, in the same stroke, the whole of his history with Tiffany filed neatly under musical inspiration.

Then, with perfect naturalness, he turned to Taeyeon — eyes settling on her, voice carrying that particular blend of reliance and shared delight he saved for her.

“What does Taeyeon nuna think? Especially the tempo shift after the hook, the style pivot — I keep feeling it’s one degree short of right. Say you were the one singing it. How does it sit, from the singer’s side?”

The question was addressed to her alone, and it pulled her straight back into the center of the room. The small knot in her chest loosened by half.

She replayed the melody carefully, and answered as a professional.

“It’s stunning. The section-to-section splicing is genuinely bold, but the hooks are unforgettable. It’d be a real challenge to sing — and real room to perform.”

She paused. Her eyes slid to the project title on the screen, and the next question came out half tease, half probe.

“These lyrics, though… they all look strangely familiar. They sound like things I’ve said. Or things I’ve heard someone say…”

Ji-ho laughed.

“Not ‘like.’ Accurately speaking — they’re you. All of you.”

“What?” Two voices, perfect unison.

“Part of the inspiration came from… mm, that atmosphere when nunas get together — loud and private at the same time. The fashion talk, the little dating complaints, the whole ‘I’ll be exactly myself, thanks’ attitude.”

He explained it smoothly, steering wide of any source that might feed Taeyeon’s vinegar another drop.

“I think this retro-electro-hip-hop pop hybrid, with the layered vocals and all the sound design — it’s built for exactly the kind of turn Girls’ Generation could take next. It would show off how differently each of your voices can hit.”

“If this song someday went to nuna and Girls’ Generation… that would be the dream.”

The sour residue in Taeyeon’s chest washed out under a wave of pure delight. A song this good — tailor-made — written by him — and he wanted it in her hands, her team’s hands.

It wasn’t just professional respect. It was closeness. It was keeping her in mind.

Tiffany seized Taeyeon’s arm, vibrating.

“Taeyeon! Did you HEAR that! This song is US! Wah — Ji-ho, you are… the absolute best!”

Her opinion of him climbed another sheer cliff face. The nuna he means — that’s me, isn’t it? We’re the ones always talking music. Some of those lyrics are literally my complaints, word for word. He took all that inspiration from me and built THIS out of it.

First the face pulls you in. Then the talent finishes you off.

Objectively: perfect partner material.

The production room warmed by degrees. Three heads clustered at the screen, dissecting the track bar by bar; Taeyeon and Tiffany couldn’t resist humming along with the demo’s spikier phrases, trading lines, laughing when they collided.

Ji-ho watched the two of them burrow into the arrangement, and let out a breath only he could hear.

Threading between half-spoken ambiguity, honest admiration, and naked ambition — it was walking on lake ice in spring. It was also, he had to admit, weirdly exhilarating.

The road is long. But I’m holding real tickets now — hers, and hers. And the future I’m scoring has both of them in it.

✦   ✦   ✦

Music talk devours clocks. None of the three noticed the hour until Tiffany’s phone shattered the bubble.

She fished it out, reluctant — then saw the name.

“Oh — looks like our maknae’s done for the night.”

Incoming · Seohyun

“Hello, Seohyun-ah?”

“Me? I’m in the production room.”

“Really? Got it — I’m coming now.”

Taeyeon watched her hang up, and slid one earphone off.

“What’s going on?”

“No idea.” Tiffany shrugged. “Seohyun wants me in the conference room.”

“DaeDae, come on, let’s go.”

Taeyeon’s eyes made the briefest detour past Ji-ho. She scratched the tip of her nose and produced two small, unconvincing coughs.

“Um. Did Seohyun ask for me?”

“Nope.”

“Then I’ll pass.” She held up the headphones as evidence. “I still want to listen to the songs.”

“Fine, fine — I’ll go see what it is.”

Tiffany didn’t think twice. She loved the track, she’d just banked a folder of new arranging tricks, and honestly she was more reluctant to leave than Taeyeon looked. Out she went.

The door clicked. The production room went very quiet.

A long moment. Then Taeyeon broke the silence, too casually.

“You… and Tiffany. You talk a lot?”

There it is. Micro-expression control was a beautiful thing; his face produced an easy, sunny smile on command.

“Mm. Tiffany nuna’s really into arranging.”

“It’s just…” He let his shoulders drop, rubbed his temple, sighed the sigh of the long-suffering. “I finally understand the specific pain of being somebody’s teacher.”

“Pfft—” The laugh escaped before she could stop it; she slammed the stern face back on a half-second too late. “Studying arrangement together. A shared hobby. Must give you SO much to talk about.”

He didn’t answer. His gaze settled on her face instead and stayed there — tracing, inch by inch, from the fine-boiled precision of her brows and eyes, down to the aggrieved press of her lips, to the jawline that had gone a little thinner than he remembered.

She began to squirm under it.

“What— why are you looking at me like that?”

He broke into a slow smile.

“So this is what jealousy looks like.”

“Mwo?!” Scarlet, instantly, to the hairline. “Who— who’s jealous! I— I—”

“You’ve lost weight, nuna.”

Four words. They went through Taeyeon’s heart like an arrow through paper.

Her eyes flooded red on the spot; her nose stung; she bit down hard on her lower lip and held on.

He came toward her slowly. She retreated step for step — until her back met the wall, and there was nowhere left to go.

She tipped her chin up at him. His hands rose and cradled her face.

The room was quiet enough to hear both sets of breathing go ragged; quiet enough to hear two heartbeats syncing into one low drumline; close enough to see half a month of missing each other surfacing in both pairs of eyes.

He bent, unhurried, and kissed the high line of her nose.

Then lower — onto the soft warmth of her lips.

Not a brush-and-retreat. A slow, patient, city-taking siege.

Strawberry, faintly — her lip balm, warmed through. He decided he liked it very much.

The hands that had cradled her face slid down along her back and settled at the soft narrow curve of her waist — once, again, again — as if half a month’s absence were a debt that could be repaid by touch alone.

Eyes closed, up on her toes, arms wound around the back of his neck, she paid it back with everything she had.

Tak. Tak. Tak. Tak.

Heels. Approaching.

The haze in Taeyeon’s eyes snapped clear and she shoved at his chest — but his arms only tightened, and his mouth refused entirely to negotiate.

The footsteps grew louder. Tiffany’s laugh, and Seohyun’s answering giggle, now clearly audible through the wall.

Terror. Thrill. Terror and thrill — Taeyeon’s heart was slamming against her ribs like it meant to escape, and all she could do was beat her small fists against him, frantic, silent, melting.
