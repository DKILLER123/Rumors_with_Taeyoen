Chapter Thirty-Three

Just Kidding

“January ’94.”

“Really?!” Sulli’s whole face kindled. “I’m March ’94! Then from now on you’re Ji-ho oppa — let’s be chingu!”

“Deal. Sulli-yah.”

Then her expression turned suddenly grave. She bent into a full ninety-degree bow, voice ringing earnest.

“Ji-ho oppa. Thank you. Really — really, truly, thank you. If it hadn’t been you, last night I— I can’t even let myself imagine what—”

A shudder ran visibly through her; her hands had gone cold just constructing the sentence. What that man had planned — what her life would have become on the far side of it — was a pit with no bottom, and she’d stood on its exact edge without knowing.

Ji-ho looked at that clear, earnest face and deliberately punctured the gravity.

“You SHOULD thank me properly. Here’s my price: one hundred million won a year, every year, until you retire at sixty. Deal?”

“Huh?” She blinked — then actually thought about it, brows knitting with real arithmetic — then nodded hard. “Okay. I promise.”

“You AGREED?! I was joking!” The laugh escaped before he could stop it.

“So was I~” She stuck out her tongue, impish, and the two of them cracked up together — and just like that, the air went light.

Delighted, she hooked his arm and towed him inside, setting the basket down and presenting him to the room like a prize exhibit.

“Everyone — this is Ji-ho. The one who saved me.”

“Hello, sunbae-nims. I’m Ji-ho.”

“Aigoo—!” Goo Hara was up and across the room before anyone else moved, seizing his hand in both of hers. “What ‘sunbae’! We’re keeping you — it’s nuna from here. Thank you. THANK you for saving our Sulli.”

Hara and Sulli were sisters in everything but paperwork — years of shared history knotted tight. The news last night had scared her badly enough to torch her entire morning schedule and race to the hospital. Her gratitude had no performance in it at all.

And looking at her, Ji-ho felt an ache he had to work to keep off his face. This one too. The broken home. The wrong man, and the damage he’d done. And in the timeline Ji-ho remembered — after Sulli, only weeks after Sulli — Hara following her friend down the same terrible road. He sighed somewhere deep where it wouldn’t show, and kept his smile warm.

“Ne, Hara nuna.”

IU stepped up next, bowing with that soft-spoken gravity of hers.

“Hello. I’m IU. Thank you for saving our peach. Truly.”

“IU sunbae-nim — please, none of that.” He half-raised his hands to wave the bow away.

She straightened and offered a small pale hand, smiling.

“And none of the formal speech either. Let’s just be comfortable.”

“Ji-eun nuna. Hello.”

At the sound of her real name she tilted her head, considering — and let it stand.

Then Victoria came forward — and greeted him in careful, warmly accented English.

“Ji-ho. Hello. Thank you for saving Sulli. Call me Qian-jie — like a big sister, yes? We foreigners in this company — we take care of each other. Anything you need here, you come find me.”

As f(x)’s leader — a full seven years older than Sulli and Krystal — Victoria mothered her members like a hen with two chicks, and last night’s news had frightened her badly. Her relief had converted directly into fierce goodwill toward the boy responsible. And toward a fellow foreigner grinding it out far from home, the sympathy came doubled — she’d walked that road from the other direction.

“Qian-jie. Then let’s trade contacts?” He had his phone out already, smiling.

“Good, good!”

KakaoTalk added, numbers exchanged.

“V-mom, why the English all of a sudden?” Krystal wrinkled her nose — then unwrinkled it, worked out the answer herself, and switched lanes without signaling, sticking out her hand with pure West Coast ease. “Wait — you’re the LA kid, right? San Francisco.” A thumb at her own collarbone. “We’re the same age too. Thanks for saving Sulli. Just call me Krystal.”

“Krystal. Hey.” He shook it — and noted her hand ran exactly as cool as her face.

Amber and Luna followed with warm, earnest thanks of their own. With this many people wrapped around Sulli, his job here was clearly done; he sat a while for courtesy, then rose to take his leave. Sulli walked him to the door.

“Ji-ho oppa. You…” The sentence stalled; something flickered behind her eyes and retreated.

“I what?”

“…Nothing.” A small shake of the head. “Get home safe.”

“Anything comes up — I’m one Kakao away. Okay?”

“Mm!” The nod she gave him used her whole body.

✦   ✦   ✦

Home again, with no company schedule to answer to, Ji-ho sat down and went mining — dredging his memory for the classics, every future hit he could still hum.

As a working lounge singer he could perform practically all of them; writing out lyrics was trivial. The wall he hit was arrangement. Melody writing, harmonic structure, song form, instrumentation and production — a vast interlocking body of craft he’d never once studied systematically. He could hear the finished records in his skull with perfect fidelity and could not, for the life of him, get them onto paper whole. The company’s classes touched the subject, but only the shallow end.

So he did what he could: filled page after page with song titles from memory, a treasure map to be excavated later — and resolved to attack arranging theory properly, so his catalog wouldn’t live or die on system rewards alone.

He was deep in the list when the phone rang.

Incoming call · Wendy

“Hey, Seung-wan-ah.”

“Great Hero-nim. Where are you right now?”

“Me? Home. Awaiting further orders from the company.”

“So when are you coming back to practice?”

“No idea — company’s call. Why? Miss me?”

“Die!! I’m CALLING to ask what happened last night! The recording — was that when you hung up on me?? It was, wasn’t it!”

“Confidentiality agreement. No comment.”

“YAH! Ji-ho!” — and then the voice went soft and syrupy, deploying full aegyo — “Pleeease? If I don’t find out I won’t sleep a WINK tonight. Mmm~ please? Pretty please?”

“Call me oppa and I’ll leak you one — ONE — tiny detail.”

“Yah!” A pause. A struggle, audible. Curiosity winning. “…Oppa~?”

“Kidding. Not telling you anyway.”

“YAH!! AISH— SONG—”

— call ended · line muted —

Across the city in the practice building, Joy and Seulgi leaned in with faces full of hope, and even Irene’s ear had drifted noticeably in the call’s direction.

“Well?? What did he say?”

Seung-wan lowered the phone with the expression of a woman replotting a murder.

“That absolute RAT played me! Tricked me into calling him oppa and then HUNG UP!”

She redialed. No answer. Redialed. No answer. Three more times. Nothing.

“SONG JI-HO!!! AAAGH!! Unbelievable!!”

“HAHAHAHAHA—”

Irene laughed until she had to hold the mirror bar, watching Seung-wan hop in place like a furious sparrow.
