✦   ✦   ✦

November wind stripped the last ginkgo leaves; Seoul crossed formally into late autumn. The plane dropped through lead-grey cloud into Incheon, and Ji-ho woke from a shallow nap to the cabin announcement: ground temperature, seven degrees; please dress warmly. The south’s lingering warmth was a week behind him; he shrugged into the dark-grey coat and walked down the bridge into the cold.

Arrivals was unexpectedly loud — dozens of fans with banners and cameras, whether from a leaked itinerary or simply the gravity of a rising name.

“Ji-ho oppa! Welcome BACK!”

“You worked hard!”

“We’re ready for the album!!”

He stopped, pulled the mask down, and bowed to them properly — then moved on Dong-hwan’s arm through to the VIP lane, turning twice to wave.

In the car, heater at full, hot americano in hand:

“Tonight — IU’s tour finale. Olympic Hall, Songpa-gu. You’re the special guest for the duet. Rehearsal’s at four; we’re driving straight there. Wardrobe’s already delivered.”

He nodded, watching the thinning trees strobe past under a wet grey-blue sky. Classic Seoul, late in the year.

✦   ✦   ✦
4:00 PM Olympic Hall — Rehearsal Real Fantasy · the encore, Seoul

Staff crossed the floor at speed; radios crackled in relay. He came in from the wings mid-band-rehearsal: IU center stage in an oversized grey hoodie and track pants, hair up in an afterthought, negotiating a chord voicing with her music director.

She heard footsteps, turned — and the smile that broke across her face was instant, total, and unbudgeted. She hopped down the stage steps and came at a half-run, braking hard at the last two meters into something more composed, eyes still crescented.

“You’re back. Smooth trip?”

“Very smooth.” He took her in — hoodie collar too big, collarbones showing, faint tiredness under the eyes, spirits visibly high. “And you? Final show. Ready?”

“Everything’s done that can be done. Now it’s condition, and details.” She steered him stage-ward with easy proprietorship. “PD-nim — the evening’s blocking, please—”

She listened to the walkthrough with total focus — and every time he spoke to confirm or suggest, her head turned to him first, eyes bright, before the director had finished responding. In the breaks she sipped honey water from a thermos and tracked him across the floor while he talked band arrangements.

The run-through: their duet, re-arranged for the occasion — a cello line added under the intro, deepening all that ambiguity into something closer to ache. At the held harmony they crossed to each other on the blocking marks and joined hands; the stone-faced music director was nodding before the bridge.

“Good. Exactly that feeling.” The director closed his binder, satisfied. “Tonight, let the interaction breathe — the audience lives for it. Eyes, expressions, small gestures. Don’t design it. Just let it happen.”

She nodded, ears faintly pink, shot him one glance, and looked away fast.

✦   ✦   ✦

By seven the plaza outside had become a lake of light — fluorescent yellow support sticks in a slow current toward the doors, breath steaming, nobody’s enthusiasm dented one degree by the cold.

Backstage: her first costume, a silver dress sewn dense with hand-set crystal, starfield-grade; eyes closed under the finishing brush, hands flat on her knees, breathing deliberately. One room over: black knit, clean-cut black trousers, a stylist adjusting the fall of his fringe by millimeters.

“Fifteen to doors,” Dong-hwan murmured. “She opens; you’re on for the final song.”

Eight PM. Total dark. A tidal roar — cut off clean by one deep drum hit. A single spot; a silver figure inside it.

The concert began.

✦   ✦   ✦

Two and a half hours later, her hairline was soaked, her voice carried the fine sanded edge of a night’s full spend — and her eyes were brighter than any rig in the building. The last song before the final encore ended; the hall chanted her name in one voice; her eyes went red on the spot.

“Truly— thank you. All of you. For walking to the very last night of this with me.” The mic hand was unsteady; the smile held. “Tonight — I want to call out a special friend. He landed today, from very far away… and gave his first evening back to my last stage—”

A beat. Her gaze went to the wings, and the smile turned up all the way.

“My good friend — and someone a great many of you love — Ji-ho.”

Performance

The spot slams to stage left. He walks out of the dark, black on black, and the hall’s roar breaks its own ceiling — the big screen catches the face at full resolution and the sound spikes again.

They meet at center. The duet’s intro pours out over the new cello line, and his first phrase — that low, grained warmth over the top — smooths the entire arena flat in four bars, pulling ten thousand people into the song’s held breath. Her answering line arrives clear and soft; their eyes weave.

At the confession-adjacent line, the blocking brings them together; she reaches; he takes her hand. The rig drops to deep blue-violet, a spill of light-specks like a poured galaxy.

The chorus, face to face: her chin tilted up, his head inclined, two voices fusing on the knife’s-edge of friends-and-more — precise, and true enough to hurt.

Then, in the bridge — unrehearsed — on the line begging the other person to stop locking her out: she releases his hand and touches one fingertip, feather-light, to the center of his chest. He blinks — then shakes his head, helpless, smiling. The hall detonates.

The final phrase they take nearly a cappella, air braided, the last note asking — as the song always asks — for the words themselves. It dissolves. One heartbeat of absolute silence.

Then thunder.

She bowed deep, and the tears finally came down. He bent beside her. The lightstick sea blazed and would not dim.

✦   ✦   ✦

Backstage flooded — hugs, applause, flowers; she was mobbed at the center, laughing and crying and repeating thank you, thank you as a whole tour’s weight left her body at once. He watched from the edge of it, quiet, letting the moment be hers.

When the crowd loosened, she extracted herself and came straight to him — eyes swollen, a bouquet of pale pink roses clutched against the crystal dress.

“Thank you, Ji-ho.” The voice was completely gone, and completely clear. “The final show — with you in it — was… perfect.”

“You made it perfect yourself.” He looked down at her. “Congratulations, Ji-eun. First tour — closed in full glory.”

She stepped in without warning, pressed the roses into his arms, and hugged him — brief, and complete: sweat, tears, and flowers all in one.

Then she stepped back, tipped her face up, and delivered the closing line of the night with her eyes still shining:

“Your album goes live November 7th.”

“When it does — everyone will finally see what you are.”

“So don’t forget. I’m still waiting for your answer.”

✦   ✦   ✦

He didn’t want to be handed up to. He wanted to arrive level — and she found, to her own annoyance, that something in her was delighted by it.
