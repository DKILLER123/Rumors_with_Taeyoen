Chapter One Hundred Twenty

Running Man — The X-Man Special (Finale)

Lunch followed the first game — this era’s Running Man had budget to burn, and the spread showed it. Under head PD Myuk PD’s famously brisk pacing, the second mission ran right there in the restaurant: a six-round relay of classic Korean variety mini-games.

Ji-ho, who had grown up on LA radio and American cable rather than Sunday-night Korean television, knew almost none of them — and played like it. Badly. Suspiciously badly.

“…He’s the X-Man. He HAS to be the X-Man.”

Which had him sweating for entirely accurate reasons.

Haha — small, quick, and fluent in every game ever devised — took the round, and the trade reshuffled the board:

Teams · After Mission 2

BLUE IU · Kim Jong-kook · Ji Suk-jin

YELLOW Song Ji-hyo · Ji-ho · Haha

RED Yoo Jae-suk · Lee Kwang-soo · Gary

Ninety minutes of break. The cameras capped, and everyone molted out of broadcast mode into civilian selves — the gap always instructive. Ji Suk-jin, henpecked elder on-air, was off-air a great sunbae nobody addressed carelessly. Jae-suk sat with the PDs in dead earnest, dissecting the morning’s footage. Ji-hyo curled in a corner with her phone. Haha called his girlfriend.

Ji-ho and IU rose at the same moment, collected toiletry kits from their managers, and met at the restroom sinks. Star management, lesson one: teeth, after every meal, without exception.

She gathered her hair back and asked, off-hand:

“How’s it feel so far?”

“The show earns its name.” He loaded the brush. “The cardio is real.”

“This is the WARM-UP. Afternoon’s worse.” She rinsed, arched one tidy eyebrow. “By the way — the car bit at the open. Excellent work.”

“So. Was that research…” her white teeth caught her lower lip, “…or have you been paying attention to me?”

The pure face; the loaded gesture. A whole education in contrast.

He glanced over and declined the bait — by returning it.

“Which would you prefer it to be?”

Won’t answer, won’t refuse to answer, kicks the ball straight back. Scoundrel or villain — no third option.

“Oh? Do my preferences matter to you now?” Her eyes did a slow shimmer.

He appeared to weigh it as a genuinely hard problem.

“Matter? Let’s not overstate. It’s like asking a detective how he cracks cases. Is it because he studied every file in advance — or because, from the very beginning, he was paying particular attention to one particular person?”

“The truth usually has more than one floor. So let’s save that answer… for when we know each other better.”

And he brushed his teeth with great diligence.

A real opponent, she thought, rinsing. Finally. Much more fun this way.

Four PM. Mission three: Soraepogu Station.

Mission 3 · Subway Survival
All teams ride together. At each station, one representative per team disembarks and duels. Round 1: palm-or-back. Loser stays behind. Survivors ride on; Round 2: rock-paper-scissors at the next station. After three rounds, the team with the most members at the final station wins.

First station, first duel: IU, Ji-ho, Jae-suk stepped off. No time to waste—

“Palm or BACK—!”

Ji-ho: palm. Jae-suk and IU: backs.

Eliminated. First. He stood on the platform wearing the face of a man renouncing all worldly attachment while IU cackled, bounced twice, and skipped back onto the train.

The platform crowd kept a polite documentary distance — variety shoots were street furniture in this country — and he bowed around amiably. One lively high-schooler waved.

“Oppa — eliminated FIRST?”

“Ahem. …Ne. Eliminated.” A beat, then, with dignity: “Palm-or-back is pure luck. The luck was simply not there.”

The visible reach of the excuse set the whole platform laughing.

“Next train’s coming — yeoreobun, bye-bye.”

“Bye~!”

He boarded, nursing one hope: find IU stranded at the next station, and return every ounce of the mockery. At Hogupo Station, a figure in red waited on the platform — Jae-suk.

Ah. No revenge today.

Jae-suk boarded; their eyes met; two grown men smiled through the silence.

“Ji-ho-ya.” A topic was located. “You’re not the X-Man… right?”

“Of course not, Jae-suk hyung.” — with a face of shining, cloudless sincerity, courtesy of Micro-Expression Control.

“…Certain?” Eyes narrowing, scanning.

“Really not me.” Beat. “Hyung. It’s not YOU, is it?”

“Aniya. Ji-ho-ya, it’s not hyung.”

“Really?”

“Of course.”

“Then I’ll trust hyung.”

They rode on. And when Jae-suk’s attention drifted, Ji-ho quietly crossed two fingers into a small X at his side — the second of the mandated signals, made.

The final station: Songdo. Alone on the platform, toeing the ground in boredom: IU. She spotted his face through the glass and lit up with a grin exactly as radiant as it was smug, adding a jaunty little wave.

“To think — NUNA won,” he marveled, stepping off.

“Ahh~ what a pity, Ji-ho-ssi. How WAS it, being eliminated first? Was the platform audience warm and supportive?” Eyes crescented to pure mischief.

“Final station, only Ji-eun remains — BLUE TEAM WINS!” Jae-suk applauded. “Congratulations — Blue receives the key hint regarding the X-Man!”

The Blue three huddled over the card Myuk PD delivered.

Hint · For Mission 3 Winners
X-MAN CLUE: “TEAM B.”

Kim Jong-kook, detective engine at full rev: “Team B… I’ve GOT it. The X-Man doesn’t need to survive — he needs to END UP TEAMED with whoever ‘B’ points to.”

And then the tiger reasoned his way, majestically, into the trap. The X-Man’s target partner is probably a guest — Ji-ho or IU. I’m clean. Ji-eun’s been natural throughout — not her. And Ji-ho? Played game one at full burn. Fumbled game two, sure — but the kid’s KOREAN-AMERICAN, raised in LA; of course he doesn’t know our variety games. Game three was pure luck. Besides, would production really hang the key role on a first-timer? No — the X-Man is a MEMBER. Kwang-soo or Jae-suk. Highest probability.

He pulled IU aside, low-voiced.

“Ji-eun-ah. We trade Suk-jin hyung for Ji-ho.”

“Why?” Big eyes, blinking.

“I’m not the X-Man. If the X-Man needs to team with you or Ji-ho to win — then with the three of us together, the X-Man CANNOT win. Airtight.”

“Ooh. Then I’ll follow Jong-kook oppa’s plan.” — with a face of perfect trusting innocence.

And so the fortress invited the spy inside, on the strength of the fortress’s own logic.

The buses rolled to the final ground — Incheon Park — where several hundred citizens ringed the central lawn. On the grass lay a colossal triangular tug-of-war rope, its three arms ending in red, yellow, and blue zones.

Final Mission · Triangle Tug-of-War
Each team recruits 22 citizens to form a squad of 25. Three-way pull. First team to ring the bell behind their own end wins the round. Best of three.

The recruiting stampede began: young men flooding toward Blue shouting IU’s name; flushed girls giggling their way behind Ji-ho; a healthy contingent of Ace Ji-hyo devotees massing at Yellow; and Jae-suk’s national-MC gravity filling out Red. Three roaring, evenly matched columns.

“BLUE TEAM! FIGHTING!” Jong-kook pounded his chest, muscles doing half the recruiting on their own.

“YELLOW! VICTORY!” Ji-hyo tied her hair back, blank-Ji-hyo nowhere in evidence, eyes gone sharp.

“RED! HOOOH!!!” Jae-suk led; Kwang-soo and Gary supplied the animal noises.

Ji-ho was posted at Blue’s rope-end — bell-ringer. He rolled his wrists, glanced at his counterparts — Haha at Yellow’s tail, Kwang-soo at Red’s — and felt his pulse tick up.

The assignment: lose. But not visibly.

Round one. “ONE! TWO! PULL!!” The rope snapped taut; three forces ground against each other; the center marker shivered in deadlock for nearly a full minute, sweat blooming down every line. From the tail, Ji-ho felt the surges come through the rope like sets of waves. Mid-line, IU hauled backward with her whole small frame, face scarlet.

Then Yellow found a rhythm — one clean shout from Ji-hyo — and the marker lurched their way. “NOW! PULL!!” Haha timed it, Yellow heaved in phase, and the bell sang out over the park. Yellow’s column erupted. Blue sat down in the grass, gasping; IU caught her breath and rallied everyone anyway — “It’s fine, it’s FINE! Next round is ours!”

And at the back, hidden in the crowd, the saboteur murmured a silent apology. With me anchoring — we cannot win.

Round two. Red came back possessed — new strategy or wounded pride, the rope groaning, the bell swinging within reach of three teams in turn. And at the critical moment Lee Kwang-soo — breakthrough or slipped footing, science will never say — let out a strangled war-cry, lunged backward with one giant arm—

DING-A-LING—!!

Red’s bell. Blue and Yellow howled in anguish; Jong-kook blew his fringe off his forehead in disgust; and Ji-ho arranged his face into devastated and exhaled, privately, in deep relief. Mission integrity: maintained.

Round three. Decider. Blood in every eye, the citizens’ roar threatening the tree line. Yellow’s cohesion was frightening now; the round stretched long, arms burning — Ji-ho’s included, genuinely. The bell wavered between Blue and Yellow. One full-force lunge from him, right now, might have reached it.

He did not lunge.

And once the round became a grinding war of attrition, Yellow’s heavier roster told. The rope crept their way.

“JI-HYO! HAHA!” Suk-jin bellowed.

DING-A-LING—!!!

Haha’s fingertips found the bell on the last of his strength. Yellow — two rounds, match, and victory. Their column detonated; Blue and Red collapsed where they stood. IU sat straight down on the grass, hair wrecked, pulled a small face at the celebrating Yellow — then hauled her smile back up and high-fived Jong-kook and Ji-ho.

“Everyone worked SO hard! We really did our best!”

“So close at the end. SO close.”

“Right,” Ji-ho agreed, wearing a tired, regretful smile. “So close.”

And while every eye followed Yellow’s victory lap, he made — swiftly, invisibly — his final X.

Yellow’s prize: the last hint — “MUSIC.” Which fit Gary, Ji-ho, IU, Jong-kook, and Haha. Ji-hyo and Suk-jin deliberated, and spent their trade swapping Haha out for Jae-suk.

The Final Judgment. The tug-of-war’s last-place team — IU, Kim Jong-kook, Ji-ho — mounted the isolated judgment platform under converging lights.

Final Judgment · The X-Man Revealed

Myuk PD: “Today’s first hint — ‘TEAM B.’ The B refers to the keyboard key B. On a Korean keyboard, the B key types the vowel ㅠ — read: ‘yu.’ IU.”

“If the X-Man ends the day on IU’s team… the X-Man wins ALONE.”

■ NOT THE X-MAN ■

JUDGMENT — IU:

■ NOT THE X-MAN ■

JUDGMENT — SONG JI-HO:

✖ X - M A N ✖

“MWO?!” “For REAL?!” “WAAAH!!!”

The clearing went up. IU’s eyes flew wide; both hands came up over her mouth; she stared at him in flat disbelief. And Ji-ho’s face split into the unfiltered grin of a winner, both arms thrown skyward.

“YES! It WORKED!”

“YAH! SONG JI-HO!!!” Jong-kook, torn between fury and laughter, jabbed a finger at him. “So it was YOU! No WONDER the tug-of-war kept coming up just short! I kept thinking — our power line is NOT this weak!”

Ji-ho, beaming, offered IU a celebratory handshake. She snatched her hand back, tucked it behind her, spun her face away with a loud “HMPH,” and radiated my feelings have been TOYED with at broadcast strength.

Unbothered, he redirected the handshake to Jong-kook, who gave up, laughed enormously, and hammered him once between the shoulder blades.

“Well played, kid. You fooled EVERY one of us.”

The judgment tension flashed over into carnival. Myuk PD presented the spoils: a magnificent premium hanwoo gift set, and a gleaming pair of high-end wireless headphones. Ji-ho hugged the loot through a storm of denunciations, jokes, and congratulations, and gave the camera one last, thoroughly lit-up grin.

First Running Man: closed — on a flawless deception, and a full harvest.
