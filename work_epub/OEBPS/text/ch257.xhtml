Chapter Two Hundred Fifty-Seven

It Came to Blows

LOEN Entertainment · chairman’s office one lyric revision, one OST session, and two smiles sharp enough to draw blood

Ji-ho returned to LOEN in a good mood.

The previous evening with Jiyeon had been gentler than he expected: dinner, tears, shared sleep, and the bright domestic awkwardness of waking beside someone for the first time.

But work did not pause for happiness.

The English album still needed refinement, especially “7 Years.” He had revised the lyric several times, yet the autobiographical transitions remained uneven.

Ji-ho hung his coat, sat behind the desk, and opened the notebook.

His pen moved, stopped, crossed out a phrase, and began again.

“When I was eleven…”

The generated version carried details that belonged to another songwriter’s childhood. Ji-ho needed memories that sounded like his own.

At eleven, I caught fish in the river and climbed trees for cicadas.

He tested the line against the melody.

Better. Innocent enough to make the later hunger feel earned.

A knock interrupted him.

“Come in.”

The door opened, and a petite figure slipped through.

Ji-ho looked up and stopped.

Taeyeon wore an ivory cardigan over a simple black top and a short khaki skirt. Golden-brown hair fell over her shoulders. A faint smile softened her mouth; her eyes, however, held a coolness the smile did not reach.

“Taeyeon?”

Delight brought him to his feet. They had been separated by schedules for days, speaking mostly through calls and messages.

“Why didn’t you tell me you were coming?”

Taeyeon tilted her head.

“Am I not allowed?”

“Of course you are.”

Ji-ho reached her, drew her into his arms, and kissed her with the enthusiasm of someone making up for distance.

Taeyeon startled, one hand pressing against his chest. She did not truly push him away.

When he released her, their foreheads remained close.

“Missed me?”

She looked aside.

“I’m here to work.”

“What work?”

Taeyeon escaped his arms and sat on the sofa, crossing one leg over the other.

“The Heirs OST. ‘Biting My Lower Lip.’ You assigned me as the vocalist, remember?”

Only then did Ji-ho recall that LOEN had accepted production responsibility for the track in this alternate timeline.

He sat beside her and attempted to place an arm around her shoulders. Taeyeon shifted just far enough to avoid it.

“What’s wrong?”

She answered with one unreadable glance.

Before he could ask again, the office door opened.

“Ji-ho, I heard you were back at the comp—”

Ji-eun entered carrying a thermos and stopped mid-sentence.

She saw Taeyeon.

Taeyeon saw her.

For one suspended second, neither moved.

Ji-eun wore a pale blue dress and a high ponytail, her makeup soft enough to appear almost absent. Taeyeon sat composed on the sofa with the suggestion of a smile.

The room became electrically quiet.

DREAM LIFE · RELATIONSHIP CONFLICT STATE

Ding—Two registered partners have entered an active jealousy confrontation.

ParticipantsKim Taeyeon · Lee Ji-eun

TriggerCompeting claims to private time, workplace territory, and emotional priority

RiskEscalation through performative affection, status comparison, and accumulated resentment

Unfailing Affection does not prevent anger, conflict, or physical consequences.

Ji-ho felt the beginning of a headache.

“Ji-eun-ah. What brings you here?”

Ji-eun looked away from Taeyeon and walked in slowly. She set the thermos on the coffee table.

“Ginseng tea for Hoejang-nim.” Her voice was soft, with a small edge of hurt. “You’re preparing your album and writing for mine. I wanted to thank you.”

Writing for her album too?

Taeyeon’s eyes cooled another degree.

“How thoughtful.”

Ji-eun smiled sweetly.

“Taeyeon eonni. It’s been a while.”

“It has.”

Taeyeon rose, stepped beside Ji-ho, and linked her arm naturally through his.

“I’m recording ‘Biting My Lower Lip.’ My boyfriend is producing it personally.”

She stood on her toes and kissed his cheek, then gave Ji-eun a pleasant smile.

Ji-eun’s eyelid moved once.

She did not erupt. Instead, she lowered her eyes. When she looked up again, they had grown faintly red.

She said nothing.

The wounded silence was more effective than any accusation.

Taeyeon’s teeth nearly ached.

She arrived like a storm and became a wounded flower in three seconds.

Her hold on Ji-ho’s arm tightened while her smile became brighter.

“Ji-eun-ah, since you’re here, why don’t you listen to the session?”

Ji-eun looked to Ji-ho as if waiting for permission.

He considered every possible answer and found none that improved his life.

“You’re an excellent singer,” Taeyeon continued. “Perhaps you can offer me some advice.”

Ji-eun understood the trap immediately. Taeyeon’s technique was widely considered among the strongest in idol pop. Asking IU to “advise” her was not humility. It was a challenge wearing manners.

Ji-eun’s expression remained gentle.

“Eonni is too kind. I wouldn’t dare instruct you. I’d be grateful for the chance to learn.”

“Then come along.”

Taeyeon released Ji-ho’s arm and lifted her bag.

“Boyfriend, shall we?”

Ji-ho looked at both women.

“Let’s go.”

✦ ✦ ✦

They walked to the studio with Taeyeon on Ji-ho’s left and Ji-eun on his right. The women exchanged occasional glances across him and immediately looked away.

Ji-ho kept his eyes forward.

Ryu Dong-hwan approached from the opposite direction, saw the formation, and slowed.

His gaze moved from Ji-ho to Taeyeon to Ji-eun. Understanding arrived with professional speed.

Ji-ho signaled with his eyes.

Dong-hwan stepped forward.

“Hoejang-nim, I need to report something privately. May I borrow you for a moment?”

“Of course.”

Ji-ho turned to the women.

“Wait here. I’ll be right back.”

They nodded and watched the two men disappear around the corner.

“Hoejang-nim,” Dong-hwan whispered, “what exactly is happening?”

Ji-ho rubbed his brow.

“Don’t ask. Disable the studio’s internal monitoring feed for the session.”

Dong-hwan glanced toward the waiting women. A full meter separated them, and neither acknowledged the other’s existence.

He suppressed a smile.

“Understood.”

Ji-ho patted his shoulder and returned.

LOEN · Studio A OST recording session

Ji-ho settled at the console and began preparing the track.

Taeyeon bent beside him to study the screen, close enough that her shoulder rested against his.

“Jagiya, what does this parameter control?”

He turned his head. Their faces were only inches apart.

“Nothing you need to manage. I’ll set it.”

“My boyfriend is impressive.”

She kissed his cheek again.

On the sofa behind them, Ji-eun’s fingers tightened around one another.

She said nothing. Her smile remained in place.

“Taeyeon, into the booth.”

She put on the headphones, stepped before the microphone, and gave Ji-ho an OK sign through the glass.

He began playback.

RECORDING TAKE · “BITING MY LOWER LIP” · TAEYEON

The opening arrives intimate and controlled, breath gathered close to the microphone.

Her tone is clear without becoming thin, warm at the center, and precisely supported through every transition.

The pre-chorus rises without strain. High notes remain centered; lower phrases retain weight. Head voice and chest voice connect without an audible seam.

“Quietly I bite my lower lip — and let my eyes do the feeling—”

A private feeling is carried through the line—not oversung, but impossible to mistake.

This is not merely an OST guide take. It is a demonstration.

Ji-ho recognized what she was doing almost immediately.

Taeyeon was showing her full ability.

Her condition was exceptional. Breath control, pitch, transitions, and emotional pacing all landed above the requirements of the arrangement. She was not sacrificing the song to virtuosity; she was making virtuosity look like the song’s natural form.

When the final note ended, she removed the headphones and smiled through the glass.

Ji-ho raised his thumb.

Then he glanced at Ji-eun.

Her smile had become visibly effortful.

Taeyeon emerged and moved directly to Ji-ho’s side.

“Jagiya, how was it?”

“Excellent. Genuinely excellent.”

Approval brightened her face.

She turned to Ji-eun.

“Would you like to try a take?”

Ji-eun’s expression tightened.

“I shouldn’t.”

“Don’t be shy. You’re a wonderful singer. Perhaps you’ll reveal what I should improve.”

Ji-eun wanted to swear.

Taeyeon’s performance would have challenged a dedicated solo vocalist. Entering the booth now would not be learning; it would be volunteering for comparison on Taeyeon’s chosen battlefield.

“Eonni sang beautifully. I have nothing useful to add.”

“You’re too modest.” Taeyeon took her hand with exaggerated sincerity. “Then stay until we finish, and we’ll eat together afterward.”

“All right.”

Ji-eun smiled.

Inside, she had insulted Taeyeon in several creative categories.

Ji-ho’s phone rang.

He checked the caller and his expression shifted.

“I need to take this.”

He stepped into the corridor and closed the studio door.

The atmosphere changed at once.

Taeyeon’s smile faded. She sat opposite Ji-eun.

Ji-eun leaned back, abandoning the wounded expression she had worn for Ji-ho. Her gaze became level and cool.

Several seconds passed.

“Does SM no longer have recording studios?” Ji-eun asked. “Why must you come here?”

Taeyeon gave a short laugh.

“Am I forbidden?”

“No. But I’ll be direct. I’m also with Ji-ho.”

Taeyeon had suspected it. Hearing the admission aloud still drove heat through her chest.

“LOEN is my workplace,” Ji-eun continued. “I want us to maintain boundaries and avoid humiliating each other.”

“Your workplace?” Taeyeon’s voice cooled. “LOEN belongs to Ji-ho, not IU. I came to record an assigned OST. You don’t get to treat the building like private territory.”

“Work is work. What you did in front of me wasn’t work.”

Taeyeon stood.

“I kissed my boyfriend. That is normal.”

Ji-eun stood as well.

“Your boyfriend? Are you certain that word belongs to only one person?”

Taeyeon’s face hardened.

“I respected you as a senior,” Ji-eun said, “but the performance today was obvious. You weren’t affectionate because you missed him. You wanted me to watch.”

“And the red eyes?” Taeyeon shot back. “The tea, the silence, the poor wounded expression—you changed roles the moment you saw me.”

“I brought him something because he’s exhausted.”

“And I came to sing because he chose my voice.”

The distance between them narrowed.

What followed was not elegant.

“At least I don’t need to occupy his office every day to stay relevant,” Taeyeon said.

“At least I see him often enough to know when he needs help.”

“Clinging is not the same as being needed.”

“And arriving once every two weeks to mark territory is not romance.”

Taeyeon’s breathing deepened.

“You think proximity makes you important?”

Ji-eun smiled without warmth.

“You think a title makes you permanent?”

The words landed where both women were most afraid.

“Yah.”

“Wae?”

No one later agreed on who moved first.

Taeyeon’s hand caught Ji-eun’s ponytail. Ji-eun grabbed the front of Taeyeon’s cardigan. Each shouted at the other to let go while tightening her own grip.

They stumbled against the sofa and went down onto the rug in a tangle of hair, sleeves, and wounded dignity.

“Let go of my hair!”

“You let go first!”

“You’re stretching my cardigan!”

“You’re tearing out my ponytail!”

The studio door opened.

Ji-ho stood at the threshold.

Taeyeon and Ji-eun froze simultaneously—one holding hair, the other gripping knitwear—and turned toward him.

The room became silent enough to hear the equipment cooling.

They released each other and climbed to their feet. Taeyeon tried to smooth her wrinkled cardigan. Ji-eun attempted to reconstruct her ruined ponytail. Neither looked at Ji-ho.

He took in the evidence and closed his eyes for one measured breath.

“Would someone like to explain why my recording studio has become a wrestling ring?”
