Chapter Two Hundred Thirty-Eight

Blue Dragon Best Actress Yoon-a — Could I Even Refuse?

August 6, 2013 · the small hours Grand Walkerhill · Tiffany’s room two interrogators, one confession, one ₩5B contract on the coffee table — and three women doing arithmetic they hate

Yoon-a patted Jessica’s shoulder.

“Eonni. Taeyeon eonni and Fany eonni’s war — we stay OUT of it. Our only job is to keep our distance from Ji-ho. He… is not a good person. And I doubt his list ends with the two of them.”

Jessica shook herself. Why was she suddenly wary of YOON-A, of all people? Hmph. However clever the kid was, one properly deployed eonni-slap and she’d still fold and apologize. …Probably.

Then she remembered the dinner — the moment he’d steadied her arm, and the look that had crossed his eyes at the neckline of her dress. Heat with intent behind it. She nodded slowly. “You’re right. He’s definitely not a good person.” And her thoughts, already tangled — her brand plans, Taeyeon, Fany, the memory of being dismantled across six points of business logic — tangled further. Thinking had never been her favorite sport. Right now her head felt like porridge.

“I want to go see Fany.”

“Eh— eonni?”

“Yoon-a-yah. If Taeyeon really knows — then at MINIMUM, we need to understand why Fany did it. What she was thinking. Otherwise how do we know what we’re standing in the middle of?”

Yoon-a considered. She was, in truth, burningly curious herself — and knowing the WHY was also the only real vaccine against whatever method he might someday aim at HER.

“Okay. But eonni — whatever happens in there, do NOT let slip that Taeyeon eonni might already know.”

“Aigoo, I’m not an IDIOT.”

Yoon-a rolled her eyes with love. Not an idiot, no. Just occasionally… buffering.

✦ ✦ ✦

They rinsed off, changed into sleepwear, and knocked on Tiffany’s door.

Click.

The door opened on a bare-faced Tiffany whose eyes lit at the sight of them.

“Ya — perfect timing. Come drink with me.”

The two visitors traded a glance. So she was hiding in her room drinking alone on her own birthday, with a heated pool downstairs. Eonni. WHY did you climb into this mess?

Tiffany, oblivious to the freight in their eyes, was honestly not in a bad mood. The villa sat warm in her chest — fifty billion won of warm. She was a top-team member, yes, but nobody’s personal fortune ran to that number; idol money passed through company splits, team splits, and the tax office before it ever reached a bank account. Members bought apartments on loans and paid them down over months like everyone else. She’d once told a variety show, truthfully, that the pink car had cost far less than people assumed and the paychecks were far smaller than the legend. So when the men in their world offered — dinner, a bag, a bracelet — the sums had scale. He had given her a HOUSE. He didn’t play at giving. He simply gave, at a size that rearranged what refusal even meant. That kind of man, offering that kind of future — even shared, even wrong — was very, very hard to walk away from.

Three girls settled around the low table. The soju went around once, twice, three times; the air softened into a mild, floating warmth.

Jessica set down her glass, fingers tightening around it, and jumped.

“Fany. Just now… Yoon-a and I saw.”

The pouring hand froze. Soju trembled at the bottle’s lip.

“Saw… what?”

“Eonni.” Yoon-a’s voice was quiet. “You and Ji-ho. In the corridor. We saw.”

The air in the room stopped moving. Tiffany’s face drained white, then flooded with unnatural color. She set the bottle down very slowly. Her fingers curled. Her eyes fled both of theirs.

“I… you two…”

“Fany.” Jessica’s voice came out harder than she’d planned — anger and heartbreak elbowing each other. “Taeyeon is our best friend. How could you— with HER boyfriend—”

“I KNOW!”

Tiffany’s head snapped up, voice shaking, eyes going red at the rims all at once.

“I know I’ve wronged her. I know I shouldn’t have. But—”

She bit down hard on her lip, fighting the tears and losing ground.

“Then WHY?” Jessica pressed, gentler now. “Fany. Tell us. What were you thinking?”

Tiffany looked down for a long time. Long enough that Jessica thought the answer wasn’t coming. When it came, it came from far away.

“The first time I ever saw him was in a bar.”

Neither of them interrupted.

“You know that part. Sulli and I — he saved us both.”

A smile surfaced through the wet — small, and unbearably soft.

“Then during his trainee days, he was always around — coming to see Taeyeon, asking me about music. You know what he was even THEN. Composition, arrangement — better than all of us already. I kept finding excuses to ask him arranging questions. And I watched him get better, and better, and BETTER — watched the world start agreeing with what I already knew — and somewhere in the middle of all that watching, my heart stopped taking my instructions.”

“But—” Jessica started.

“I know. I KNOW Taeyeon is my best friend.” She lifted her head, eyes swimming. “But I met him before she did. I fell before she did. I was just — one step slower to SAY it.”

“So I endured. I told myself: fine, seeing him is enough. I pulled back. I gave myself no openings. I thought time would fix it. Time did not fix it.”

Her voice began to fray at the edges.

“The better he became, the worse it got. Every song he wrote, I looped for days. Every broadcast, I watched. And when he was with Taeyeon, I stood in the corner of the room and performed HAPPY, and I got very, very good at it.”

“And then?” Yoon-a asked, softly.

Tiffany wiped her eyes, breathed in, and let the rest go.

“Then — the night of the MAMA awards in Hong Kong. I drank too much. He came to see Taeyeon, and I saw him in the corridor and thought I was hallucinating, and I—” A pause; a bitter little smile. “I kissed him. First.”

“MWO?” Jessica’s eyes went round.

“And that’s when I found out he felt it too. He said he’d had feelings for me — and buried them, because of Taeyeon. After that kiss…” Her head dropped; her voice went small. “…neither of us could keep pretending.”

Jessica’s mouth opened and produced nothing. But Yoon-a’s brow had drawn tight around a splinter. HE FELT IT TOO? His tenderness toward Taeyeon was public record — nobody performed that, not for years. So… both at once, from the start? Or— and here the cold walked up her spine on small feet —OR HE’D DONE IT ON PURPOSE. Known both women loved him. Done precisely nothing. Stood in place while two hearts walked themselves into the net, so that he never had to choose, and never had to lose either one. The thought flashed and was gone, and she kept it off her face. Saying it now would only flay a woman already in the deep water. Fany eonni was past rescue by argument.

“And there’s this.”

Tiffany stood abruptly, fetched a folder from the bed, and dropped it on the coffee table with a slap.

“See for yourselves.”

Jessica opened it — and her eyes went enormous. Yoon-a leaned in and audibly inhaled. A property contract. A luxury villa, Gangnam district. OWNER: HWANG MI-YOUNG. And in the price column, immaculate: ₩5,000,000,000.

“This is—”

Jessica’s voice had picked up a tremor.

“His birthday present.”

Tiffany sat back down, took up her glass, and drained it in one motion — as if the alcohol were a lever that could tip everything out of her at once.

“Yes. Fine. I’ll say it myself: it isn’t ONLY love. I’m vain. I’m materialistic. Judge away.”

“FIVE BILLION. Go on — go find a man. If he gives you a HUNDRED MILLION while you’re dating, I’ll concede everything!”

“The songs he writes me chart on Billboard’s front page. The house he hands me costs five billion. Standing where I stand — does ‘being the only one’ still get to be the deciding vote? DOES it?”

Neither guest could locate a word.

“He’s twenty, and he already OWNS LOEN. Where’s the ceiling? Nobody knows. If I stand next to him and climb — where do I end up? Billboard again? A second time, a third?”

“A house? Have one now.”

“Love? I love him. He loves me.”

“So tell me — will I ever find a better man than him? WILL I? Say it!”

The room went so quiet they could hear each other breathe.

Jessica gripped the contract, fingers faintly trembling, the number circling her skull and demolishing every prepared speech on its way around. She thought of the dinner again — of assuming she could use his resources and pay nothing. And now, holding five billion won of counter-evidence, she wasn’t sure of anything. IF HE PUT FIVE BILLION BEHIND MY BRAND. IF FIVE ISN’T ENOUGH — TEN? IF ALL IT COSTS IS STANDING NEAR HIM— could I refuse that? COULD I? She shook her head hard, physically, to fling the thought out.

Yoon-a sat silent while her insides rearranged. So this was the machine, assembled and running: not love alone, not money alone — love AND career AND future, delivered in quantities that made refusal feel like self-harm, given so freely and so naturally that there was nothing to push against. And the worst part, the genuinely frightening part: he seemed to MEAN it. Not performance. The real thing. Which was exactly why they stayed. She had always trusted her own cleverness to see through anything and protect her from anything. Tonight, for the first time, she wasn’t sure. If he ever turned the full apparatus on HER — five billion, a Billboard single, a film — his FIRST film had entered the all-time top five, had walked Sulli straight through Chungmuro’s front door. If the next one came with a role and her name attached to it — ‘BLUE DRAGON BEST ACTRESS IM YOON-A.’ ‘BAEKSANG BEST ACTRESS IM YOON-A.’ The words alone put a tremor through her that she despised and could not stop.

“Fany eonni…” Her voice came out dry. “Taeyeon eonni, she…”

Tiffany’s whole body flinched, and the tears came back over the wall.

“I know I’m terrible. I know what I owe her. But I can’t put it down. I TRIED putting it down — I swear I tried—”

She covered her face; her shoulders shook.

“When the tour ends, I’m going to Jeonju with her. Taking her parents in for their checkups. And then— then I’ll kneel in front of her and beg. She can hit me, she can scream at me — I’ll take every bit of it. As long as— as long as she doesn’t make me leave him—”

Jessica watched her cry, and the anger drained out through the floor, leaving only a briny, complicated ache. She put an arm around her shoulder. “Don’t cry… hey. Don’t cry.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Outside, the night had gone fully black. Far off, the Han carried the city’s lights along its dark back.

Three girls sat in the small warm room, each locked in a different arithmetic, none of them speaking. Glass touched glass occasionally. The river murmured under the window.

Jessica, eyes half-lidded: IF HE REALLY BACKED THE BRAND— just supposing—

Yoon-a, thoughts running like a fever: he’d map a route to me through WORK — that’s his way. He’d been laying stones since that night he named the exact fault lines in my acting, the idol-versus-actress problem, all of it. He’s waiting for the right project. The right offer. So do I refuse? CAN I? …Or do I pretend I can’t see the hook, eat the bait, and swim off? People do that. In theory.

And Tiffany, watching her two interrogators stare into the middle distance with THAT expression on their faces, arrived — too late — at an operational insight of her own: producing a fifty-billion-won contract to defend your honor is ALSO an advertisement. Wonderful. She’d just personally recruited her own competition. Women. Half an hour ago these two stood on the moral high ground reading her the verdict — and one look at the actual number, and the jury’s eyes had gone soft and thoughtful. Sisterhood, she thought, reaching for the bottle with the serenity of the damned. Sure. Sisterhood.
