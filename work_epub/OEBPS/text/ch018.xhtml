Chapter Eighteen

First Meeting: Red Velvet

Twenty full minutes on the floor before he could trust his own limbs again. A long, scalding shower rinsed off the sweat and most of the regret.

Eight p.m. sharp — the God of Learning’s hour, immovable as sunrise. He picked up How to Win Friends and Influence People and sank back in. Reading compounded; cognition was capital; the investment continued.

Field Notes

“There is only one way to get anybody to do anything — and that is by making the other person want to do it.”

“A person’s name is, to that person, the sweetest and most important sound in any language.”

— Carnegie’s lines flowed quietly under the lamp, settling into the architecture of his thinking.

Study done, one cup of lavender tea, lights out.

✦   ✦   ✦

Morning sunlight flooded the room. One day of leave left — and today’s mission was employment.

For adult official trainees, the company set no hard daily quota; the norm hovered around eight hours, though the true grind-lords voluntarily stretched it to ten or thirteen. Ji-ho’s own blueprint: ten hard hours daily, then four hours of part-time work after nine.

Part-Time Math · Seoul 2012

Feasible monthly (bar work)~₩1,000,000
+ SM trainee stipend₩500,000

Enough, comfortably, for a respectable Seoul life.

He dressed with intent: a crisp white cotton shirt, top two buttons undone to a clean line of collarbone, sleeves rolled sharp to the elbow over forearms just beginning to show their new definition. Light khaki straight-leg trousers doing full justice to the long legs. Simple. Immaculate. Spring-breeze boyish.

“Tch. How is one man this handsome?”

He raked his hair once in the mirror and headed for the door in an excellent mood — and stopped dead on the threshold.

On the floor sat a small, beautifully packed box of bright red strawberries. Beside it, a sticky note in neat, graceful handwriting:

On the doormat · reply

The rice cake was delicious — thank you. Here’s to being good neighbors~

No signature. But there was exactly one person it could be: the roommate he had yet to lay eyes on. A gift returned for a gift. Well — she’ll be easy to live with.

Strawberries into the fridge, door locked, and out into the Seoul morning with his eyes set.

Objective one: a well-paid part-time job. Objective two: raw skill. Objective three: debut.

He skipped the obvious bar districts and walked for Hongdae instead. The university belt meant bars, cafés, and restaurants stacked shoulder to shoulder — jobs everywhere, foot traffic heavy, tips generous. And one more angle: idols and trainees haunted Hongdae constantly. He was in the industry now; every chance encounter was potential future capital.

Down the Hongdae streets he went — clean shirt, straight back, that face — and heads turned in his wake, one girl after another sneaking a second look, a few phones rising surreptitiously for photos. Old news, all of it. He wore the faint half-smile and scanned the shopfronts for help-wanted notices.

A sleek, minimalist bar appeared on his left, a posting in the window: Evening server wanted. ₩8,000/hr, dinner included, tips separate. Strong presentation and communication skills required.

Upscale room, real traffic, and they’re hiring for looks and talk. That’s my posting. He straightened his collar, breathed once, pushed open the door.

Minutes later he walked back out. No part-timers wanted after all. On to the next.

The next turned into the next, and the next turned into nightfall, and nightfall turned up nothing. He refused to be discouraged — job hunts were built this way, and his cash reserves could hold the line for two months yet. Tomorrow was day one as an official trainee; the smart move now was rest. Home, one hour under the God of Learning, and bed.

✦   ✦   ✦

He woke early and dressed for the occasion: an ivory wide-sleeved linen shirt, breathable and loose over a white tee, wide-legged khaki trousers, white trainers. Dance-ready, and effortlessly fresh on the eye.

Today’s outfit — Style Index: 60

Reborn or not, the peacock in him had survived fully intact — he’d never once regretted money spent on clothes.

Eight a.m. found him at the gym nearest SM Entertainment. Training the body was a fixed block in his day now, always before company hours. He’d been a year-round gym rat in his last life; the machines were old friends. Four minutes easy on the treadmill to lift the heart rate, then two sets of jumping jacks to wake the limbs and core.

Mid-jack, his peripheral vision snagged on a slender figure stretching nearby — a fitted tee sitting exactly right on a graceful frame, not an ounce of softness at the waistline, the whole silhouette all clean lines and quiet discipline.

Wait — her?

He finished the set, thought a moment, and detoured to the counter for two cups of instant coffee.

“Sunbae-nim. Good morning.”

“Oh? It’s you?”

Irene was deep in a lunge stretch — high-waisted quick-dry training pants tracing the curve of the movement, all healthy spring-loaded athleticism.

“Ji-ho. Just passed the official evaluation.” He held out one of the coffees with a smile. “Sunbae — for the coffee, that night. Consider the debt paid.”

She knew him, of course. For a solid week this boy had left the building after her, every single night — she’d never once managed to shake him. So the news that he’d passed genuinely pleased her. She took the cup, and the smile was real.

“Congratulations.”

“EONNI!!”

A foghorn of a greeting. Ji-ho and Joo-hyun turned in unison — three girls were waving their way over: Kang Seul-gi, Son Seung-wan, and Park Soo-young.
