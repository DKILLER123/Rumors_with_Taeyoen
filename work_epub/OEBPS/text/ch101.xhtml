Chapter One Hundred One

The Partner Development System

All those months on the ab board and under the squat rack had made Ji-ho frighteningly strong.

Taeyeon, for her part, had a dancer’s flexibility and stamina well beyond the average — and even so, the night had wrung her out completely. She slept like something anchored, the fine heart-shaped oval of her face still carrying the faint salt-tracks of tears.

He had been gentle. She was simply… very small.

He eased into the sleeping bag with the greatest possible care and gathered her in. The movement stirred her; her brow creased faintly, and she mumbled, mostly asleep:

“No… more.”

“…Have mercy.”

A laugh escaped him despite everything, and he pressed a light kiss to her smooth forehead.

Sleep, however, wanted nothing to do with him. He was wide awake — lit up from the inside.

He had, finally, the woman he loved in his arms.

And he could afford the calm he’d worn in front of Lee Soo-man and Kim Young-min — he had a second lifetime and a system at his back. But Taeyeon? The company would never willingly wreck its money tree, true — yet where its core interests were concerned, sentiment counted for exactly nothing. TVXQ had been the biggest act in Asia; contracts went sideways, and shelving followed anyway. For her to plant herself beside him in that office had required a courage he did not intend to forget.

A girl that true, held now against him with nothing in between — the fullness of it was past language.

He read the quest list twice, weighing it.

Singer — obviously. No argument with the track.

Quest one was manageable. Vocal growth past 80 got steep, but Taeyeon had the talent, the discipline, and nearly three years of runway; whatever techniques the system fed him, he could pass along on every stolen evening.

Quest two he’d have pursued without any system prompt at all. The idea of her ever going under a knife — she was this beautiful, now, tonight — no. He would move that anxiety where it belonged: into performance, into writing, into the music itself. That, he could do.

Three, four, five: the gradient climbed. Her musical literacy was high — she could arrange, could write — but at an ordinary level, for now. His own craft, system-fed, had crossed into professional territory. So the dates would double as masterclasses.

Two people who love music, talking music while they fall deeper in love. Not one wasted minute.

Then he remembered: the Celebration Package from the day the wager was struck — still sealed in the warehouse.

Idol Aura. Now THAT is a keeper.

Skin management was an arms race in this industry — top artists burned small fortunes on it yearly. Sleep was the other currency: fame arrived and sleep left, four hours a night if you were lucky, and the aura would rebuild him nightly regardless. And the joint repair — half the idols of the first generation limped into their thirties on knees and lower backs ground down in trainee years. BoA had gone under the knife for hers.

As for the diehard-fan conversion — those who knew, knew. Practically a cheat code.

As expected of Taeyeon. Even the rewards she brings are top-shelf.

He curled around the warm, sweet weight of her, decided to field-test the aura’s sleep function immediately, closed his eyes — and was gone in moments.

✦   ✦   ✦

Taeyeon woke inside a feeling she had no precedent for — warmth and a deep, humming soreness braided together.

Touch came back first: her back against a firm, heated chest, his arm barred across her waist, holding her in with unambiguous possessiveness.

The early-summer sun hadn’t cleared the ridge; the mountain air was cool. But his skin ran hot as a stove, and between the pressed-together warmth and the thin blanket, the little pocket of heat had turned frankly indecent. A fine sheen of sweat tacked their skin together.

“So hot… and STILL holding on this tight.”

A drowsy, luxurious grumble. She turned over, propped up her fair shoulders, and tapped the tip of his nose — once, lightly — mouth curving into pure happiness.

“Honestly. Completely hateful.”

He seemed to register the touch, produced one vague sound through his nose, and sank straight back under.

She lay still and studied him.

The profile. The full brow. The nose-line, straight as a ridge. The clean jaw. Like a woman who has come into an heirloom, she couldn’t stop herself — she traced it all, fingertip by careful fingertip. The thick brows first. Then down the bridge of the nose. Then her soft palm laid flat against his cheek, reading skin smooth as good jade.

How is his skin THIS good? I’m actually jealous.

Her finger came to rest, finally, on the pale, well-cut line of his mouth — the mouth that had pressed a hundred burning kisses into the dark last night, and sung the gentlest song she’d ever heard.

There was a tiny cut at the corner of it. Her doing. She hadn’t quite managed restraint.

That must sting… She blew on it softly, twice, genuinely contrite — for approximately three seconds.

Hmph. That’s what you get for bullying me. I BEGGED, and did you stop? Serves you right.

She lifted the blanket for an audit. Sure enough: faint scratch lines across his chest, also hers. She winced at her own fingernails, then glared at them with real menace. Daring to wound MY man. You’re all getting cut the minute we’re home.

…My man.

Heat climbed her face. This unguarded, sleeping, absurdly handsome person was — by verified, official, mutually-confessed fact — her boyfriend. The warmth of his arms, the steady heartbeat, the occasional sleep-mumble: hers. All of it. Taeyeon’s.

“How can anyone… look like this?”

A soundless sigh. By the standards of an industry that had numbed her to top-percentile faces, this one still sat, without argument, at the very tip of the pyramid.

And it was hers.

A happiness close to vertigo, shot through with the shy after-warmth of the night, rolled over her from crown to toe. Fingertips stopped being enough. She tipped her chin up and pressed kisses to him — brow, nose-tip, cheek — each one inflating the fullness in her chest another degree.

Mine. My Ji-ho.

The leader of Girls’ Generation — radiant on stage, adored by millions — lay in a tent on a mountainside doing exactly what every ordinary girl in love does: drowning in her boyfriend’s face, packed to the brim with sweetness and pride, very nearly overflowing.

She burrowed back into his arms, cheek against the warm skin of his neck, listened to his pulse — and smiled herself back to sleep.

✦   ✦   ✦

When she woke again, the space beside her was empty.

Her heart clutched; she sat up fast — and the soreness yanked its leash. Her sparse brows knotted.

“Sss—”

Beside the pillow, folded and waiting: clothes he’d laid out for her. She hooked them over with one slim porcelain arm, dressed — beige knit vest, shirt, loose warm-toned shorts; clean, crisp, quietly elegant — crawled out of the tent, stepped into her little white sneakers, and looked up.

He was cooking ramyeon.

“Nam-chin!”

He turned at the word — boyfriend — and smiled at the sight of her.

“Morning, nuna~”

“Noodles are almost ready. Go wash up first.”

She set off toward him at a happy run — managed two steps — pulled up with a wince — and downshifted to a slow, dignified, extremely careful walk.

She arrived just as he was tasting the broth off a spoon.

“Mm~ perfect.”

She rose onto her toes and kissed him. Thoroughly. It was a while before she let go.

“Morning. My nam-chin.”

He wiped the corner of his mouth.

“Go wash up and eat — we need an early start back today.”

“Are you SICK of me already?” Instant ferocity. “I haven’t even complained that you taste like ramyeon broth!”

“Who’s sick of you—” Caught between laughing and pleading, he bent and kissed her several more times until the storm system dissipated.

Washed and seated on the folding stool, she inhaled the steam off the pot, pink tongue darting at the corner of her lip. Last night had burned through a great deal of fuel. She was starving.

“Thank you — I’m digging in!”

She lifted a small coil of noodles, blew on it, and vacuumed it in.

“Mm~!” Her eyes lit. “Masitda! Your ramyeon game is almost catching up to mine.”

Pfft— cough, cough, cough.

He’d been mid-sip of broth. It did not survive the sentence.

YOU? Taeyeon can cook ramyeon? If I hadn’t watched your variety appearances with my own eyes, that sincere little face might’ve fooled me. The shortlist of people on this peninsula who can make INSTANT NOODLES taste bad has your name on it — and you have the nerve—

She was already whipping out tissues, all concern.

“What happened? Nobody’s racing you for it.”

“Hah~” He accepted the tissue, wiped his mouth, and delivered the counsel of a man defusing a bomb. “Nuna. Listen carefully. You must never — EVER — cook ramyeon on a variety show.”

“Why not?”

“Because then your fans in every country on Earth will know you can’t even cook RAMYEON.”

“Mwo?” She bit down on her pink lip and leveled a glare of maximum wattage. “You think I can’t even make ramyeon properly?”

“Nuna. Have you ever made it?”

“Of course… not. Seohyun makes it. Or Yoon-a, or Yuri.”

Zero field experience; total, unshakable confidence.

“But how hard can it BE? Water, noodles, egg, scallions, seasoning packet, tteok, cheese — boil. Done.”

His mouth twitched through several unspoken drafts.

“Nuna. When you’re back at the dorm — make it exactly once. For the Girls’ Generation members. First.”

“They’ll give you an HONEST review. If it’s good — congratulations, new skill acquired.”

“And if it’s bad… at least you embarrassed yourself in front of your little sisters, and not in front of your fans.”

“That’s… actually reasonable.” She tilted her head through the logic. Losing face in front of the members isn’t really losing face. In front of the fans — that’s REAL. “I’ll make it the day I’m back.”

“As expected of Taeyeon.” He applauded. “Execution: flawless.”

She glowed. The members are in for a treat — the first bowl of ramyeon Taeyeon has ever made!

He smiled, said nothing, and buried the deed deep.

Somewhere in the future, eight members of Girls’ Generation, in unison: yah — THANK you, Ji-ho.
