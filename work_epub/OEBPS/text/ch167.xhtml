Chapter One Hundred Sixty-Seven

The Argument

The same night Banpo — the living room, then a parked car hard truths, and who can bear to hear them

Sunny herded the couple bodily out of the study.

“Go, go, OUT. Honestly — leaving the three of us stranded out there. You two have all night to be disgusting.”

Closing the door behind them, she stole one last glance at the milk glass on the desk.

In the living room, Jessica lay draped across the long sofa with a fashion magazine, legs crossed at leisure. Yoon-a, dishes conquered, was slicing fruit at the island. He settled into the armchair with Taeyeon, folded her soft hand into his, and sighed without sound.

By all natural law, this hour belonged to a warm girlfriend and a closed door. Instead: hand-holding under supervision, with three high-wattage bulbs in attendance — including two women whose emotional intelligence was famous, and mysteriously off duty tonight.

Sunny lobbed a grape into her mouth and lobbed a topic after it.

“Ji-ho — your film starts soon, right? I think I saw the articles.”

“Mm. Pre-production’s nearly wrapped. Cameras roll next month.”

“That FAST?” Yoon-a arrived with a plate of neatly cut fruit and real surprise. “It’s locked? Film has so many more variables than drama.”

“The announcements are out and final auditions ended today. It’s real.”

“Ani—” She sat beside Jessica and picked up an apple slice, and her tone shifted into something rare and serious. “I mean — your first time acting, and you go straight for a film LEAD. That’s an enormous risk. Ji-ho, you need to understand something.”

She set the apple down and leaned in.

“Film and drama shoot on completely different logic. Dramas air while shooting — the story runs forward, the actor’s emotions run WITH it. Film shoots out of order. Today you play the ending’s despair; tomorrow, the opening’s innocence; the day after, the middle’s big fight. You have to jump between emotional states — between entire life stages — with total precision, on command. That’s hard for trained actors.”

“And on a theater screen, one unnatural blink — one stiff smile — gets blown up thirty feet tall, and the audience falls straight out of the story. Stage actors can fill a theater with big gestures. Drama actors get small flaws carried away by the episode’s momentum. Film forgives nothing.”

She finished with a small self-deprecating laugh.

“That’s why I don’t dare touch film scripts yet. I keep feeling like my acting needs more seasoning first.”

He listened — and then looked at her with an expression she couldn’t classify.

“Why are you looking at me like that? Is there something on my face?”

He leaned back, still idly playing with Taeyeon’s fingers, and went straight through the armor.

“Nuna. Is it that you don’t DARE take films… or that films aren’t offered?”

“MWO?”

Her face went up like a struck match. She lunged across Jessica and Taeyeon both, grabbing for his arm — he was up and behind the sofa before her fingers closed.

“Oh? Someone’s upset by an honest question?”

“Aish! Come HERE! Let’s see where you hide!”

The chase looped the sofa cluster at full kindergarten velocity. Taeyeon startled, then burst out laughing and tucked herself against Jessica to spectate. Jessica bumped her with a shoulder: “Taeyeon-ah. That’s your BOYFRIEND being chased. Why are you giggling?” — which recalled the leader to her duty. She snagged Yoon-a’s wrist mid-lap.

“Ya! Yoon-a! What exactly are you two doing in front of me?”

Yoon-a converted the arrest into an embrace, latching onto Taeyeon’s arm and pointing across the room with maximal grievance.

“Eonni, LOOK at him! I already get torn apart over my ratings, and he goes straight for the wound! Eonni, avenge me! Your boyfriend is bullying me!”

Taeyeon scratched the tip of her nose and her eyes drifted — because, honestly, some part of her thought he had a point. Yoon-a read the face in one second flat, sniffed, and released her.

“Hmph. Of course. A woman whose heart has moved is a woman without loyalty.”

She flung herself at Jessica instead, who received the impact with zero ceremony and one repelling foot to the waist.

“Ya! You’re heavy! Away from me.”

Exiled to the sofa’s far corner, Yoon-a hugged a cushion and radiated betrayal.

But he wasn’t done. He came back around the sofa — not sitting, leaning against the backrest.

“Nuna. No malice in this. But since You Are My Destiny, both dramas you’ve picked cratered in the ratings — and you took the beating for it in the press. All I’m saying is: raise the bar on the scripts you accept. Or wait. Don’t rush.”

“Ya! You think I pick the scripts? The scripts pick ME!” The hurt in it was real now, her voice climbing. “Yes, the company pushes acting resources my way — they try. But the company’s drama pipeline is the tallest dwarf in the village. Last year’s show flopped and I got flayed for it — wasn’t that enough?”

Her eyes reddened fast, the words tumbling.

“You think I don’t WANT a Kim Eun-sook script? A Hong sisters script? Who doesn’t know their shows hit? Who WANTS to keep doing lukewarm dramas that get panned? But my acting isn’t elite, I didn’t train at a film school, I have no industry pipeline of my own, the company can only reach so far — WHAT exactly am I supposed to do? Am I not trying hard enough? Is that it?”

Real tears cleared her lashes and fell. Taeyeon flinched and tugged his hand — “ya, stop” — and he squeezed back gently: not yet.

Watching her cry, his own read stayed steady, and complicated. The problem wasn’t effort. It was structure. Her calendar belonged to Girls’ Generation first — promotions, tours, variety — leaving acting whatever scraps remained. Her pattern wasn’t “wait for the right script”; it was “fill the schedule gap with whatever script exists.” And the best material never went hunting for an idol whose acting reputation was unproven — so no time to sharpen, so no great roles, so weaker shows, so the “ratings poison” label thickened, and the wheel turned again. To be fair, the other side of the ledger gleamed: as an idol she stood at the summit, and no drama flop could dent that income or that fame. But he thought, involuntarily, of the counter-example he knew best: a certain soloist whose early acting had been doubted just as loudly — who, owning her own schedule, had simply out-waited and out-worked the doubt, one visibly better performance at a time. Slow, planned, compounding. Yoon-a’s version was the opposite: a decade of acting in the gaps, and a craft that never got its breakthrough season.

Jessica had seen enough. She wrapped an arm around the crying girl and turned a genuinely cold face on him.

“Ji-ho. Are you FINISHED? She’s upset enough and you keep pressing on the bruise. Where exactly is your manners setting tonight?”

“Soo-yeon nuna. This is a career conversation between me and Yoon-a nuna, and it comes from a good place. Stepping in with that tone — isn’t that a little past your lane?”

“PAST MY LANE?” Her breath caught on the audacity; her voice went up a floor. “Yoon-a is my sister. I watch you make her cry and I’m supposed to sit quietly? And what has she done wrong, exactly? She’s an idol, not a trained actress — carrying full group schedules AND acting to this level is already extraordinary. And YOU — a newcomer whose first film hasn’t even OPENED — where do you find the qualifications to lecture her? Are you a Best Actor? A famous director?”

“My qualification is that I’m a friend who means it — and that everything I said is observable fact.” He didn’t retreat; he took one step forward and met her anger levelly. “Idol-to-actor is a hard road, agreed. But ‘she’s trying hard’ can’t be the shield that blocks every honest assessment. Nuna — you’ve acted too. Objectively: would you call your own work in Wild Romance ‘strong’? You know what the viewers and critics said. So do I.”

Direct hit, on an old wound. The reviews from that show had been pointed, some of them cruel — and her face went crimson, chest rising, finger trembling as it aimed at him.

“You—! FINE. Excellent. Ji-ho, what a SILVER tongue. Since your standards are so lofty — let’s all watch your little self-written, self-starring debut film together, shall we? Pray it doesn’t flop so hard the critics eat you alive and it slinks out of theaters in a week! We will SEE!”

She surged to her feet and hauled the still-sniffling Yoon-a up with her.

“Yoon-a, we’re leaving. We don’t sit with people who don’t know what respect is. Let him enjoy his own righteousness alone!”

“Wait.”

This time his voice was aimed past Jessica, at the girl with the stiffened back. Yoon-a stopped. Turned, red-eyed, uncertain.

“Yoon-a nuna. Honest words are bitter — if you were anyone else I’d have said nothing at all. And after tonight I won’t bring it up again. So let me finish it once, properly.”

“Six years since debut. As an idol, you’re standing on the summit — genuinely, be proud of that. But as an ACTRESS — forgive me — your position and your foundations are no different from a rookie’s. If you want the acting road to run long, ‘a drama when there’s a schedule gap’ can’t be the operating system anymore. The scripts that will make you shine won’t fall out of the sky. They get taken — by preparation heavy enough to be undeniable.”

He let one beat pass, then softened it, deliberately.

“Think it over. And if someday you’ve thought it through and want the specific version — come call me ‘hyeongbu’ twice, nicely, and if I’m in a good mood I might share the practical parts. Your choice entirely.”

Jessica nearly ignited all over again — and Yoon-a’s hand closed quietly on her wrist. The younger girl looked at him for a long second, tears still hanging, then at Taeyeon’s worried, wordless face, then at Sunny’s tactical silence on the sofa.

“…Let’s go, eonni.” It came out thick.

The door clicked shut behind them, and the living room’s silence rang.

Taeyeon crossed to him and pinched his arm, scolding in a whisper.

“You— your mouth is TOO blunt. You made Yoon-a cry and practically set Sica on fire.”

“The content was probably right,” Sunny sighed, setting down the cushion she’d been hiding behind. “But the delivery drew blood. That kid looks sunny, but her pride runs bone-deep.”

He caught Taeyeon’s pinching hand and folded it into his palm.

“Bitter medicine. If fear of bruising her feelings means watching her circle the same mediocre dramas and take the same beatings for another five years — THAT’S the unkind option. Some realities hurt less the earlier you face them.”

He looked toward the closed door, voice dropping.

“I just hope… this time it actually lands.”

The car door slammed; the cabin pressure dropped to something breathable only with effort.

Jessica hurled her bag into the back seat.

“UNBELIEVABLE! Who does he think he IS? Some rookie with a pretty face and a hit album, talking to us like that! How did Taeyeon end up with someone that arrogant — all that talent wrapped around such an infuriating core!”

She rounded on the driver’s seat.

“Where was HE when we were clawing through this industry? Yoon-a — don’t you listen to a word of it. A little success and suddenly he’s qualified to grade everyone. Stay away from people like that!”

The driver’s seat stayed strangely quiet. Yoon-a sat against the headrest, face turned to the parking garage’s dim concrete, tear tracks dry, eyes down.

“Yoon-a. Don’t be sad. His opinion isn’t worth it. You’ve done SO well. Truly.”

She turned then, and the smile she produced was thin and worn.

“Eonni. I’m okay. And… what he said was ugly. But it wasn’t all wrong.”

“Yoon-a!”

“I KNOW, eonni.” She shook her head, and the exhaustion in it was older than the evening. “I know those problems better than anyone. The acting foundation isn’t solid. The scripts I can actually reach are limited. My acting schedule is whatever’s left over after team schedules. I know ALL of it.”

She looked down at her own folded hands.

“But eonni… what am I supposed to DO? Everything I have — everything ‘Yoon-a’ is — is built on the sign that says GIRLS’ GENERATION. Without it… what is Yoon-a, exactly? Is this face really irreplaceable in this industry? And my figure—”

The questions came one after another, each heavier than the last, and Jessica found her mouth open with nothing in it — because the same fears had guest rooms in her own chest. The group’s halo was both crown and cage, and they all knew it.

“Inside the team, I’m the face. My schedule HAS to put the group first — and I’ve been glad to do it. Comeback: I give everything. Overseas tour: personal auditions get dropped. Team needs exposure: the acting classes get postponed again.” A hollow laugh. “It’s not that I don’t want to sharpen and wait for the right role, like he said. I don’t have the TIME. And I don’t have the… standing. Outside the SM halo, auditioning cold against real actresses for real scripts — what are my odds? He’s right. At the level of winning great roles, I’m starting from a rookie’s line — with the ‘idol’ label as extra baggage.”

She started the car. Jessica watched the garage lights slide by, her anger cooling into something more complicated, and when she spoke again her voice had gone quiet and level.

“Yoon-a. I understand all of it — for the team, we’ve all given things up. Six years of it. But… have you ever thought about this: hasn’t Girls’ Generation basically REACHED its ceiling?”

Yoon-a’s head turned sharply.

“We’re not the teenagers who debuted anymore. The name will stay top-tier — but the explosive growth years are done. Idols have shelf-lives. Nobody does this forever.” A pause, and her profile looked lonely in the passing light. “Twenty-four isn’t young — for an IDOL. But it’s very young for a LIFE. We have to think about what we stand on when the halo fades. Our own work. Our own names.”

She looked over.

“So — hateful as he is — what he warned you about is exactly the problem you were going to have to face anyway. Isn’t it.”

Yoon-a didn’t answer. The city’s neon ran molten down the windshield. His harsh sentences; Taeyeon’s swallowed worry; Sunny’s diplomatic silence; and now Jessica’s cold arithmetic — all of it braided into one long thought. She knew Girls’ Generation couldn’t be everything forever. She knew he’d named the thing she’d been dodging for years: that Actress Yoon-a needed her own plan, not a slot in the group’s calendar. But leaving a fortress you built your whole self inside — how?

“Eonni.” Barely audible. “What should I do?”

Jessica looked at her for a long moment — because she didn’t have the answer either. She was groping along her own version of the same wall, and lately she could feel her own scale tipping, quietly, toward “self”: a business of her own someday, something built from her own taste that could carry her own name. A road covered in thorns, presumably. All the real ones were.

“Sleep on it. Don’t decide anything tonight.” A pause, and then the part that mattered: “But Yoon-a — whatever you choose, be sure WHY. Not for the group. Not for the company. Not even for the fans. For Yoon-a.”

The car ran steady through the Seoul night, carrying two women doing arithmetic on their own futures, toward a tomorrow neither could see yet.
