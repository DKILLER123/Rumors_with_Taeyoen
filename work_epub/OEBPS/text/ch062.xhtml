Chapter Sixty-Two

The Soft Little Tummy

Pulling “Some” out of the vault was a decision he’d turned over for a long time. The vault was deep, but it opened one drawer at a time — and this had to be the exact right drawer.

Field Notes

The case for “Some”: the phenomenon duet of 2014 — the song of that year, full stop. It would ignite an entire industry trend of male-female duets, achieve total national saturation, and still stand a decade later as one of K-pop’s definitive collaborative classics.

The 2014 ledger: Gaon triple crown — year-end digital, download, AND streaming charts. Four million–plus downloads. Melon year-end #1. Eleven music-show trophies. The year-end sweep: Golden Disc digital daesang-tier honors, Seoul Music Awards digital prize, MAMA’s best collaboration — twenty-two trophies in total, with the writers and producers collecting year-end composition and production awards of their own.

And one more factor, filed under both business and personal: Taeyeon was a perfect duet partner for it. The vocal — beyond question. The star power — beyond question. And the song itself was about two people circling each other before the beginning begins… which happened to describe, with uncomfortable precision, the exact current state of affairs between the writer and the vocalist.

Weighed publicly, weighed privately, “Some” won on every axis. And the contingency was equally clear: if he laid this song on the table and SM still refused a solo debut — he would simply change companies.

Which was why he’d ended practice early: he’d asked Taeyeon to help record the demo for the company song library.

✦   ✦   ✦
May 26, 2012 MBC “Music Core” — TTS Promotion Stage the nurse-uniform stages · an untroubled #1

Taeyeon’s month had been a blur. TaeTiSeo’s fire had outrun both the company’s projections and her own; the promotion calendar had eaten her alive. Today’s “Twinkle” stage at Music Core ended the way they all did lately — with a trophy — and after the encore the three of them shed their stage outfits and collapsed into the van.

“Ssss— ah.” Tiffany peeled off her heels and kneaded her feet. “Oppa, do we have anything else today?”

“Nothing,” the manager said from the wheel.

“Company or dorm?”

“DORM. Obviously.” Then, with feeling: “That stage outfit was impossible to move in. I nearly died out there.”

“Taeyeon? Seohyun?”

“Dorm for me too, oppa,” Seohyun murmured from against the seat back.

Taeyeon was reading her latest KakaoTalk: [Nuna — schedule finished?] She considered for exactly one second.

“Oppa — I’ve got something. Drop me at the company first.”

“Got it.”

“Taeyeon-ah?” Tiffany’s curiosity arrived on schedule. “What’s at the company?”

For a girl group at its absolute peak, romance was radioactive — and whatever this thing with Ji-ho was, it wasn’t defined yet, and it certainly wasn’t for group consumption. She blinked, maximally innocent.

“No idea. They just told me to come in.” And then, wheel hard over: “Oh right — Fany, you’ve been deep in that arranging kick lately. Learn anything good?”

Direct hit. Tiffany lit up instantly.

“I met this AMAZING teacher recently! He’s taught me so many arranging tricks — I tried them and everything got instantly better!”

“Really?” Seohyun stirred, interested — she’d been attempting arrangements herself. “Eonni, could you introduce me?”

“Ah— well…” You both know him. But when I introduce him, it will NOT be as my arranging teacher. A hair-flip bought her a half-second. “He’s… particular. Temperamental. Later — once he and I are closer, I’ll introduce you.”

“…Okay.” Seohyun deflated politely.

Taeyeon, satisfied the two of them were safely entangled, returned to her phone:

KakaoTalk · Ji-ho
Taeyeon
Schedule’s done. At the company in ~20.

Ji-ho
[sticker: you worked hard!]
I’m in the production room. Got you Starmix gummies.

Starmix?! Her whole body perked. Her favorite gummies on the planet — and suddenly the ride felt slow. Though whether the urgency was for the gummies or for the boy holding them, she declined to investigate.

✦   ✦   ✦

Six o’clock sharp, she reached the company — greeted her way down the corridor, stopped outside the production room, checked her makeup in a compact, straightened her clothes, and only then knocked.

The door opened at once. And both of them briefly forgot the script.

His radically simple styling routed every drop of attention to the face and frame — clean, easy, unfairly handsome. And she — dark green cropped tee over tight jeans, a slice of pale midriff, long waved hair loose over her shoulders — was that particular 2012 Taeyeon: languid, luminous, the cool face with its faint fragility underneath. Genuinely, stoppingly beautiful.

She enjoyed exactly as much of his stare as dignity permitted, then waved a small hand through his eyeline.

“Are we standing in the doorway all night?”

“Nuna — this outfit. You put it together yourself? It’s perfect on you.”

“Obviously I did.” Lips pressed against a smile; pride leaking anyway.

“Your eye is genuinely elite.” He steered her to a seat. “Next time I buy clothes — will you be my consultant?”

Dress Ji-ho? Her eyes kindled. That frame was a mannequin from heaven — and besides, there was that particular proprietary pleasure every girl knew: the clothes I choose look best on you was a form of invisible tagging, the private delight of this version of him, only I know.

“When you shop, come find nuna. Consultation free of charge.”

“Thank you.” He produced the Starmix bag and presented it. “And as gratitude — your snacks are on me from now on. Standing order. Just name cravings.”

“Then I won’t hold back~”

She tore in delightedly, tucked one into her cheek, and slumped back into the chair, eyes narrowing to blissful crescents as the strawberry sweetness spread. She knew perfectly well his motives weren’t pure. She had also decided she was fine with that — she was studying him right back, after all. Next year. Maybe sooner than next year—

She tipped her head — and found his eyes fixed, unmistakably, on a specific location. She followed the gaze down.

Her stomach. Which — between the slouch and the chair — had gathered into one small, soft, entirely human fold above her waistband.

Combustion was instantaneous. She shot upright, face on fire.

“YAH! What are you LOOKING at?!”

“HAHAHAHA—”

“STOP LAUGHING!” Both hands clamped over her midsection; she scanned the room for a hole to disappear into. “Shut UP! No laughing!”

“Nuna — every girl on earth has a tummy when she sits. It’s normal—”

“DON’T SAY IT! Be QUIET!” The glare could have stripped paint. “I’ve just been too busy for the gym lately, that’s ALL—”

“Understood.” He smiled at her — and then, gently, didn’t let it go. “For the record, though? The soft little tummy is nice. I like it.”

She went scarlet from hairline to collarbone, cherry-ripe, the pink climbing down her neck.

“Who— who ASKED you to like it—”

“Can’t help it. Liked it at first sight. Soft is cute.”
