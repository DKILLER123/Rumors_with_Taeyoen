Chapter Two Hundred Fifty-Two

Kim Taeyeon: I’m Not Afraid of Lee Ji-eun—I’m Afraid of Losing You

Jeonju to Jirisan National Park a family excursion, a hidden medical concern, and the confession neither best friend knows how to survive

While Ji-ho and Jessica devoted themselves to the first framework of their business proposal, Tiffany was driving Taeyeon and her parents toward Jirisan National Park.

The road from Jeonju was long enough to settle everyone into a comfortable rhythm. Tiffany kept both hands steady on the wheel and occasionally checked the rearview mirror.

Taeyeon’s father sat beside the window, watching summer scenery stream past. Her mother leaned close every so often to point something out or share a quiet remark. Neither often allowed themselves this kind of unhurried trip, and relaxation had softened both their faces.

Taeyeon occupied the passenger seat with gentle music in her earbuds. Every few minutes, she turned to include her parents in the conversation.

Tiffany watched the family’s easy warmth and smiled despite herself.

Her bond with the Kims reached back to the hardest years of her youth in Korea. When she arrived from California, the language still unfamiliar in her mouth, her family’s financial circumstances deteriorated sharply. Holidays were the loneliest. Other trainees went home for Chuseok and Seollal; Tiffany often went to Taeyeon’s.

TRAINEE YEARS · THE KIM FAMILY HOME

Taeyeon’s mother always kept a hot bowl of tteokguk for her.

Taeyeon’s father asked whether practice was going well and whether anyone at the company had treated her badly.

No one announced that she belonged there. They simply set another place at the table until she stopped feeling like a guest.

Tiffany had never forgotten.

“Fany-ah,” Taeyeon’s mother said from the back, “isn’t the turn coming up?”

Tiffany checked the navigation display.

“Ne, eomeonim. The entrance is just ahead.”

The car entered the national park and came to rest in a shaded parking area.

Taeyeon’s father stretched beside the car and looked toward the distant peak.

“Cheonwangbong. Nineteen hundred and fifteen meters. I could climb it in one push without losing my breath.”

His wife laughed immediately.

“You stop five or six times on every mountain.”

Taeyeon and Tiffany exchanged a look and broke into matching smiles.

“Let’s take it slowly, Appa.” Taeyeon linked her arm through his. “We’re not racing anyone.”

They followed a trail beside the valley stream. Sunlight filtered through overlapping leaves and scattered itself across the path. Clear water moved between stones; the air smelled of grass, bark, and damp earth.

Taeyeon’s parents walked ahead, stopping occasionally to admire wildflowers. Tiffany and Taeyeon followed at an easy distance.

“Abeonim and eomeonim look healthy,” Tiffany said.

“They refuse proper checkups.” Taeyeon’s brow creased. “No matter how often I ask.”

Tiffany glanced at her, then let the words she had almost spoken remain unspoken.

At an open platform beside the stream, the family stopped to rest. Tiffany distributed fruit and drinks from her backpack. Then, with studied casualness, she reached beneath everything else and produced two envelopes.

“Eomeonim, these are for you and abeonim.”

Taeyeon’s mother examined them.

“Health-screening vouchers? Fany-ah, this is too much.”

“A sponsor gave them to me during an endorsement campaign,” Tiffany lied smoothly. “They’re senior packages, so I can’t use them. If you refuse, they’ll expire.”

“Even so…”

“I’ve eaten enough meals in your house to owe you several hospitals.” Tiffany slipped one envelope firmly into her hand. “If you want to thank me, make extra food tonight.”

Taeyeon’s father waved away his wife’s hesitation.

“It’s the child’s sincerity. Accept it. Waste is worse.”

Tiffany smiled in victory.

Taeyeon watched her with conflicted eyes.

The vouchers had not come from a sponsor. Tiffany had bought them herself and invented the story so her parents would accept without guilt.

If Taeyeon did not know about Tiffany and Ji-ho, the gesture would have moved her beyond words.

But she did know.

Gratitude, anger, grief, and affection crowded together until she could no longer separate one from another.

“Taeyeon?”

She returned to herself. Her parents had already risen.

“Coming.”

The family reached a temple halfway up the mountain. Taeyeon’s mother bowed before the Buddha and obtained protective charms for everyone. Later they followed another trail to a waterfall, listening while her father retold stories from his youth. The day ended at a nearby folk village over mountain-vegetable bibimbap and local chicken.

For several hours, they were simply a family enjoying summer.

✦ ✦ ✦
Jeonju · the Kim family home after everyone appears to be asleep

Tiffany waited until the house quieted, slipped into the bathroom, and called Ji-ho with her back against the door.

VOICE CALL · TIFFANY TO JI-HO

“It’s me. I came to Jeonju with Taeyeon and took her parents out for the day.”

“Jeonju? Why so suddenly?”

“I want them to have proper health screenings. They keep refusing hospitals, so I thought I could use this trip to persuade them.”

“Then ask for a cardiology consultation, not merely a standard package.”

“Cardiology? They look healthy.”

“That doesn’t rule out silent cardiovascular disease. Tell the doctor their age and family history and ask what testing is appropriate. Start with the specialist’s assessment and noninvasive tests; if something appears, the cardiologist can decide whether coronary CT angiography or invasive angiography is justified.”

“You sound unusually serious.”

“If you’re going to check, check responsibly. Some risks stay quiet until the day they don’t.”

“All right. I’ll speak to the hospital tomorrow.”

“When are you returning to Seoul?”

“In two days. I want to stay with them a little longer.”

“Good. Be careful.”

Tiffany ended the call and remained before the mirror.

She had not mentioned the confession she intended to make.

Some truths demanded a face across from you. Over the past weeks, she had begun to suspect Taeyeon already knew. That should have made speaking easier. Instead, every warm exchange between Taeyeon and her parents closed Tiffany’s throat again.

But delay would only deepen the wound.

Within the next two days. She would find the moment.

✦ ✦ ✦

Elsewhere, Ji-ho lowered his phone and sat with the future pressing heavily against the present.

Taeyeon’s father had died of an acute myocardial infarction on March 9, 2020—Taeyeon’s thirty-first birthday. Ji-ho remembered the date because cruelty that precise was difficult to forget.

A routine examination might not predict an acute event years in advance. But underlying coronary disease and significant narrowing could sometimes be identified and managed before catastrophe.

He could not know which intervention would rewrite fate. He only knew that doing nothing would preserve the future he remembered.

And Jiyeon’s father—hadn’t he suffered a serious illness as well?

The next time they met, Ji-ho would remind her to encourage a thorough, physician-directed screening.

✦ ✦ ✦
The following morning Jeonju · comprehensive hospital a precaution becomes a warning

Tiffany and Taeyeon accompanied both parents to the hospital early.

The physician reviewed the screening request and raised an eyebrow at the cardiology note.

“Does either patient have chest discomfort, shortness of breath, diabetes, or a strong family history?”

“No known symptoms,” Tiffany said. “We simply want a proper risk assessment rather than ordering tests blindly.”

The doctor nodded and adjusted the plan. Routine measurements came first, followed by a cardiology review, exercise testing where appropriate, echocardiography, and coronary imaging only after the clinical assessment supported it.

Taeyeon’s mother still protested the expense.

“Why examine so much when we feel perfectly fine?”

Tiffany linked their arms.

“Think of it as repayment for every meal I stole from your table. If everything is normal, we buy peace of mind.”

The older woman tapped Tiffany’s forehead with fond exasperation.

“This child.”

The morning passed beneath fluorescent lights and the measured rhythm of hospital corridors. Taeyeon and Tiffany waited side by side while one examination followed another.

Several times, Tiffany turned as though she meant to speak. Each time, the words retreated.

After the results, she promised herself.

The hospital called the following morning.

“Ms. Hwang? The reports are ready. One finding requires the family to return for a cardiology consultation as soon as possible.”

Color drained from Tiffany’s face.

“What finding?”

“Mr. Kim’s coronary imaging is abnormal. The physician will explain in person.”

Half an hour later, all four sat in a consultation room.

CARDIOLOGY CONSULTATION · KIM FAMILY

Early coronary artery disease detected

Routine screeningBlood pressure, standard lipids, and resting ECG without major warning signs.

Specialist findingCoronary imaging indicates moderate narrowing in the mid-left anterior descending artery, estimated in the narrative at approximately 50–60%.

Immediate meaningNot an active heart attack and not automatically an emergency procedure, but clinically important disease requiring treatment and follow-up.

ManagementCardiologist-directed medication, risk-factor control, smoking and alcohol cessation where applicable, appropriate exercise guidance, and scheduled reassessment.

Emergency planSeek urgent care for persistent chest pressure, unexplained breathlessness, cold sweating, faintness, or pain spreading to the arm, back, neck, or jaw.

Medication and procedures must be individualized by the treating physician; no single regimen is appropriate for every patient.

“The important thing is that we found it before an acute event,” the doctor said. “Coronary artery disease is chronic, but progression can often be slowed and risk substantially reduced.”

“You’ll need regular follow-up. We’ll discuss a statin and other medication according to your full risk profile. Antiplatelet therapy is not automatic for everyone; the cardiologist will decide after reviewing bleeding risk and the final diagnosis.”

“Avoid sudden extreme exertion until we complete the exercise guidance. Long term, appropriate activity is usually part of treatment—not something to abandon entirely.”

Taeyeon’s father listened in silence and eventually nodded.

“Understood.”

Outside the hospital, no one spoke at first.

Taeyeon’s mother held her husband’s hand tightly, eyes red. Taeyeon felt as though the strength had left her legs.

What if they had not come?

What if the disease progressed silently for years and announced itself only when it was too late?

Her father squeezed his wife’s fingers.

“The doctor said it was found early. I’ll take the medicine and do what I’m told. Don’t bury me while I’m standing here.”

Taeyeon looked at him with sudden severity.

“Appa, let oppa handle more of the shop. No more pretending work doesn’t tire you.”

He smiled at her determination.

“Arasseo.”

Tiffany stepped closer.

“Abeonim will be all right.”

Taeyeon turned to her. Her voice had gone hoarse.

“Thank you. Truly.”

Tiffany shook her head.

“Let’s go home first.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Back at the house, Taeyeon’s parents disappeared into the kitchen together, speaking in low voices while preparing dinner. Taeyeon watched them from the living room, emotions packed too tightly to name.

Tiffany decided the confession should wait until Seoul. Her father could not be burdened with fresh turmoil.

Taeyeon caught the look.

“Fany. Is there something you need to tell me?”

“No.”

“I know what it is.”

Tiffany went still.

“Come upstairs. We need to talk.”

Kim family rooftop · sunset the confession

Jeonju lay quiet beneath the evening sky. Distant mountains burned orange at their edges; sheets on the rooftop line moved gently in the wind. Somewhere nearby, dinner carried its warm scent into the air.

Taeyeon stood at the railing for a long time. Tiffany waited one step behind her.

“Fany. Thank you for today.”

“You don’t need to thank me.”

Taeyeon turned.

“How could I not? Without you insisting on the examination, we might never have known.”

Tiffany held her gaze.

“The heart screening wasn’t my idea.”

“What?”

“Ji-ho told me to ask for a cardiology assessment. I called him that night and mentioned the checkup. He said silent heart disease could be missed if we relied only on a basic package.”

Taeyeon stared at her.

Ji-ho.

Even from far away, he had reached into this day and changed what might happen to her family.

Tiffany watched understanding move across Taeyeon’s face and drew a shaking breath.

“There’s something else.”

Taeyeon said nothing.

Tiffany lowered herself to her knees.

Taeyeon’s eyes widened.

“Fany, what are you doing?”

“Mianhae.” Tears were already gathering. “Taeyeon, I’m sorry. Ji-ho and I…”

The sentence broke.

“I fell in love with him.”

Tears rolled down Tiffany’s face.

“I know what that makes me. I know I betrayed you.”

Her hands closed around the fabric at Taeyeon’s knees like someone clinging to the last solid thing above water.

“When he helped Sulli and me, something started. Later I kept asking him about arrangement and production. At first, I didn’t know you were together.”

“When I found out, I stopped. No calls. No meetings. Nothing.”

Taeyeon remembered that period: Tiffany’s abrupt distance, the polite greetings where laughter used to be, the way she had left rooms early.

“But I couldn’t make it disappear.” Tiffany’s shoulders shook. “I tried. I couldn’t sleep. I saw him in strangers. In Hong Kong, I drank too much, and when he came looking for you, I kissed him first.”

“I knew he loved you most. I never wanted to take your place. I only…”

She could not finish.

Taeyeon stood motionless while anger, grief, betrayal, gratitude, and the terror of the hospital room tore through one another.

She had imagined this confession. In those imagined versions, she slapped Tiffany, shouted, demanded an ending.

Reality offered her Tiffany on her knees—and the knowledge that Tiffany’s care, guided by Ji-ho’s warning, might have saved her father’s life.

The conflict hurt too much to become anger cleanly.

Taeyeon stepped back. Tiffany’s fingers slipped from her clothes.

“Taeyeon…”

She turned toward the stairs.

“I won’t steal him from you,” Tiffany called through tears. “I couldn’t, even if I tried.”

“Please… don’t make him leave me.”

Taeyeon did not look back.

She descended as if escaping a collapsing room.

Tiffany remained on the rooftop while the sun disappeared behind the mountains.

“Mianhae, Taeyeon-ah,” she whispered. “I used your kindness.”

“I never wanted your place. I only wanted somewhere I was allowed to stay.”

✦ ✦ ✦

At dinner, Taeyeon’s father tried to lift the mood.

“The doctor said we caught it early. I’ll take the medicine. Stop looking as though I’ve already gone somewhere.”

“Ne, Appa.” Taeyeon forced a smile. “But you’ll follow every instruction. Less salt, no smoking, no heavy drinking, proper sleep, and no pretending exhaustion is bravery.”

Tiffany added softly:

“Taeyeon earns enough for you to rest now, abeonim. Let her spoil you.”

He raised both hands in surrender.

“Arasseo. Eat before everything gets cold.”

Taeyeon’s childhood bedroom · late night two best friends, one fist-width apart

After washing, Taeyeon and Tiffany lay side by side with a narrow distance between them.

Neither slept.

Moonlight entered through a gap in the curtain and drew a pale line across the floor.

“Taeyeon.”

“Mm.”

“Do you remember when I first came to Korea?”

How could she forget?

THE FIRST YEARS · TAEYEON & TIFFANY

A small California girl with a round face, nervous eyes, and Korean that bent in unexpected directions.

“Annyeong haseyo. I’m Hwang Mi-young. Please take care of me.”

“I’m Kim Taeyeon.”

“Ne, Kim Dae-yeon sunbaenim.”

For a month, Tiffany called her Dae-yeon no matter how carefully Taeyeon corrected her.

In Tiffany’s Korean practice diary, Taeyeon once found a crooked sentence: DaeDae corrected me again today, but DaeDae is pretty when she smiles.

After that, DaeDae became Tiffany’s name for her alone.

“And the ‘Sukjeong sunbaenim’ incident,” Taeyeon said.

Tiffany laughed despite the tears still hiding in her voice.

“Don’t. It was humiliating.”

Years ago, a notice reading Sukjeong—Silence—had hung outside a practice room. Tiffany and Jessica, still learning Korean, became convinced it was the name of an extraordinarily diligent senior who practiced there every day.

They finally asked an instructor why Sukjeong sunbaenim never came out.

The instructor laughed until he could not stand straight.

As Tiffany’s self-appointed Korean teacher, Taeyeon felt personally disgraced and made her write the word one hundred times. When Tiffany finished with an aching hand and wounded eyes, Taeyeon’s resolve collapsed.

Then · The Sign on the Practice-Room Door

There had been a strip of paper taped up by the practice room, printed with two large solemn characters: Sukjeong — “Silence.”

Tiffany and Sica had studied it at length, deeply troubled. Who was this Sukjeong? How was she in the practice room every single day without fail — and how had they never once seen her?

“She must be an incredible sunbae,” Tiffany had said to Sica, in deadly earnest.

“Maybe. Practicing every day like that. So diligent.”

They had even gone and asked a teacher, formally: “Is Sukjeong sunbae-nim not in today?” The teacher had stared at them for a full five seconds and then folded over, laughing so hard she had to hold the piano.

When word reached Tae-yeon — caught somewhere between rage and helpless laughter, and, as Tiffany’s self-appointed Korean teacher, personally dishonored — she grabbed her by the collar and sentenced her: write the two characters one hundred times.

“Korean is just too hard,” Tiffany had muttered, writing.

“And after?” Tae-yeon asked, into the dark.

“I finished. All hundred.” A beat. “…And then you took me out for tteokbokki.”

Tae-yeon said nothing.

She remembered it exactly. Tiffany’s hand worn to a claw, her eyes wet and wronged, looking up. Tae-yeon had meant to punish the little fool — and then those eyes had got hold of her, and her heart had folded like wet paper.

“Come on. Tteokbokki. My treat.”

And Tiffany had lit up like a struck match — eyes bent into crescents, hanging off her arm: “DaeDae is the best!”

It was so good, then. The thought went through Tae-yeon, and left sourness behind it.

“And the fan cafe,” Tiffany said again, very softly. “When I tried to join your personal fan site. And got rejected.”

“Whose fault was that?” Tae-yeon snorted. “You got the posting format wrong.”

“How was I supposed to know it had to be ‘Kim Tae-yeon Hwaiting!’ — seven syllables?” Wronged, fifteen years late. “I wrote six. The admin bounced me on the spot. My Korean was a disaster already, and they were so strict—”

Tae-yeon remembered her marching over with the phone, thrusting the rejection notice at her, muttering what the heck, what the heck. Half furious, half charmed. You little fool. If you wanted in, you only had to say it to my face. Why go sneaking through the message board?

“And then you got angry and built your own, didn’t you?”

Tiffany went quiet.

She had. A site that existed for one purpose only: Kim Tae-yeon’s photographs. No camera phones worth anything in those days — so a camera, then the photos onto a computer, then up onto the site, one by one, in the dark of the dorm after lights-out.

Tae-yeon knew that site. It had gone quiet after debut. It had never been taken down.

“Do you remember Sulli?” Tiffany asked, suddenly.

Tae-yeon’s heart drew tight, once.

“I remember.”

Sulli. The tiny, milk-white kid — eleven years old the day she walked into the company. They’d put the three of them in one dorm room: Tae-yeon the mother, Tiffany the big sister. Sulli homesick, crying into her pillow with the discipline of a child who didn’t want to be heard — and Tae-yeon holding her, patting her back in the dark, while Tiffany told jokes an arm’s length away. Told the Kim Dae-yeon story for the hundredth time, worse each time, until Sulli was laughing into the tears.

“The three of us, back then…” Tiffany’s voice clogged. “It was so good.”

Tae-yeon said nothing.

It had been good. It had been, genuinely, good.

Then · Everything At Once

Worn out from training, back at the dorm, Tae-yeon would climb into Tiffany’s bed uninvited and nuzzle into her shoulder like a cat claiming a chair. Tiffany complained every single time — “you’re like a cat” — and indulged her every single time, sometimes with a hand in her hair.

Midnight hunger: the two of them slipping out for tteokbokki. Caught, and blaming each other instantly — “You wanted to come out!” “It was you!” — then sentenced to scrub the practice room together, and laughing the whole way through the sentence.

Dance until their legs shook; they’d knead each other’s calves on the floor. Singing until the voices frayed; they’d pass one bottle back and forth until it was gone.

When Tiffany’s pronunciation slipped, Tae-yeon drilled the syllable until it sat right. Tiffany would puff her cheeks — too hard, too hard — and Tae-yeon would fold, every time. Tomorrow, then.

When Tiffany cried under her blanket for home, Tae-yeon went in under it with her, fitted herself along her back, and sang — of all things — a song from her hometown in America. “How do you know that one?” “Learned it in secret.” And Tiffany had cried twice as hard, and Tae-yeon had cried too, and the two of them had cried themselves into one wet, hopeless knot.

When Tae-yeon cracked under pressure, Tiffany did faces, did jokes, dragged her out walking, bought her ice cream. Once — one time — Tae-yeon shattered after a vocal run and hid in the stairwell, and Tiffany found her and said nothing at all. Sat down beside her on the step. Waited out the whole storm. And when it was over, held out a single candy.

“Is it sweet?”

“Mm. Strawberry.”

And Tae-yeon had held it in her mouth and discovered that it was true — it hurt a little less.

That is why, to this day, Kim Tae-yeon loves strawberries.

Shopping, too. Tiffany chose; Tae-yeon paid and carried. Tiffany would eye something, glance back — Tae-yeon would nod, and reach for the wallet. The members teased them for years: Tae-yeon is Mi-young’s personal purse. Tiffany only laughed and took her arm — “Obviously. DaeDae is the best.”

Whatever she wanted to eat, Tiffany bought. Wherever she wanted to go, Tiffany went. Whatever she said, Tiffany listened. Whatever she did, Tiffany stood behind her.

From trainee days until now. Ten-odd years.

Ten. Whole. Years.

A tear slipped from the corner of Taeyeon’s eye and disappeared into the pillow.

“Fany.”

“Mm?”

“We were so good to each other.” Her voice was barely more than breath. “How did one man turn us into this?”

The room went completely still.

Tiffany’s breathing broke first.

She turned, eyes already wet, and reached toward Taeyeon before stopping halfway.

“Mianhae.”

The apology collapsed into sobbing. Tiffany crossed the small distance and wrapped both arms around her, face buried against Taeyeon’s shoulder.

Taeyeon’s body went rigid.

Then, slowly, the resistance left it.

“I tried to forget him,” Tiffany cried. “I’m sorry. I tried, and I couldn’t. I regret hurting you. I regret that night in Hong Kong.”

Taeyeon closed her eyes.

She understood the distinction Tiffany could not say aloud: she regretted the betrayal, not the love itself. If she regretted loving Ji-ho, she would have said the relationship was over.

She had not.

“You can hit me. Curse me. Just don’t stop speaking to me.” Tiffany clung tighter. “I can’t bear you disappearing from my life.”

Did Taeyeon hate her?

Part of her did.

But beneath Tiffany’s shaking arms, thirteen years rose against the anger: dorm rooms, trainee evaluations, whispered jokes, shared food, black oceans, first wins, and nights when one of them had kept the other alive without ever using those words.

“Why did it have to be you, Fany?” Taeyeon’s voice broke. “Why you?”

Tiffany cried harder.

Taeyeon’s hand lifted and came to rest on her back.

She did not know whether it was comfort, surrender, or simply an old reflex stronger than betrayal.

“What am I supposed to do with the two of you?”

For a long time, neither had an answer.

Then Tiffany spoke into the damp fabric at Taeyeon’s shoulder.

“What if… we stop fighting each other?”

Taeyeon pulled back enough to stare at her.

“Yah. Have you lost your mind?”

“DaeDae, Ji-ho isn’t a man one person can control.” Tiffany’s voice was fragile but urgent. “Your real danger isn’t me. It’s Ji-eun—and every woman who wants the place you have.”

“I won’t take that place from you. I’ll help you protect it. I’m still your best friend.”

“Stop talking.”

“You love him. That’s why this hurts so much. I love him too.” Tiffany held on as if Taeyeon might vanish. “If we turn on each other, someone closer to him every day will step between us.”

“I said stop.” Taeyeon wiped her face with one hand. “Let me think.”

Tiffany finally fell silent.

Taeyeon stared into the darkness.

Lee Ji-eun was not what frightened her most.

Losing Ji-ho terrified her.

But losing Tiffany—the girl who had grown beside her, slept beside her, failed Korean beside her, and held her through every version of herself—felt like losing part of the life that had made Kim Taeyeon who she was.

Fany-ah, I’m not afraid of Ji-eun.

I’m afraid of losing you.

Let me think. Just a little longer.
