Chapter Fifty-Five

Hearts in Motion

“Ji-ho-ssi! Hi — I’m Mark Lee!”

“Ne, hello.”

“That song was SERIOUSLY incredible! Oh — right—” a bright-faced boy with bangs pushed in, beaming, “—Ji-ho-ssi, I’m Seo Young-ho!”

“Hello. Lee Tae-yong.”

The judges had barely cleared the doors before the trainee swarm closed in — a wall of eager greetings from every direction, dense enough that extraction was physically impossible.

At the edge of the hall, Wendy folded her arms and watched the mob scene with open indignation.

“Look at them. LOOK at them. Couldn’t spare him a hello for a month, and now they’re climbing over each other. Absolutely shameless—”

“Seung-wan!” Joo-hyun’s face changed on a dime; her eyes swept the surroundings fast, and her voice dropped to dead-serious registers. “You cannot say things like that here. You’re a trainee. Words travel.”

The severity startled her — but she knew protective when she heard it.

“…Understood, eonni.”

The sternness melted; a hand ruffled Seung-wan’s hair gently.

“Come on. He’s got our bet on his mind — he’ll come find us soon enough.”

One last glance at the human wall around Ji-ho confirmed the logic, and Seung-wan trotted off after Joo-hyun and Seul-gi.

Ji-ho, for his part, had zero appetite for weathervane friendships. He worked the circle with polite efficiency, manufactured an excuse, and slipped free — because there was somewhere he actually wanted to be.

The arrangement of “A Boy” owed Taeyeon real debts, and now the song had taken first place. The news was burning a hole in his chest, and there was exactly one person he wanted to hand it to first — in person, watching her face when it landed.

Out of the performance hall, phone out, her number dialed from memory.

“Hello?”

“Nuna.”

“Mm.”

“Where are you right now?”

“Company practice room. Why?”

“Are you busy? I have good news.”

“The evaluation? How did it go?!”

“I want to tell you in person.”

Something soft collided, gently, with the inside of Taeyeon’s chest. She was quiet for a beat.

“…Then come over.”

“Okay. Wait for me.”

She hung up and stood there slightly unmoored. His first thought — the FIRST person he wanted to tell — was me. The feeling of being someone’s first call spread through her like warm honey, and she was entirely unequipped to be annoyed about it.

“Yah — look, look.” Choi Soo-young nudged Yoon-a’s shoulder, voice dropping. “Taeyeon’s spacing out AGAIN.”

Yoon-a tilted her head, big eyes narrowing to investigative slits.

“Something’s wrong with her. Definitely. Last night she was lying in bed grinning at the CEILING. This morning she laughed out loud while brushing her teeth. There is a CASE here.”

“Could it be… dating?” Sunny slid in like a small fox that had smelled something interesting — she’d been running her own surveillance for days.

“No way.” Yuri leaned in, curious. “The dating ban doesn’t lift until next year.”

“So what?” Choi Soo-young — who had recently met a certain Jung Kyung-ho through church and was very much enjoying the early returns — floated the trial balloon. “Even if someone dated, would the company necessarily know? A woman’s youth is only so many years. Don’t tell me you’re ALL planning to sit obediently single until next year.”

Glances ricocheted around the circle. Nobody answered — which was, of course, an answer. Everyone had a private file open on this subject. Dating would happen; nobody intended to grow old alone on schedule. But standards were standards: love wasn’t marriage, but the face, at minimum, was non-negotiable.

Sprawled comfortably on the floor, Jessica watched the whole daydreaming circle with a light, unbothered scoff. Look at them — heads full of romance. Not me. Career first. An idol eats off her youth, and that meal doesn’t last forever.

Ji-ho pocketed the phone and set off for the new building at a pace just short of undignified. Ten-odd minutes on foot — and then he was standing in front of it: the tower where the pre-debut teams and the debuted artists lived their working lives.

Soon. Through the front door, with my name on the roster.

Girls’ Generation had a performance tomorrow at the LG 3D festival, so the full group was in — deep in “The Boys,” running it wall to wall. He arrived at their practice-room door and leaned quietly against the frame, watching the run-through.

A chart-topper for a reason: full American hip-hop swagger, and nine dancers cutting through it sharp and clean — the turns, the hip work, the lines. …Ahem. The “thigh generation” reputation: thoroughly earned. Respectfully noted.

The run ended.

Clap-clap-clap-clap.

“A feast for the eyes AND ears. Girls’ Generation, ladies and gentlemen.” He walked in applauding.

“Yah — Ji-ho?!” Tiffany lit up on sight. “What are you doing here?”

“Hello, nunas. I’ve got some business with Taeyeon nuna — called ahead, she told me to come.”

“Hold on.” Choi Soo-young’s eyes went knife-sharp in a single frame. “That call just now. That was YOU?”

He blinked at the sudden voltage, but honesty seemed safest.

“Ne?”

Yoon-a, Soo-young, Yu-ri, and Sunny exchanged one single glance, and the verdict passed between them wordlessly, unanimously: CASE CLOSED. The man responsible for the ceiling-grinning, the toothbrush-laughing, the chronic spacing-out — LOCATED.

The four of them closed in like a friendly street gang and commenced inspection, up and down, item by item.

Height: roughly 182. Pass.

Face: sword brows, starlit eyes, precision features. Pass — honestly, overqualified.

Styling and proportions: pass and pass.

Then Soo-young remembered the word “trainee,” and shook her head slowly. Major deduction. Yoon-a and Sunny ran the age math — nineteen, Korean reckoning — and shook theirs too. A nuna-dongsaeng romance… tricky. Tricky.

Yuri, meanwhile, had both eyes shining like festival lanterns. Taeyeon-ah, you found yourself a handsome young WOLFHOUND?! Unfair. UNFAIR. I want a handsome wolfhound too…
