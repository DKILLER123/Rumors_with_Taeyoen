Chapter Three

Achievement Quest: Win the Fans

Knowledge crashed into Ji-ho’s mind like a tide breaking through a seawall.

Refined vowel placement. Breath control and support. Chest, head, and mixed resonance; consonant articulation; the thousand small crafts of a trained voice — every principle of vocal technique snapped into his awareness with perfect clarity. And alongside it, Korean settled into him like rain into dry earth: not the kitchen-table Korean his grandmother had given him, patched and heartfelt, but the whole language — every honorific level, every idiom, every soft Seoul vowel — flawless and native, as if he’d never lived a day anywhere else.

Twenty points of Vocal. The God of Learning. Perfect Korean.

His heart slammed against his ribs; excitement roared up through him like a struck match hitting gasoline.

Breathe. Stay calm. Don’t let it carry you off.

He closed his eyes and pulled in slow lungfuls of alley air until the shaking stopped. Fifty-four had been his raw, untrained baseline at this age. The extra twenty was clearly his future — a decade of smoky lounge nights, converted and credited back to him. Vocal: 74. That figure was already beyond plenty of main vocalists who had debuted years ago.

Add this face on top, and even with his dancing still an honest disaster, he had every confidence he could survive SM’s notoriously merciless trainee evaluation.

The dream that had taken root in his teens had tangled around him for twelve years — a knot he’d never once managed to cut loose. This time, Ji-ho swore it to himself: whatever stood in the road, he would see this through to the end.

✦   ✦   ✦

Apgujeong. The slightly weathered building on the corner was SM’s old headquarters — the SM Celebrity Center. The gleaming new SM Studio Center stood over in Cheongdam-dong. By custom, trainee auditions and early training happened here in the old building; only those who survived evaluation and made a pre-debut team earned the move to the new one.

Ding— “Achievement quest triggered: [Win the Fans]. Check the Quest Hall for details.”

Ji-ho paused mid-step. Right — beyond the main line, the game loved springing side quests, and their rewards tended to be generous. He pulled up the details at once.

Achievement Quest · Win the Fans

Objective: You have 116 subscribers on MeTube. Fans are the bedrock of any artist’s career. Upload consistently, treat your supporters well, and grow your true fanbase to 1,000.

Reward: Second social-platform fan slot unlocked  +  Ab Trainer Board ×1

Current fan count116

He tapped open the item description.

Item · Ab Trainer Board

Precisely isolates the abdominal chain, protects the lower back, corrects form, and slashes injury risk while multiplying training intensity. Three months of consistent use: clearly defined abs, guaranteed.

An ab board! Now that’s a proper piece of loot. He was lean, sure — but abs? At present count: one. And for anyone planning to stand on an idol stage, a flawless body was half the arsenal. He wanted that reward. Badly.

Ji-ho pulled out his phone and opened MeTube.

His channel was small the way a shoebox is small: a dozen-odd videos, all shaky recordings of himself learning choreography in a garage back in LA. The face had done its work — 923 casual subscribers — but most had clicked out of idle curiosity and moved on. His actual fans, the ones who came back, numbered 116.

The latest upload was the one he remembered too well: the video where he’d looked into the lens and sworn, with all the flammable confidence of eighteen, that he was flying to Korea to become an idol trainee. Five hundred twenty-three views. A hundred likes. A dozen-plus comments.

MeTube · Comments (14)

#1 Bro. Please think this through. “Idol trainee” is not a real career path 💀

#2 Fanboying rotted your brain fr. You really think you’ve got it? What would your parents say?

#3 Wait, why NOT though? With that face?? Dude, I’m rooting for you!!

#4 Subscribed for the follow-up. Do NOT leave us hanging…

Watching his own teenage bravado on loop, Ji-ho winced. He remembered exactly how this had gone the first time: after posting it, he’d bombed more than ten auditions in a row, been too humiliated to face the channel, and deleted the app entirely.

But fate had dealt the hand again. This time he intended to play it.

He flipped the camera on. Video quality in this era was nothing to write home about — 480p and 720p ruled the platform — but nobody knew what they were missing yet, and honestly, it was fine. He framed up the SM building first.

“Guys. I told you I’d do it, and here we are — this is the headquarters of the biggest entertainment company in Korea right now. SM Entertainment.”

The lens swung around to his own face, and he let the grin come out — the confident one.

“I’m walking in there right now to audition as a trainee. Wait for my good news.”

He skimmed the playback once, found nothing worth reshooting, and hit upload. The moment the little “processing” wheel appeared, he pocketed the phone and walked toward the SM building.

✦   ✦   ✦

“Hello — I’m here for the trainee audition.”

The Korean rolled off his tongue smooth as river stone, native in every syllable. Somewhere behind his sternum, something quietly marveled at it.

The receptionist answered with practiced courtesy.

“Of course. May I have your name? Do you have an appointment?”

“Ji-ho. I have an appointment.”

“Ji-ho…” She scanned the booking list and found him quickly. “Yes, here you are. Your passport, please.”

He handed over the navy-blue American passport. She checked it, made a brief call to the staffer assigned to him, then returned it along with a temporary visitor’s badge.

“Ji-mi, take him down to the audition room on B1.”

“Yes. This way, please.”

Ji-ho followed the young woman named Ji-mi toward the audition room, stealing curious glances at the interior of the famous “star factory” as they went. The building wore its age on the outside, but inside everything was orderly — and crowded, mostly with kids in their mid-teens, every last one of them startlingly good-looking. Prospective trainees, all of them.

“Here we are. Please go in.” Ji-mi knocked lightly and gestured him forward.

“Thank you.”

“Come in.” A deep male voice, from inside.

Ji-ho straightened his collar, set his breathing, and pushed open the door — calm as still water.

Inside, a boy of fourteen or fifteen was just stepping up for his turn. Ji-ho took a spot along the wall and waited.

The boy folded into a deep, reverent ninety-degree bow.

“Hello, teachers. My name is Song Ji-yong.”
