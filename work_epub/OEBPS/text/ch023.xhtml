Chapter Twenty-Three

Sulli’s Family

As the melody climbed from hush toward soar, Ji-ho let tenderness do the work force never could — rendering the flutter and the free-fall of love with such fine control that the song seemed to wrap gently around every listener in the room and pull. No theatrics. No strain. Just an invisible tide, and everyone in it.

Up along the second-floor gallery, a girl drifted out of her booth as if hooked by the collarbone, folded her arms on the railing, and stared down at the singer, eyes shining.

“Hyo-yeon eonni — come LISTEN to this!”

Leaning on the rail with a drink in hand, Kim Hyo-yeon strolled over at the summons, narrowed her eyes, and gave the voice a long professional listen. Her hand found her chin.

“Wow. That’s trained. That’s seriously trained.”

“Right?!” Sulli’s eyes were bright as wet glass. “Isn’t it the most beautiful thing?”

A luminous young idol whose fearless affection and vulnerable honesty make her impossible to overlook.

A handsome man wandered over to the railing, weighed the singer below with a practiced glance.

“Voice like that, face like that — trainee, guaranteed. Someone’s company is sitting on a gold bar.” A pause. “By the way, Hyo-yeon — when’s Tiffany getting here?”

“Fany’s almost here. Why so impatient, Nichkhun? You’re really doing this, huh — really going after her?”

Nichkhun just smiled and declined to answer — which was an answer. Tiffany: sweet-faced, gorgeous, and a member of Girls’ Generation at the summit of their fame. Any man with a pulse would look twice. He was simply honest enough with himself to keep looking.

“Sulli-yah! What are you staring at? Come back — your glass is getting lonely.”

The man ambling over looked thirty-something, with a wide, doughy face that never quite agreed with its own features — Choi Jae, a rapper with a modest name in hip-hop circles and a talent for being wherever young idols drank. His gaze slid across Nichkhun and Hyo-yeon as he came.

“Hyo-yeon, Nichkhun — don’t tell me the tolerance is going soft already?”

Nichkhun looked at Choi Jae — and at the heavily tattooed man loitering at his shoulder, the kind of company that announced itself — and felt a familiar distaste rise. He knew the type; he knew the reputation. Choi Jae’s corner of the scene did not play clean. Nichkhun liked a good time as much as anyone, but he kept his lines where he could see them — and for the life of him he couldn’t fathom why Kim Hyo-yeon ran with people like this. Still. Tonight he needed Hyo-yeon’s bridge to Tiffany, so the thought stayed behind his teeth. Once he and Tiffany were properly acquainted, the bridge could retire.

“Sunbae — we’re only warming up.” The smile he produced was technically a smile.

“Ha! Good, good — drink freely, everyone, you’re all Sulli’s seniors here! Tonight’s on me!”

And with that, Choi Jae’s hand closed around Sulli’s wrist.

Her face changed — a flash of something cornered — and she twisted free.

For half a second his expression flickered ugly. Then the smile was back, smooth as poured wax, and he shepherded her toward the booth without touching her again, playing the gentleman at exactly arm’s length. He raised his glass across the table.

“Sulli-yah. A toast — to you.”

“Thank you, oppa.”

She lifted her glass obediently and drank it straight down. The alcohol bit; her brows knitted; a flush bloomed across that pale face like a peach blossom forced open in the wrong season — and she was, unfairly, all the lovelier for it.

Watching her, Choi Jae’s throat moved. He wet his lips. Then, catching her wince, he snapped on concern like a lens filter.

“Sulli-yah, easy — are you okay? That one’s strong. Want me to switch you to something fruity?”

Warmth rose in her chest, unbidden. He wasn’t much to look at, oppa, but this — this gentleness, this attention — it landed somewhere old and hungry.

“It’s fine, oppa. Really.”

And then — because she had just refused him, and refusal was dangerous, refusal made people leave — she rose quickly, took up the bottle, and refilled his glass with both hands, every motion arranged to please.

The corner of Choi Jae’s mouth curled, a flicker of triumph he didn’t fully hide. By every law of the industry, a man like him should never have gotten within a postcode of Choi Jin-ri — Sulli, the idol whose face half the country called perfect.

But he had done his homework on her.

Her family had come apart when she was seven — the divorce, the door closing. At eleven, she’d walked into SM as a child trainee. Uncounted nights of exhaustion she had cried through alone, clutching the stuffed doll her mother gave her, hugging it hard enough to stand in for everyone who wasn’t there. A childhood like that builds a particular architecture: the pleaser’s reflex, the constant weather-eye on other people’s moods, the bone-deep certainty that one wrong move means abandonment. A father-shaped hole. A mother’s indifference laid over it like frost.

She had never once been loved in a way she could keep. And the company was no refuge — executives steering her to drinking engagements she couldn’t refuse, while online, the malicious comments fell like weather, day after day after day. Under the double weight, something in her had already begun to crack. The depression was quiet, and it was real, and it was getting worse.

Choi Jae had seen all of it — and moved in through the gap. An introduction through Kim Hyo-yeon. The gentle-gentleman costume, fitted and pressed. Attention, warmth, futures painted in soft colors for a girl standing at the edge of her own despair. They weren’t officially together — but her gratitude had already deepened into trust, and her trust into dependence.

Only one door stayed shut. Her father’s leaving had left her flinching from touch — any touch, every escalation. And Choi Jae was getting tired of waiting.

The booth door swung open. Tiffany hurried in, already apologizing.

“Hyo-yeon, sorry I’m late—” The sentence died as she registered the room: not just Hyo-yeon. An audience.

“Tiffany sunbae-nim!”

“Tiffany-ssi, hello, hello.”

Sulli, Choi Jae, and Nichkhun rose in a ragged chorus of greeting. Hyo-yeon crossed the room laughing, caught Tiffany’s hand, and towed her to a seat.

“Fany-yah, come, come — we were only waiting on you.”

“Hi, everyone…” The smile held while she leaned into Hyo-yeon’s ear, voice dropping to a hiss. “What exactly is this?”

“Just drinks, just fun.” Hyo-yeon’s whisper came back conspiratorial. “Nichkhun’s interested in you. He asked me to set the table.”

“Mwo? YAH!”

“Volume.” Hyo-yeon squeezed her hand, all mischief. “Look — if you think he’s interesting, explore a little. The dating ban’s basically expired anyway; be a bit careful and it’s fine. And if he bores you? Congratulations, you drank for free.”
