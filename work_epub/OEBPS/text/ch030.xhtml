Chapter Thirty

Famous

Ji-ho laid the night out end to end — the storeroom, the recording, Sulli seizing the phone and marching into the confrontation, the tattooed man’s violence, Choi Jae’s collapse, the gallery of crimes — every detail in its place, nothing softened, nothing embellished.

From the head seat, Lee Soo-man’s fingertip kept slow time against the table. When it was finished, his eyes moved down the row.

“Anything to add?”

Sulli shook her head, eyes rimmed red. Hyo-yeon and Tiffany confirmed the account in turn. Nichkhun managed a silent nod — tonight had left him nothing worth saying out loud.

“Directors. Thoughts?”

Kim Young-min adjusted his glasses.

“SM protects its artists. That has always been the company’s line, and tonight we prove it. Legal—” his voice hardened mid-sentence, “—first thing tomorrow: report to the police, hand over every shred of evidence in a bound file, and sue Choi Jae formally. Call in every relationship we have. I want that animal buried to the full depth of the law — and I want the entire industry to hear the shovel. Anyone who touches an SM artist learns the price. Publicly.”

“Understood. I know exactly how to proceed.” The legal director was already on his feet.

Park Jin-young’s worry ran along a different track — 2PM was mid-ascent, and Nichkhun’s showing tonight had been… survivable, at cost. Something would have to be traded to keep his image intact.

“The blast radius on this will not be small. Our two houses coordinate — the statements, the narrative steering, all of it. Watertight or nothing.”

Behind his glasses, Kim Young-min’s mind was already three moves ahead. SM had a colossal bet riding on the Chinese market: EXO, the group they’d spent a fortune building — eight Korean members, three Chinese, one Chinese-Canadian, twenty-three debut teasers and counting — was weeks from launch. And Girls’ Generation’s new sub-unit TaeTiSeo sat nocked on the bowstring right behind them. A scandal at this exact moment was a hazard — or, handled with sufficient artistry, an asset.

He straightened, addressing the artists along the wall.

“From this moment: not one word outside this room without company clearance. Silence is the only correct answer.”

“Tiffany. Kim Hyo-yeon. You’re being admitted to hospital tonight — and understand me, your injuries are serious, and you require complete rest.”

“Sulli.” His tone actually gentled. “The company will not let you carry this unfairly. Hospital for you as well — and a psychiatrist, a proper one. Rest as long as you need.”

Finally his gaze settled on Ji-ho — and lingered. He’d read this trainee’s file: vocals rated exceptional, visuals well above line, high-potential asset.

“Ji-ho. You did well tonight. The company will remember its debts.”

“All right. Everyone out.”

Staff whisked Tiffany, Hyo-yeon, and Sulli off to the hospital. Park Jin-young drew Nichkhun aside with the expression of a man about to conduct surgery without anesthetic.

Only when the corridor was empty did Ji-ho let the long breath out. His palms were damp — not fear. Voltage.

Sulli’s road just forked away from the cliff. And I’m now a name in Lee Soo-man’s head and Kim Young-min’s ledger. The stone landed alive.

The map forward drew itself: Sulli’s gratitude was banked — and her circle happened to include IU and Goo Hara, connections most rookies would trade years for. Tiffany of Girls’ Generation now owed him an evening — top-shelf networking by any measure. The memory of the piggyback ride surfaced — warm curves through thin fabric, jasmine — and the corner of his mouth crept up before he caught it.
