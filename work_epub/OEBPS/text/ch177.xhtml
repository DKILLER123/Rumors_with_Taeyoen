In the other car, Sulli’s manager glanced sideways at the girl fiddling with her replacement phone, hesitated the full length of a block, and finally lowered her voice.

“Sulli-yah. That… should I stop at a pharmacy? For the — morning-after kind. Just in case.”

“MWO?!” The blush detonated instantly, apple-ripe, hands flapping. “N-no! Not needed!”

Eonni had it entirely wrong — nothing that blush-worthy had happened.

But there was no explaining it. Any accurate account would be believed by exactly no one.

The manager, reading the scarlet face as confirmation that the two had at least been sensible about precautions, sighed and softened. “Eonni worries, that’s all. You’re young, you’re rising — some things demand care. If anything ever does happen, you come to me first. The company side… I’ll find a way to manage it.”

The genuine concern in it landed. Sulli’s flush eased; she turned serious. “Eonni. Thank you, really. But Ji-ho Oppa and I aren’t — that. Going forward we’re just very close — brother and sister. Mm. That’s all.”

“Understood. As long as you know your own lines. Just — don’t get photographed doing anything that needs a press release, yes?”

“I know, I know. I’ll be careful~”

Back at the dorm, the first order of business was a long hot bath — road fatigue and the last stubborn molecule of sea-chill dissolved together. She emerged wrapped in a soft robe, tore open a red-ginseng pouch, and folded herself into the sofa corner, sipping.

The newest member of the household — the white kitten she’d named Lucky — sat tucked in the opposite corner, amber eyes running a full security audit of the room.

“Lucky-yah. Come here.”

The kitten deliberated, then padded over and permitted itself to be gathered in. She stroked the soft fur and her mind drifted, entirely without permission, back to Gapado — wave-sound, bonfire, and a warmth she could still feel against her cheek if she was careless.

“Myau~”

“Okay, okay. I’m here.”

She was leaning down to bump foreheads when the new phone rang. The screen said: Goo Ha-ra.

“Eonni! I’m back in Seoul, just got to the dorm— …eonni?”

The background on the other end was silent, and the voice that finally came was low, sanded raw, thick through the nose — a voice that had been crying for a long time.

“Sulli-yah… good. You’re back.”

She sat up straight, kitten and all. “Eonni, what’s wrong? Your voice — are you sick?”

Several seconds of silence. Then, pressed flat to keep from breaking:

“Jun-hyung and I… we broke up.”

“MWO?!” She nearly came off the sofa; Lucky bailed out in protest. “Why?? Eonni — you two were good! His father adored you publicly! How does it suddenly—”

Ha-ra sniffed and gave the short version. Schedules colliding. Meetings thinning out. Conversations misfiring. The feeling of drifting further every week — the entertainment industry’s single most common cause of death for a relationship.

“Eonni, where are you right now? Home? I’m coming over. NOW.”

“Mm… home.”

“Wait for me. I’ll be quick.”

She hung up with her chest in a knot. Hara and Yong Jun-hyung: spring 2011, the spark; June, Dispatch’s hand-holding photos; both agencies confirming with unusual grace. After that they’d never hidden it — his father had said warm things about her on the record, and the fan-named couple had carried one of the industry’s biggest cheering sections. THAT relationship — the one everyone rated stormproof — had lost to four plain syllables: too little time together.

She thought of Ha-ra buying a house near his agency just to shave minutes off the distance. Of the light in her eyes whenever the topic was him. And of Ji-eun’s old verdict, delivered with that cool tilt of the head: “Ha-ra eonni gives too much of herself. Love matters, but YOU matter more. Weigh things. Guard your own heart and your own future — because when it ends, the one who poured in more is the one who bleeds more.”

Back then, she and Ha-ra had both thought Ji-eun too calculating — cold, even. Wasn’t love supposed to be total, unreserved? If you’re auditing the ledger, is it still love?

But now—

Now she thought of her own Oppa. A certified philanderer — and yet what he gave her was real, and burning, and when danger came he had put his body between her and it without one second’s arithmetic. Put his LIFE on the wrong side of the equation, twice.

So was that kind of giving wrong?

No. She hadn’t been wrong. Ha-ra eonni hadn’t been wrong. Loving with everything isn’t the error. The error is timing, or luck, or — the person. And Ji-eun’s way was just armor of a different alloy. No right answer. Only choices.

She shook the philosophy off — the priority right now was a heartbroken eonni. She changed fast, then crouched by the kitten shadowing her ankles.

“Lucky-yah, come somewhere with me? I have a very precious eonni and she’s very sad right now. You’re this cute — if you keep her company, maybe her heart hurts a little less. Deal?”

“Myau~” A small head bumped her palm.

“You agreed! Good kitten!”

Into the carrier, bag over shoulder, out the door at speed.

He smacked his forehead. “Ah — right. That nearly slipped the queue.” A glance at the calendar; a beat of thought. “Today’s for catching up. Tomorrow morning I’ll go to Cube directly and record them through.”

“Good. I’ll confirm the slot with Cube now.”

Si-won, efficient as ever, was gone on the word. The office went quiet — and he leaned back, finally free to open the reward that finishing the film had banked.

Finally.

The directing ability he’d been circling for a year — and at the advanced tier. It meant he was no longer merely an actor or a producer of music: he now held the core capital for independent screen authorship. Standard idol dramas, commercial features — comfortably within range.

Two thoughts arrived instantly, both smiling. If Sulli ever wanted a drama, he could build one to her measurements personally. And there was a certain actress affectionately nicknamed “the ratings poison” who had never once stopped dreaming of being taken seriously.

Yoon-a nuna. You don’t want to keep being called box-office poison either… right?

Recurring quests.

Meaning: keep creating, and every qualifying work pays out another rung of the craft. A growth track built to his exact shape. The bars weren’t low — a 20% average in this market, five million admissions against this competition, both demanded real muscle and real luck. Which was, of course, exactly what made it fun.

Reply 1988. Train to Busan.

The warmest summit K-drama ever climbed — and the film that lit the zombie genre across all of Asia, commerce and acclaim in one body. His pulse actually picked up. This rotation’s luck bordered on indecent. Shot properly, either one became a defining work.

Then the cold math, right behind the thrill: his newly minted advanced craft could probably not yet do full justice to Reply 1988’s long, fine-grained, era-soaked storytelling — nor to Train to Busan’s knife-tension riding on top of real human depth. Classic scripts deserved classic execution. He refused to waste them.

So: apprentice pieces first. Clear the recurring quests, level the craft, then take the two peaks.

The path organized itself in a minute flat. His first life’s memory was a warehouse of proven ratings hits and box-office winners; reconstructing a few as training grounds was entirely feasible. Build experience, build reputation, build self — then climb.

Last: the two upgrade cards. He didn’t deliberate for even a second. Both went into the Boyfriend Halo.

DREAM LIFE — AURA UPGRADED

⟨ DING ⟩ Boyfriend Halo → LV3

FIELDWhen you and a partner share deep sleep in one bed, your life-rhythms resonate gently, forming a small nourishing field.

His fist actually pumped. The timing could not have been better. Every one of them — Taeyeon deep in the grind of a decade-long career, Joo-hyun mid-trainee-hell, Sulli fresh off a film shoot and a near-drowning — carried an idol’s standard inventory of hidden damage: knees, lower backs, ankles, the quiet tariffs the industry collects from every body it employs. “Vital Cultivation” was a slow medicine, not a miracle — but compounding nightly, it was exactly the kind of repair none of them could buy anywhere else.

And somewhere in there, the faint silliness of the ability’s name got replaced by something that felt uncomfortably like responsibility. This, too, was a form of protection. He wasn’t fickle, he informed himself, with a straight face. He simply ached for what their dreams were costing their bodies.

Of course, new effects also demanded… rigorous testing. And he missed his girlfriend. Both things could be true.

Taeyeon was still in Japan. Which meant—

He stood, grabbed the car keys, and went home to change into something maximally forgettable. Irene was about to get a surprise.

SM Entertainment. A practice room, music loud, air thick with work.

The trainees drilled as if debut were tomorrow — because none of them knew it wasn’t. At the mirror, Irene ran one movement over and over, adjusting force and expression by the millimeter, bangs pasted to her forehead with sweat, eyes locked.

Against the wall, her phone erupted at maximum volume — the special ringtone reserved for exactly one person.

She froze mid-count. Then crossed the room in a few quick bounds, swift as a startled rabbit, and snatched it up. The name on the screen did what that name did to her pulse.

[MESSAGE]  I’m in the alley behind the company. Waiting.

One line. The corners of her mouth rose without authorization; the fatigue of four hours of drill evaporated; what replaced it was sweetness with an urgency attached. She swept her water bottle and towel into her bag in one motion.

“Seul-gi-yah.” The words came fast, aimed at the girl mid-stretch on the floor. “Cover for me with the teacher later — say something urgent came up and I had to step out.”

“Ne, eonni. Don’t worry.”

Seulgi nodded with her whole honest face — and the door was already swinging shut on a faint trail of shampoo-scented wind.

“Where’d Joo-hyun eonni go?”

Joy had just walked in, curious.

“Ran.”

“That fast? Did something happen?”

Wendy looked up, concerned.

“Don’t know. Said it was urgent.” Seul-gi scratched her head. “But when she looked at her phone just now… her smile was really, really sweet.”

I’d sooner believe we debut TOMORROW than believe that eonni — gentle with everyone, distant with everyone, eyes containing nothing but practice and debut — is out there dating!”

In their eyes, Irene was beauty, discipline, and composure, every gram of her allocated to the debut that had to come. A person standing at the absolute maximum distance from a word like “love.”
