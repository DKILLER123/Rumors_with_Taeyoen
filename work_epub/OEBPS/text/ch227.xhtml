Chapter Two Hundred Twenty-Seven

Shining Alone

August 2, 2013 · morning Ji-eun’s villa · and every screen on the peninsula a system chime, a nine-o’clock announcement — and an industry losing its collective mind

Riiing— riiing—

The alarm clock held its ground with the stubbornness of its species. A pale, slender arm emerged from the edge of the summer duvet, patted around the nightstand for a while, and finally silenced the noise. Then a fluffy head burrowed up out of the covers — long hair loosely curled, several strands sticking up in cheerful rebellion.

Ji-eun rubbed her eyes and let out a long, satisfied breath.

She had slept WONDERFULLY. Every joint unknotted, warmth soaked through to the bone, the fresh sheets smelling of sunlight — a rather stark contrast to how the previous set had ended up.

“Awake?”

An arm came around her from behind and gathered her in.

She turned over to face him, the summer blanket slipping off one shoulder, and traced an idle circle on his chest with one fingertip, voice soft and sleep-sticky.

“You’re really… something else.”

The memory of the night made her cheeks warm, and her voice dropped lower.

“If I’d known… that night in Hong Kong, I never should have turned you down. All that time — wasted.”

The complaint came out adorably petulant, and he laughed.

Ji-eun really was a singular woman — the sweet, clean face with its crescent-moon smile and next-door warmth, the composed public manner, and underneath both, a curiosity and boldness that surprised him even now. Winning over this particular woman — fame, talent, and iron will in one small frame — carried a satisfaction unlike anything else in his collection of impossible things.

So when she started making noises about getting up, he simply reached over and pulled the slipped blanket back up over the both of them.

“Mmph— stop it—” She burrowed against him, voice muffled. “I really have a morning schedule—”

“Cancel it.”

“I CAN’T— Hoejang-nim, have mercy on your employee~”

“Hahaha— begging NOW? Too late.”

And with impeccable timing, a chime rang inside his skull.

SM Entertainment. A practice room.

Irene sat cross-legged on the floor, browsing a furniture list for the new apartment, when the news push slid down over the top of her screen — and her finger stopped dead.

She opened it. Her cool, quiet eyes went very wide, and the phone slipped out of her hand onto her lap with a small plastic clap.

“Eonni, what’s wrong?”

Seulgi, mid-stretch beside her, scooted over at the strange, frozen look on her face.

“He… bought the company?”

“Mwo? Who bought what?”

Joo-hyun didn’t hear her. Her mind was spinning its wheels somewhere far away. Her man — overnight — chairman of LOEN. No WONDER the apartment had come so easily, handed over like a bag of tangerines. She had made her peace, more or less, with not being the only one; the grievance and the unease still surfaced at odd hours, uninvited. But at this exact moment, what she mostly felt was the sheer vertigo of the gap — and a new, sharper version of the old anxiety. She took a slow breath. It didn’t help much.

Seul-gi picked the phone up off her lap, scanned the screen — and her eyes went perfectly round, mouth falling into a small o, gaze jumping between the headline photo and the name, twice, three times.

“How— how is that POSSIBLE?!”

It was him. THAT Ji-ho. The one who’d shared late-night practice rooms with them for months. The one who’d bought them dinner.

At the same moment, in the water-cooler alcove, Wendy saw the same push notification, and the glass slipped straight out of her hand and burst on the tile.

“Seung-wan-ah! Are you okay?? Did it cut you?”

Joy jumped at the crash and hurried over — but Seung-wan didn’t seem to hear her at all. She was staring at her phone with her whole face.

Her chingu. Was a CHAIRMAN?? She’d always known he was exceptional. But exceptional to THIS degree — was this real?

And in a hundred group chats across the industry — every artist, actor, and staffer who had ever crossed paths with Ji-ho — the same four messages were arriving in the same order: Real or fake? / DAEBAK. / How did he DO that? / …do we have to call him Hoejang-nim now?

That morning, the whole peninsula’s industry vibrated on one name: Chairman Song.
