Taeyeon had given him the trait he wanted most in the world: Heavenly Voice.

Vocal charm — nine.

Natural chest resonance; the clean and the dark ends of his tone in perfect balance — pure, and a little feral. His whole voice had unfolded: the clarity magnified, the lows gone velvet-thick, the highs bright without a single sharp edge.

For a singer, this wasn’t a wing. It was a second pair.

From today, there is very little I can’t sing.

✦   ✦   ✦

Leaving the sajang-nim’s office, Taeyeon felt like she’d crossed back from another lifetime.

He actually… did it.

Most idols, caught by their company, folded on the spot. Ji-ho had planted himself in front of her without one second of hesitation — and then talked Lee Soo-man seonsaeng-nim and Sajang-nim Kim Young-min around with nothing but talent and nerve.

How is he THIS cool?!

A man could draw a woman a hundred different ways — the face, the humor, the voice, money, power, kindness, drive, artistry. Every woman weighted the list differently; that was why charm came in so many currencies.

But Ji-ho had a theory of his own: there was one core magnetism that worked on everyone, and it was the ability to solve the problem. A man who didn’t flinch, who could carry weight, who could fix the thing she couldn’t fix — that man handed a woman the rarest thing on the market: safety. And safety, once felt, was almost impossible to walk away from.

“Come with me.”

Ignoring staff and artists alike, she towed him openly by the hand into the elevator and hit the button for the third floor.

“Yah — Heechul-ah. That’s Taeyeon, right?”

“Didn’t you swear she’d never actually date that trainee? Because that does not look like ‘never’ to me.”

Kim Heechul stared at the closing elevator doors, then erupted in wounded outrage.

“Ani — this brat! Dating and she didn’t even TELL me?!”

“Ha. Unbelievable.”

“Get a man and forget your hyung. Romance over loyalty. Every time.”

✦   ✦   ✦

Girls’ Generation’s usual lounge — the inner room, empty.

She locked the door and pulled the curtains, and the room dropped into dimness: a world of exactly two people.

Then she launched herself straight into his arms, hands locking behind his neck, incandescent.

“We did it — Ji-ho, we DID it!”

“You were incredible!”

His arms closed around her waist, his own grin unstoppable.

“Nuna’s the real hero.”

“Given any option at all, SM was never going to burn its bridge with you.”

Low, warm, textured — the kind of voice that slowed a heartbeat just passing through the ear. Had his voice gotten BETTER? Was that possible — or was it the filter of finally, officially being his?

The lover’s filter, probably. Her eyes went soft and glimmering; she caught her lower lip in her teeth.

“You’re the one who gave me the courage, you know.”

“In front of Sajang-nim my legs were actually shaking. I was scared to death.”

“But the second you took my hand — there was nothing left to be scared of.”

Shaking legs, she said. What he mostly registered was that her waist was very soft, and very warm through very thin fabric.

“Ji-ho. We’re dating now.”

“Mm.”

Her lips curved — water-bright — and she rose onto her toes and kissed him first.

Cool at first touch, then soft, then softer. Strawberry balm. Intoxicating.

The world melted slowly at the edges; all she could do was hold on tighter. Her small frame went pliant by degrees, pink blooming across the fine oval of her face.

They parted slowly, breath tangled.

Her wide eyes glared at him — glistening, entirely unconvincing.

“Bad boy.”

“Already? I haven’t even done anything bad yet.” Maximum innocence.

“Haven’t—?!” The flush went crimson; she swatted his hand away from where it had wandered. “Then what exactly was THAT?”

An abashed grin. Habit. Entirely habit.

She turned her back on him, face burning, setting her clothes to rights, and asked, quietly:

“So what’s your plan now?”

“JYP? YG?”

The question snapped him to business; his expression sobered.

“No. I’m going to LOEN Entertainment.”

Her hands paused mid-adjustment.

“LOEN?”

“Mm.”

He’d done the homework, thoroughly. LOEN sat under the SK Telecom umbrella and owned Melon — the largest music platform in the country. Backed by the biggest carrier and the biggest storefront, LOEN held something close to a monopoly position in distribution, promotion, and chart real estate. And unlike the idol factories — SM, JYP — LOEN wouldn’t bury him in variety schedules and image packaging. It was built for exactly one kind of artist: the kind whose center of gravity was the music itself.

“Nuna, I checked — LOEN just launched ‘Loen Tree’ and formally moved into artist planning and management. They’re actively scouting new solo acts.”

“A label that new means a contract that flexible — good terms, and behind them, the best distribution channel and chart exposure in the country. For me, there’s no better fit on the peninsula.”

She thought of his catalog, and that increasingly unfair voice, and had to admit the logic was airtight — a singer-songwriter plugged directly into Melon’s pipeline. IU’s entire career was the proof of concept.

Then a different thought landed, and she gave him a distinctly odd look.

“Wait. You were IN the pre-debut team. Why do you know this much about LOEN?”

He gathered her hands, unrepentant.

“The company’s split was too low. I couldn’t accept it.”

“Wanting better terms meant colliding with a mature system that had no reason to make me the exception.”

“So from the first day we disagreed, I started studying the alternatives.”

“SM’s packaging and star-making are still the best in the business — that part’s real. So I gave it an honest try. It failed. Here we are.”

“Wah, Ji-ho. You’re this calculating?” She pulled a face of theatrical alarm. “Did you have designs on me from the start too? You’re this clever — seonsaeng-nim literally calls you the most gifted student he’s ever seen—”

“—and yet when I taught you, you couldn’t learn a THING.”

“Only figuring that out now?” His grin turned wolfish, and he reeled her back in. “Too late. The little white lamb already belongs to the big bad wolf.”

“You— let GO of me~”

She squirmed exactly as hard as someone who did not want to be released. He dipped his head and pressed a kiss to her warm, soft lips.

She went quiet immediately, and burrowed into his chest.

The Same Evening A Hotel Room — “To the Beautiful You” Production the news finds Sulli

Choi Sulli wrapped for the day, made it back to the hotel, and dropped face-up onto the bed.

“Aigoo… so tired.”

Phone up. Thumb flicking. Notification, dismiss. Notification, dismiss. Notifica—

#KimTaeyeon_SongJiho

#SM_InHouse_Romance_Exposed

Her thumb stopped dead. Her eyes nailed themselves to the screen.

Taeyeon eonni and… oppa? Dating?

Her breath simply quit. Her pupils crushed down to points.

She opened the tag with shaking fingers — and there it was, blurred but unmistakable: Ji-ho with Taeyeon gathered in his arms, the two of them close, the angle intimate beyond argument.

A dull roar filled her skull and wiped it white.

Taeyeon eonni? And oppa? Since when? Last night? They’re… together? How? He never said anything — on the clifftop he listened to me so gently — his shoulder — I only just leaned on it—

Then the panic hit, sharp as a cramp around her heart.

The photo’s everywhere. Is oppa in trouble? He was ALREADY deadlocked with the company over his contract — now a scandal with Taeyeon eonni on top of it? What will the company do to him? What will the fans do to him? He’s so proud and works so hard — how is he supposed to carry all that hate?

And arriving in the same heartbeat as the panic: regret, floor-to-ceiling, wall-to-wall.

On the clifftop the sun had been so warm and the sea wind so gentle, and he had been sitting right there — right there. Leaning against him, feeling his warmth hold her up, her chest had been so full it nearly spilled. The urge to kiss him had been real, and enormous—

And she had flinched. Because of the ugly little voice that said you’re hideous right now. Because of the poisonous one that said you don’t deserve him yet. Because losing the warmth she had seemed scarier than never getting more of it.

She bit her lip until she tasted faint rust.

What if I’d been braver? Just a little? One breath braver — up on my toes — telling him everything the hug was actually saying? Would it be different now? Even if it changed nothing else — when the storm came for him TODAY, I could have stood somewhere real. Not out here. Not as “the girl he once saved,” useless and watching.

But there was no what if. She had flinched, and chosen wait and become better — and the world had not waited.

Were they real, though? The photo, the arms, that atmosphere— Fragments began connecting themselves against her will: the soft look that sometimes crossed Taeyeon’s face when it turned toward him; how, whenever he talked music, Taeyeon was always the stillest listener in the room. Each little stitch pulled the dread tighter.

The panic climbed her body like a vine. Call him? And ask what? As WHAT? Comfort him — when what he needs right now is to align stories with Taeyeon eonni and survive the company? A bystander calling in would just be noise. Call Taeyeon eonni?? Worse. I don’t even know if it’s REAL—

Her fingertips had gone cold around the phone. On the screen the stolen photo kept being reposted, enlarged, dissected; every speculation and every slur dragged across her nerves like a blade being sharpened.

She had lost her mother’s twisted version of love already. Was the one light she’d been keeping — carefully, secretly, like something holy — about to become unreachable too, just like this?

No. NO.

She caught her thumbnail in her teeth, and something feverish flickered up in her eyes. Mine. Oppa is MINE.

…Then again. Maybe they weren’t actually dating. The fever flickered out; something small and hopeful replaced it.

She picked the phone back up. Her finger hovered over his chat window for a long, long time — hope slowly deepening into something darker, a glimmer of obsession braided through the testing.

✦   ✦   ✦

They didn’t linger in the lounge; both had far too much to do.

Ji-ho signed the private wager agreement with SM, leaving “Growl” and its two siblings in the company library. Then the paperwork: amicable termination signed, trainee contract dissolved, library credentials deactivated.

SM issued no immediate public statement — how do you announce that they’re not dating AND that he’s leaving the company in the same paragraph? — and the PR department was still bleeding over draft language.

Which meant the fans got nothing. And in the vacuum, the extreme wing went hunting. Within hours, someone had matched the photo’s background to a street. The address hit the fan chatrooms and began to spread.

PR found Ji-ho as he finished his exit paperwork with one request: hold the news of his departure; clarify only the scandal; let the fever break first, and the exit would slip out quietly after.

He didn’t refuse.
