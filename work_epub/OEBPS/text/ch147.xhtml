Chapter One Hundred Forty-Seven

Red-Carpet Highlight

November 30, 2012 · 4:00 p.m. Hong Kong Convention and Exhibition Centre 2012 Mnet Asian Music Awards — the red carpet

Rehearsal, in the end, was simple: he and IU walked the stage marks, ran “Some” once through, and were released to wait.

Then came the carpet.

At four o’clock sharp, the 2012 Mnet Asian Music Awards red carpet opened. It unrolled from the entrance in a river of crimson, walled on both sides by a bristling forest of long lenses and a fan zone already at full roar, sound crashing in waves.

Inside the van, he noticed his palms were faintly damp.

Beyond the glass: voices, music, staff barking sequence calls, one gleaming act after another led out into eruptions of noise. Host Song Joong-ki first. Then the presenters — Han Ga-in, Han Chae-young, and the Hong Kong film contingent led by Jackie Chan, with Li Yuchun and Eddie Peng among the guests. Then the nominated groups: BIGBANG, f(x), KARA, T-ara, Wonder Girls, Girls’ Generation-TTS…

Sulli. Jiyeon. Taeyeon. Tiffany. The woman beside him in white. And, watching the broadcast from a sofa in Seoul — Irene.

Honestly? He was a little terrified.

Then the nominated soloists: Psy, Park Jin-young, Ailee, Jeong Eun-ji, HyunA… and then it was their turn.

✦   ✦   ✦

The van eased to the carpet’s mouth.

He stepped out first, and the fan zone detonated. He lifted one hand in a small wave and was paid back in screaming. Then he turned, offered his hand, and closed it around IU’s pale fingers, steadying her down.

She took his arm as if she had done it all her life — warmth and softness against his sleeve, and the faintest thread of her clean sweet scent.

The carpet zone was a battlefield of light: spotlights, camera flashes, the side-field floods, all warring at once. The violent shift in brightness made him narrow his eyes for half a second.

That half-second was all it took.

A soundless ripple moved outward with him at its center. ABSOLUTE FOCUS — active.

Every lens scattered along the carpet — trailing the previous group, adjusting angles, idling — swung around as one, drawn like iron filings to a magnet. The click-density of continuous shutters jumped a full order of magnitude. Not just ahead of him: from the sides, from behind, cameras that had come to catch IU’s gown found themselves locked onto him instead and stayed there.

And the eyes. Eyes from the press pens, from the fan walls, from the artists queued across the carpet. Curious, appraising, astonished, hungry—

Then I AM A SUPERSTAR triggered, and the STAR PRESENCE field unfurled without a sound. The tailored black suit sat across straight shoulders and a lean waist. The faint stiffness of nerves in his stance dissolved. His spine opened; the line of his jaw lifted a degree. He had not yet taken a single step — he was simply standing there with IU on his arm — and the entire carpet was already looking at nothing else.

IU felt it before anyone.

Her hand tightened almost imperceptibly on his arm. She tilted her face up, and something crossed those beautiful eyes — surprise, and a flash of unguarded fascination. She leaned in, voice pitched for exactly one listener:

“You were built for red carpets. Did you know that?”

Her breath brushed warm past his ear.

He turned his head and met her eyes — and felt it himself, rising from somewhere unfamiliar: command. The tie was suddenly too proper, too tight, entirely wrong for whatever was currently burning through him under all those lights.

So, in the gap between two volleys of flash, he lifted his free hand, hooked one finger under the knot, and tugged it loose. An inch. No more.

The simplest gesture in the world. Barely even grooming.

BOOM—

The shutter-storm that answered nearly swallowed him whole. White light fused into a single blazing sea, close to erasing the black suit and the white gown both. The press pen hit a fresh peak of chaos, voices stacking on voices:

“Ji-ho! This way!”

“IU! Closer together — yes! There!”

“Ji-ho-ssi! One look this way — just one!”

“Eyes! Give us the eyes!”

Along the fan walls the sound climbed past screaming into something else entirely, fiercest wherever his name and the sunset-crimson lightsticks clustered. Even eyes that had been fixed on other artists came around, dragged by the light and the roar toward the pair at the carpet’s center.

✦   ✦   ✦

Taeyeon watched the two linked arms, and her gaze went deep and dark. Teeth on her lip. Breathing slow and getting slower. Genuinely irritating. Not angry. NOT angry. Colleagues. They are colleagues.

Tiffany saw nothing to fuss about — with the number of times IU and Ji-ho had collaborated, of course they’d walk together.

“Ji-yeon. Ji-yeon?” A nudge from Park Hyo-min. “What are you staring at?”

“Nothing. Nothing at all.”

Months ago, she had been the ace of an idol group at its zenith, and he had been a trainee with no visible future. Now she was the fallen one the whole internet spat on — and he was the ascending juggernaut no one could price a ceiling for. She liked every song he wrote. She had quietly joined his fan cafe. Fate had a strange sense of humor. …Did he even remember that tangled, breathless kiss? Probably not. Probably better that way.

A few seats down, Krystal looped her arm through Victoria’s and asked, out of nowhere: “Eonni. Do you think IU and Ji-ho are dating?” — “Why would you ask that?” — “They’ve collaborated three times in a few months. All love songs. And look at how she LOOKS at him. That is not a ‘friend’ look.” Victoria, having no ruling, offered none.

“They are definitely not dating!” Sulli cut in abruptly, face gone cold. “Ji-eun and oppa would never date. They’re just good friends.”

“Because of me.”

Krystal and Victoria looked at the rare full-frost seriousness on Sulli’s face, wisely said nothing further, took a hand each, and changed the subject. But underneath, Sulli’s calm had already capsized. She knew Ji-eun too well. That look just now had NOT been nothing. Why — why did they all keep coming for her oppa? It was because he was too good; that was the whole problem; these women could smell it. No. No more sitting quietly by. Time to move.

And in Seoul, Irene had taken a rare night off practice to watch the broadcast live. She didn’t doubt him. But the sight of those linked arms raised a small weather-front of crisis in her chest anyway. Her boyfriend was, perhaps, slightly too popular. …Should she learn two more dishes? Her mother had always said it: what matters most in a woman is a warm hand and a well-kept home. Mm. Decided. Two more dishes. Next time, a full table, cooked by her.

✦   ✦   ✦

He walked the carpet unhurried, IU close on his arm, her smile flawless for every camera that called her name.

But she could feel it — the steadiness coming through the arm she held, setting their pace, choosing their line. In the space of a few steps, this man had taken the road that made whole careers hyperventilate and quietly converted it into his own showcase.

Carpet walked, board signed, soundbites delivered, the two of them passed into the hall and made for their seats.

Except — seriously, WHO had drawn up this seating chart? He and IU were placed in the same row as TaeTiSeo. Directly behind: f(x). One diagonal over: T-ara.

Taeyeon, Sulli, Tiffany, Jiyeon — four sets of eyes, four different temperatures, all pointed at him.

He could feel the sweat starting.
