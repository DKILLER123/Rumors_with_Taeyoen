Chapter One Hundred Twenty-Eight

However You Tell It, Yoon-a, You Seduced Me

Day three took the shoot deep into old-growth forest — the director wanted raw texture, so the location was an undeveloped stand on Mont Blanc’s southern flank: canopy shutting out the sky, root-braided ground, wet moss, the smell of earth and rotting timber.

New EIDER waterproof shells, high-cut boots, lightweight packs. The stylist bound Yoon-a’s hair into a high, no-nonsense ponytail and lacquered down the flyaways.

“Today’s movement load is heavy,” the director explained through the translator, hands sketching the route. “You cross this woodland — running, jumping, scrambling, a stream crossing — one continuous sequence. What we want is the most natural, most ALIVE state of outdoor sport.”

The trail run went in one take — his stride steady on the leaf-fall, her ponytail whipping bright behind. The triple pit-jump: he landed each one clean, knees soft; she cleared two and stumbled the third landing, waving off his reflexive half-reach with dignity intact.

The slope was the killer: fifty meters of steep grade under wet pine needles and moss, meant to showcase flexibility and boot grip. Take one, she slid half a meter, elbow rasping bark, shell fabric shrieking. Cut. Again. Take two, he topped out first and turned, hand extended; she took it and hauled up, arriving at the crest gasping, sweat-dark strands pasted to her skin.

Threading forest like this ate stamina alive. Her breathing shortened take by take.

Lunch was bread eaten standing. The afternoon was the stream: two meters wide, knee-deep, fast, fed straight off the mountain.

He crossed first — steady, few steps, done. Then her turn: careful hops, stone to stone, over the knee-deep rush—

And on the last step to the bank, her boot came down on a moss-slick round stone.

“AH—!”

Her whole body swung backward, right ankle wrenching at an angle ankles are not issued for — and the direction of her fall was the streambed itself, studded with bared, edged rock.

“YOON-A!” — her manager’s scream.

The crew froze; someone lunged, hopelessly far away. And in the sliver of a second before her center of gravity fully departed — Ji-ho, already on the bank, moved at a speed that didn’t look entirely plausible, seized her pack’s shoulder strap in a death-grip, and hauled backward with everything in him.

“Ugh—!”

The force snapped her back off the fall-line and slammed her into his chest; her arms locked around him on pure instinct.

One second of total silence. Then the bank exploded.

“BRAVO!!”

“SONG! Magnifique!!”

Applause, whistles; the director sagged with relief and crossed himself twice. Yoon-a shook against him in delayed terror, ankle screaming.

“It’s over. You’re fine.” His face was drawn tight, hair dripping, chest heaving; the grip on her arm was hard enough to bruise. His eyes swept her head to foot. “Where’s the damage?”

“Foot— the right ankle, I think it twisted—”

He got her seated on dry ground; the crew converged. The director killed the day on the spot — the footage bank was full enough — and the team medic’s field exam said sprain, clinic, now. Which surfaced the next problem: no vehicle access. The road was nearly a kilometer of forest track away.

“I’ll carry her.” He was already crouching.

“Ji-ho-ssi, let us — we can rotate—” A burly French grip stepped up, entirely sincere.

“No need.” He was planted in front of her, back offered. “Up.”

She hesitated — glanced once at the kind, enormous, tobacco-scented grip — felt honest gratitude and honest reluctance in the same heartbeat — and folded herself onto Ji-ho’s back, arms around his neck.

He rose smoothly, hands hooked under her knees, adjusted the load once.

“Hold on.”

She was light, and years under the squat rack were built for exactly this — but a kilometer of root-woven track with branches at face height was work, and everyone knew it. Twice the manager offered a rotation. Twice—

“Ji-ho. Take a break,” she said herself, guilt overriding preference.

“It’s fine. I’m not tired.”

His breath stayed level. Only the fine sweat at his temple, catching light, said otherwise. She lay quiet against the broad warm span of his back, feeling the muscle work beneath her, watching a drop of sweat trace the clean line of his jaw and fall to his collar — and something in her chest bumped, soft and sour-sweet, against its own walls.

At the road, the van ran them straight to the Chamonix clinic. Verdict: mild ankle sprain, minor ligament strain, no fracture. Ice; a diclofenac gel for massage; and — the doctor having clocked her pain tolerance — two days of ibuprofen, after meals.

✦   ✦   ✦

Back at the hotel he carried her the last stretch to her room and set her on the bed. The manager vanished into damage-report calls. She wrapped the ice pack in a towel and flinched at the cold.

“Gel.” He came back from washing his hands and picked up the tube. “Doctor said massage it in.”

The ankle had ballooned, red and angry. She hesitated.

“I can do it myself—”

“I’ve studied sports-recovery massage. Pressure control your twig arms can’t match.” Entirely matter-of-fact, already unscrewing the cap. “Say something if it hurts.”

He pulled a stool to the bedside, set her foot on his knee with care, and worked the cool gel around the swelling — featherlight at first, then slow deliberate pressure tracking the ligament line. The menthol chill sank in under his warm fingers; a strange mixed signal of ache and relief spread up from the joint.

She wiggled her toes, deeply unsure where to put her eyes, and ended up on his downturned face: the focus, the straight nose, the pressed lips. Objectively, infuriatingly handsome. The room held only the far-off wind off the massif and the soft friction of hand on skin, and a faint cool medicinal scent, and something else in the air with no ingredient label. Her own heartbeat was embarrassingly audible to her.

Ten minutes; he stopped, wiped his hands.

“Done. Ice on schedule. No weight on it.”

And he got up to wash his hands, exactly that unbothered.

She lay back against the headboard, re-set the ice, and let the cold clear her head — which promptly filled with the last three days on replay: the lounge sparring, the snow fight and the fall and the arms, the hand he’d offered on the slope, the stream, the kilometer of his back, the sweat on his jaw.

Heart rate: elevated again. He insisted on carrying me himself — special treatment? And this flustered, plucked-string feeling of mine—

She shook her head hard. Suspension-bridge effect. Textbook. Adrenaline mistaken for attraction. Peak experiences aren’t love; love has to survive dish duty and schedule conflicts. I am CLEAR-HEADED.

And then she thought of Taeyeon’s recent strangeness, and the rumors that never quite died. Taeyeon eonni and Ji-ho — that is NOT nothing. But if it IS something, and he does all this for me anyway — then isn’t he just… trash?

Her eyes cooled. Test him.

When he came out of the bathroom towel-drying his hands, she had rearranged herself into something softer and more helpless, voice pitched gentle.

“Today… I really owe you. Without you I’d have gone straight down onto those rocks.”

Up through the lashes: dependence, and lingering fright, in careful proportion.

“Anyone would’ve done it.” He sat down across from her, unmoved.

“But only you DID.” Head tilted a few degrees, voice softer still. “When you were carrying me back… I felt really safe.”

Eyes lowered on cue. Ears pinking on schedule.

He watched this performance with no particular expression, and raised one eyebrow.

No reaction. Fine. She leaned for the water glass on the far table — “accidentally” waking the bad ankle — produced a small perfect “sss,” and turned wounded-fawn eyes on him.

He moved at last. Stood — walked right past her — poured a glass of warm water, handed it over, sat back down, and said, evenly:

“Are you alright? Running a low fever, maybe? Because it FEELS like… you’re trying to seduce someone.”

Yoon-a: system failure.

The manufactured softness combusted into genuine full-face scarlet. If not for the ankle she’d have launched.

“SONG JI-HO!” She abandoned the glass and the act simultaneously, chest heaving, and went direct: “You and Taeyeon eonni. WHAT are you, exactly?”

On the table, his phone lit up and buzzed. Caller ID, in full view: Nuna 🐿.

He glanced at it, held up one polite finger — moment, please — and answered in front of her, voice going soft as flannel.

Incoming · Taeyeon

“Hello — nuna.”

“Mmm… Ji-ho-ya. Missed you… it’s so early here, the sky’s not even properly up…”

“Why are you awake this early? Sleep more.” A pause; lower, warmer: “I miss you too.”

Yoon-a sat fossilized on the bed, eyes going rounder by the syllable, expression management in complete collapse. The call was short and utterly mundane — and the intimacy in it came through the speaker like heat through glass.

He hung up, turned, and regarded her shell-shocked face with a small smile.

“What? Never seen anyone in a relationship?”

“You— you and Taeyeon eonni are ACTUALLY—?!”

“Mm.” No hedging whatsoever.

Three seconds of blank. Then a pressure spike of unexamined fury, and the sofa cushion was airborne.

“SONG JI-HO, YOU ABSOLUTE— you’re dating Taeyeon eonni and you still— you did all THAT to me!”

He caught the cushion, wearing the world’s most wronged expression.

“Did WHAT to you?”

“Carried me! And— and TOUCHED my foot!” Scarlet, halfway between mortified and murderous. “Pervert! Trash man! You have a girlfriend and you—”

“Stop, stop, STOP.” He raised a hand, expression pivoting to grave, put-upon patience. “Yoon-a-ssi. FIRST. I carried you because when the crew offered — especially the large gentleman with the beard — your entire body flinched backward. As your—” a cough — “—brother-in-law, more or less, looking after you was basic duty. Do you have any idea how heavy a kilometer of that was?”

“SECOND. The massage happened because I train, I know sports-injury protocol better than anyone in that room, and my pressure control is medically superior to whatever you’d have done to yourself. The motive was: heal faster, hurt less. And in return—”

He shook his head slowly, the picture of a good deed punished.

“Honestly? I’m starting to think the guilty conscience here is YOURS. That whole act just now — the eyes, the voice, the ‘accidental’ wince. Could it be that YOU’VE developed ideas about ME — and now that you know about Taeyeon, you’re terrified she’ll find out, so you’re striking first to clear yourself?”

Her lungs nearly ruptured.

“I— IDEAS? About YOU?! Which EYE of yours saw that?!”

“Both of them.” He folded his arms, luxuriously comfortable. “Just now. After I washed my hands. That gaze, that tone, that maneuver — if that wasn’t an attempt to—hm?”

“I was TESTING you!”

“Testing what? Whether I’m interested in you? And WHY would you need to know that?” The logic advanced like infantry. “Unless, subconsciously, you WANTED me to be. Or you’d already developed the interest yourself, and needed confirmation.”

“THAT’S NOT— you’re INSANE—”

“Haa.” A heavy, sorrowing sigh; a face of deep moral pain. “Taeyeon treats you like a blood sister. Trusts you. Dotes on you. If she knew you’d developed feelings for her boyfriend… imagine the heartbreak. …Don’t worry. I won’t tell her. This never happened. You just — reflect on yourself. Deeply.”

And he turned and walked out — door open, door shut, one clean motion — sealing her incandescent “YAH! SONG JI-HO! GET BACK HERE! THAT IS NOT WHAT HAPPENED! LISTEN TO MY EXPLANATION—!!” neatly inside the room.

In the corridor, his step paused. The corner of his mouth climbed, held a moment, and settled back to neutral as he walked on.

Enough for tonight. Overplay it and it spoils. From here forward, Yoon-a — however the story ever gets told, YOU fell first, and YOU did the seducing.

Inside the room, Yoon-a stared at the shut door, chest heaving, ankle throbbing, skull throbbing worse. She threw the second pillow at the door, missed meaningfully, and collapsed back onto the bed with both hands over her face.

It’s over. I could jump in the Han and not wash this off. In HIS version I’m the impure-hearted hoobae secretly in love with her brother-in-law who TRIED TO SEDUCE HIM. Ji-ho, you absolute BASTARD—
