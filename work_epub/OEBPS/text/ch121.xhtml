Chapter One Hundred Twenty-One

The Memory That Came Back

The Running Man shoot wrapped; lights came down; crews began striking equipment. The carnival noise thinned to scattered conversation.

Ji-ho, back in his own clothes with the conspicuous premium hanwoo gift set in hand, scanned the dispersing crowd. IU stood with her manager, her profile gone noticeably cool in the evening light. She caught him approaching, gave him exactly one glance, and accelerated toward her van.

“Ji-eun nuna.”

He kept pace easily, smiling audibly. She ignored him and pulled the van door open.

He didn’t hurry. He simply raised the heavy gift box a few inches — enough to bring the glossy marbled-beef photography and the word PREMIUM into her sightline — and drawled:

“No one to share the prize with. Guess I’ll have to finish the whole set alone… I hear the tenderloin’s marbling in this one is art-grade.”

The door-climbing motion stopped mid-execution.

She was starving — recording always did that. And that was TOP-GRADE hanwoo. She’d been dieting for weeks; grilled meat had become a rumor. And above all: this scoundrel had toyed with her feelings on national television. Eating an expensive meal at his expense wasn’t indulgence. It was JUSTICE.

She turned, face still officially cross, eyes already conducting unauthorized reconnaissance of the gift box.

“…Just the two of us?”

“Nuna decides.”

Alone with Ji-ho? Vetoed instantly. Things between them were already running ambiguous; the man was handsome AND wicked; add alcohol, and whether she conquered him or he conquered her, the timing was wrong either way.

Her phone was out, thumb flying.

“Location: my place. My grill setup’s complete. BUT—” she looked up with a firm don’t get ideas expression, “—I’m calling Sulli and Ji-yeon. More people, more fun.”

Especially Ji-yeon. With everything crashing down on T-ara lately… a gathering, a few drinks, some talk. Exactly what was needed.

“Good.”

That Evening IU’s Villa — Bangbae-dong, Seocho-gu top-grade hanwoo, soju, and one empty seat waiting to happen

IU’s villa rarely saw its own kitchen used, but the equipment was flawless. On the long wooden table the electric grill was already preheated and whispering; the hanwoo lay sliced to perfect thickness, marbling gleaming under the lights; banchan, lettuce, perilla leaves, and several bottles of soju and beer excavated from the refrigerator’s deep archives completed the field.

The doorbell rang, and Choi Sulli swept in like a gust of sweet wind, carrying braised chicken feet and dried squid.

“Ji-eun! Ji-ho oppa!” She deposited the snacks and went straight for the glasses with the confidence of family. “WAH, this MEAT! Look at it! Today I eat like a queen!”

Jiyeon arrived minutes later — thinner than at their last meeting, exhaustion imperfectly hidden under immaculate makeup. At the sight of warm light and familiar faces, her rigid shoulders dropped a nearly invisible centimeter.

“Ji-eun. Peach. Ji-ho. Good evening.” The clear voice ran lower than usual.

“Ji-yeon-ah, come, come!” IU took her bag and installed her at her own side. “Perfect timing — the meat just went on.”

Ji-ho assumed grillmaster duty. Fat hissed; the room filled with that irresistible smell.

“First piece — for the hero who provided the venue.”

He laid the first perfectly seared slice into IU’s lettuce leaf, adding kimchi and a dab of ssamjang with suspicious thoughtfulness. She had planned at least one more round of dignity — but the aroma bypassed all committees. One “hmph,” one lightning-fast wrap, one bite — and her eyes narrowed into pure bliss as top-grade hanwoo, cool lettuce, and sour kimchi achieved constitutional perfection. The residual “anger” left orbit entirely.

“Good, right?”

“…It’s passable.” Muffled. Ears: pink. Soju: urgently self-administered.

Sulli, oblivious, laughed and wrapped her own, chattering the room warm. Jiyeon quietly ate everything IU kept stacking on her plate, occasionally surfacing a small smile.

A few glasses in, the room loosened. Meat flew; lettuce dwindled; the talk slid from variety-show nonsense to daily life — and then, inevitably, toward the thing pressing down on one of them.

“…It’s nothing. Just tired.” Ji-yeon rotated her glass, watching the clear liquor tilt, voice gone bitter at the edges. “Sometimes it feels like — whatever I say is wrong. Whatever I do, someone’s watching for it.”

The table went quiet for one beat.

IU set down her chopsticks and pulled Ji-yeon’s shoulders in hard.

“Nonsense! Our Ji-yeon is the BEST one. The people talking garbage don’t know one ten-thousandth of you.” Protective fury, zero hedging.

“Exactly. Eonni, ignore them. We’re all right here.” Sulli, jokes fully holstered, nodding hard.

Ji-ho said nothing. He simply transferred the newest, tenderest batch of sirloin — all of it — onto Ji-yeon’s plate, refilled her empty glass, poured his own, and raised it.

She looked at the mounded plate. At his eyes — calm, with something unreadable folded underneath. At IU and Sulli pressed close on either side. And something she’d been holding upright for two weeks quietly developed a crack.

She lifted her glass, touched it to theirs, and drank deep.

“Thank you,” she said, very small, eyes rimming red.

“Thanks NOTHING. Eat meat!” IU swept an arm, reinflating the room by force. “Ji-ho — don’t you dare stop grilling! Peach — all the alcohol in the fridge, retrieve it! Ji-yeon — the beef rib fingers are ELITE, try them immediately!”

The room refilled: grill-sizzle, pouring sounds, laughter, escalating commentary. Beyond the windows the city’s lights ran like water — and inside, food-warmth and friend-warmth wove Jiyeon a small temporary harbor.

Ji-ho turned the meat in silence, his gaze returning to her at intervals.

✦   ✦   ✦

Deep in the night, full of meat and alcohol: Sulli draped on IU, humming; Ji-yeon finally wearing a loosened, real smile as she rose and headed for the bathroom.

Ji-ho waited a moment. Then followed.

The bathroom door opened; humid warmth met cool hallway air. Ji-yeon came out head-down, tipsy, moving fast — and her forehead collided, entirely undefended, with something firm and warm.

“Mmph—”

She rocked back half a step, hand over brow, and raised alcohol-hazed eyes. Ji-ho stood in the doorway wearing a calm with no legible emotion on it. She rubbed her head; the point of contact prickled with heat.

“Sorry—” she mumbled by reflex, angling to slide past — and her feet stopped on their own.

Wrong. Something’s wrong. Not the collision just now — deeper. A broken, alcohol-pickled sense-memory of THIS: his body heat. His breath, this close. And — the temperature of his lips—

“At first I thought you were embarrassed. Now I’m fairly sure — you’d simply forgotten.”

The sentence turned like a key in a lock rusted shut — and the door behind it blew open.

Recovered · The Somaek Night — the missing reel

The living room. Chandelier light swimming. Empty bottles rolled across the carpet. Hara curled asleep in the sofa corner; Sulli breathing evenly into a cushion.

“Ji-yeon, stop drinking—” — Ji-eun, somewhere overhead.

“Don’t pull me! It’s MY turn, isn’t it!” — her own voice, thick-tongued, her body lurching up after the bottle on the coffee table. The floor tilted; she fell into a soft, sweet-smelling catch. Ji-eun. The two of them collapsing into the sofa together.

“Ji-ho, could you carry Sulli and eonni to the guest room first? Second door on the right, up the stairs.”

At the swimming edge of vision: a tall figure rising — he’d drunk plenty too, steps dragging, pressing his brow before bending to lift Sulli with great care.

Cut. The master bedroom. Pink, rose-scented, unmistakably Ji-eun’s. Herself on the bed, impossibly soft. Someone tucking a blanket over her, very gently.

“Where are you going?” Her hand shooting out on its own authority. Cotton collar in her fist. “Drink with me.”

“I’m going to get more.”

“Liar. You’re trying to trick me into sleeping.” Drunk instinct — or pure belligerence. She dragged his face into focus: amber eyes gone honey-dark in the bedside light; a nose line unfairly clean. And a thought, absurd and fizzing, surfacing like a bubble through soju: this good-looking… even if he saw, it’s not exactly a LOSS, is it?

“You saw, didn’t you?” — out of her mouth with no committee approval whatsoever, her hand hauling her own collar askew, daring him, past shame, past sense.

He froze — reached out — to fix her clothes? Unacceptable. He would not escape on a technicality. “You DEFINITELY saw!” One hard yank—

The world rotated. Warm weight; a clean, foreign, masculine scent occupying every sense at once; breath at her neck raising shivers. A voice in her head screaming WRONG, and a body voting the other way.

His lips — one touch, parted instantly. Her own giggle: “Hehe. NOW we’re even.” A pause. A puzzled lick of her lips. “…That’s it? That’s no different from a peck. Where’s the ‘spring arriving’ the eonnis promised?”

Confusion, promoted to research. “Maybe it was too short?” The second kiss — not brief. Alcohol lighting the fuse; curiosity and abandon feeding it. Air going thin. His hand — the one sent to fix her collar — somewhere it should not be, palm burning along her waist. His mouth tracing jaw, throat, collarbone, downward— her blood all rushing to one place, thought dissolving, her own hands shoving up his shirt hem, palms mapping hard, unfamiliar heat—

And then: one needle of clarity through the flood. The heat at her chest — not metaphorical. A hand. SHAME, arriving like a building collapse.

“Ah—!” The shove. A heavy thud of a body hitting floor. Ragged breathing in a silent room. Then his voice — hoarse, pressed flat, tangled in the same wreckage and something like remorse:

“I’m sorry.”

“…When you’re sober — let’s find a time and talk.”

Footsteps. A door. The film snaps.

The corridor’s cold white light returned.

Jiyeon stood petrified. The blood left her face entirely — then returned all at once, at roughly triple pressure, red enough to bleed. Her ears roared with her own heartbeat.

Kissed him — TWICE? Touched— and was— first base, second base — TOTAL COLLAPSE ALONG THE ENTIRE FRONT!

The sensations, temperatures, images — all of it broke over her in merciless high definition, every detail an individual indictment. So the mark below her collarbone really HAD been a kiss mark.

Insane. INSANE. Jiyeon, you drunken madwoman, WHAT DID YOU DO?!

She snapped her head up and stared at Ji-ho — who stood there looking (to her, at least) infuriatingly composed — pupils in full earthquake.

“S-s-s-SORRY! Excuse— excuse me!”

She squeezed the words out of a closing throat, ducked like a startled rabbit through the gap beside him, and fled — toward the living room, mid-course correction, toward the balcony — footsteps stumbling, her entire retreating back one legible sentence: let me vanish from this planet immediately.

Ji-ho stood a while, watching the space she’d fled through. Then he raised a hand and rubbed his jaw, mildly troubled.

So — she remembered. And the reaction is running… somewhat hotter than projected.

One eyebrow ticked upward. He turned back toward the living room, where the grill still sizzled, IU was lifting a perfect slice of hanwoo, and Sulli was laughing over a raised glass — no one, apparently, having noticed the small seismic event by the bathroom door.

Except for the one conspicuously empty seat.
