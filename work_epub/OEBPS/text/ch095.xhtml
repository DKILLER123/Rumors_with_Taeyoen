Chapter Ninety-Five

The Stranger at Door 801

7:00 PM Songpa-gu · Samhaksa-ro 19-gil, Building 6 the address the chatrooms found

Ji-ho dragged his tired feet back to the apartment building.

The elevator climbed to eight. The doors chimed open — and the smell hit first: faint, foul, organic, wrong.

He frowned and stepped out fast.

And stopped dead, pupils contracting.

The corridor’s clean walls and floor were covered in screaming red spray paint — crooked Korean lettering, line after line of venom.

Corridor · 8th Floor — Building 6

SONG JI-HO, SON OF A DOG

STAY AWAY FROM TAEYEON EONNI!

BREAK UP — OR DIE

SSIBAL YANKEE BASTARD — GET OUT OF KOREA!

door 801: spray paint edge to edge, “DIE” in letters a forearm tall · a rotting dead rat wired to the handle · the mat buried under garbage — rotten greens, smashed eggs, and a Taeyeon poster torn into confetti

And in the middle of the wreckage stood a figure that did not belong.

A young woman, back to him, frozen in front of his door — chin tilted up at the graffiti, either double-checking the unit number or simply failing to digest the scene. Plain T-shirt, jeans, slight frame, long hair loose down her back.

His stomach dropped. Reporter? Or one of the extreme ones?

He pressed the anger and the chill down flat and moved up quickly — identify first, react second.

“Excuse me, are you—”

The words never finished.

The fire-stairs door — the one that was always shut — burst open with a bang.

A man in a black mask and baseball cap came through it like something released from a cage, eyes red-rimmed and fixed, and in his hands — a baseball bat.

“Ji-ho! You’re Ji-ho, aren’t you?!”

The address from the chatroom. The photos from the chatroom. Recognition, instantly — and with it, all that unhinged pressure found its outlet. No more words.

“DIE! You bastard — you seduced Taeyeon!”

The bat came around in a full arc, swinging for his head.

Air screamed off the wood.

“LOOK OUT!”

— not for himself; for the woman petrified in front of his door.

His body was already moving. Months of dance training layered over a Koreatown adolescence that had taught him early and thoroughly how a swing telegraphs — he snapped back and sideways, and the bat ripped past his jacket and slammed into the wall. A flat concussive BANG; plaster dust sifted down.

“Aah—!” The woman finally unfroze, one short scream, arms coming up over her head as she shrank aside, face bone-white.

The miss made the man wilder. Cursing, he hauled the bat back around in a waist-high scythe.

Ji-ho’s eyes had gone cold and very clear. In the narrow corridor he dropped low — the bat hissed over his head — and the man’s own momentum dragged him off balance.

NOW.

He came up out of the crouch like a cat off a wall — one fast, vicious side-kick, dead into the man’s midsection.

A choked grunt; the man’s whole body folded, staggering backward until his spine cracked against the opposite door. The bat clattered to the floor.

Ji-ho didn’t pause. One step, and his foot came down precisely on the wrist groping toward the weapon — and pressed.

“AAGH— let GO, you bastard—!”

The man clawed at Ji-ho’s leg with his free hand. Ji-ho leaned more weight in, tracking the free hand — until the man abandoned the bat entirely, headbutted blindly at his shin, and, the instant the pressure eased a fraction, tore loose and scrambled up.

He knew he was outmatched. One last glare — hate and fear in equal parts — and he bolted for the fire stairs he’d come from.

“Stop!”

Ji-ho went after him.

But the woman — the frozen one — had broken for the same door seconds earlier, desperate to be anywhere else, fleeing down the stairwell on unsteady legs.

The running man hit the stairwell and slammed straight into her.

“MOVE! Out of the way!”

He didn’t even look. One brutal, full-arm shove.

“Kyaa—!”

No time to catch herself. No time to do anything. She went over backward —

— and behind her was the flight of bare concrete stairs.

Ji-ho reached the doorway exactly in time to watch it happen: her slight body tipping past its balance point, the terror freeze-framed in her eyes, and then the long, sickening tumble — the dull percussion of a body meeting step after step, a broken whimper — until she came to rest on the mid-landing.

And did not move.

“F*ck—!”

The American came out of him raw. He let the attacker go — footsteps already hammering away down the stairs — and threw himself down the flight to the landing.

She lay curled on the concrete, unconscious. A scrape on her forehead seeped threads of blood; her cheek and forearms were blooming with contusions; worst was the knee — jeans shredded, the flesh beneath torn open and welling red through the fabric. Even unconscious, her brow was clenched with pain.

He crouched, two fingers to her carotid, then her breath — alive, but hurt badly enough that hospital was the only word that mattered.

Only then did he actually see her face. Dust-streaked, blood-flecked — and delicately pretty, with a kind of built-in gentleness.

He froze.

Then the recognition landed.

Younger — greener — than the version his first life remembered, but this was unmistakably the face that would later be praised across the industry for its clean beauty and quietly formidable acting: Chae Soo-bin.

Real
Chae Soo-bin (Bae Soo-bin)
Aspiring Actress · Ji-ho’s Neighbor
Pre-debut · stage and screen trainee
Born: July 10, 1994

A quietly determined young actress supporting herself through small jobs and stage work; she will adopt the name Chae Soo-bin because actor Bae Soo-bin already uses her birth name professionally.

Why is SHE here? …Wait. Was she standing at my door because — is she the neighbor?

Not the moment. He stripped off his jacket, tucked it over her, and — as carefully as his shaking adrenaline allowed — lifted her onto his back. She weighed almost nothing. He moved like she was made of glass anyway.

Down the stairs, out the doors, one arm flagging the street — a taxi, the nearest ER.

✦   ✦   ✦

The emergency room took her fast — nurses, a doctor, a gurney. Ji-ho fronted the emergency fees and gave his details at the desk.

He watched the treatment-room doors swallow her, leaned back against the cold wall, and let out one long breath.

Then he took out his phone. First call: SM.

Outgoing · SM Entertainment — Emergency Line

“It’s Ji-ho. Listen carefully — my address leaked into the fan chatrooms. A man was waiting at my apartment with a baseball bat. A bystander is in the ER.”

“I’m handling the police. What I need from YOU is this: protect Taeyeon. Whatever her schedule is — security, cars, everything. If they came for me on day one, do not assume she’s safe. Do you understand me? PROTECT her.”

SM understood exactly how bad it had gotten. The clarification statement — stalled all day in drafting — went out within the hour, and Taeyeon’s manager posted a synchronized clarification from her official account.

Then, without a second’s hesitation, the next call.

Outgoing · 112 — Police

“Hello — I need to report a crime. The address is Songpa-gu, Samhaksa-ro 19-gil, Building 6, apartment 801. I was attacked tonight — premeditated, with a weapon — and there are death threats painted through the whole corridor. A bystander was injured; she’s in the emergency room now—”

— the full account, item by item: the graffiti, the dead rat, the ambush, the bat, the shove, the fall.

The night deepened. The hospital lights were the color of skimmed milk.

He looked at the treatment-room doors one more time, then pulled up a different contact and pressed call.

Taeyeon. If she hears about tonight from anyone but me — with that personality, it’s not anger I’d be dealing with. It’s every worst-case scenario her heart can build at 2 AM.

The line began to ring.

✦   ✦   ✦

A man who doesn’t flinch, who can carry weight, who can fix the thing she cannot fix, hands a woman the rarest thing on the market: safety. And safety, once felt, is almost impossible to walk away from.
