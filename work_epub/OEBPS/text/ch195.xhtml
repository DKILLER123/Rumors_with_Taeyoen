Chapter One Hundred Ninety-Five

Naïve, or Just Foolish

Late May 2013 · evening A Gangnam three-star · the hillside villa a grand package, a business plan dismantled — and one very dangerous almost

Before leaving to meet Jessica, he remembered the unopened box: the Charm Grand Package from crossing 70 had been sitting in inventory since the night everything else happened at once.

A thought, and the interface bloomed.

DREAM LIFE — CHARM GRAND PACKAGE · CONTENTS

⟨ DING ⟩ Partner authorization expanded — current partners 4 / 12.

⟨ DING ⟩ Special skill acquired: GRAFT AND TRANSFER.

⟨ DING ⟩ Acquired: DREAM FAMILY FUND.

⟨ DING ⟩ Acquired: MINOR CAREER WISHING ORB.

⟨ DING ⟩ Special buff acquired: FAIR WINDS (CAREER).

Five chimes, each one landing like a coin hitting a full purse. He read them item by item.

GRAFT AND TRANSFER

EFFECTWithin romantic encounters, special effects may be acquired — and transferred as gifts to partners, refining them toward their ideal.

Interesting. The industry never lacked for passing sparks — but most sparks made poor partners. This turned them into raw material: extract a strength — craft, voice, charm, even a trait — and hand it to someone he actually treasured. Dew converted to nutrition.

DREAM FAMILY FUND

EFFECTNext-generation AI quantitative fund. Stable annual returns of 50–100%. No leveraged capital accepted. Eligible deposits: player’s idle funds, and idle funds of the player’s partners only.

Rain on schedule. Taeyeon, Tiffany — brilliant EQ, thin financial education, real money and no idea where to put it. In the old timeline Taeyeon would eventually get burned on real estate. Now there was a printing press with a family lock on the door.

MINOR CAREER WISHING ORB · uses: 3

EFFECTWish a career plan into the orb; it will be realized — one hundred percent. Restriction: minor wishes only. Impractical wishes cannot be granted. Use with care.

“Minor.” Defined how? Only one way to find out.

He tested it: Acquire T-ara.

The system considered for several seconds.

⟨ SYSTEM ⟩  Player wishes to acquire T-ara. Against current assets and standing: difficulty high — cannot be realized. NOTE: per the player’s career plan, if LOEN is successfully acquired while T-ara stands at its lowest valley, the acquisition wish becomes realizable.

So it COULD read conditionals. A wish engine for anything “feasible under specified circumstances.” Genuinely useful.

One more test: Acquire Girls’ Generation.

⟨ SYSTEM ⟩  Cannot be realized.

Fair. The nation’s girl group was SM’s crown; not a current-generation target.

FAIR WINDS (CAREER) · passive buff

EFFECTCareer runs smooth: fewer walls, frequent benefactors, quality opportunities arriving unbidden, industry reputation compounding steadily.

Plain-looking. Devastating. He was about to walk into the LOEN acquisition — and the dice had just been quietly weighted in his favor at every table in the city. A worthy reward for the 70 threshold.

He breathed once, straightened his collar, and left to meet Jessica.

✦   ✦   ✦

She’d booked a Michelin three-star in Gangnam — dark wood, warm low light, luxury with its volume turned down, privacy engineered into the floor plan.

She was already seated when he entered — and she had dressed with intent: a champagne silk blouse, collar open over fine collarbones; black tailored trousers; slender heels; hair in loose waves; makeup precise. The whole composition read cool, expensive, deliberate.

Then she looked up at him and visibly stalled — eyes widening a degree, lips parting, the face of a woman looking at something that shouldn’t be structurally possible.

He knew exactly what she was looking at. Face at ten did this now.

“Annyeonghaseyo, Sica nuna.”

“Ah— annyeonghaseyo.”

She surfaced, faintly pink, privately furious at herself. Gaping like a trainee. Get a GRIP. But — objectively — too handsome. Taeyeon’s luck was genuinely obscene: the face, the talent, and now — four hundred sixty billion.

Why hadn’t SHE met him first?

…Wait. Where did THAT come from? She shook the thought off physically.

“Sorry I’m late.”

“You’re not. I was early.” She flagged the server. “Let’s order first.”

Orders taken, door closed, she lifted the decanter herself and poured them both.

“Ji-ho.” She raised her glass, and her face went formal. “This glass is an apology.”

“An apology?”

“That day at Taeyeon’s — the fight about Yoon-a’s acting. I lost my temper at you.” Color climbed her cheeks — wine or honesty, unclear. “I thought about it a long time afterward. You were right. You said those things because you’re genuinely on Yoon-a’s side. I shouldn’t have gone at you like that.”

She emptied the glass in one line.

He watched her — the flush, the level eyes. This was authentically Jessica: proud as weather, and clean about her own errors when she finally ruled against herself.

“Nuna’s being too formal. I’d already forgotten it.”

“Then — page turned?”

“Turned.”

She smiled — a rare, unguarded brightness — and refilled both glasses. “Then this one’s to friendship.”

“To friendship.”

Dinner warmed from there, unexpectedly easy. She’d set the ice-princess frame aside entirely and was, of all things, complimentary — the songs (“I genuinely love both of them”), Taeyeon’s glow (“your doing, obviously — sweet love feeds a person”), the screenwriting (“is there anything you can’t do?”) — toast following toast. He received all of it gracefully, and they ranged wide: music to film, fashion to investment. She discovered his conversation had no edges she could fall off — whatever she raised, he met it, and met it with angles she hadn’t considered. The double crush of it — that mind in that face — did nothing to slow her third glass.

By which point her cheeks carried a proper wine-blush, chin propped on one hand, eyes gone slightly soft-focus on him.

“You know… sometimes I think — if Taeyeon hadn’t met you first, would we—”

She let it die there. The meaning survived intact.

He smiled and declined the bait entirely.

No history, no foundation — this was scaffolding. She was building rapport toward something. Testing, watching, waiting for a door. Which meant tonight had an agenda beyond apology and dinner.

Sure enough — several sips later, she reached into her bag and set a fine leather notebook on the table.

“Ji-ho. Actually — there’s something I wanted to ask your advice on.”

“Please.”

She opened it: pages dense with notes, sketches, clipped references.

“An idol’s career basically ends at thirty.” She began carefully, like a person laying out cards she’d counted many times. “The thirty curse — you know it. Girls’ Generation is still at the summit, but a summit is a summit BECAUSE the road after it goes down. So I’ve been planning the second life.”

The family money had trained her eye early; her trainee-era cosmetics had been the dorm’s black market currency; her early styling had won headlines. Somewhere in there she’d concluded she had the gift. The magazines never left her bag. Fashion was the industry where her fame converted directly to revenue — the ideal second act.

“I want to found a fashion brand.” Her eyes had gone bright with it. “I even have the name. Blanc & Eclare — French. ‘Pure, and radiant.’”

She turned pages: hand sketches, a logo study, product boards. “Positioning: modern minimalism. Launch with eyewear, ready-to-wear and denim, then expand into cosmetics. I’ll serve as chief designer myself. With ‘Girls’ Generation’s Jessica’ on the label, the fans will carry the opening — look, this is the logo draft — these are the first-line concepts—”

She talked for a long time, and she talked well — the empire in her head unrolled from starter lines to couture ambitions, fans and fame and momentum, all of it lit with total conviction.

Then she closed the notebook and looked at him with open expectation.

In her accounting the plan was airtight: recognition, taste, demand. All it wanted was capital. And if the capital were HIS — the ideal partner: vision, resources, and four hundred sixty billion won.

He had listened without interrupting. And he had to concede: the ambition was legitimate, even reasonable. Her long conflict with the other eight reduced, at bottom, to a difference of vectors — she wanted a self; they wanted the team. No villain in it.

But the plan itself—

Naïve. Or, said kindly: pure.

He set down his glass. “Nuna. The idea has real merit. And there are several problems we need to walk through honestly.”

Her smile flickered. “What problems?”

He picked up a pen and began writing in the margin of her own notebook.

“One. Positioning versus price. Modern-minimal light luxury means a hoodie at two hundred to two hundred fifty thousand won; dresses north of five hundred. That’s Sandro and Maje territory — mature brands with mature supply chains. Your fanbase is students and young office workers. Can they afford it? More precisely: will they pay it twice? And — does the design and the fabric actually justify the number?”

Her mouth opened. Nothing came out.

“Two. The customer. Early sales will be fan economy — one commemorative purchase of ‘my idol’s brand,’ and then silence. Repurchase collapses; the brand never escapes the fandom’s gravity into the general market.”

“Three. The field. At that price band you’re against international light luxury, domestic designer labels, and the premium lines of the fast-fashion giants — with no edge in design distinctiveness or brand recognition beyond the name on the door.”

Her face had begun to lose color.

“Four. Design capability. Nuna — ‘chief designer.’ Do you hold professional design skills? Or does the actual work devolve to a hired team while the title stays on you?”

A beat. His tone dropped further into gravity.

“Five. And this is the one that matters. The team. The brand trades on ‘Girls’ Generation’s Jessica.’ Have you thought about the other eight? They’ll see the group’s collective name monetized — with none of it flowing back. And SM? The contract expressly bars private ventures. They will squeeze — and the endgame of that squeeze is a door with two exits: the brand, or Girls’ Generation. Choose one.”

She’d gone white entirely. Under the table, her hands had closed into fists.

“Six. Money and law. Fashion eats capital and pays back slow. One break in the funding chain and the brand implodes. And if the partner you sign happens to be the wrong kind of partner — you inherit lawsuits, and debts with your name on them.”

He set the pen down.

“Nuna. I’m not attacking the dream. But founding a brand — a FASHION brand — is crueler and more complicated than this notebook believes.”

The room went long-silent.

From anyone else, she’d have swatted every word of it. But this was the man she now knew to be — genuinely, provably, four-hundred-sixty-billion-won successful. The same sentence weighs differently from your cousin and from a billionaire. That was simply how ears worked.

Her head stayed down. Her shoulders were trembling slightly. Months of construction — the magazines, the hundred sketches — reduced, in one pass, to a sieve.

“So—” She looked up at last, eyes rimmed red. “You think it’s childish. Is that it?”

“Yes.” He didn’t decorate it. “Childish, and unmoored from the real conditions. As an investment: a guaranteed loss. If someone tells you otherwise — they’re selling you something.”

“You— on what BASIS—” Her voice shook with the unfairness of it. “Do you know the fashion industry? Do you know my fans?”

“I don’t need to. I know business logic. That’s sufficient.”

He rose and reached for his jacket. “Nuna — thank you for dinner. The plan — think it through again. Properly.”

“Wait!”

She was on her feet, her hand closing around his wrist — cold fingers, faint tremor.

“There’s — no other way? Any way?” The plea had crept into her voice despite her. “I just want a road out. The idol life ENDS, and I can’t just—”

“Can’t what?” He turned. “Can’t spend a life on fan money? Nuna. What you earn in one year, most people don’t earn in a lifetime. Invested with a functioning brain, it keeps you comfortable until you’re ninety.”

“But COMFORTABLE isn’t what I want!” Her voice climbed. “I don’t want ‘comfortable’! I want to LEAVE something! Prove something!”

He looked at the stubbornness burning in her eyes — and understood the shape of it at last.

Not commercial ambition. Existential terror. A girl raised at the center of every room, staring down the day the lights move on — terrified of becoming ordinary.

He exhaled. “I’m no expert in fashion commerce either. But the branded-label road — as drawn in that notebook — doesn’t work.” A shake of the head; the tone stayed gentle. “And it isn’t a money problem. If I wired you a billion — ten billion — a flawed model just loses it slower. Water into sand.”

Her hand loosened, finger by finger. Her head dropped; her shoulders came down with it.

And why WOULD he help her? She was nothing to him — a teammate of his girlfriend’s. That he’d sat through the whole pitch was already Taeyeon’s face at work.

“I— understand.” Barely audible. “Thank you for — telling me the truth.”

She turned and pressed the service bell. By the time the server came for the bill, the ice princess had been fully reassembled — save a residual pink at the rims of her eyes.

Both of them had drunk; neither could drive. He reached for his phone to call Ryu Dong-hwan — and she stopped him.

“No need. I — came prepared.”

One call: “Soo-jung-ah. Are you here?” — “Mm. Parking level. Amber came too.”

Five minutes later, two figures appeared at the private-room door: Jung Soo-jung, doll-faced and visibly unpaid for this labor; Amber Liu, short-haired, boy-cut jacket, mid-yawn.

“Eonni—” Krystal’s lip was fully deployed. “Dinner, no invitation. Chauffeur duty, mandatory.”

“Seriously,” Amber agreed, around the yawn. “We JUST finished schedules.”

Jessica ruffled her sister’s hair. “Big meal next time. Tonight, help eonni.” She turned. “Soo-jung drives my car and takes you home first. Amber follows in yours.”

Thorough, even three glasses in.

“Thank you both for the trouble.”

“No trouble at ALL~” Krystal’s sulk inverted instantly to full wattage. “Ji-ho Oppa — I’m a FAN. ‘Spring Day’ is my entire life!”

“When does the film open, Oppa? I’m first in line,” Amber added.

The two of them, bright and loud, rinsed the last of the heaviness out of the air.

✦   ✦   ✦

Krystal drove the white BMW; he rode shotgun; Jessica sat behind, silent, watching the night pour past — her profile, in the strobe of the streetlights, gone cool and distant again.

The road climbed toward the hillside; the villa’s gate swung open on a three-story modern build, white walls and glass luminous against the dark.

“Wah—” Krystal’s eyes went round. “Oppa, your HOUSE—”

Behind them, Amber — following in his car — whistled audibly through the open window.

The cars pulled up the ring drive. He stepped out. “Thank you both. Come in for a drink?”

“No no — it’s late, we still have to deliver eonni.”

Jessica got out too. She stood by the car a moment; the hill wind moved her hair and the hem of the silk. It ran cooler up here — her arms crossed against it, instinctive.

“I’ll — walk you in,” she said.

He glanced at her. “All right.”

They followed the flagstones toward the door, the two in the cars waiting behind. A few steps in, she wavered — the mountain air had blown the room’s warmth off her, and the wine, on cue, arrived all at once. Stiletto heels; uneven stone; one wrong plant of a foot, and she pitched forward—

“Careful—!”

He had her before the fall finished starting — one arm around her waist, the other hand closing on her arm. Which left her, in the arithmetic of momentum, entirely against his chest.

The champagne silk had slipped its geometry; the open collar had become considerably more open; and the moonlight was being unreasonably specific about porcelain skin and a fine dark edge of lace.

His breath checked, briefly.

He was not a saint. He had never once claimed to be. And the composition at close range — the ice princess disheveled, flushed, unfocused, warm in his arms — was direct fire at everything unsaintly in him.

She noticed. All of it.

The first response was reflex — the flare of heat in her face, the instinct to shove clear. And half a second behind it, arriving on a completely different channel, cold and clear as a struck bell:

He has a weakness for women. She’d seen him with Taeyeon; she’d heard Tiffany describe the studio — the focus, and the danger under it. The man ran on appetite and had never pretended otherwise.

Could that be USED?

Her pulse went ragged at her own thought. She lifted her head, slowly. In the moonlight the face above her was implausible — and the eyes on her held open, unhidden appreciation, and underneath it, unhidden want.

“S-sorry—” she said, very quietly. And did not step back.

Her hand, instead, closed lightly on the front of his shirt — a woman steadying herself, technically.

Their breath tangled in the cold air. He could smell the top notes of her perfume burning off over the wine, and under both, something warmer with no product name.

“Can you stand?” His voice had gone low and grained.

“Maybe— still a little dizzy.” Hers had gone soft — with, unmistakably, a thread of aegyo wound in. “Steady me? Just a little.”

The corner of his mouth rose, one slow degree.

He saw the entire mechanism — the calculation behind the softness, plain as a blueprint. And? Let her scheme. Schemes this pretty deserved an audience.

He kept his arm at her waist and walked her, swaying, up to the door. At the keypad, the lock clicked open into the waiting dark of the house.

“Coming in for a minute?” The night made his voice lower than he’d planned it. “Tea. For the wine.”

Her heart was hammering now, honestly hammering.

In. Or not in. A door — and a lever, waiting to be picked up.

She looked up at him, and for once the famous eyes hid nothing: hesitation and struggle, fully legible, changing shape by the second.

“…Better not,” she said at last, barely voiced. “Soo-jung’s waiting.”

She’d meant to use his appetite — as a tool, nothing more. But tonight ran too dangerous by half. She wanted leverage. She had no intention — none — of actually stepping into Taeyeon’s love story.

…None. Probably.
