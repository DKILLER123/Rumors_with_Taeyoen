Chapter One Hundred Fifty-Three

The Victory Banquet

December 2012 Seoul — airports, stages, and the Grand Hyatt the year-end sprint, and a crown

The airport concourse churned — voices, announcements, the grind of suitcase wheels.

Dong-hwan followed the line of Ji-ho’s gaze to Ji-eun, standing a distance away with a face like closed weather, and leaned in, voice lowered.

“Did something happen between you and Ji-eun?”

“Nothing at all. Hyung — what’s the schedule looking like?” The pivot was seamless.

Dong-hwan let it go and produced his notes.

“Music shows first. Then Europe — the L’Oréal and Gucci signing events, plus the campaign shoots. And I’ve closed several new endorsements: Samsung Galaxy, an SK compact washing machine, Binggrae banana milk, and a razor brand. All vetted, all clean.”

“Understood.”

“Oh — and your new place is settled. Want help with the move?”

“January. New year, new house — a proper fresh start.”

“Done.”

✦   ✦   ✦

And with that, the machine of December engaged its gears.

DECEMBER 2012 · SCHEDULE BOARD

Dec 1 — Album fan-signing: queues out the doors, every signature personal.

Dec 2 — Samsung Galaxy campaign shoot.

Dec 7–9 — Music Bank, Music Core, Inkigayo: first place, first place, first place.

Dec 10 — Europe. The L’Oréal signing ceremony — tailored, fluent, at ease among the executives — then straight into the campaign shoot against old-world streets.

Dec 14 — The Gucci launch event; back to Seoul the same week, and the Inkigayo triple crown sealed.

Dec 17 — Fan-signing tour: the China stop.

Dec 18 — Gucci campaign shoot.

Dec 21 — Back on the peninsula: music shows, endorsement shoots, variety spots, corporate stages — stacked edge to edge, then the Southeast Asia signing tour.

Year-end: the whole industry spinning like a top. IU disappears into a drama shoot. Girls’ Generation crosses time zones weekly. Everyone busy; nobody’s calendar aligns.

Time slid past inside the blur, and suddenly it was Christmas Eve. On the peninsula, the borrowed holidays had long since taken root alongside the old ones — and for entertainers, the season had one great mercy: when everyone is this busy, a phone call counts as presence, and gifts can wait for the next real meeting.

✦   ✦   ✦

He came back to the company dragging the fatigue of a fan meeting behind him — and Dong-hwan came off the elevator at a run and hugged him hard enough to knock the tiredness loose.

“Ji-ho! SUNYE passed Super Junior’s sixth album — you’re the YEAR’S ALBUM SALES CHAMPION!”

“Really?” The exhaustion evaporated by half on the spot.

“Mm!” Dong-hwan was nodding hard enough to hurt himself. “Domestic: 330,000. China: 230,000! Southeast Asia: 60,000! Gaon cumulative downloads around 6.6 MILLION — and Chinese platforms add another 4.8 million on top!”

He ran the arithmetic at speed. Super Junior still led him by fifty thousand at home — but overseas there was simply no contest. The Chinese market’s scale was exactly why SM had loaded EXO with Chinese members. And there was a quieter current in the numbers too: plenty of listeners who wouldn’t buy a Korean-American artist’s physical album still downloaded the songs — which sent his digital figures into orbit. Stack the +30% Boost Card on top of an album that had earned its “masterpiece” tag honestly, and a rookie soloist had just out-sold every idol team in the country.

“Come on — office. Details inside.”

✦   ✦   ✦

He stood at the office window, watching the Christmas lights run their circuits down the Seoul streets, his phone buzzing itself hoarse in his pocket — congratulations, presumably, in bulk.

“The company’s throwing your victory banquet tomorrow — Grand Hyatt.” Dong-hwan came in with a planning folder and a grin he had no intention of suppressing. “The CEO approved extra budget himself. This one gets celebrated PROPERLY.”

“First soloist in nearly a decade to beat every idol team outright.” He set the folder on the desk. “The media’s lost its mind — you’ve been Naver’s number one trending search since morning. The entire industry is watching you right now.”

He leafed through the plan. Ballroom, black and gold, curated press list — the company was spending real money on this one.

“Will Ji-eun come?” Asked with studied carelessness.

Dong-hwan hesitated a half-second. As the company’s leading lady she’d certainly been invited — but the temperature between those two lately read arctic, and he genuinely couldn’t call it.

“Invitation’s sent. She’s still on set — no confirmation either way.”

A small nod. Nothing further.

✦   ✦   ✦
December 25 · Christmas night Grand Hyatt Seoul — the ballroom black and gold, and one arrival in ivory

The ballroom wore black and gold — restraint and money in equal measure. On the center screen, the SUNYE music videos rotated with six months of highlight footage: every frame a rung on a ladder climbed appallingly fast.

He stood at the entrance in a new-season black Gucci suit, receiving the room — industry peers, press, executives — with a bow calibrated to each.

“Ji-ho-ssi, congratulations! Twenty years in this business and I have never seen numbers like these—” A veteran critic had his hand and would not release it. “At your age! The future is genuinely frightening!”

“Thank you. I’ll keep working.” The bow was exact; the modesty landed.

Halfway through the evening, CEO Park Sung-hoon took the stage.

“Tonight we gather for a historic moment. Ji-ho — LOEN’s pride — has proven that the essence of music lies not in format but in QUALITY. SUNYE is more than a record. It is the crystallization of a dream, and a sincere gift to everyone who loves music—”

He listened politely from the floor. Executives did love the sound of boilerplate.

Then it was his turn at the microphone.

“Thank you all for coming tonight. This result doesn’t belong to me. It belongs to every fan whose support turned into fuel. It belongs to the company team who built the stage under my feet. It belongs to every person who helped me get here—”

The applause was warm and real. And standing up there, he made a private discovery: it turns out the boilerplate writes itself the moment they hand you the microphone.

The press scrum followed — microphones rising like a tide.

“As a soloist who beat every idol group for the sales crown — how does it feel?”

“What’s the secret of this success?”

“Plans for next year? Would you consider acting?”

He fielded them one by one, composed and quick.

And near the end of it, the ballroom doors eased open — and a familiar figure stood in the frame. Ji-eun, in a plain ivory coat, no styling to speak of, and the room’s attention bent toward her anyway.

His answer caught for exactly one syllable, then leveled: “…music will always come first. But given the right script, the right role to test myself against — I’d be willing to try, and widen the territory.”

When the interviews released him, he crossed to her.

“I thought you weren’t coming.”

“Don’t flatter yourself.” Her voice stayed low; her eyes stayed elsewhere. “I just didn’t want rumors of a feud getting out. It’d be trouble for the company — for everyone. Too much hassle.”

The air between them had a pressure signature the whole room could apparently read; people gave them a courteous radius.

Shin Min-hyuk arrived, glass in hand, smiling with executive precision.

“Ji-ho, Ji-eun — a toast.”

“Ne, bonbujang-nim.” Two glasses rose from the passing tray; three glasses touched.

“Geonbae!”

“You two are this company’s pride. The best female soloist. The best male soloist. I want us all to go a long way together — you understand what I mean, yes?”

There were words inside the words. The division head had heard the same building gossip everyone had, and wanted nothing on the books that could dent the company’s interests. He trusted the two of them to be smart about it.

A beat of silence — then Ji-eun produced a smile of professional sweetness.

“Of course, bonbujang-nim. We’ll bring the company even more to celebrate.”

“You have nothing to worry about. We’ll give it everything.”

“Good, good.” Satisfied, he moved along.

“Can we talk?” Her eyes came to Ji-ho at last, carrying something serious.

He nodded, and they slipped the crowd for the terrace.

✦   ✦   ✦

Seoul at night, the wind honed sharp, N Seoul Tower burning bright against the Christmas dark. She folded her arms on the railing, the city’s lights doubled in her eyes.

“You really are good at doing the things nobody believes.” She broke the silence first, something wry in it.

He drew his collar closed against the wind and looked at her profile.

“Thank you.”

“For what?”

“Our business. You never told Taeyeon—”

“Told her WHAT? That I lost?” She cut him off, chin lifting. “Ji-ho. Answer me honestly. Besides Taeyeon — are there other women?”

A hesitation. Then a slow nod.

“There are.”

“Shibal. Unbelievable.” The laugh that escaped her had no floor under it. A pause; then, quieter, with real regret: “I feel sorry for Taeyeon. Genuinely.”

“You’re not the fish. How would you know whether the fish is happy?”

“…What is that supposed to mean?”

“It means: whether it’s worth it isn’t your call to make.”

He left the accounting unspoken: Taeyeon had grown four full centimeters under his influence and now stood at 162, insufferably pleased about it daily; her popularity was climbing toward the group’s front rank. Whatever else his greed cost the women in his orbit, what they got in exchange existed nowhere else on the market — except, of course, the one thing that made the exchange defensible.

“Hah. The self-satisfaction on this man.” She shook her head. “Fine. In public and at the company, we keep it civil — ordinary colleagues. Cleaner for everyone.”

“No need.”

“Mwo?” She turned to stare at him.

“Ji-eun. I told you. I won’t spend my life as a performer who smiles for a living. What I said in Hong Kong — I’m going to do it.”

Somewhere across the city a bell began to swing, announcing Christmas into the cold air. She studied the man in front of her, and the ledger recalculated itself against her will: debut to All-Kill in three months. A CF song that made him advertising’s favorite face. And now — the year’s album crown on a debut record. Every line of it read like a statistical error. A ridiculous thought surfaced: he might actually pull it off. She drowned the thought on reflex — three hundred billion won, IMPOSSIBLE — but it bobbed straight back up. And if he did… being his — no. And yet. The man kept manufacturing miracles.

“I keep my word, Ji-ho. But I won’t wait forever.” Her eyes came up, resolute and clear. “I’m twenty. I’ll give you THREE YEARS — and for three years I won’t date. If you can buy LOEN within three years, I’ll honor the bet, all of it. And if you CAN’T—” a pause, precise as a contract clause — “then when I open my own company someday, you come work as my producer. On MY terms.”

“That’s a new clause.”

“Because I, Ji-eun, am WORTH the premium.”

Sober now, she’d concluded the original wager underpriced her badly. And underneath that — the itching suspicion that he wasn’t bluffing at all.

He looked at the stubborn, luminous girl in front of him, and suddenly laughed.

“The name Ji-eun is worth it. Deal.”

A cheer went up inside — through the glass, Dong-hwan was raising a glass in their direction, beaming.

“We should go back.”

“Mm.”

They turned together and walked back into the light — cake, champagne detonating gold under the chandeliers, the room full of laughter. She stopped at the crowd’s edge and watched him be surrounded, lit up like the year’s brightest object.

This man… might really be capable of anything.

In the car afterward, eyes closed as the manager drove, he sank into the interface instead of sleep.
