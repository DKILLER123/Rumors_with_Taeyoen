Chapter Fifty-Nine

Accepting That Life Won’t Always Comply

“Ji-ho hyung — water.”

A bottle arced across the practice room; he caught it one-handed.

“Thanks.”

“What’s with the formality?” Hou Minghao, well into his own break, patted the floor beside him. “Sit a minute, hyung.”

“I’m good — going to stretch it out.”

He set the bottle down and sank into a lunge, working the front of the thigh. Minghao cracked his own bottle, took a sip, and lit up.

“Hyung. FIRST place at monthlies. You’re actually insane. Unlike me — I got chewed out today.”

“Your dancing was fine. That song was just brutal.”

“It WAS brutal.” A head-shake. “But you know how it is for us foreign trainees — first year here, nobody listens to a word we say. No standing at all.”

“Yeah.” The agreement came out quieter than he intended. “It’s hard.”

“But hyung — this month you made every foreign kid in this building walk taller. Seriously.” Then the grin dimmed a shade. “Just… watch your back, okay? The number of people jealous of you right now is not small.”

“Noted. Thank you.”

“Okay — back to it for me.”

Minghao bounced up and rejoined the mirror. Ji-ho switched legs and let the stretch burn, thinking. Hou Minghao — a year and a half at SM before returning to China, where things would go rather well for him. The resentment they both navigated had a simple root: trainee competition was already a bloodsport, and then EXO’s debut roster had handed three slots to Chinese members and one to a Chinese-Canadian. To the passed-over locals, every foreign face in the building now looked like a stolen seat. It made the two of them — the American and the Chinese kid — allies of circumstance, friendly in the way of soldiers in the same trench.

Stretching done, he was soaked through — shirt heavy enough to wring. In the changing room, he did exactly that, over the communal bucket in the corner: the bucket that filled, most days, more than half-deep with wrung-out sweat. That was the Peninsula’s trainee system, measured in liters. Two to four shirt changes a day was simply the weather here.

Five minutes later, clean and dry in fresh training clothes, he retrieved his phone from the locker. KakaoTalk: several unread. Taeyeon. Sulli.

He opened the pink cartoon avatar first.

KakaoTalk · Sulli
Sulli
[photo] Oppa!! This is the Hyundai Accent — Hara eonni says it’s pretty, great fuel economy, not expensive, perfect for me. What do you think?
[photo] And this is the Kia Soul — manager eonni says the space is great for celebrities but I think it’s SO ugly?? Right??
[photo] And THIS is the Kia K3 — Ji-eun says it’s this year’s new model, high specs, amazing value, and she thinks I should get it. And honestly Ji-eun has a point…

The photos loaded one after another, each captioned in her particular blend of agonized indecision and pure sharing-joy. He could picture her exactly: phone in both hands, brows knitted, hope leaking out around the edges.

He smiled, leaned back against the cool locker, and thought before typing — because for Sulli, “pick this one” was worse than useless. She didn’t need someone to decide for her. She needed to believe that a decision this size was hers to make. The license had been a small step; the car was the next, larger rep in the same training program. And worst case? With her income, a wrong first car was a rounding error. The stakes weren’t the car. The stakes were the muscle.

KakaoTalk · Sulli
Ji-ho
Sulli-yah. The license was YOUR call, start to finish — and look how that went. The car works the same way.
The Accent is economical. The Soul is practical. The K3 is new and sharp. Every recommendation is someone’s genuine kindness — filtered through THEIR priorities.
But you’re the one driving it. What YOU love when you look at it — that’s the only spec that matters.
And don’t let the price tag make it sacred. Drive it six months and you’ll see — it’s a tool that takes you places. That’s all.
Trust your own pick. And if it turns out less than perfect? Also fine. Choose the thing your heart actually wants — then accept whatever comes with the choice, good and bad, with your chin up.

Accepting that life won’t always comply — that’s the step that makes a person an adult.

No reply came; she was likely asleep. That was fine. Words like these didn’t work instantly anyway — they worked like seeds. Given time and quiet, they rooted.

He opened the second thread — her avatar a photo of her own back, gazing somewhere far away.

KakaoTalk · Taeyeon nuna
Taeyeon nuna
21:43 — First monthly first place. You’re overjoyed, admit it.
So how exactly do you plan to thank me? [sticker: puzzled puppy]
22:53 — …You took FIRST and you’re still practicing this late?

Two messages, seventy minutes apart — the first all playful credit-claiming, the second all sideways concern. He could picture both postures precisely: the tilted chin behind message one; the small figure glancing at her phone every few minutes after message three failed to arrive. The corner of his mouth curved, and he typed:

KakaoTalk · Taeyeon nuna
Ji-ho
Just finished practice — saw your messages before the sweat even dried, and suddenly the muscle ache is gone. Medical miracle.

Flat on her bed across the city, Taeyeon saw the reply land and her mouth curled up entirely without permission.

“Hmph. Silver-tongued little…”

The grumble carried no weight at all; her eyes had already gone crescent. She admired hard workers — and admired even more the ones who stayed hungry after winning. Practicing to midnight on the day he took first. Seeing her message and answering instantly, gladly. And here she was, glowing at her phone because of it. The feedback loop was, she had to admit, extremely pleasant.

She flipped onto her stomach, phone in both hands, bare feet swinging up behind her — toes curling, broadcasting the mood their owner refused to say aloud — and typed:

KakaoTalk · Taeyeon nuna
Taeyeon nuna
Not bad, not bad. First place and no swelled head, extra practice past midnight… almost catching up to young me. [sticker: fake senior dignity]
But don’t overdo it either.
You’ve already done SO well. Slacking off occasionally is permitted. [sticker: cat head-pat]

Send — then face buried in the pillow to the eyes, which stayed fixed on the screen, waiting for it to light again.

In the changing room, Ji-ho read the reply — that signature paper-tiger tsundere register of hers — and laughed out loud at his locker.

When a girl’s heart already has you in it, the topic doesn’t matter — she’ll carry the conversation herself. These exact lines, sent to someone indifferent, would read as pure grease. Sent into the current between him and Taeyeon, they landed perfectly. Grease, after all, was just charm with no positive feedback.
