Chapter Seventy-Eight

The Insecurity of Youth

Without noticing, they had passed through the Huinnyeoul tunnel — a moment of cool shadow, then the sea opening wide again — and started up the gentle wooded path toward Songdo. Green shade, an easy grade, jewels of water glinting through the leaves below. Her story ran on like a slow stream, the years of pressurized silence pouring out, unguarded, in front of the one man she trusted with them.

“Later… Mom entered me in SM’s youth contest.” Her tone turned complicated. “I took the appearance award in the youth division. Joined the company. Became a trainee. Then almost immediately I was picked as a model trainee for the tenth-anniversary celebration… and that same year I played the young princess in ‘Seodongyo.’”

“It was like — overnight — the weird kid nobody would play with turned into ‘SM’s little princess.’” A small self-mocking laugh. “The opportunities just kept coming. And then… f(x). Debut.”

She stopped walking and looked out to where the sea dissolved into sky, and her voice carried something rare in it — struggle held up to the light, and examined honestly.

“Oppa — the truth is, careerwise, my mother DID push me forward at the start. Without her insisting on the academy, without her entering me in that contest… maybe there really is no Sulli. So even though I almost never felt what other kids call a mother’s love — I handed her my earnings willingly. All of them. I thought… maybe that was the one way I could repay her. And maybe it was the last thread still tying us together.”

Her voice cracked again.

“I just never thought she’d go this far. Every won of savings — gone. My FUTURE income — borrowed against, without me knowing. As if my whole value is a credit card that never maxes out. What she screamed at me today… it tore the last pretty lie in half.”

They reached the summit platform. Empty — years away yet from the film location fame that would one day crowd it. A great flat boulder made a natural bench, and they sat side by side above a panorama that erased thought: Busan Harbor’s outline in the haze, the sea seaming itself to the sky, gulls wheeling and crying below the cliff edge.

The wind took her hair. She pulled the sunglasses off entirely — the swollen eyes bare to the light, fragile, and impossibly clear — and tipped her head sideways, resting it on his shoulder. The motion was natural as gravity: this was, at this moment, the only harbor she had.

“Oppa.” Half to him. Half to herself. “Have I ever lived a single day inside my OWN expectations? The obedient daughter. The sweet idol. The company’s little princess. I played every role they handed me, perfectly, because I thought that’s how you buy love and safety. And at the end of all that performing… it feels like I never actually got to hold anything.”

She paused. Then turned her head, tilted her face up, and looked at his profile from very close — the jawline cut clean by the low sun, the clean scent of him carried over on the wind.

This was the man who had dropped out of nowhere at the darkest hinge of her life and pulled her off Choi Jae’s path. Who had crossed the country today without hesitation, walked her out of a house she was drowning in, and defended the borders of her — with a precision and a fury no one had ever spent on her behalf.

Something hot and enormous surged up in her chest without any warning at all.

She stared at his mouth, a breath away, while a voice in her skull screamed: Kiss him. Tell him. Tell him how grateful, how anchored, how much you—

And then, at the exact threshold, an older and far stronger force seized her and hauled her back — the fear that lived in her bone marrow, the self-doubt with her name on it.

Look at yourself. Bare-faced. Eyes cried into slits. Ugly, wrecked, dragged straight out of a family war zone. And look at HIM — that good, that strong, sunlight walking around in a body. What right do you have? And if you say it — if you say it and it breaks THIS — the one warm thing you have—

Sulli. You don’t get to be this greedy. You don’t get to be this careless.

She crushed the rising tide back down into her chest, where it turned into a thin, sharp ache.

Wait. Just wait. Get better. Get worthy. Become someone who deserves the spot beside him — and THEN tell him.

She dropped her lashes fast, hiding everything churning behind them, lifted her head off his shoulder, and stood — even producing a passable imitation of a light smile.

“Oppa — the wind’s picking up. Let’s head down? I’m getting hungry.”

He’d caught the flicker — the sudden weather change, the too-neat pivot — but couldn’t name it, so he simply nodded, warm as ever.

“Okay.”

She walked a few steps ahead of him, hair and hem streaming in the sea wind, the thin back held very straight.

And she could not possibly have known that this retreat — born of fear and a bruised sense of her own worth — would someday become the story she told AGAINST herself, with great indignation, at regular intervals, long after everything had come true.

Flash-forward · a lazy sunlit afternoon, years from now

Curled in his arms, watching their baby sleep in the crib, she suddenly pinches his arm — hard — with the aggrieved energy of an old, unsettled account:

“Yah! Ji-ho! The more I thought about it the more I REGRETTED it! If I’d known what a menace you were — how many girls you had circling — I should never have overthought it on that clifftop at Huinnyeoul! Ugly, not ugly, worthy, not worthy — I should have just KISSED you and claimed you on the spot! Do you know how long I agonized after that, because of YOU?!”

By then her voice is all sweet outrage and bright eyes, the shadows long since washed out of her — the girl who once bartered obedience for scraps of love, grown into a woman who teases, complains, and regrets out loud, at full volume, with the total confidence of someone deeply loved.

And perhaps the hinge of all of it was this afternoon — on a windy clifftop over the Busan sea, where she pressed down the urge to beg for love, and planted instead the resolve to become strong enough to stand beside it.

Dependence and self-doubt. Longing and cowardice. Broken, and rebuilding. Retreating — and, underneath the retreat, advancing. All of it braided together on the way down the mountain; all of it, in the end, the path by which Sulli walked out of her childhood and toward her own happiness.
