She disliked the thought as soon as she had it. The contract was real. So was the instruction, and the stage they were about to stand on. Luck did not become worthless because it had first looked at somebody else.

Her breath stopped for a moment.

“And whether I can bear your love… I don’t know. That’s the truth. My life is full of complications. People you know about, and people you don’t. You’re right to ask. Whether they can bear it, I don’t know that either.”

Her lips pressed together. Fresh tears gathered, slower this time, while she waited for whatever came after the honesty.

He wiped the wetness from the corner of her eye with his thumb. There would have been easier things to tell her. She could see him choosing not to say them.

“But I do know one thing.”

His fingers slid into her hair, his palm settling behind her head. He held her gaze, gently, insistently, leaving the question between them instead of looking away from it.

“From now on, when you want to cry, the first person you turn to has to be me. When you’re happy, I have to be the one you want to tell first. When you’re jealous or angry, when you start wondering whether any of us can bear it, bring that to me. You have to face me with it.”

She listened with both hands still against him. He had not promised her an empty world. He had not even promised that loving him would be easy.

“Whether I can bear your love isn’t something I get to decide alone.”

His voice softened. It sounded like coaxing. It also sounded, dangerously, like a promise he would expect to be made to keep.

“You’ll have to stay beside me and find out. Are you willing?”
