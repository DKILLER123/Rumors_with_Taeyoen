Chapter One Hundred Twenty-Five

One Album to Hold Twenty Years of Ballads

“Strawberry Gummies” came back to the production room with him. The board now held four: “A Boy,” “If You,” “Strawberry Gummies,” “Eyes, Nose, Lips.”

Eight slots left.

The original plan — quiet, tasteful B-sides that wouldn’t crowd the titles — had died the moment the Sales Gauntlet quest posted. Diamond package or nothing. Which meant: only monsters allowed on this tracklist.

SUNYE was a ballad album — his home terrain. So: which ballads would the next decade of K-pop produce?

The first answer arrived with a grin attached: EXO. The ballad-richest catalog of the coming years, and most of it sang beautifully solo.

Leave those songs for SM and EXO to profit from? After everything? Absolutely not.

He wrote fast: the December miracle song. The first-snow song. The mermaid’s-tears ballad — all three arriving next year, all superb, all perfectly suited to a single voice. Then the moonlight duet from 2014, and the reminiscing ballad from 2017.

Strip-mine the entire vein.

Three more. Twelve years of bar sets earned their keep again, and the pen kept moving: the untitled 2017 ballad that would drop straight to #1 on eight charts and All-Kill by morning — G-Dragon’s. The Gwanghwamun song — Kyuhyun’s 2014 solo debut, nine-chart real-time All-Kill inside twelve hours, Gaon podiums across the board. And the beautiful-girl song whose first release would underperform — and whose quality would drag it back up the charts on pure word of mouth later.

one album. twenty years of K-pop balladry. no filler permitted.

Twelve title-grade songs on one disc — wasteful? Not remotely. Half of them were due out within two years; fail to claim them now, and the money simply accrued to someone else. And the longer plan made the extravagance rational: after this album, the next campaign aimed at the WEST. The peninsula was the proving ground; the real money was global. Return from an American breakthrough, and he’d re-enter this market at an altitude no one could argue with — money, fame, standing, all at once.

So: burn the whole vault NOW, and build the peninsula’s definitive ballad album — the kind that comes along once and never again. No exaggeration: the publishing splits from this single tracklist could keep a man comfortable for life. And afterward — top-producer certification achieved — the SM wager’s settlement clause would kick in at the top rate. The famously iron-fisted company, contractually obligated to pay HIM a top producer’s 50%. Kim Young-min and Lee Soo-man’s faces at that meeting: priceless. And every royalty deposited was a brick in the war chest labeled LOEN, 2014.

Decision made, he became a printing press — reconstructing melody and structure from a memory twelve years of nightly sets had made photographic, then reworking lyrics to fit his own voice and story.
