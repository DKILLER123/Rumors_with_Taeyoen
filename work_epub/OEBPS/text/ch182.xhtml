Chapter One Hundred Eighty-Two

Cried Angry

Minutes later Ha-ra’s living room → the front step a confession, a cushion barrage, a broom — and one kiss too many

The air in the living room had set like cement.

Jiyeon couldn’t have named what she was feeling. She had no claim on the man — none — and yet the smile wouldn’t come, and neither would a single syllable of congratulation.

Ji-eun’s gaze had gone dark and fixed, boring straight into him.

Before, Peaches clung and he kept the distance. So what changed?

Peaches had passed twenty, but the interior hadn’t fully caught up — impulsive, all-in, consequences later. If Ji-eun breathed one word about Taeyeon, there was no forecasting what the girl might do. So she hadn’t. And beneath that noble restraint sat layers she liked less to look at: the two collaborations that had made them both rich and famous — a channel she had no intention of burning. The half-formed calculations about what that channel might someday be worth. And — fine — the film-lead question. If this movie hit, and there was a certain ambiguity in place, then next time, the female lead might be…

But. If this man, already dating Taeyeon, sat here tonight and openly confirmed Sulli too — then the slap was non-negotiable. She would deliver it herself.

His own processor was running hot. He and Sulli had never spoken the word; no system chime had ever ratified it; but conduct had long since signed what language hadn’t. Confirm it, and Ji-eun’s reaction was unmodelable — and if she detonated the Taeyeon disclosure, Sulli would shrug… but the Ji-yeon file would go from difficult to alpine.

The hesitation lasted exactly one breath.

Since the night in the sea: no more hiding.

A man’s flaw isn’t wanting; it’s wanting without standing behind it. And just as he drew breath to nod—

“Aniya, eonni. Oppa and I aren’t dating.”

Sulli said it first, smiling, perfectly bright.

He stared at her.

Ha-ra’s face did something complicated. “I SAW you two asleep on the sofa wrapped around each other. That’s not dating?”

Sulli remained serenely unbothered.

Dating? Ha-ra eonni and her boyfriend were the nation’s blessed couple — and it didn’t survive two years. What’s the girlfriend title actually worth? Should I fight Taeyeon eonni for it — hair-pulling, the works? She was my ROOMMATE. And Ji-eun — who thinks she’s so well disguised — kept inserting herself between us all through those practice sessions. Didn’t understand it then. Understand it PERFECTLY now: with Oppa’s nature, there’s something there too. Should I wade into all of that wearing a title? No. The little sister moves freely through every camp. Whoever ends up wearing the girlfriend badge has to court the little sister. I’ll be on “her side,” she’ll hide nothing from me, she’ll even come to me for strategy. A darling in front of Oppa; a loyal ally in front of the girlfriend; and when they’ve all worn each other out with jealousy — the one still standing next to him is me. Pleasing and enduring: the skills I learned before I learned anything else. He freed me from ever needing them again. For him, I’ll pick them back up.

So the smile she wore was radiant, and it was even real.

He looked at that flower of a smile, could not read a line of the ledger behind it, and understood only one thing: silence now was cowardice. They had slept skin to skin in one bed; “siblings” was no longer a sentence he could say with a straight face.

He drew his arm gently out of hers — and wrapped it around her waist instead.

“Actually — Sulli and I are together.”

“I KNEW it.” Ha-ra laughed despite everything. “When I woke up and saw you two on that sofa — and this one says ‘not dating,’ I nearly believed I’d dreamt it. Fine. Date. Everyone here is family; nothing leaves this room. But BE careful — if Dispatch catches you—”

WHUMP.

A sofa cushion, launched at maximum velocity, took him in the side of the head.

“Ji-eun, what are you DOING?!”

“SHIBAL, you—! Isn’t your girlfriend KIM TAEYEON?” She was on her feet, cushion cocked for the follow-up, firing as she swung. “Mwo? Dating SULLI? You kept your distance from her for a YEAR — what did you DO to her on that shoot? Shibal!”

“Yah — have you lost it? AGAIN with this?”

He released Sulli and slipped the second cushion.

“YES, I’ve lost it!” The hotel night — the last time she’d bombarded him with soft furnishings — resurfaced and doubled her fury. “You dare toy with Peaches? I’ll END you!”

“Ji-eun-ah, what ARE you doing?!” Sulli caught her arm.

“Peaches, this bastard has had a girlfriend the WHOLE time!” She shook her off. “Let GO — today he and I settle this!”

Ji-yeon sat entirely still, running a full system reboot. What… is happening?

Ha-ra grabbed Ji-eun’s other arm. “Meaning WHAT? What girlfriend? Ji-eun — talk. What do you know? Since WHEN?”

Admitting the shape of her own entanglement being unavailable, Ji-eun settled for pointing at him like an indictment. “This bastard is dating KIM TAEYEON! ‘Some’ was written FOR her! And now he’s dating Sulli on top — two boats, one foot each! Shibal — DOG of a man!”

Ha-ra turned to him very slowly. “Ji-ho. Is that true?”

Having decided on honesty, he nodded — calmly.

“SHIBAL!”

Ha-ra went off louder than Ji-eun had. Of all of them she loved Sulli the most — same broken childhood, same soft spots — and the sight of THIS man playing with THAT girl’s heart burned through every fuse she had. She looked around, seized the broom from the corner, and charged.

“EONNI, NO—!!” Sulli got both arms around her.

“Sulli, let GO of me! YAH! Dog-man! Come HERE! Aish — you really are—!”

“Oppa, GO! I’ll explain to eonni — ppalli ppalli!”

He ducked Ji-eun’s renewed offensive and ran the cost-benefit in half a second: two furious women, zero chance of productive dialogue. He made for the door at speed.

“Yah — Ji-eun, stop chasing—!”

“Peaches, let go of my ARM! Ji-ho! STAND STILL!”

Three women in a tangle; one man in flight; one white cat observing from the high ground with judicial detachment.

Ji-yeon had finally assembled the full picture. Two boats. TWO. So the handsome ones really are all rotten. And then the colder thought, arriving with a shiver: that night at Ji-eun’s — the kiss, the hands — had he been taking advantage of a drunk girl ON PURPOSE? Yes, she’d initiated. But a decent man pushes away, doesn’t he? He must have enjoyed it. DELIBERATELY. Hateful!

She got up and went after him.

✦   ✦   ✦

Outside, on the villa’s front step, he exhaled at length into the night air.

Well. There goes the good-guy reputation. All of it.

And the second-generation groups lived in each other’s pockets — would Ha-ra call Taeyeon directly? What would Taeyeon do with the truth, delivered raw like that? He had no answer. But he didn’t regret tonight’s honesty either.

Click.

The door swung; a figure came through it fast. He tensed — saw it was Ji-yeon — relaxed, and produced an awkward diplomatic smile.

“Right. I’ll be going, then.”

“Stand still.”

Her face was thunder. He stopped, genuinely puzzled. By his own accounting he hadn’t even STARTED wronging this one yet.

She closed the distance and swung.

He caught the slender wrist mid-arc. “You—?”

She pulled. It didn’t move. The other hand came around; he collected that one too. Pinned at both wrists, she fought the grip with everything she had, to no effect whatsoever.

“Dog whelp — let GO of me!”

“Dog whelp?” The temperature of his face dropped. He took two steps forward; she gave ground on instinct until her back met the door, both hands pressed to the panel on either side of her head, the whole of him looming into her space.

“I don’t think you’ve got the standing to curse at me.”

“I don’t have—? You’re toying with my FRIEND’S heart. What standing do I need?” A wrench of the arms; nothing. Her eyes went narrow and vicious, and her knee came up.

He read it before it launched and closed the last inch, pinning the leg with his own.

Which left them flush, and left him with an unavoidable, full-length awareness of exactly what the fan-nickname “dragon cub” failed to convey.

Immobilized, compressed, furious, and blushing to her hairline, she tipped her head back. “Bastard. Let go.”

“I’ll let go,” he said, breath warm against her cheek, “when you stop swinging. What’s between Sulli and me is not your business.”

“Like HELL it isn’t! You toy with Sulli AND you took liberties with me — that account gets settled!”

“Liberties?” Genuine bafflement. “When did I take liberties with you?”

“At Ji-eun’s! The night we all drank!” Her glare could have stripped paint. “Kissing, touching — what do you CALL that?”

“Are you unwell? I carried you to bed as a courtesy. YOU pulled ME in and kissed me. Twice — TWICE. I’m not a monk. I’m a functioning adult male, several drinks in, being kissed twice in a row by a great beauty — what exactly was the expected outcome? And if it was such an outrage, where has this complaint BEEN for months?”

Her teeth ground. “Because BEFORE, I thought you were a good person — handsome, kind, so I let it go! Now I’ve seen the real you: a heart-playing, two-boat RAT. Which means that night was probably DELIBERATE. An accident I can forgive. But if you did it on purpose — you saw a pretty drunk girl and just — just took advantage—!”

Reconstructing the past with today’s verdict. Unbeatable. Truly.

“Yes — I’m greedy, and I like beautiful things. You included. I like you quite a bit, actually. But I have never once been low enough to touch a drunk girl who couldn’t answer for herself. If I’d wanted to ‘take advantage’ that night, Jiyeon, you’d be naming the baby by now.”

“You—!” The word LIKE went through her like a static shock — she knew precisely what he was, and the treacherous flicker of pleasure arrived anyway, which made her angrier than anything else had. “What BABY! What are you SAYING?!”

“Am I wrong? If I’d been direct that night, the kid would be calling me appa. The record shows: YOU kissed ME first — twice — then had a lovely time at my expense, and now, months later, I’M the molester? Have you no shame? Or does a man’s honor simply not count?”

“MWO? I had a — I ENJOYED—? I— PAH! Your mouth tasted VILE! I brushed my teeth for three days! And another thing — you’re a TERRIBLE kisser, I felt absolutely nothi—”

“Mmph—!”

Her eyes flew wide.

This bastard! AGAIN!!

Hands pinned, leg pinned, she struggled with her entire body, and her entire body reported back a rising, mutinous heat. Stubborn-mouthed women, he reflected, invariably kissed soft and sweet — this one all clean fruit and summer.

A long moment later he released her.

“Well? My technique’s poor? And — vile, was it?”

The technique was — fine. Better than fine. And there was that cool, clean scent again, the one that— THAT IS NOT THE POINT, YOU ABSOLUTE—

“AAAGH — SONG JI-HO! You and I are NOT DONE!”

“Louder. Bring the neighbors out. ‘Ji-ho and Jiyeon: Late-Night Doorstep Romance’ — strong headline. I’d read it.”

Her volume collapsed to a hiss. “You BASTARD.”

“Established. Next insult, bring new material.”

He let her go. Her legs, which had been fighting a losing argument with gravity for some time, gave — and she slid down the door and sat.

Bastard. Bastard bastard BASTARD. Philandering radish!

Couldn’t out-fight him. Came out to avenge a friend; got kissed instead. The unfairness of the entire evening arrived all at once, and the tears simply came — small pearls, sliding one after another.

He looked down at her, hugging her knees on the doorstep and crying, and passed a fair verdict on himself: genuine scum. Also — the memory insisted on filing itself — soft, and sweet. Drunk girls were off-limits. Sober ones, apparently, were… entertaining. He filed that too, under things not to examine closely.

But making her hate him for real served no one.

He let a moment pass, and when he spoke the voice was level, unhurried — the negotiating voice.

“T-ara’s situation. I’ve heard things.”

The crying hitched.

“If you want to pull T-ara out of the pit — if you want to stand back up in this industry — come find me.”

A pause.

“You’ll think I’m talking big. Fine. I wrote a song for Apink — wait for their comeback, then decide. Or wait for the film. Either one will do as a reference check.”

He turned to go, and left the last of it over his shoulder.

“I’ll be waiting, Jiyeon.”

That ambition of yours that never accepted a small life. Your sisters. Ji-yeon-ah — you won’t be able to resist.

The night wind moved along the street. She sat on the step, and at some point the tears had stopped on their own.

She watched the dark where his back had disappeared, and her fingers — entirely without instruction — came up and touched her lips.

The cool, clean scent was still there. And under it, something with a temperature to it.

Something dangerous.
