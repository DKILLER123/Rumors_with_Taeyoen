Chapter Ninety-Eight

The Promise

He stepped out of LOEN into hard noon light and looked back once at the logo on the building.

First step: planted.

Now came the race against the clock — stack enough money to lever open the door marked acquisition. Two and a half years to the end of 2014. No time to spare. But the road, at least, was finally visible all the way down.

As for the debut song — honestly, “A Boy” couldn’t match “Some,” and he knew it. The real problem was that at this moment in history, SM would never in a thousand years let Taeyeon record a duet with him.

And if it went to IU? Fair verdict: her inward, fine-grained, empathy-first tone — singing like a friend telling you the story over tea — fit the song even better than Taeyeon’s did.

He tipped his head back at the sky and exhaled.

His ringtone went off — the bright little falling-for-you hook he’d set for exactly one contact.

“YAH!!! SONG JI-HO!!”

Wendy’s shriek came through at hull-breaching volume. He jolted, held the phone at arm’s length, and counted to thirty before bringing it back.

“Yah, Ji-ho, are you LISTENING?”

“Listening.” He strolled toward the parking lot.

“You TERMINATED with the company?!”

She’d never believed the scandal for a second — and sure enough, it had been clarified the next day. But then he’d stopped showing up to practice, and she’d assumed he was resting. Today the company had announced he was “taking a break” — and then, in the bathroom of all places, she’d overheard staff gossiping that Ji-ho had actually already terminated. Hence: the call, at volume.

He had no intention of hiding it from her — in a month it wouldn’t be hideable anyway.

“Mm. Already terminated. Seung-wan — keep it quiet inside the company for now.”

“So it’s TRUE?!” Hearing it confirmed secondhand had felt like betrayal; hearing it firsthand somehow made it worse. “You terminated with the company and didn’t TELL me?! Am I even your friend?!”

He pulled open the car door and dropped into the driver’s seat, answering with complete seriousness.

“Of course you’re my friend. This had to stay secret — and I just told YOU, didn’t I?”

“…Really?” Her weather changed instantly to sunny.

He checked the clock. Lunchtime.

“Seung-wan-ah, have you eaten?”

“Not yet. Was just heading to the cafeteria.”

“Cafeteria, please.” He started the engine. “Grab Joo-hyun nuna and Seul-gi. Lunch is on me.”

Ji-ho’s treat? The cafeteria’s grim diet trays flashed before her eyes; she swallowed audibly.

“Okay! We’ll wait for you!”

“Mm. About thirty minutes.”

Twenty-five minutes later, three minutes out from SM, waiting on a red light, he texted her: Three minutes away. Wait for me at the bus stop out front.

She read it without thinking twice — he’s coming by bus, obviously — and rallied the troops.

“Eonni, Ji-ho’s almost here — he says wait at the bus stop.”

“Ah, no thanks,” Seulgi refused on reflex. “In THIS sun? I’ll tan.”

“He’ll be here in three minutes. And he’s paying.”

The payer outranks everyone. Seul-gi cast one martyred glance at the blazing sky and sighed.

“Fine. Let’s go.”

Irene watched Seul-gi’s tragic shuffle, smiled faintly, and followed.

Twenty-nine degrees, lunchtime asphalt pushing it past thirty-one. At the bus stop, Seul-gi touched the sweat-tack on her arm and glowered down the empty road. Joo-hyun freed her ponytail and shook her hair loose for the airflow. Seung-wan bounced on her toes, scanning for a bus that wasn’t coming.

“He SHOULD be here…”

A black Sonata slid smoothly into the stop and halted directly in front of them. The window came down; Ji-ho leaned across.

“Hot, right? Get in — the AC’s cold.”

“SONG JI-HO?” Seung-wan’s eyes went perfectly round. “You bought a CAR?”

“Mm.” The locks clicked open. “Get in first.”

Seung-wan, closest to him by seniority of friendship, claimed shotgun without discussion; Seul-gi hauled Joo-hyun into the back. The chilled air swallowed the whole day’s stickiness at once, and Seul-gi’s eyes drifted shut in bliss.

“Ohh, that’s the stuff. I’m alive again.”

Seung-wan buckled in and immediately began touching everything in reach.

“Yah — when did you buy this?!”

“Day before yesterday.” Eyes on traffic, easing out.

She knew roughly how his finances worked, and worry beat curiosity.

“Where’d the money come from? …Wait. Not the bravery award?”

“Mm.”

His eyes flicked to the mirror and collided squarely with Irene’s. She froze a beat, then turned her face to the window with great deliberateness.

“So what’s your plan now?” Seung-wan pressed, frowning at the obviously-not-cheap interior.

“Spend it, earn it back.” He grinned, then glanced around the car. “Anyway — seafood okay? That way none of you flunks weight check.”

“Seafood. Seafood is CORRECT.” Seul-gi applauded the motion. “Carried unanimously.”

Taegi’s Shellfish Hot Pot — a local institution, packed as always. They took a private room and ordered the signature set.

While they waited on the food, Joo-hyun finally asked the question she’d been carrying.

“Why haven’t you been at practice lately?”

Seung-wan sighed inwardly and quietly busied herself pouring lemon water.

“Actually — I’ve terminated with the company.”

Seul-gi was a good friend; Joo-hyun was… something a shade closer than that. He hadn’t planned on hiding it from either.

“Terminated?” Joo-hyun sat up, urgency breaking through her usual stillness. “WHY? Wasn’t the thing with Taeyeon sunbae cleared up?”

“Nothing to do with the scandal.” He shook his head. “The company and I couldn’t agree on a contract.”

The contract. Joo-hyun went quiet. SM’s terms were notorious even inside SM. Ji-ho… what a waste. He worked SO hard— Same nineteen-year-old start, same last-light-out doggedness; the empathy hit her somewhere unguarded, and it ached.

“So what now? Train at another company?” Seung-wan set the water in front of him gently, brow creased. “That won’t be easy either.”

“Thanks, Seung-wan.” He took a sip. “This morning I signed an artist contract with LOEN.”

“MWO??” Three voices, one pitch, three identical faces of disbelief.

I’m over here worrying about how you’ll SURVIVE, and you’re telling me you already signed an ARTIST contract??? Am I the clown here???

“Debut should come pretty fast.” He was all smiles. “But — even though I didn’t leave over the scandal, the timing is sensitive. So for now, secret.”

The other three couldn’t find their smiles. They forgot even to say congratulations.

Seulgi: trainee since 2007. Five years — long enough to blow out her vocal cords and be rerouted into dance.

Irene: trainee since 2009. Three years — and if SM held a vote for its hardest-working trainee, she won unopposed.

Wendy: newly arrived, but stacked with gifts — a serious vocalist who also played piano, guitar, flute, saxophone.

Effort? They had it. Talent? They had that too. And not one of them could see a debut anywhere on the horizon.

Meanwhile Ji-ho: three months to the debut team, dared to negotiate with the company as a rookie, walked when the numbers didn’t work — and by the next morning had signed elsewhere, debut imminent.

Is this the distance between a true genius and a hardworking one?

He read the slump around the table and smiled.

“Hey. All three of you will debut. And when you do — I’ll write for you.”

“REALLY?” Seung-wan reinflated instantly. “A man’s word is his WORD. I’m writing this down! The day I debut, I’m collecting a song!”

Seul-gi and Joo-hyun brightened too — his writing, at least, none of them doubted.

“My word is my word.” He looked at Seung-wan dead-level. “You debut, I write for you.”

Half of Red Velvet’s future catalog is genuinely excellent — and SM is NOT keeping all of it.

Seung-wan looked at that suddenly serious, suddenly very close, unfairly handsome face — and her heart broke into a sprint without permission, heat creeping up her cheeks.

“Ahem.” She seized her water and drank half of it as cover. “Guess I’d better practice even harder then. I HAVE to debut.”

She set the glass down and dabbed her lip.

“Only… it’s a shame we won’t be at the same company. Once you’re busy, we’ll barely see you.”

She meant nothing by it. He heard everything in it — and something moved. Red Velvet debuts… second half of 2014, wasn’t it?

“Seung-wan-ah.” His tone had gone oddly light. “Being at the same company isn’t actually hard.”

“I’ll open a company. You all come over. Same company again — see?”

“HA. You? A COMPANY?” She looked him up and down and shook her head sadly. “So I should plan to join you at… forty? Fifty?”

“Excuse me? Who’s waiting till fifty?” He took the pitcher and refilled her glass. “For all you know, I’ll have my own company before you’ve even debuted at SM.”

“Pfft. Big talk.” She’d grant him the writing and the singing. But his own company? A decade minimum. And I will NOT still be undebuted in ten years… will I?

“Never mind whether it’s big talk.” He pressed the glass into her hand. “Just answer the question: if I DO open it — what then?”

She took the water, side-eyed him, and — humoring the absurdity — committed.

“If you actually open it, I’ll join your company.”

“Joining’s not enough.” He shook his head. “You’ll also owe me one unconditional yes. One thing, no questions.”

“On WHAT grounds?!” she protested — it was a joke, obviously, but the terms were outrageous. An unconditional anything, promised to a MAN? What if he asked something improper?

…Although. If it’s Ji-ho. Maybe it’s not… entirely unacceptable.

“We’re friends, aren’t we? If you come to me, you think I’d shortchange you?” — delivered with the total confidence of a street vendor naming his first price.

“Then YOU’RE clearly profiting here! One unconditional yes is way too cheap for you — make it THREE—”

“No! One! We said ONE!” She grabbed her glass and thrust it out in panic. “Deal! Cheers! Clink and NOBODY takes it back!”

“Seul-gi? Nuna?” He raised his glass at the other two. “In? Same terms~”

Joo-hyun rolled her eyes with her whole soul.

“How childish.”

“Or is nuna scared?” — silk-smooth. “Scared I’ll have a whole company before you’ve debuted?”

“SCARED? Me?” Losing was the one thing Irene’s constitution could not process; the provocation landed with its usual hundred-percent success rate. She snatched up her glass and hauled Seul-gi’s arm up with it. “I refuse to believe you’ll have a COMPANY before I debut, Ji-ho!”

Seul-gi clinked entirely on inertia, face a perfect blank. Wait. Why am I participating in this? What IS this?

Glasses down, the delayed shame arrived — Joo-hyun and Seung-wan simultaneously realizing they had just pinky-sworn a playground bet with a grown man. So childish. And we PLAYED ALONG.

His own company inside two years — neither of them believed it for a second.

Ji-ho wasn’t certain either. But the promise had teeth now, and teeth were motivation.

And if I DO pull it off — that promise stops being a joke.
