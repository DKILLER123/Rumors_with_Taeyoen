Chapter Eighty

First Meeting: Jiyeon

Goo Hara eyed the somaek with naked longing and sighed from the soles of her feet.

“Yeah. Truly tragic.”

(On a peninsula where the pressure came standard in every direction, a non-drinker was a statistical anomaly.)

“So — what actually happened today?” Concern took the wheel back. “Why did Victoria eonni say you’d gone completely dark?”

The light dimmed out of Sulli’s face. She built a second somaek, drank it straight down, and began.

“Today…”

✦   ✦   ✦

“HOW CAN AJUMMA BE THIS— THIS—!!”

Hara heard the whole account to its end, boiled over, and brought her palm down on the table with full conviction —

“AH— sss— OW!”

— dealing one point of damage to the table and roughly three to herself.

Sulli had dissolved again, crying in broken little runs behind her hands. Ji-ho silently raised a glass and drank it down. Three people at this table. Three casualties of the houses they were born in.

Field Notes

Goo Hara’s ledger read no gentler than Sulli’s. Her mother left when she was nine; the divorce followed; the woman surrendered custody of both children, paid nothing, looked back never. Her father — shattered — came close to ending everything, was pulled back, and spent the years afterward on construction sites across the country, sending money home. She and her older brother were raised by their grandmother and aunt.

To lighten the load on the people carrying her, she was modeling for online shopping malls while other kids did homework — and auditioning for agencies before she was old enough to understand the odds. Which is why, watching Sulli fold in on herself across the table, she understood the despair down to its exact serial number.

Watching her friend shake, Hara’s fury climbed another floor. One hand raked up through her hair; her breath came fast.

“Your savings — ALL of it? She left you NOTHING?”

A nod.

“Haish—!” A long, scorched exhale. “And she borrowed against your FUTURE income too?”

Another nod.

Hara shot to her feet.

“AAAH!! HONESTLY!!”

The fire in her had nowhere to go. She knew the numbers as well as anyone: SM had treated Sulli WELL — prime acting placements as a trainee, f(x) performing solidly since debut. The money had been real. A mother spending her daughter’s earnings — fine, whatever: clothes, household, even luxuries, Sulli would never have counted. But to burn through ALL of it — then mortgage the girl’s future income — and tell her NOTHING? That wasn’t mothering. That barely qualified as human.

She felt her own breath jam, popped two buttons at her collar — and seized the full glass in front of Ji-ho, tilting it toward her mouth—

“Nuna — STOP!” His hand closed around hers mid-lift. “You drink that, nobody drives.”

“HAISH—!”

One enormous breath. She surrendered the glass. Then she wrapped Sulli up in both arms, and the two of them cried at each other and comforted each other in alternating currents, and Ji-ho — judging this the correct medicine — quietly worked through the banchan and kept his own glass company.

Then, abruptly, Hara hauled Sulli upright by the shoulders.

“Yah. Up. We’re packing ALL of this to go. Back to Seoul — tonight I’m getting you PROPERLY drunk.”

“Eh?” — in stereo, from both of the others.

Sunglasses on. Mask on. She turned to Ji-ho, already in motion.

“What are you sitting there for? Go have the owner box everything up.”

“Nuna — it’s almost seven. We’d hit Seoul near ten.”

“EXACTLY. Eat first, drive after — otherwise I get sleepy at the wheel.” She was already fitting Sulli’s sunglasses onto her tear-streaked face. “And anyway — you live on this peninsula now, adapt to the schedule. Ten p.m. is when drinking STARTS.”

The logic was somehow unanswerable. He got up, meekly, to find the proprietress.

When the boxes were packed and he reached for the bill — it was already settled. The younger woman who’d arrived last had paid on her way in. He blinked, then let it stand: on the Peninsula, same-age tables split, senior-junior tables meant the senior paid. And the errand-running, naturally, fell to the junior — so he and the proprietress carried the food out to the car together.

Hara drove. Three headed north.

Night Bangbae-dong, Seocho-gu — Seoul a three-story villa, lights on

Hara pulled up before a three-story house and killed the engine.

“We’re here.” She popped the trunk. “Sulli — call Ji-eun, tell her to come down and carry food. Ji-ho — you and me, first load.”

“Ne, eonni~”

He hefted takeout boxes and glanced up at the house.

“…Ji-eun nuna’s place?”

“Mm. She moved in last month. My place is still mid-renovation — so we drink here.”

Bangbae-dong. Prime Gangnam-adjacent real estate, a good hundred and eighty square meters of it. Ji-eun, certified young woman of means.

“PEACH!”

IU came out in a small floral pajama set and knee shorts, bright as a lamp, and folded Sulli into a hug.

“Yah—” Hara staggered past under two stacked boxes, “—less hugging, more CARRYING.”

IU took in the procession of takeout containers — and the bottles — with rising wonder.

“Eonni. What exactly is today?”

“A drinking day. Stop dawdling. Lift.”

“Understood, understood~”

She and Sulli gathered the rest, falling in beside Ji-ho.

“Ji-ho. We meet again.”

“Ji-eun nuna. Good evening.”

“So — peach — how did the three of YOU end up in one car?”

“I’ll tell you everything upstairs.” A pause; a small grin. “Oh — and there’s a drinking-enthusiast friend of yours here too, apparently?”

“Who?”

“You’ll see~”

✦   ✦   ✦

In the broad living room, Ji-ho laid out the raw seafood platter and the soy-marinated crab while Hara reheated the sea-urchin rice and mains in the kitchen. IU dug out an electric grill for the eel and scallops; Sulli established a somaek production line.

And into the middle of the bustle floated a girl’s voice with a faint, milky tail on every syllable:

“Ji-eun-ah — where’d you put your hair dryer?”

“Not in the bathroom?” IU thought about it. “Might be in the bedroom — I’ll get it.”

“JI-YEON?!” Sulli lit up. “The drinking-enthusiast friend is YOU?!”

Jiyeon rounded the corner, hair still damp — took one look at the table assembling itself — and abandoned all other priorities on the spot.

“Sulli! It’s been forever— wah, DAEBAK.” She grabbed IU’s sleeve and towed her toward the food. “Forget the hair dryer. Sit. LOOK at this spread — what does hair matter at a time like this?”

“Ah— hot-hot-HOT—” Hara came trotting in at speed, set the sea-urchin rice down with millimeter care, and immediately clamped both hands over her earlobes to cool her fingertips.

“Hara eonni! Good evening~” — delivered with maximum impish charm.

“Oh — Ji-yeon-ah. Hello, hello.”
