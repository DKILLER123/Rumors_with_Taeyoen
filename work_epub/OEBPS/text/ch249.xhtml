Chapter Two Hundred Forty-Nine

Bae-chu

After 1:00 a.m. Seoul · hillside villa district a sober answer is postponed, a quiet suspicion takes root, and one path begins to outweigh another

Bzzzt—bzzzt.

Jessica’s phone vibrated between them, breaking the silence. She glanced at the caller ID, muttered an apology, and stepped back unsteadily before answering.

VOICE CALL · PARK MIN-YOUNG

“Sica, I’ve reached the bottom of the hill. I’m checking in like I promised.”

“And listen—whatever you decide, decide it sober. Call me in the morning. Understood?”

“Mm. Understood.”

The call ends only after Min-young receives a clear answer.

Jessica lowered the phone and looked at Ji-ho. Beneath the streetlamp, nervousness had begun to show through the alcohol.

“Min-young left. Have you decided?”

Ji-ho studied her flushed face and uncertain balance, then drew a slow breath.

“When you wake tomorrow, if you still stand behind everything you said tonight, I’ll give you my answer.”

“I’m not drunk. I know exactly what I’m—”

She took two steps and nearly lost the road beneath her.

“I know,” he said, catching her. “I’m the one who needs time. For tonight, you’ll sleep in my guest room.”

Jessica’s lips moved as if one last argument had reached them. In the end, she let it go and leaned most of her weight against him.

His current house was less than five minutes away by car. She remained quiet throughout the drive, her cheek still warm with drink, every breath carrying a faint sweetness of wine.

Ji-ho helped her into the guest room and seated her carefully on the edge of the bed.

Jessica clung stubbornly to his sleeve.

“You said you would consider it.” Her voice had softened into a tired murmur. “I’ll wait. Just… don’t make me wait too long.”

“I won’t.”

He loosened her fingers one by one, removed her shoes, and drew the duvet over her. Her eyelids were already losing their battle with the alcohol.

“Sleep. We’ll speak tomorrow.”

She whispered something he could not make out and finally slipped into deep, even breathing.

Ji-ho remained beside the bed for a moment, looking down at her unguarded face. Awake, Jessica carried herself like polished ice. Asleep, she had the still, self-contained beauty of a cat curled beyond anyone’s reach.

He left the guest room door slightly open, placed a bottle of water and a wastebasket within reach, and sent Min-young a brief message confirming that Jessica was safe.

Irene’s apartment · deep night one lamp left burning

A warm floor lamp glowed in the living room when Ji-ho returned.

Irene was curled into one end of the sofa with a book open in her lap, her head tilted as she fought sleep by sheer discipline. At the sound of the lock, her eyes opened. When she recognized him, she held out one hand without thinking.

“You’re back?”

“Mm.” Ji-ho crossed to her and took her hand. “Why did you get up?”

“I couldn’t sleep without you.” She rubbed one eye, voice soft with exhaustion. “Is everything settled?”

He hesitated for half a beat.

“For tonight.”

Irene asked nothing else. She leaned into him and closed her eyes.

There was a fragrance on his coat that did not belong to him.

Her lashes trembled once.

“Go shower,” she said quietly.

Ji-ho kissed her forehead, carried her back to the bedroom, and tucked the blanket around her before entering the bathroom.

Water began to run.

Irene lay staring at the ceiling, one hand tightening slowly around the edge of the duvet before forcing itself to relax.

A TRACE LEFT ON HIS COAT FRAGRANCE

Scent · Byredo Pulp

Character · ripe blackcurrant, fig, red apple, and dark fruit over a restrained floral-wood base

Her inference · cool sweetness, carefully chosen; a woman with controlled taste and a sharper interior than she first appears

Irene knew the fragrance. She also knew better than to invent certainty from a trace of perfume.

Still, she remembered IU’s careful questions at LOEN and the promise she had asked of Ji-ho only hours earlier: no absent women in this room.

The water stopped.

She closed her eyes and arranged her breathing into sleep.

The mattress dipped. Ji-ho returned carrying only the clean scent of soap, slid beneath the covers, and drew an arm around her waist.

Irene went still, then deliberately relaxed. She turned into his chest and listened until his breathing became slow and even.

In the darkness, she opened her eyes.

Moonlight outlined the clean angle of his jaw and the calm of his sleeping face. She lifted one hand and traced the line of his brow with the lightest touch.

Whatever questions waited beyond the room, he had come back.

For tonight, she held that answer and closed her eyes.

✦ ✦ ✦
6:00 a.m. Irene’s bedroom the evidence hidden inside ordinary mornings

Sunlight entered through the floor-length windows and spread over the white sheets.

Irene’s trainee life had trained an internal clock more reliable than any alarm. At six precisely, her eyes opened.

Ji-ho remained asleep beside her.

She turned onto one side and rested her chin on her hand, studying him.

Sleep softened the authority he wore during the day. Without the gold-rimmed glasses or chairman’s suit, he looked young again—young enough to remind her that she was three years his senior.

He rarely called her nuna anymore. Somehow, she had stopped objecting.

You really are the calamity written into my fate.

She traced the shape of his brow in the air, then stopped.

Her own hand looked different.

The skin was unusually clear and fine, her nails naturally pink and healthy. She touched her cheek. It felt as if she had just left an expensive treatment room.

Irene pushed back the blanket and examined herself more carefully.

It was not imagination. Her complexion seemed lit from within; her dark hair lay across the pillow in a smooth, glossy sheet.

And this was not the first impossible morning.

For months, whenever she woke beside Ji-ho, fatigue seemed to have vanished overnight. She had attributed it to happiness—the easy fiction that love improved sleep, appetite, and health.

Now the small inconsistencies began arranging themselves into a pattern.

IRENE · THE ORDINARY IMPOSSIBILITIES

Once, she had caught a cold and sent him toward the guest room. He refused, held her through the night, and by morning the fever and congestion were gone.

Another time, a fruit knife opened a shallow cut across her finger. Ji-ho’s face had turned frighteningly serious while he bandaged it. The next day, almost no mark remained.

After punishing rehearsals, her back and legs often ached. One night in his arms, and the soreness receded as though several recovery days had passed.

Each event was explainable alone. Together, they were not.

Someone sharing his pillow—and paying attention—could not miss changes this consistent forever.

Irene looked at him with curiosity, unease, and something dangerously close to wonder.

In one year, he had crossed from nearly penniless Korean-American trainee to a fortune worth hundreds of billions of won. Song Ji-ho… what exactly are you?

His lashes moved.

Irene quickly settled her expression and pretended she had only just awakened.

Ji-ho opened his eyes and smiled when he found her watching.

“Morning.”

“Morning.”

Her voice came out tighter than she intended.

He gathered her into his arms and kissed her softly.

Warmth spread through her almost at once—not merely desire, but a deep physical ease, like lowering herself into sun-warmed water.

Again.

She had always named the sensation love.

Now she was no longer certain love was the whole explanation.

“What’s wrong? Are you uncomfortable?”

“Ani.” She hid her face against his chest. “I had a strange dream.”

“What kind?”

She waited before answering.

“You disappeared.”

His arm tightened around her.

“I won’t.”

“Will you leave me someday?”

“No.”

“Never?”

“Never.”

Irene did not ask what forever meant to a man whose life contained so many locked doors. She only held him more tightly.

The morning was still young. Their conversation dissolved into closeness, then into a private interval the narrative leaves behind the bedroom door.

Later, sunlight had climbed higher across the room.

Ji-ho looked at Irene’s luminous complexion and smiled.

“You’re absurdly fair this morning.”

“I’ve always been fair.”

“Then I should call you Bae-chu.”

She stared at him.

Bae-chu—the Korean word for napa cabbage, turned into a teasing nickname by joining her surname, Bae, to her pale complexion.

“Yah. Absolutely not.”

“Bae-chu, you should find time to get your driver’s license.”

“I said don’t call me that. And I’m supposed to debut next year. When exactly am I meant to take driving lessons?”

“Take them or don’t. I’m buying you a car either way.”

“Yah!”

“Nothing extravagant. I know you. A practical commuter car.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Later that morning, Irene slipped from Ji-ho’s car in the company garage with the guilty posture of someone avoiding a dispatch photographer. She checked both directions, found the level empty, and hurried behind a concrete pillar.

She had asked him to drop her outside. He had ignored her.

Ji-ho lowered the window.

“I’ll take the driving test,” she conceded. “But after I pass, I choose the car. Don’t buy anything first.”

“Fine.”

He seemed to remember something.

“And, Bae-chu—over the next two months, you may grow a little taller. Don’t replace your entire wardrobe or buy too many shoes yet.”

“Mwo?”

“I’ll see you later.”

The car pulled away, leaving Irene behind the pillar with her mouth slightly open.

Taller?

She was twenty-two years old. Gaining several centimeters now belonged in fantasy.

Then she looked down at her hand—skin clearer than it had any reasonable right to be—and remembered every impossible recovery she had catalogued that morning.

Unless fantasy was precisely the category Song Ji-ho belonged to.

Hillside villa district · late morning the canary, or the businesswoman

Ji-ho returned to the villa district with Jessica’s proposal still turning in his mind.

The canary was the simpler choice.

His Dream Family Fund could place capital behind her business and generate extraordinary returns. From a purely financial perspective, almost no ordinary investment could compete with a structure that demanded little attention and promised stable growth.

But Jessica was not built for a cage.

Kept dependent and idle, her pride would search for somewhere else to fight. Inside Girls’ Generation, that meant friction—first with Tiffany, who might tolerate Taeyeon’s place but would never quietly tolerate Jessica’s challenge; then with Taeyeon; eventually with Yoon-a and every other fault line already running beneath the group.

And if all he wanted was an ornamental companion, his youth, money, appearance, and fame gave him no shortage of easier candidates.

Jessica’s value lay elsewhere.

PRIVATE STRATEGIC ASSESSMENT

The Canary or the Businesswoman

CanaryImmediate control, low independence, high emotional volatility, and constant competition for attention.

BusinesswomanA demanding investment of time and capital, but one that gives Jessica an identity outside the group—and turns restless ambition into productive work.

Long-term bondAffection could open the door. Shared interests, interdependent assets, and a future neither could easily abandon would keep it from closing.

Ji-ho’s conclusion: independence should be cultivated, but never allowed to become complete separation.

If Jessica built something real, she would have less time to audit every unanswered message and every woman standing near him. Success would also become visible proof to the others: remain beside him, and the futures they wanted could become possible.

The thought was calculating—colder than love deserved. Ji-ho knew that.

But love alone was fragile. Careers changed. Desire cooled. Fear distorted memory. Shared interests, obligations, and ambitions could become another kind of promise—less romantic, perhaps, but harder for either party to discard in anger.

He reached the villa door with the decision nearly settled.

Not the canary.

The businesswoman.
