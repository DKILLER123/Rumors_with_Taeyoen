Chapter One Hundred Sixteen

Absence, and What It Does to the Heart

The new routine: mornings of one-on-one vocal and dance coaching with LOEN’s instructors, salted with stage-presence work, posture management, the occasional acting class. Afternoons: today a magazine shoot and press interviews; tomorrow a small fan-sign and a high-school campus performance; the day after, another signing. Evenings: album pre-production, and vocal-preservation training.

Busy — but next to the big three, where rookies ran on three or four hours of sleep, LOEN’s calendar practically qualified as philanthropy.

And in a blink, the weekend arrived.

Saturday · 7:00 AM Gangnam — A Quiet Residential Lane whatever the trophies say, here he is always the student

Seoul was still rubbing its eyes as his car slid through the waking streets. On the passenger seat, a carefully wrapped gift set swayed gently with each turn: CheongKwanJang red ginseng, and a set of vinyl records.

The One’s house sat on a quiet lane in Gangnam. Ji-ho parked, stood at the door with the gifts, and took one long breath. However many trophies, however large the stages — walking through this door, he was a student. Always.

The door opened on the familiar stern face.

“You came.”

“Seonsaeng-nim. Pardon the intrusion.”

He bowed and offered the gifts with both hands.

The living room smelled faintly of coffee. The framed photographs on the wall kept the years: a young The One in a recording booth; group shots with a generation of singers he’d raised. One frame stood out — a teenaged Taeyeon, green and visibly nervous at her teacher’s side, clutching the first singing-contest trophy of her life.

“Seeing you like this—” The One nodded at the television, where a music-show rerun had caught Ji-ho mid-song, wholly inside the music. “It’s good.”

And he meant it. He’d taught the boy barely two months — by his own accounting, he’d contributed almost nothing. And yet the boy had left SM, caught fire, and still came to this door with gifts and a bowed head.

They talked for a long time. Album production and the weight of public opinion; the mechanics of high notes and the loneliness of practice rooms after midnight. The coffee was refilled twice.

Field Notes

The master’s counsel, over the second cup: “Fame is a tide. It rises fast, and it goes out fast. Right now you feel like you’re standing on the crest — but the real ocean is underneath the wave. Plenty of rookies catch fire and forget how to swim. They only want to stand on the crest. Then the tide goes out, and they find themselves beached.”

“Protect your throat — but protect the part of you that loves music more. Less trending charts, more sheet music. Less counting rankings, more feeling the stage. And—” a pause, the gaze going bright and hard, “—remember who you are. Not the one the media describes. Not the one the fans imagine. The one who walks out this door, goes back to his own room alone, and looks out of the mirror.”

Younger men hear such things as platitudes. Slightly older men recognize them as a whole life, distilled.

Ji-ho nodded, and carved every word somewhere permanent.

The antique clock on the wall struck twelve.

“Stay for lunch. My cooking’s not bad.”

And precisely then, the phone buzzed.

Message · Taeyeon 🐿

12:01 PM

Free this afternoon. Address: Banpo-dong, Acro River Park, Building 9.

His heartbeat skipped once. The image assembled itself unbidden: she’d have just finished practice — oversized hoodie, shorts, curled into the sofa, message sent, and now rolling back and forth across the cushions in unbearable anticipation.

(At that precise moment, in her new living room, Taeyeon was indeed rolling back and forth across the sofa, phone hugged to her chest.)

“Seonsaeng-nim—” He looked up, genuinely apologetic. “I truly want to stay. But… I have a very important appointment this afternoon.”

The One’s gaze took in the sudden light in the boy’s eyes, then the phone unconsciously gripped tighter — and he smiled the smile of a man who has been young, sees everything, and says nothing.

“An important appointment, is it.” A nod; a hand on the shoulder. “Then go quickly. And remember what I said.”

“Ne, seonsaeng-nim. Until next time.”

✦   ✦   ✦

The car turned toward Banpo-dong — with two stops.

A flower shop first, cap low, mask sealed, Utterly Unremarkable humming quietly: not a single second glance came his way. He chose a bouquet of red roses edged in pink, eucalyptus threaded through — unusual, and built to be looked at twice.

Then a cosmetics counter, where he recited two shades from memory: the gentle rose-brown she’d been wearing lately, and a new watery red for summer. Her usual sunscreen went into the bag as well.

Utterly Unremarkable: genuinely elite. Nobody so much as blinked.

Acro River Park. Star Counter-Surveillance: silent — no lenses anywhere. The security here was serious: visitor identity checks, resident confirmation. He gave the unit number; the desk called up; the gate opened onto a townhouse quarter folded in greenery, privacy engineered into every sightline.

He stood at Building 9 with flowers and shopping bag — and the door opened before he could ring.

Oversized grey hoodie, exactly as predicted. Comfortable shorts. Hair loosely gathered, the pale line of her neck exposed. Light makeup, fresh-faced, eyes shining at him — with one lightning detour to the roses in his arms.

Then her hand shot out, pulled him inside, and the door shut behind them.

He hadn’t set anything down before she was on her toes, arms around his neck, warm lips pressed to his — a kiss with urgency in it, and half a month of missing him, and the faint peach of her lip balm.

He froze for exactly one heartbeat, then melted into it, answering slowly, thoroughly. She kissed him almost fiercely — she had watched quite enough of him and IU this week, and required confirmation, in person, at length.

They drifted backward toward the living room between breaths; in one gap he tossed flowers and bag onto the sofa and got both arms properly around her — soft weight, warm breath, a low sweet jasmine winding through everything. Their eyes tangled; the air went thin; she rose on her toes again.

“Quickly. Carry me to the room.”

He obliged.

The way to the bedroom seemed unaccountably long, and left a trail behind it. The door shut — loudly, as if someone couldn’t wait.

✦   ✦   ✦

The sun kept diligent hours, as always — from high noon’s blaze to the long amber slant of evening, tick by tick, never late.

The brand-new bed had lost all discipline. From somewhere beneath the mounded comforter came a muffled, slightly hoarse female voice.

“I’m hungry.”

“Of course you are. You’ve been clinging to me all afternoon — and unlike SOME people, I never got lunch.” The male voice had no strength left either.

A soft small hand crept to his waist and pinched, with prejudice.

“Who— who was clinging to WHOM? YOU were clinging to ME!”

“Ah— ow— yes! Me! I was clinging to you! Entirely me!”

“Better.” A small foot emerged and prodded him. “Go. Make me ramyeon.”

“Ramyeon, at a time like this? I’ll order delivery.”

“NO!” The prodding intensified. “You made me humiliate myself in front of the members. Sentence: you cook ramyeon tonight. No appeal.”

He surrendered, lifted the blanket, and regarded the small foot planted against him — the fine ankle, the fair skin, the round little toes with their whisper of pink.

First: I am not a weirdo. Second: it’s just objectively soft and pleasant. Ahem.

He got up on legs that had opinions about the afternoon, and headed for the shower.

“There are pajamas and new clothes for you in the closet.”

“Got it. Thank you, nuna.”

The closet held several matching couple-pajama sets. He chose the cream-white cotton — first-love aesthetic, silk-soft against the skin. On his way out he paused at the bed and patted the comforter. It wriggled for a moment, then produced one small flushed face, bangs sweat-stuck to the forehead.

“Whaaat~” — at maximum aegyo.

He scraped a knuckle gently down her nose.

“Is there ramyeon in the house?”

“There is. I bought it at noon.”

He kissed the flushed little mouth once.

“Thirty minutes. Then come out.”

Her eyes curved shut.

“Arasseo.”

✦   ✦   ✦

The refrigerator stopped him for a full second.

Scallion segments and shiitake in the crisper. Peeled fresh shrimp in a storage box. A small container of frozen beef stock. Beside them, a library of ramyeon brands — from the classic spicy standby to the black-bean flavor she’d mentioned wanting on a broadcast last week. Eggs in their dedicated tray.

Can’t cook. Prepared EVERYTHING. That’s the whole woman, right there.

Then his gaze fell on the counter: a brand-new induction burner. Stockpot and frying pan — tags still attached. Spatula: sealed in plastic.

…And has the gas even been connected? I praised too early.

Half the preparation time went to unboxing the kitchen itself. Then: water on. Noodles in. Stock melting in the small pot, shrimp in, shiitake sliced and simmering the sweetness out. Luncheon meat seared to a browned edge. Two eggs, fried sunny.

The smell unfurled through the rooms — and the brand-new, slightly echoing house acquired, for the first time, the smoke and warmth of someone living in it.

In the master bath, water hissed. Taeyeon stood under the spray humming something tuneless and happy, toweled off, drew a clear stripe through the fogged mirror with one finger, met her own reflection — cheeks rosy, eyes disgracefully soft — and covered her face with both hands for a moment. Then the matching cream pajamas; then a lightning sweep of the room, all evidence bagged into black plastic with guilty efficiency.

She crept to the kitchen doorway and eased it open a crack.

Warm yellow light. A tall figure in pajamas identical to hers, back to her, bent slightly over the pot, wholly absorbed. He lifted chopsticks, tasted the broth, tilted his head — and the profile alone did unfair things to her pulse.

A person, in the kitchen of a home that is hers, cooking her a bowl of ramyeon.

A dense, grounded happiness filled her chest wall to wall — and a measurable percentage of the IU-shaped crisis alarm quietly switched itself off.

She withdrew without disturbing him, dried her hair at the vanity, and applied — carefully — the grapefruit lip balm with all the girlishness left in her hands. The mirror returned a person with glossy lips and lit eyes.

When she came out, the living room lights were dimmed to a single warm cone over the table. Steam rose off the ramyeon — golden egg, pink shrimp, seared meat, scallion green scattered over everything. Two glasses of ice water. He was wiping down the table, and looked up with a smile.

“Perfect timing. Come — eat while it’s hot.”

She crossed the room — and didn’t sit. Instead she wrapped her arms around him from behind and pressed her cheek between his shoulder blades, into the soft identical cotton.

“What is it?” He went still, voice gentle.

“Nothing.” Her voice came muffled through the fabric, saturated with satisfaction. “Just… my ramyeon prince. Your cooking’s really good.”

He laughed quietly and covered the hands at his waist, fingers lacing through hers.

Beyond the window, the lights were coming on across the Han. Inside this one cone of warmth, the steam blurred the too-new walls — and made the outline of something called home sharper than it had ever been.

So this is what happiness is. My ramyeon prince. I’m not letting anyone take you.
