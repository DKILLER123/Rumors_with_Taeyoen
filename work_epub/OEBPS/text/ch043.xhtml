Chapter Forty-Three

The Slow Junior and the Genius Senior

That Evening SM Entertainment — 2F Vocal Studio one teacher, one extremely strategic student

“One breath — ‘si’! Go!”

“You’re inflating a HUGE balloon! A balloon! Not blowing out birthday candles!”

“Si~~~~”

He complied. The results were… adequate. Deliberately, precisely adequate.

Taeyeon sighed, pressed a hand to her forehead, and closed her eyes in genuine pedagogical suffering. Seonsaeng-nim, I finally understand your life. This junior of ours is actually a little dense.

“Nuna… I’m sorry.” Head drooping, shoulders down — a portrait titled Dejection.

Her heart caved on contact. Well… my own first day with mixed voice wasn’t exactly glorious either. She thought for a moment — and arrived at the one method she’d been holding in reserve. The one that was, admittedly, a little… intimate.

She glanced up at him. Okay. Fine. The junior is genuinely absurdly handsome, so as trades go, this isn’t a loss.

She took his hand — lightly, matter-of-factly — and placed it flat against her own abdomen, expression all business.

“Yah. I’m going to sing a full verse in mixed voice now. You feel EVERYTHING. Understood?”

“Ne, nuna.” Total focus. Cathedral solemnity.

Performance

— she draws breath, and Koyote’s “Fire” opens the room: the classic duet, bright dance-pop with real teeth, its verses trading between voices, its chorus demanding clean mix transitions at the top — Her voice runs clear and ringing through the opening line — a declaration of single-minded love delivered at full spring-water clarity — while under his palm, the mechanics broadcast themselves: the even bloom and fall of her support, breath disciplined into perfect waves. The thin satin of her top carries heat efficiently; the point of contact goes from warm to burning by the second phrase, and he is scrupulously, professionally not thinking about that.

There was an old play he knew by heart. Back in LA, a bartender friend of his had fallen for a girl who swam laps every week. The man arranged a “coincidence” at her pool, confessed to being unable to swim, and asked her to teach him. Swimming is an afternoon’s lesson; he stretched it across two full weeks of doting instruction — and by the end had acquired both the backstroke and the girl. Had she known he was faking by day three? Of course. She’d simply chosen to keep teaching. A little harmless closeness, offered and accepted, compounds faster than any interest rate: one teaches, one learns, and somewhere in the back-and-forth, two people quietly arrive.

“—did you feel it?” She finished the final phrase and turned to him, eyes bright and expectant. “The vibration — the wave?”

He withdrew his palm with scholarly slowness and nodded, brow furrowed in performed analysis.

“I felt it. The ‘si’ held across longer bars — like a wave. Rolling.”

“BINGO!” Her whole face lit with the particular joy of a teacher whose student finally shows a pulse. “A wave! Exactly! Okay — your turn.” And she set her hand flat on his abdomen in return — and pressed, once, absently. …Oh? Firm.

A small laugh escaped her, tinged with unmistakable, cheerful larceny. The junior’s been doing his core work. Not bad. Not bad at all.

Ji-ho — who trained on that ab board like rent was due, and who had strategically held his breath at the exact moment of contact — monitored her expression and confirmed: the investment was performing.

Right. Time to show a LITTLE product.

Performance

— the same song, the male verse: he takes it in soft mix, half-murmured, conversational — a line about a friend who became something more — control sitting easy on every syllable — Her eyes go round. Lips part. Did he just— did the dense junior UNLOCK? …or am I simply a phenomenal teacher? Then the chorus: he shifts to full mix — and under-fuels it, deliberately. The pressure sags; the emotion never lifts off; his support flutters fast and shallow.

She frowned at the flaw — real progress, but the ceiling still visibly on him — and simultaneously, helplessly, glowed. Enormous improvement in one evening. I AM a teaching genius. Taeyeon: vocalist, idol, educator. Hah. The self-satisfied grin surfaced without permission.

“Nuna. How was it? Any progress?”

“Not progress — HUGE progress!” No stinginess in her praise whatsoever. “But listen — the chorus entry. Your sound pressure collapsed. That moment is a STORM breaking — everything before it is wind and dark clouds, and when the rain finally falls, that’s the whole song’s feelings coming down at once. Full mix, FULL pressure. Lift the emotion on the breath — shove it skyward!”

“Ohhhh.” Enlightenment bloomed across his face, reverent and total. “THAT’S what it is. Nuna — you were born to teach. That explanation was perfect.”

Being around Ji-ho, Taeyeon reflected, was suspiciously good for the mood: handsome, endlessly compliment-forward, and — crucially — accurate in his flattery, always praising the exact things she privately believed. The corners of her mouth had not come down in half an hour.

“Nuna — the chorus one more time? Listen for me?”

“Of course.” Mood soaring, she stepped in and laid her right hand flat over his chest, smiling up at him. “This time we do it together — I’ll track your breath rhythm directly.”

“Understood, nuna.”

Performance

— and the duet assembles itself: her voice bright and ardent on the melody, his rap and harmony threading beneath it, the two parts interlocking the way the song was always built to — promises of return, of belonging, traded line for line —
✦   ✦   ✦

Byun Baekhyun drifted down the corridor with a coffee in hand, feet unconsciously marking out debut choreography. Four days. Four days to debut, and the members were all still in the building at this hour, polishing the routine to a mirror shine.

Passing the second-floor vocal studio, a sound hooked him mid-step.

Koyote — “Fire?”

A female voice, clear and ringing and deep with feeling. A male voice, warm and loose, answering it. The two lines braiding together, trading and returning, complementary as designed. Just listening lifted his mood; his free hand started keeping time on its own.

Except— the female voice. The longer he listened, the more it resolved into a voice he knew note by note. Taeyeon sunbae? Still practicing at this hour? And who— whose voice is THAT? I’ve never heard him before.

Curiosity won. He crept to the door, eased it open a single centimeter, and put his eye to the gap.

And what he saw went into him like a blade.

They stood close — close — Taeyeon’s right hand resting on Ji-ho’s chest, her head tipped back, swaying gently side to side, her free hand keeping the beat. Ji-ho’s eyes were closed, singing with complete unguarded absorption. Beautiful woman; handsome man; the whole frame lit like a scene from someone’s drama — and no version of the word “friends” survived contact with it.

Taeyeon sunbae… so you and Ji-ho are already…

He stumbled back two steps, the corridor suddenly very long and very empty. One step late. Every step late.

He turned, quietly, and walked away.
