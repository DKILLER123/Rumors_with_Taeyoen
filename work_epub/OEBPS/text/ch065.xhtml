Chapter Sixty-Five

Lies Don’t Wound — Truth Is the Blade

Strictly audited, it wasn’t soft rice at all: he’d ride her name to debut, and she’d ride “Some” one full tier higher — a clean win-win. But the rookie was undeniably getting the sweeter end, which was exactly why he’d cleared it with her before anyone else. And judging by the glow on her, the bowl was being served gladly.

“Then it’s settled. I’ll take the solo plan to the company.”

“Go DIRECTLY to Lee Soo-man seonsaeng-nim.” She was suddenly all business, eyes steady on his. “He treasures talent and he listens — play him the songs, tell him your honest plan, and he’ll genuinely hear you. He thinks in decades, and he cares about the global picture more than anyone in that building. But do NOT take this to the CEO’s office first — not sajang-nim, not Director Tak Young-jun. And don’t breathe a word of it to anyone else.”

Kim Young-min and artist-management chief Tak Young-jun were pure businessmen. Lee Soo-man was a businessman too — but he was also the founder, and toward kids chasing the dream he kept a wide, well-documented soft spot; there was a reason every artist in the company bowed a few degrees deeper for him.

And THIS is why soft rice tastes so good — the life advice alone is worth the price of admission.

“Thank you, nuna. Understood.” He checked their surroundings once, fished a single gummy from the bag, lifted her soft little hand, and set the candy ceremonially in her palm. “Reward. One additional candy.”

“Yah — am I a CHILD?” She rolled her eyes at him magnificently, the phoenix eyes glittering with exactly zero genuine objection.

“In front of me? Obviously.”

By psychological age, he had seven or eight years on her — but she couldn’t know that, and her processor found the other available parse: …is he calling me youthful? Well. As the certified resident baby-face, I suppose I AM basically a child. Pleasantly cosseted, she popped the gummy in. Sweet~

He pulled on the monitoring headphones and opened the fresh recording. A demo didn’t need perfection — at their two skill levels, take one was already overkill — but the trimming and micro-alignment still made for real work, and production-room access at his rank came rationed: finish tonight, or wait three days.

“Nuna — today was a huge favor. I’ve got about an hour of cleanup left. Go home and rest — next time, I’m buying you a proper feast.”

She was, in fact, deeply unwilling to leave — the quiet of sharing a room with him while music got made had turned out to be her favorite flavor of evening. So naturally, her neck stiffened and the theater began:

“MWO? ‘Next time’?” A gasp of magnificent, wounded disbelief. “Yah — I carry you through the biggest favor of your career, and you fob me off with a POST-DATED CHECK?” Her face cycled from shock to a slow, devastated headshake. “Heavens. How does a man like this exist? I misjudged you. I misjudged you COMPLETELY.”

Her acting was terrible — the grin kept shining straight through the tragedy — and yet the ability to broadcast delight and devastation simultaneously was, in its own way, virtuosic. He addressed the hopeless ham with weary patience.

“Nuna. You know exactly what I meant. You’ve been running ‘Twinkle’ promotions for weeks — I want you to go REST.”

Words she’d heard a thousand ordinary times, from managers and staff and members — and yet out of him, just now, they landed somewhere warm enough to melt the whole performance. Her voice went soft on the spot.

“…For me, listening to music IS rest.”

(The claim was fraudulent. Her actual rest was phone games. The fraud went unprosecuted.)

“Fine — if you’re sure you won’t be bored, keep me company.”

Bored? How could a person with a PHONE be bored? The concept did not parse. She produced said phone with a magician’s fluency, opened her gacha game, topped up, and commenced pulling — occasional unexplained giggles escaping into the room.

One of them worked in complete absorption; the other gamed in complete absorption; neither disturbed the other in the slightest.

Eventually, gamed out, she looked up and simply watched him — the serious line of his profile in the monitor glow — and let herself drift. Talking music together was good. Playing together was good. But this — this exact thing — outranked all of it: both of them inside their own favorite worlds, zero effort, zero performance, and the certain knowledge that the person you liked was one meter away. For a career introvert, this was the platonic ideal of company.

Ideal enough, in fact… to get sleepy in.

Time wandered on, unhurried.

“Ahh — finally. Done.”

He stretched hugely — and got no reply. He turned. Taeyeon had melted sideways into the leather chair, fast asleep: shoulders slack against the backrest, fine chestnut strands stuck to her temple, the whole small person gone soft as the gummies she’d demolished.

That sleepy — and she still insisted on staying.

He pulled the storage drive, shut the rig down, and crossed to her quietly, bending for a long, unhurried look. The palm-sized face; the full cheeks; skin pale and luminous with expensive care; the soft aegyo-sal that made the whole arrangement read gentle; jasmine, faint and sweet, rising off her.

Genuinely, unfairly beautiful.

He tapped her shoulder gently.

“Nuna. Nuna — wake up.”

“Mmh~” Her eyes cracked open, bleary; a small fist knuckled across her cheek. “…Demo’s done?”

“Done. Come on — I’m buying you something delicious.”

“Next time. I’m sleepy…” A yawn cracked her whole face; she raised both arms overhead in a long stretch — the green tee riding up with the motion, a wide band of pale skin flashing into the light. She really is impossibly fair, he observed, with the detachment of a scholar.

“Let’s go.”

She dropped her arms and led the way out. He closed up the production room and lengthened his stride to fall in beside her.

She’d worn five-centimeter mid-heels today — chosen for comfort this morning, and privately blessed tonight: at least the height kept her from being COMPLETELY dwarfed at his side. She snuck a sideways glance to check — level eyes brought her nose in line with roughly his upper lip.

“You know, nuna — walking together like this, you really are about the size of a kid.”

Her feet stopped dead. A KID? Her pupils contracted as the parse completed. This — this LONG-LEGGED— he’s calling me SHORT.

“YAH! SONG JI-HO!”

All drowsiness detonated into vapor. She spun in front of him, hands on hips, small chin cranked to maximum elevation.

“‘Like a kid’ — you’re mocking my HEIGHT, aren’t you!”

He genuinely hadn’t been — and stood blinking down at the sudden tribunal, brow creasing in honest confusion.

“What? No — of course not—”

Height was THE forbidden coordinate. Call Taeyeon ugly and she’d laugh at you. Call her singing bad and she’d laugh harder. But call her short — and you would learn things about consequences.

Lies, after all, never wounded anyone.

Truth was the blade.
