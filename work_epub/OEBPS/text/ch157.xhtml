Chapter One Hundred Fifty-Seven

The Rescue Siblings

January 2013 SBS Mok-dong — Running Man set → a hot-pot room a surprise, delivered over beef hot pot

The Running Man recording wrapped at SBS Mok-dong, cast and crew trading the day’s-end litany of bows and thanks.

“Good work today.”

“Everyone worked hard.”

“Thank you — good work.”

“Sulli-yah, let’s head back to the company together.” Choi Min-ho’s glance carried the standard label-mate concern.

“No need!” The smile she gave him was pure mischief. “I have plans today. Bye~”

She peeled off the Running Man vest, shrugged into her puffer, made her polite goodbyes to Yoo Jae-suk and Kim Jong-kook — and then took off at a light, bounding run, a deer let out of a pen.

She spotted the car from the address in his message on the first pass. She waved with her whole arm, jogged over, pulled the door open, and dropped in.

“Waah, it’s WARM.” The heated cabin folded around her; she wriggled out of the puffer, the plain white sweater underneath entirely failing to be plain on her.

She reached for the seatbelt — then glanced at him, and a small idea arrived, and she developed sudden amnesia about seatbelts.

“Oppa, did you wait long?” Head tipped, smile sweet enough to sell.

“Ani. Ten minutes, barely.”

“Fun day?” He gave her flushed cheeks a once-over — the cold and the excitement had painted them peach.

“Ne, so fun! But special episodes are EXHAUSTING.” A pause; a sly look. “Oppa. You drove yourself. Aren’t you scared someone photographs the two of us and posts it?”

“What’s scary about us being photographed?”

“They’d post it and everyone would coo about the devoted brother and sister.”

She honestly couldn’t decide if that classification was good news or bad news. Probably good? If she and oppa ever did have a scandal, at least the fans wouldn’t riot… much.

“You haven’t eaten, right?”

She patted her flat stomach, deflated backward into the seat like a punctured balloon, and turned the full wattage of piteous eyes on him.

“Ne, oppa. I’m STARVING~”

“I know. I’m taking you somewhere good.” He moved to start the car — and the seatbelt light glowed accusingly. He turned. She wore the expression of someone facing life’s final boss.

“Sulli-yah. Belt. We’re leaving.”

She commenced a performance of struggling with the buckle — fumbling, frowning, achieving nothing with tremendous effort.

“Huh? Why won’t it pull?”

“Haa~” He sighed the sigh of a man who knew exactly what was happening, unclipped his own belt, and leaned across. “Hold still. I’ll do it.”

Cedar — clean and close. She breathed it in before she could stop herself. And her own soft sweetness drifted the other way across the console, an even trade neither of them acknowledged.

“Sulli-yah. What exactly are you doing?” He hadn’t missed the deep, unsubtle inhale.

“Oppa, you smell really good.”

It was out of her mouth before her brain voted. Her face went scarlet on a half-second delay.

“Ani— I mean— um~” Hands flapping, salvage operations underway. “What perfume does oppa use! It smells nice! That’s all!”

“Not perfume.” He finished the buckle with a click, entirely unbothered. “L’Oréal toner. I’m the ambassador — occupational hazard.”

She studied his profile at close range — the focused eyes, the straight nose, the mouth with its permanent hint of a smile — and her pulse did something unprofessional. When the belt clicked home at her hip, a small current went straight up her spine, and something warm and unnamed spread out from the middle of her chest.

She bit her lip, gathered herself, and met his eyes with shameless sparkle.

“Oppa’s amazing, though. Two top-luxury ambassadorships in your debut YEAR.” A wink. “A man earning that much cannot fob me off with ONE meal.”

“Aigoo.” His hand landed on her head and thoroughly scrambled the short hair. “Understood, little glutton. Call me whenever you’re hungry.”

Hair that no other living person was permitted to touch — being ruffled like a golden retriever’s. And instead of outrage, she sat there grinning like a child holding candy. The whole fun of hair-ruffling is the victim’s resistance; faced with this level of happy surrender, he gave up and let go.

He eased the car into the current of traffic.

“What are you hungry for?”

She tilted her head through a full deliberation.

“Hot pot?”

“Works. Beef hot pot?”

“BEEF hot pot?” Instant full resurrection — eyes blazing, a small swallow, the tip of her tongue crossing her lip. “Perfect. Oppa. Go-go-go.”

✦   ✦   ✦

Light disguises at the door, a private room, the signature set for two.

The broth arrived first — radish and kelp simmered clear, seasoned gently, sweet and clean on the steam. Then the beef: snowflake-marbled, sliced to translucency, fanned out like something from a gallery, flanked by crown daisy, glass noodles, and fat shiitake caps. Light, beautiful, exactly right for two people whose faces were their jobs.

They ate, and it was easy, and somewhere between servings she remembered.

“Oppa. The surprise you promised in Hong Kong. What IS it, actually?”

He smiled, reached into the bag beside him, and set a bound script in front of her: The Girl in the Sun.

She took it, curious — and he laid it out: the female lead, hers if she wanted it. A man saves a cat; the cat returns as a girl to repay him; they fall in love. But a cat’s years are few, and the farewell comes at the exact peak of the love — except a cat has nine lives, and so the story bends into another turn of the wheel instead of an ending.

She fell into it completely — the strangeness, the romance. And underneath the story, the real headline: playing opposite HIM. The thing she hadn’t let herself want out loud.

She hugged the script to her chest.

“Oppa. I’ll convince the company. I don’t care what it takes — I LOVE this role.”

“I went to see Kim Young-min sajang-nim this afternoon.” The smile was gentle. “He already agreed.”

“REALLY?!”

She nearly left the seat. This feeling — everything arranged, every door already opened, someone walking ahead of her clearing the road — she loved it so much it frightened her. The joy hit the top of the gauge, and the girl who never inhibited a single impulse in her life did not inhibit this one.

She crossed to him, went up on her toes, and kissed his cheek — quick and soft. Then she bolted back to her seat like a startled rabbit, cheeks flaming, eyes anywhere but his face, both hands strangling the hem of her sweater.

What will oppa say? Will he answer me? Please answer me—

“Sulli-yah.” He touched the kissed cheek thoughtfully.

“Mm~ oppa.” Her head hung lower, face red as ripe tomato, lit soft under the room’s lamp.

“You got oil on my face.”

“MWO?”

Her head snapped up, eyes enormous — shock first, then the full landing: outrage, humiliation, and fury in one package. She shot to her feet, script raised overhead in both hands, marched to him, wound up for the killing blow— and looked at that unforgivably handsome face, and couldn’t. The script descended at zero velocity. She stomped once, hard enough to tremble the floor, spun, and minced back to her seat in tiny furious steps.

Did he really not see what that meant? Or did he see it perfectly and dodge on purpose? No — oppa’s too smart not to know. So why won’t he name it… and why won’t he refuse it either?

The questions tangled into a knot she couldn’t pick apart, so she resolved the situation the traditional way: by ferociously overfilling her mouth with beef.

✦   ✦   ✦

He drove her back to the dorm after. And by the time she was through the door, script hugged tight, the knot had loosened into something floaty and bright — she drifted to her room walking three inches above the floor, flopped onto the bed, and rolled back and forth giggling at the ceiling.

The noise summoned housemates. Victoria and Krystal appeared and colonized the end of her bed.

“Sulli, what is HAPPENING with you?”

“Seriously — what good thing? You’re glowing.”

She sprang up and held the script overhead like a championship trophy, eyebrows dancing.

“Ji-ho oppa wrote a movie — and he asked ME to be the female lead!”

“Wah, REALLY? Sulli, that’s incredible!”

“Wa-ah, Sulli. You’re so lucky it’s actually unfair.” Krystal’s eyes held a perfectly visible drop of envy. “He treats you SO well. Having an oppa like that — you must be so happy.”

“Yeah.” She hugged the script tighter, and the smile took over her whole face. “I really, really am.”

Oppa not answering me — he has his reasons. Sulli-yah, a girl should know when to be a little dumb. Dumb when it makes him soft. Sharp every other minute of the day. And I WILL find out his reason. Soon.

ENTERTAINMENT WIRE · January 12, 2013

The “Rescue Siblings” Reunite On Screen: Sulli Confirmed to Star in Ji-ho’s Film Debut “The Girl in the Sun”

SM Entertainment and LOEN Entertainment jointly announced today that Sulli will play the female lead in The Girl in the Sun, a fantasy romance written by and starring Ji-ho. The film — his screen debut — follows a man who saves a cat that returns to him in the form of a girl; they fall in love across the boundary of a cat’s short years, and the story turns on reincarnation, repayment, and a love that outlasts a lifetime. LOEN’s film division produces with CJ Entertainment investing.

SM stated: “We are delighted for Sulli to join a work of such sincerity and artistry. It will be an important milestone in her acting career.”

Fan reaction: instantaneous meltdown. The nickname “Rescue Siblings” floods every community — and the top-voted comment under every article is the same four words: “It’s the cat repaying him!” — fans mapping the film’s premise directly onto the real history between the two, and declaring the casting fate itself.
