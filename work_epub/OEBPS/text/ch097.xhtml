Chapter Ninety-Seven

The Audition He Gave Himself

The attacker was caught within days.

Ji-ho entertained no talk of settlement. Through a retained lawyer, the charges went in as a full stack: stalking and harassment, special assault, defamation and criminal threats, infliction of bodily injury — six months to two years of prison time on the table, plus civil liability for the cleaning costs, his own distress, and every won of Bae Soo-bin’s medical bills, lost income, and damages.

Out of SM, out of the practice rooms, Ji-ho suddenly owned his own hours.

First order of business: a car.

The road ahead was full of driving, and life without his own wheels was genuinely inconvenient. The license came first — and came easily. He’d been driving since sixteen, LA-raised, freeway-fluent; the Korean licensing course barely noticed him passing through, done in a single day.

Then ₩40 million on a Hyundai Sonata — sharp enough styling, generous cabin, well-equipped for the price. And on the peninsula, domestic cars held their value; the used market was deep, and a Hyundai would be painless to sell when the time came.

June 20, 2012 LOEN Entertainment — Gangnam the door he chose for himself

2012 was LOEN’s pivot year — the start of its transformation from a distribution powerhouse into a full entertainment production house.

And Ji-ho had chosen it for more than the pipeline.

Field Notes

The two-year window, as only a regressor could see it: in 2014, the peninsula’s Fair Trade Act revisions would force holding companies to own subsidiaries either outright or below the threshold — pushing SK Group to choose between buying out LOEN completely or exiting. At the end of 2014, SK would sell its 61.4% controlling stake to the private equity firm Affinity Equity Partners.

That was acquisition window number one. A beloved artist raised inside the house, bidding against a faceless fund? The optics — and the odds — favored the artist, if the artist had the money. And if 2014 slipped past, there was still 2016, when Affinity would sell LOEN onward — to Kakao. Against Kakao, no chance. So the real shot was end of 2014.

One more prize inside the box: LOEN held 7.19% of JYP. Take LOEN, and you were suddenly a major shareholder of JYP — in 2014, a JYP bruised by its American misadventure, financially strained, stock in the basement. And in 2015, TWICE would debut, and that stock would climb like it had somewhere to be. Two years. TWO years. Nobody dreams of working for someone else forever.

Ji-ho straightened his jacket and walked through LOEN’s front doors unhurried.

He’d booked ahead. The front desk announced him, and he was shown into a wide conference room — floor-to-ceiling glass, the Gangnam sprawl glittering beyond it, and across the long table, the people who would decide whether he walked out signed.

Shin Min-hyuk: head of the Music Division, around forty, composed, eyes like a ledger — the man who held the keys to artist planning, album production, and every resource that mattered. He was leafing through Ji-ho’s slim file, face giving away nothing.

Kim Si-won: head of the production center, a veteran producer by pedigree, loosely curled hair, black-framed glasses, visibly more interested in the music than the paperwork.

Lee Ji-yeon: chief of Artist Development, a crisp, quick-eyed woman whose job was smelling market potential on new faces.

And — a mild surprise — seated beside Shin Min-hyuk, in a plain hoodie and an air of fresh-scrubbed curiosity, studying him with open interest: Ji-eun.

At the sight of him, surprise crossed her face — then a smiling nod. She’d come in to discuss schedules and been drafted by the division head to lend a musician’s instinct as “informal reference.”

“Ji-ho-ssi. We’ve reviewed your materials.” Shin Min-hyuk set down the file and went straight at it. “SM’s pre-debut team — and then some well-publicized turbulence. We’d like your real reasons for choosing LOEN. Not the ‘I admire your platform’ speech.”

Pressure, applied immediately.

Ji-ho met the row of eyes, voice even.

“I chose LOEN because it isn’t just the largest distribution platform — it’s a complete ecosystem for maximizing what music is worth. And what I bring is the value itself.”

He stepped past any comment on SM and cut to the center.

“Rather than talk — let me play.”

Talent is the strongest chip at any table.

He ignored the expensive monitors on the wall and walked instead to the idle upright piano at the side of the room. Sat. Kim Si-won’s eyebrow went up.

“First — ‘A Boy.’”

Performance

His hands come down — and the room jumps.

A driving, percussive piano attack, nothing like the track’s electronic skin: stripped to keys alone, the melody shows its blade — all momentum and boyish defiance. His voice rides it clear and bright, and the hook detonates with the confidence of a finished headliner, not an applicant.

Shin Min-hyuk’s finger taps once against the table. Lee Ji-yeon’s pen is suddenly moving. IU has her chin in her hand, entirely still.

The last chord rang off. He didn’t pause.

“Second — ‘If You.’”

Performance

The piano turns over into something soft and grieving.

He drops his register — low, textured, a fine grain of huskiness — and over the barest accompaniment, the song becomes pure storytelling: loss narrated so intimately the room forgets to breathe. Kim Si-won straightens without noticing he’s done it. A producer knows exactly how rare this is — a song that needs nothing but melody and a voice to draw blood.

IU’s expression climbs a staircase: appreciation… astonishment… her mouth a small round O.

That flawless tone. The invisible breath control. That emotional throughput—! IU’s current ideal type, as she’d tell anyone who asked, was “someone who knows music.” Lately she’d been quietly admiring Jang Ki-ha. But this Ji-ho seemed… stronger. And unfairly better-looking.

“Last one — ‘My Destiny.’”

Performance

The style pivots a third time: deep, open-hearted balladry, sung like a vow. Three songs, three genres — swagger, heartbreak, devotion — each carried to full completion with one piano and one voice, each a self-contained world.

The final note faded. The conference room held its silence for several seconds.

“Wow—”

IU broke it first, applauding, delight entirely unconcealed.

“Ji-ho-ssi — the writing, the arranging concepts — all of that is YOURS, alone? The melody of ‘If You’ is… genuinely beautiful. Heartbreak-beautiful.”

Peach said he was good. Peach undersold him CONSIDERABLY. No wonder three months had been enough for the debut team — and for The One, of all people, to take him as a disciple.

Kim Si-won let out a long breath and turned to the division head.

“Bonbujang-nim, there’s nothing left to discuss on musical merit. Writing, vocal versatility, tonal identity, emotional delivery — this isn’t rookie-tier. Honestly, among ACTIVE soloists this is rare. And the writing is title-track complete, as-is.”

Shin Min-hyuk finally produced his first visible smile of the day — the smile of a man who has just found treasure in a filing cabinet.

“Ji-ho-ssi. Your ‘greeting gift’ carries considerable weight. LOEN is systematically preparing a new solo-artist project, and what we need is precisely this — an artist with complete, self-driven musical capability. Not another idol waiting to be fed.”

The negotiation that followed went almost suspiciously smoothly — because both sides could see the value in plain daylight. LOEN, sitting on SKT money, lacked nothing except talent.

LOEN Entertainment · Exclusive Artist Agreement — Key Terms
Revenue

Albums and digital music: 50% of net revenue to the Artist.

Copyright

The Artist retains full ownership of all self-composed works. LOEN holds exclusive agency and operating rights; proceeds split per industry standard.

— the decisive victory: the clause SM would not write.

Activity Guarantee

Within six months, the Company commits resources to produce and release at least one mini-album built around the Artist, with matching promotional budget.

Producer Rider

Songs written for other Company artists: ₩10–30M per album track, ₩30–50M per title track, plus 20% participation in album/digital revenue.

No trench warfare — just two parties who had both seen the same number. Ji-ho got the platform and the freedom he needed to compound capital and leverage at speed. LOEN got a star who required no sculpting, already lit from inside.

The pen crossed the paper. Signed.

Ji-ho — LOEN Entertainment artist.

✦   ✦   ✦

With the formalities done, the starch went out of the room. In the adjoining music room, IU was noodling on a guitar.

“Signed?”

“Mm.” He offered a hand. “Take care of me from here on.”

“I’M the one who’ll be imposing.” Her fair little hand took his lightly, smile all mischief. “Talent like that — when I come asking for songs, you’re not allowed to refuse.”

“I’m very expensive, you know.”

An eyebrow arched.

“Do I look short on money to you?”

They grinned at each other and let go.

Coffee arrived; the room loosened into small talk — until Kim Si-won lowered his cup, visibly remembering something.

“Ji-ho-ssi, one thing’s been eating at me. A university classmate of mine works in SM’s production division. Over drinks he mentioned you’re holding a duet — male-female — that he called SERIOUSLY good. But today’s three were all solo pieces. That duet everyone whispered about… do we get the privilege?”

Heads turned. Shin Min-hyuk and Lee Ji-yeon leaned in; IU’s chin came up immediately, eyes gone bright and greedy, WANT TO HEAR written across her whole face.

So word of “Some” traveled even here.

“There is one. It’s called ‘Some’ — a duet about that undefined space before dating. The almost.”

“‘Some’? The ALMOST?” IU sat forward. “Now I’m even more curious.”

“If it’s the song that made your internal reputation, let’s hear it,” Shin Min-hyuk said, interest fully lit. “Ji-eun’s here anyway — nobody reads atmosphere better. She can even give you notes.”

The expectant glances ping-ponged between Ji-ho and IU.

“Ji-ho-ssi — need a stand-in partner?” She was already half-laughing, playful and perfectly poised, dissolving any possible awkwardness before it formed. “I have to say, ‘the almost’ is a subject I find very interesting.”

“Then I’ll trouble you, Ji-eun-ssi. Want to see the lyric sheet first?”

“No need.” A breezy wave — the confidence of a top vocalist meeting a new chart. “Walk me through the hook and the transitions on piano. I trust our ears.”

He sat back down at the piano; she took her natural station at its shoulder, angled to listen. He played the topline through, singing it quietly; she tracked it with small nods, fingers ghosting the rhythm against her thigh. Two passes — three — and she had the architecture cold.

“Okay. Let’s try it.”

Performance

The intro breathes out — that signature late-night city warmth, two glasses of wine into the evening.

His verse comes first: soft male exasperation, fond and helpless. Then she enters — clear, sweet, with that storyteller’s diction that is entirely her own. No pyrotechnics. Just a girl narrating the almost: the waiting, the second-guessing, the tiny irritations she can’t quite put down, rendered in breath control and micro-shifts of tone so precise it feels like a friend whispering her most private account directly into your ear.

The two voices braid. Her timbre runs like springwater through the song, giving the ambiguity a light, lived-in dailiness; his warmth pulls against her fineness in perfect tension — push, and pull, and push.

Last chord. One beat of pure silence — then the room breaks into real applause.

“DAEBAK. Just — daebak.” Kim Si-won didn’t ration the praise. “Ji-eun’s tone and narrative instinct pulled the entire more-than-friends-less-than-lovers atmosphere out into the open. That’s the song, fully alive.”

Shin Min-hyuk’s eyes had gone sharp with a different kind of light. He leaned in, tempo rising.

“This is EXACTLY what we need, exactly now. Ji-ho-ssi — the heat from the Taeyeon episode hasn’t fully cooled. The public is still curious about you. This is the window for your solo debut, and your skills are beyond sufficient.”

“Now imagine the debut is a high-grade male-female duet with our own top vocalist — with IU. The talk-value maxes out on its own. From ‘Taeyeon’s rumored boyfriend’ to ‘the voice dueting with the nation’s little sister’ — we barely need to spend on marketing. It builds your prodigy image at the highest possible starting altitude, and Ji-eun’s reach and public goodwill carry you straight into the quality tier of public attention. ‘Some’ can bear all of that weight. Easily.”

“Agreed — it solves a rookie’s two impossible problems in one stroke: attention, and a quality endorsement,” Lee Ji-yeon added, nodding along. “And for IU it’s a strong stylistic left-turn. Win-win.”

IU herself was plainly in love with the song.

“It really is wonderful. If we end up doing it together — the honor’s mine.”

Her verdict all but notarized the plan. Every gaze in the room converged on Ji-ho, waiting for the overjoyed yes.

A flawless, risk-free, perfect opening. On paper.

Ji-ho was silent a moment.

“Thank you — truly — for valuing it this highly. I understand exactly how powerful this plan is. And Ji-eun-ssi’s read on it showed me a side of the song I hadn’t heard. It’s only—”

A small pause.

“In my own planning, ‘A Boy’ was the debut track. That song carries a particular season of my life — the momentum of it. And ‘Some’… holds some private meaning for me. I may need a little time to decide whether it should be my first word to the public.”

The room’s temperature dipped a degree.

“Ji-ho-ssi, I understand a creator’s attachment,” Shin Min-hyuk said — understanding, and utterly unmoved. “But the company is responsible for your long arc, and that means choosing the highest-probability, highest-yield path. ‘Some’ with IU is timing, terrain, and personnel all aligned. Resources will tilt fully behind it. ‘A Boy’ is excellent — it goes on your album in the second half. But the debut shot has to be loud, distinctive, and strong enough to flip the whole board.”

“Cherishing your work is right and proper,” Kim Si-won added gently. “But good songs also need the right moment, and the right form, to shine. ‘Some’ meeting Ji-eun — that IS its moment.”

Ji-ho looked around at the people he’d now be working beside — commercial reason and professional judgment in every face. He knew they were right. By any business logic, unimpeachable.

And the private meaning of a song about the almost — against the great turning gears of an industry — weighed precisely nothing.

Taeyeon’s face crossed his mind. The spray-painted wall. The two-year vow he’d made himself at LOEN’s front door.

Private feeling on one pan of the scale; the whole grand design on the other. The needle swung.

At last he raised his eyes, clear again.

“I understand. Please give me a little time to consider it properly — it deserves that. Either way, I’ll prepare my debut with complete professionalism.”

“One more thing — I’d like my signing kept confidential for now.”

Shin Min-hyuk considered a beat.

“That’s… fine.”
