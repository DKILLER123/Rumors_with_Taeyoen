Chapter Two Hundred Sixteen

Eonni… I Think I Just Saw Fany Eonni

Morning, late July 2013 Jeju Island · a walled seaside pension — and a resort suite across the island two women, one island — the first live exercise begins without a starting gun

Jeju, first light.

For all its fame as a resort island, Jeju kept its best secrets away from the loud coastline — tucked into the quiet folds of the shore, scattered two-story pensions with real character and no neighbors.

Ryu Dong-hwan worked fast. Within hours he’d found exactly what Ji-ho had asked for: a walled courtyard house by the sea, two floors, low stone fences of black volcanic rock, a handful of Jeju tangerine trees inside the wall. Push open a second-floor window and the morning sea lay right there, blue and glittering.

Ji-ho drove in alone. Tiffany rode the passenger seat curled small, drowned in his oversized jacket, cap and mask on, only her eyes showing.

The car rolled through the gate. He came around, took her slightly cold hand, and led her inside. The living room was simple and warm; sandwiches, milk, and fruit already waited on the table.

“Nuna, rest here today. The second the promo schedule wraps, I’m back.”

He took the jacket as she shed it, hung it up — and turned around into her. She folded herself against his chest, arms locking around his waist, cheek pressed over his heartbeat.

“Arasseo.” She tilted her face up, the crescent eyes wet-bright, voice going soft and spoiled. “Come back early, okay? I’ll miss you.”

“I hate leaving you here alone as it is.”

His fingers moved gently through the loose chestnut curls.

“Don’t worry. I’ll cut it as short as I can.”

They ate breakfast quietly. Her eyes barely left him the whole time.

When the clock said go, he stood, pressed a kiss to her forehead, and picked up the car keys.

✦ ✦ ✦

Outside the gate, Ryu Dong-hwan was already waiting by the second car. Ji-ho took the passenger seat; the manager pulled smoothly onto the coast road, the dawn air clean and damp through the vents.

Dong-hwan’s eyes flicked to the closed gate in the mirror. His hands tightened on the wheel. Twice he opened his mouth. Twice he closed it.

As Ji-ho’s manager, he understood better than anyone how complicated his golden goose — and boss — was when it came to women. These past days he and Choi Sulli had been practically inseparable at the hotel. Back at the company, the way IU looked at him wasn’t normal either. And a hundred small threads suggested there were others still.

Honestly? In the great dye-vat of the peninsula’s entertainment world, two consenting adults barely registered as a problem. Far dirtier, far uglier things were everywhere you looked. With LOEN backed by SK Telecom’s weight, an ordinary dating scandal — even photographed — could be managed with resources to spare. Half the so-called “collapses” in this industry were just selectively released dirt from someone’s leverage game. Everyone on the inside knew the score. And with that uncanny counter-surveillance instinct of his, Ji-ho simply did not get caught.

But this. Two women. On the same island. At the same time—

Dong-hwan’s chest squeezed. He’d watched too many careers burn down over tangled feelings — that killed far more surely than tabloid fluff ever did. His pay, his standing, his say in this industry — all of it rode on this man staying upright. He wanted exactly zero accidents.

“Hyung, are the latest box-office numbers in?”

“Huh? Box office?” Dong-hwan surfaced from his spiral, and excitement flooded in to replace the worry. “In! Cumulative admissions — eleven point one two million! Ninth place, all-time! At this pace, top five by the end of the run is genuinely possible!”

A pure love story clearing ten million admissions: all but unheard of in peninsula film history. Two OSTs turned a strong opening into a national event.

A romance breaking the ten-million line was the kind of thing that happened once a decade. The genre’s ceiling had been A Werewolf Boy’s seven million; The Girl in the Sun had been projected into that same band — until two OSTs lit a nationwide fuse and the box office simply refused to come down. A finish in the ₩50 billion class. Rookie acting awards at every year-end ceremony were now less a question than a scheduling matter — for him and for Choi Sulli both.

“After the Jeju promo, the film’s main schedule is basically done,” Dong-hwan went on, planning aloud as he drove. “Second half of the year, we can start building your album comeback—”

✦ ✦ ✦

At the same hour, in a resort suite on the other side of the island, Choi Sulli woke in the wide soft bed and reached sideways out of habit — and found only cool air.

Her eyes dragged open. The space beside her was empty. She was awake instantly.

“Oppa?”

She sat up hugging the duvet, silk-black hair sliding off one shoulder, her just-woken face pale and translucent with its natural wash of pink. She called twice more, louder. Only the faint wind off the water answered.

Something in her chest went hollow for no reason she could name.

She slid out of bed, bare feet on soft carpet, dressed, and made the rounds — bathroom, empty; the small lounge and the terrace, empty.

“Weird. Where’d he go?”

She murmured it with a small crease between fine brows, went back for her phone, and dialed.

Voice Call · Oppa ♥

“Yeoboseyo — Peaches. You’re up?” — background noise, wind and street, somewhere outdoors.

“Oppa, where are you?”

“Out buying your breakfast. What are you feeling? Miyeok-guk?”

“Mmm~” — he’d remembered her sleepy mumbling from last night — “and some multigrain porridge. And the bean-sprout salad.”

“Got it. I’ll be back soon — stay in bed a while.”

“Mmm~”

He remembered. Her mood took off like a startled bird, straight up. She hung up thoroughly satisfied and drifted into the bathroom humming something with no particular tune, and warm water sluiced the last of the sleep off her skin.

She was blow-drying her hair, mood excellent, freshly zipped into a strappy little sundress, when the doorbell rang. She crossed the suite like a happy deer, half-skipping.

Ji-ho stood in the corridor with the takeout bags, tall frame backlit by the hallway.

“Oppa!”

Her eyes curved into crescents. She let him in — and then launched, a fledgling returning to the nest, up on her toes, soft lips already puckering for the kiss.

He caught her waist one-armed, bags in the other hand, and bent to meet her — mint toothpaste, warm skin, the just-showered give of her.

And in the second their lips met, Choi Sulli’s delicate nose wrinkled.

Perfume. Strong perfume. Not hers — hers ran fresh, floral-fruity, light. This was something else: a sweet, warm, opulent floral, and the base note was… familiar.

She stepped back half a pace, frowning, and tipped her small face up. The sparkle in the clear eyes had gone out; something uneasy pooled where it had been.

“Oppa.” She caught her lower lip in her teeth. “Were you with a woman?”

Something flickered deep in his eyes; his face didn’t move. He set the breakfast in her hands.

“Put these out first. Let me shower — then we’ll talk.”

She pressed her lips tight, took the still-warm containers, and set them down on the little table with more force than necessary. She watched his back disappear toward the bathroom, and the doubt in her chest doubled with every step he took.

The moment the water started running, she snatched up her phone, thumb flying.

The socials were wall-to-wall with it: GIRLS’ GENERATION RETURN FROM TOUR. Taeyeon eonni — already back on the peninsula? So Oppa went to see Taeyeon eonni, fresh off the plane? Chased all the way to Jeju the same night? The thought was a mouthful of vinegar.

…No. Wrong. She dragged the perfume back across her memory. That rich, sweet-warm register wasn’t Taeyeon eonni’s taste at all. It was—

The gate of memory swung open. Back when she’d shared the company dorm with the Girls’ Generation eonnis, Fany eonni had loved exactly that kind of scent — unforgettable, unapologetically womanly. Sulli had even snuck a few sprays out of curiosity and been laughed at for it: a kid wearing a grown-up’s perfume. Taeyeon eonni’s perfumes ran pale and cool and quiet.

Her heart dropped. An absurd premonition stood straight up and stared at her.

✦ ✦ ✦

In the seaside pension, Tiffany had just finished her own shower. Wrapped in a bathrobe at the second-floor window, wet hair loose, she watched the light move on the water, and a sweet, involuntary curve kept tugging at her mouth.

Last night replayed itself without her permission — heat and tangle and whispered closeness — and her cheeks burned all over again. She’d only ever known this one man. And from everything the internet had taught her, this one man was… more than enough. From now on, she thought, hugging her own elbows, I get to be happy.

Her phone shrilled. The name jumping on the screen stopped her heart for a full beat.

Taeyeon.

She steadied herself, took three slow breaths, and answered with a voice ironed carefully flat.

Voice Call · Daedae

“Fany-yah, when are you coming back? The company scheduled a last-minute joint interview today — about the song results. They need us both.”

“Ah— Daedae, I’m… not feeling great. I might need to rest today. Could the interview—” — the words coming a shade too fast.

“Mwo? Not feeling well? Gwaenchana?? Is it bad? Should you see a doctor?”

“Ani, ani — just tired. I’ll be fine after some rest. I think I just… didn’t sleep well last night.” — the voice floating slightly, the way voices do over a guilty conscience.

“Aigoo. How many times have I told you to go easier on the drinking? Every single time. Fine — rest properly. I’ll square the interview with the company.”

“Thank you, Daedae. Saranghae.”

“Mwo-ya, so greasy all of a sudden.” — a laugh down the line. “Hanging up. Rest well.”

The dial tone buzzed. Tiffany stood at the window a long time without moving, phone still in her hand. Sunlight lay full on her face and couldn’t touch the shadow underneath it — the guilt of lying to Taeyeon, and the pull of Ji-ho she could no longer climb out of, twisting around her heart like two ropes tightening.

✦ ✦ ✦

In the Girls’ Generation dorm, Taeyeon lowered her phone with a faintly lost look. Yoon-a, passing, bumped her shoulder.

“Eonni, what’s wrong?”

“Yoon-a-yah. Can you hear waves in Seoul?”

“What are you even saying, eonni? Seoul’s inland.”

“Is it?”

“Why are you asking that?”

“Nothing. Go wash up.”

Taeyeon smiled and sent her off with a light smack on the backside.

“Ah~ eonni!! You’re actually a pervert.”

“Mwo? Pervert? I’ll SHOW you pervert—”

“Ahaha— eonni, I was wrong, I was wrong!”

✦ ✦ ✦

In the hotel room, the water shut off.

Ji-ho came out toweling his hair, a bath towel at his hips, water tracking down the lines of his chest. Choi Sulli sat hugging her knees on the sofa by the floor-length window, face turned to the glass, her profile gone thin and desolate.

He crossed to her, crouched, and took her small face in both hands, turning it toward him. Her eyes were rimmed red — and glaring.

He didn’t say anything. He leaned in and kissed her, deep and unhurried.

Her body went rigid — then answered on pure instinct — and then remembered it was supposed to be angry, and her teeth closed on his lower lip, not gently.

“Ss—”

“Serves you right! I hope it hurts!” She glared at him, eyes swimming, something between grievance and fury. “Who is she? Don’t you dare smooth this over!”

He looked at her in silence for a few seconds. Somewhere outside, gulls were crying over the water.

Some things couldn’t stay buried. And — if he was honest — some part of him didn’t want to keep lying to this girl who had handed him her whole weight without conditions. He’d also been circling a colder thought for weeks: of all his girls, Sulli and Tiffany were the two he was surest of. If he was ever going to learn how much truth they could carry… it would start here.

“Last night,” he said at last, evenly, “Fany nuna came.”

“MWO??!!” Her eyes flew wide. The guess confirmed hit harder than the guess itself ever had. “Fany eonni?? It’s really Fany eonni?!”

The perfume matched. The person matched. Taeyeon eonni’s closest sister. Her own former roommate. The eonni she used to look up to — and her boyfriend—

“Wow, Oppa.” The smile she dragged onto her face didn’t fit right, and her voice shook underneath it. “You really are… a bad man.”

The tide came up fast — grievance, anger, and a strand of absurdity she couldn’t even name — and just as she felt her grip going, a cool, mild weight pulsed once at her throat.

The pendant seemed to flash, faint as a thought. A strange, settling current moved through her, and the churn in her chest miraculously stilled. Her head went clear and quiet.

She took one deep breath. Then she seized his forearm, bent her head — and bit down. Hard.

“SSS—!”

This one genuinely hurt. Muscle jumped tight under her teeth. He didn’t pull away.

She bit until the faintest taste of iron touched her tongue — then let go all at once. A deep, clean ring of tooth marks stood in his skin, threaded with blood, ugly against the pale of his arm. And the second she saw it, all the ferocity drained out of her; hurt and regret rushed in behind it. She lowered her head and, like a small animal tending a wound, touched her tongue softly, apologetically, to the marks she’d made.

“Does Oppa know?” She looked up. Her eyes were still red, but steady now. “Taeyeon eonni. Fany eonni. And me. For a while, back then — the company had us in the same dorm.”

He visibly stalled, then shook his head. “I didn’t know.” It was the truth; that history was news to him.

“Then does Taeyeon eonni know — about Fany eonni and you?”

“Not yet.” His brows drew in slightly. “I haven’t found a way to tell her that she could… survive hearing.”

He lifted a hand and swept his thumb under her damp lash line, voice dropping low.

“Not everyone is my Peaches — the one who understands Oppa without being taught.”

“Tch.” She turned her face out of his hand, insides a tangle she couldn’t sort.

Understands him? Did she, really? She couldn’t even say. She’d signed on knowing about Taeyeon eonni. But Oppa’s heart, it turned out, wandered further than she’d ever guessed.

She was still assembling her next sentence when her phone screamed again. She glanced down at the screen — and her heart skipped hard.

Taeyeon.

She raised her eyes to Ji-ho, and a smile of unreadable intent curled up one corner of her mouth.

“It’s Taeyeon eonni. Oppa — should I answer?”

His breath checked, almost imperceptibly. Those clear eyes seemed to see straight through him; that wicked little smile didn’t help. For once in his charmed life he felt something like nerves. His throat moved. He leaned down, kissed her lightly on the lips.

“Peaches, I—”

“I know.” She cut him off, and the smile sweetened. “Who told me to be Oppa’s ‘little sister,’ hm?” She leaned on the word deliberately. “This was my own choice. I know that. Relax, Oppa.”

The sheer good-girl composure of it loosened something in his chest — and dragged a strange cold fingertip down his spine at the same time.

“Thank you, Peaches. Whatever you want — tell Oppa. It’s yours.”

She didn’t look at him again. Her fingertip slid across the screen, and her voice flipped, seamless, into its everyday sugar.

Voice Call · Taeyeon eonni — speaker within earshot

“Yeoboseyo, sister-in-law~”

“Sulli-yah,” — distracted, somewhere else — “you’re on Jeju right now?”

“Ne, sister-in-law. Oppa and I are both here. Film promo wraps today, a few interviews tomorrow, then back to Seoul.”

“Arasseo. And Ji-ho, he’s… everything’s fine with him? He’s… doing okay?” — the hesitation of someone braced against an answer.

Choi Sulli held the phone and let her gaze drift out to the flawless blue sky — then slid it sideways to Ji-ho, who had stopped breathing entirely. The wicked curl returned to her mouth.

“Sister-in-law—”

And her voice went suddenly halting. Hesitant. Words picked up and put down again, wavering, unsure.

Ji-ho’s brows snapped together. His hand shot out for the phone.

But Choi Sulli twisted away like a slick little fish, bare feet skipping backward across the carpet, out of reach — and into the phone, in the lost, helpless voice of a girl who has seen something she wishes she hadn’t, she said:

“Eonni— I think… I just saw Fany eonni.”
