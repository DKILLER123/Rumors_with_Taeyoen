Chapter Two Hundred Forty

First Wisdom Tooth

August 8–9, 2013 Seongsu-dong, 15th floor · LOEN production suite · SM Entertainment, 6th floor a toothache, a masterpiece rebuilt from memory — and a trainee walks back into SM as its visitor of state

Thursday, August 8, 2013. Seongsu-dong, fifteenth floor.

Morning came through the floor-length windows like a veil, laying itself gently across the pale pink bed.

Choi Sulli slept wrapped around him like a lazy cat — skin near-translucent in the early light, cheeks warm with their natural pink, one round thigh thrown over him with imperial confidence, the other leg escaped from the duvet entirely.

“Master, phone call~ Master, phone call~”

The ringtone punctured the quiet. A pale arm emerged from the covers, patted blindly across the nightstand, secured the phone, and retracted into the warmth.

“Yeoboseyo…”

Sleep-soft, sugar-thick.

“Ha-ra eonni?”

She surfaced a few degrees, blinking her eyes clear, and sat up, raking her sleep-mussed hair back with one hand.

“Mm, eonni — I got back two days ago.”

“Your tooth HURTS?” A small worried crease appeared. “Didn’t the dentist say it was coming in straight? That it would erupt fully and shouldn’t be pulled?”

“Mm. Understood. See you in a bit, eonni — bye bye~”

She hung up, turned — and found Ji-ho awake and watching her with lazy interest.

“Oppa~”

The smile detonated across her face, bright as the window; she tossed the phone somewhere unimportant and landed on his chest.

“Ha-ra’s call?”

“Mm. I have to go out.” A quick kiss on his cheek. “Ha-ra eonni’s wisdom tooth is acting up — she wants company at the dentist.”

He’d caught the gist, and wisdom teeth were nobody’s idea of a joke. He patted her back.

“Go on. Drive carefully.”

“Mm. It’s still early — Oppa, sleep more.”

The whole bed smelled like her — that particular sweet warmth — and getting up genuinely lacked appeal. He nodded and burrowed back in.

She rose, showered, chose something comfortable, and sat at the vanity working through the ritual — powder, brows, a soft slick of color — then took the keys to the BMW he’d given her and was gone.

✦ ✦ ✦

He’d intended a second sleep. The idol-aura constitution had other opinions — the body simply refused to be tired — and after ten minutes of ceiling inspection he gave up and rose.

Two days of Peaches had been glorious and expensive: the backlog was real. Red Velvet’s extraction. The OST for The Heirs. His own album. Ji-eun’s album. The song promised to Sulli. And — the item that had acquired sudden urgency — Yoon-a. A certain Lee-surnamed entertainer had begun telling broadcasters his ideal type was Yoon-a, and while Ji-ho didn’t know she had already turned down the man’s first dinner, the headlines alone had him feeling the clock. Cabbage this good did not stay unclaimed in the field.

One thing at a time. And writing music was the lightest work he had.

The Heirs first. He knew that drama’s soundtrack well and had never loved it — he’d taken the commission purely to spite a certain former vice president, so there was no call for heroics; deliver a strong ballad and be done. He turned the catalog of that OST over in his memory, weighing one candidate against another, and settled on the one whose smolder and restraint would sit perfectly in Taeyeon’s voice. Rework the arrangement a shade, hand it to the most emotionally precise vocalist on the peninsula, and it would outperform its original run by a comfortable margin. With him in the timeline, her OST-queen crown was simply a scheduling matter.

Then — f(x). Harder question. Their lane was singular: not sweetness but electronic experiment, concept narrative, a complete aesthetic system that had shaped half the girl groups who came after. Every album a thesis. Whatever he handed SM had to clear THAT bar, or they’d shelve it politely. He ransacked his memory for the right fit— and stopped. Wait. The second full album. The wisdom tooth concept. THE PINK ALBUM’S CROWN JEWEL. How had he forgotten it — the title track the global press would canonize, the record Billboard would one day rank first among the decade’s K-pop albums? Delayed in this timeline by the film’s success, still unwritten, sitting there like an unclaimed throne. And Ha-ra’s toothache had all but hand-delivered the reminder.

✦ ✦ ✦

He drove to the company and shut himself into the production suite.

PRODUCTION LOG · UNTITLED — “FIRST WISDOM TOOTH” · DAY 1

Core colorMiddle-Eastern funk — overdriven guitar, Phrygian modal flavor, winding melodic line

Rhythm spineMarching-band snare — crisp, clipped, on the off-beat for that strange stepping lift

FrameworkElectro-pop chassis · syncopated mute-strum guitar riff carried end to end

LayersMinimal synth arpeggio under verses · bright electronic lead joining guitar + vocal in the chorus — three melodic strata

Low endShort, sprung bassline locked tight to snare and riff — the whole track “bounces” alive

AccentsSnare rolls + cymbal crashes into the chorus — the sudden jolt of a tooth’s first ache · clap and stomp samples for body

Concept + experiment + pop, balanced on a knife. Keep the strangeness. The strangeness IS the hook.

The off-beat snare fought him for an hour — placed conventionally it marched; displaced, it DANCED — and when it finally clicked into its odd, stumbling-forward pocket he laughed out loud alone in the room. A composition stat in the eighties was a powerful thing: whatever he could hear in his head, his hands could now build. The arrangement was ferociously complex, and rebuilding it from memory would have been impossible a year ago — but he wasn’t last year’s trainee, and where the original’s transitions had always struck him as abrupt, he smoothed the seams with his own solutions and kept the body intact. Guitar as the memory-hook, snare as the skeleton, electro-pop as the frame, dislocated rhythm as the mood. By the time he leaned back, the sun had crossed the whole window. One full day. Two songs: the wisdom-tooth track rebuilt to its bones, and the ballad for Taeyeon adapted and printed.

✦ ✦ ✦

Next morning. The LOEN production department.

“Director Kim Si-won.”

“Ne, Hoejang-nim — you’ve come down yourself?”

He handed over the ballad’s production file.

“Send this arrangement to SBS. Tell them my view: Taeyeon is the right voice for this song. If they agree, they can approach SM to commission her.”

“Ne, Hoejang-nim.”

“Good work.”

He was barely out of the department before Cho Young-chul intercepted him at a half-jog, file folder in hand.

“Hoejang-nim — finally. The documents sent up two days ago, have you had a chance to—”

Documents. He had not so much as opened the folder. He assembled a dignified expression.

“Ah — Sajang-nim. I’ll review them when I’m back.”

“Back? You’re going out again? Where—”

“SM. Whatever it is, it keeps until I return.”

“SM? Hoejang-nim— Hoejang-nim—!”

But the chairman was already gone down the corridor, and the CEO was left holding his folder and his sentence.

✦ ✦ ✦

“Hoejang-nim. SM.”

The car settled to a stop. He stepped out with his secretary and aides behind him and looked up the familiar face of the building.

He had left this building as a trainee. Returned once as a newly-minted star. And now the third arrival: chairman of LOEN. The same glass doors. Three different men walking through them.

Today was for Red Velvet — so today was a BORN ARISTOCRAT day. The black suit sat like armor plate; the gold-rimmed glasses added the scholar’s frost. The field spread out around him as he moved, and the negotiation odds bent accordingly — the effect only grew with his net worth, and his net worth had lately done a great deal of growing. The five of them. He was leaving with all five.

“Hoejang-nim?”

“Let’s go.”

He led from the front, unhurried, and the pressure rolled off him in rings.

The girl at the front desk simply froze — mouth slightly open, greeting forgotten — until a senior colleague’s elbow arrived in her ribs and she folded into a bow, stammering. Down the corridor, staff pressed themselves to the walls in a rolling tide of bent backs. A few risked an upward glance at the legend — trainee to chairman in one year — and were pushed back down by the sheer weight of the presence.

And as he walked, a hundred nights of this building ran through him like fast film: sweat-drenched midnights in the practice rooms; his head lowered under a dance instructor’s tirade; the isolation, the bullying; then the first flares of talent and the sudden crowding of admirers. Now he crossed the same lobby in a suit that cost more than that trainee’s year, and no one dared meet his eyes. It was, he admitted to himself with clinical honesty, extremely satisfying. This was what the whole climb had been FOR.

Lee Soo-man’s secretary arrived at a trot, professionally radiant.

“Chairman Song — the Chief Producer is expecting you in his office. This way, please.”

“Thank you for the trouble.”

Measured. Unhurried. A voice with ballast in it.

✦ ✦ ✦

The elevator doors opened on a burst of laughter.

“Hyung— hahaha— stop it!”

“Ya, Baekhyun-ah. Grown bold, have we? Fighting back now?”

“Ahaha—”

EXO came spilling out — and saw him — and the laughter cut off mid-note, as if a fader had been pulled. Nine faces cycled through shock, awe, and several harder-to-name things at speed.

Kim Jun-myeon recovered first, snapping through ninety degrees.

“Chairman Song! Annyeonghashimnikka!”

The rest folded after him in a ragged wave.

Park Chan-yeol, bent at the waist, stole a sidelong look at the profile going past and remembered a debut-album shoot from another lifetime — the polite trainee who had called him SUNBAE-NIM every time they crossed paths. One year. The distance between them now needed a telescope.

And Byun Baek-hyun, bowed, stared at a pair of immaculate black shoes while something old twisted in his chest. Taeyeon. The name had lived under his sternum since his own trainee days — the small figure blazing on stage, the leader of Girls’ Generation, impossibly far above a nobody in a practice room. He had run the fantasy a hundred times: debut, rise, get a LITTLE closer, then a little closer than that. He had debuted. He had risen. And then he had walked past a practice-room window and seen her with Ji-ho, easy and close, and the rumor mill had confirmed the rest within weeks. That night he’d sat on the dorm roof with half a case of beer, and Chan-yeol had found him and said nothing and stayed anyway. The splinter had been in there ever since: ONE STEP. I WAS ONE STEP TOO SLOW.

And now, folded in half before the man himself, the splinter — absurdly, mercifully — dissolved. Ji-ho greeted the group with mild courtesy, passed his eyes once across Baek-hyun without weight, and stepped into the elevator. In that half-second of eye contact, Baek-hyun saw all the way down: calm without bottom, a stillness that reflected the whole noisy world and was moved by none of it. THAT — that altitude, that ease, that gravity that commanded the room without asking — was not a thing he would reach in this lifetime. It wasn’t about fame anymore. A firefly does not negotiate with the sun.

The elevator closed. Baek-hyun straightened, and laughed — and it came out clean.

“Baekhyun?” Chan-yeol touched his arm, worried.

“It’s nothing.” The smile, for once, weighed nothing at all. “Let’s go. We’ll be late.”

He gave the closed doors one last look, and said the rest silently. TAEYEON SUNBAE-NIM: BE HAPPY. AND SONG JI-HO — LOSING TO YOU, I CONCEDE WITH A WHOLE HEART.

✦ ✦ ✦

In the elevator, Ji-ho’s eyes were unhurried and very deep. He knew about Byun Baek-hyun’s old torch — had known for a while — and that brief crossing of eyes had told him exactly what weather had just passed through the younger man. And? Anger was a currency for men with thin reserves. The strong didn’t rage at other men’s wanting; they simply stood where the wanting could measure itself against the having, and let arithmetic do the rest. If Baek-hyun had made his own peace — ideal. If someday he hadn’t— the corner of Ji-ho’s mouth ticked up a cold half-degree. There were always methods for helping a man’s peace along.

Ding. Sixth floor.

Lee Soo-man’s office door stood ajar at the end of the corridor.

Ji-ho squared his cuffs, drew one long breath, and stepped out with the field spreading before him.

The talk behind that door was the day’s true engagement. Five contracts. One legend across the table. Now the real game.
