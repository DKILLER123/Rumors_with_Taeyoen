Chapter One Hundred Twenty-Nine

Visiting Sulli’s Set

Three days later, Yoon-a’s ankle had mostly healed, and the party began the long haul home — another twenty hours of connections that deposited them at Incheon looking like frost-bitten produce. The goodbyes were monosyllabic; everyone scattered.

In the van, Ji-ho slumped against the seat with his eyes shut — planes had never let him sleep. Dong-hwan rallied enough to clear his throat.

“Hard trip. Rest properly tomorrow — after that it’s all hands on the album.”

“You worked hard too, hyung.”

Dong-hwan had privately classified the trip as subsidized tourism, but accepted the sympathy. He patted Ji-ho’s shoulder and produced the good news with a flourish:

“Beginning of next month, the first big settlement lands. Two top-tier endorsements plus the ongoing ‘Some’ splits and fan-club revenue — the number is substantial. You can start thinking about where to buy property.”

Ji-ho’s eyes opened.

Seed capital: incoming.

The spare hours lately had all gone into finance texts under the God of Learning, cross-referenced against a memory full of the next decade’s inflection points. Wages never compounded fast enough. Money had to work for money — the 2014 LOEN window was not going to fund itself.

Home: the pretty neighbor across the hall was out — theater rehearsals, presumably. A scalding shower took the eighteen hours of travel off his skin, and sleep took the rest.

✦   ✦   ✦

Next morning, Aura-restored and fully charged, he permitted himself a rare lie-in with the news feed.

The airport photos from yesterday were already up — him and Yoon-a walking side by side, no particular interaction, but the visual pairing alone generating traffic. The discussion stayed benign: the CM collaboration, the schedules. No wild theories.

Then one headline snagged him:

Portal · Entertainment Desk

this morning

“To the Beautiful You” Ratings Hit New Floor — Just 4%. SM’s Drama-Production Capability Under Fire

The idol-led adaptation continues to slide… critics argue the pure “flower-boy casting” formula has exhausted itself… the production company is advised to “return to its core business and focus on raising idols.”

— the sole element drawing consistent praise: Choi Sulli’s clean, striking short-cut transformation for the role.

He counted back — over a month since he’d last seen Sulli. A set visit, then. Support in physical form: a coffee truck with his name on the banner would give the sagging set a jolt, and if a few of his own fans got curious about the drama on his account, that was free wind in her sails.

Decided; done. Dong-hwan booked the familiar coffee-truck vendor, and by afternoon Ji-ho’s car was rolling onto the set.

The atmosphere on location was visibly deflated — sliding ratings had laid a film of pressure over everything. Then the truck with the SONG JI-HO CHEERS FOR “TO THE BEAUTIFUL YOU” banner pulled in, and the set audibly reinflated.

Sulli was waiting near the monitors — and at the sight of him stepping down from the car, she lit up entire and came bounding like a spring deer.

“Oppa!” She seized his arm in both of hers and moored herself against his side. “What are you DOING here?!”

The short cut suited her absurdly — it made the small face smaller and sharper, traded some of the long-haired softness for wit and speed. The one thing the whole country agreed on about this drama, and the country was right.

“Came to see you. And refuel you.” He nodded the truck into service, letting her keep the arm. “I hear our Sulli’s been working herself to pieces.”

“That’s because oppa TOLD me to take acting seriously~” She beamed up at him, cloudless.

She towed him to the truck, flagged an assistant for photos, wrapped both arms around his bicep, tucked herself into his side, and threw a V at the lens with maximum sweetness. The picture was on her feed in minutes.

Sulli · Official SNS

just now

[ photo: coffee truck, a V-sign, one thoroughly claimed arm ] Thank you for the support, oppa — a happy day~ 💚

Replies: “friendship forever!!” · “Sulli’s smile is BACK” · “thank you Ji-ho for taking care of our girl” — two fandoms, one warm comment section, the rescue history still paying dividends.

Director Jeon Ki-sang — the veteran hand behind a string of hit romances — knew free publicity when it drove onto his set, greeted Ji-ho with real warmth, and declared a coffee break for the whole unit.

Introductions followed: second lead Lee Hyun-woo; the second female lead, Kim Ji-won; cameo guest Hwang Kwanghee; and Choi Min-ho, familiar from events, now formally met.

Sulli dragged Kim Ji-won over by the wrist, claiming full credit.

“Oppa — this is Ji-won. I talk about you to her ALL the time, I play her your songs — she’s a real fan now!”

Kim Ji-won was fine-featured and soft-mannered, and currently blushing. She bowed.

“Hello. I really love your songs — especially ‘Some,’ and the morning-glory one.”

The voice was gentle to the point of disarming. And she was his senior in years AND debut — bowing first anyway. He returned it fast.

“Thank you for listening. I’m looking forward to your scenes in the drama too.”

She went pinker and fanned herself with one hand, earlobes gone cherry-red under the afternoon sun.

And something clicked over in his head: the album needed MVs — and for “Spring Day,” this exact presence, soft and wistful and clean, was very close to perfect casting.

“Ji-won sunbae-nim — could we exchange contacts?”

“Ne?!” — in involuntary stereo, from Kim Ji-won and Sulli both.

“Don’t misunderstand—” he laughed, “—it’s about work. A collaboration. Your manager’s number is fine too; I’ll have the company call properly.”

“Oh—” She nodded, enlightened, patted herself down in mild panic, failed to locate her phone, and surrendered her number verbally instead. “Then — please note it down. 010—…”

He saved it and thanked her; she waved both hands, laughing, overwhelmed by the courtesy.

Sulli’s gaze made one slow transit between the two of them. She DID want oppa to have more fans. And yet. Something about this itched.

She abruptly re-anchored — both arms, half her weight, fully flag-planted on his arm — and informed Kim Ji-won, in a tone equal parts aegyo and territorial declaration:

“SEE? I told you — oppa’s even more handsome than on TV, AND he’s the nicest person alive.” Then, tipping her face up, eyes shining with uncut dependence: “Oppa. You came back from France and came to see ME first. Right?”

He patted her head lightly.

“Of course. Who else would my good little sister be?”

Confirmation received, she glowed — and did not release the arm, holding on the way a child holds the one toy that is absolutely not for sharing, smile sweet as ever with something hot and fixed running just underneath it.

Break ended; cameras rolled again; he paid his respects to the director and cast and took his leave. Sulli walked him all the way out, fingers hooked in his jacket hem.

“Oppa. When are you coming to see me next?”

“When there’s time. Focus on the drama. Take care of your body.”

“Mm! I will!”

She stood at the roadside and watched his car all the way out of sight before turning back. In the sunlight her short hair lifted on the wind — and her smile settled, slowly, into something quieter and more thoughtful. She touched the arm that had held his, curved her mouth into one small satisfied arc, and walked back to set with a light step.

For her, the whole day distilled to one fact: oppa had come, and the banner had ONE name to cheer for — hers. As for anyone else… Her gaze passed briefly, mildly, over Kim Ji-won at the touch-up mirror — one flicker of coolness at the very bottom of her eyes — and then the sweet harmless smile was back in place.

He had barely settled into the car when the phone rang. Nuna 🐿.

“Finished? Did you miss me?”

“Just wrapped the set visit.” He leaned back, mouth already curving. “What do you think?”

The soft laugh down the line; a few minutes of nothing-talk stitched entirely out of things only they found interesting; an agreement to meet tonight; a mutual reluctance to hang up first.

Taeyeon put down the phone in a glow, hummed something tuneless, and bounced back into the Girls’ Generation waiting room—

—where Yoon-a, retouching her makeup at the far mirror, had watched the entire performance.

She rose, drifted over on silent feet, and deployed without warning:

“Eonni. Who was THAT call with? You look so happy.”

Taeyeon’s heart jumped; her face held. She grabbed the nearest available sibling and threw her under the bus.

“Ha-yeon. She’s coming up to Seoul — I have to keep her company tonight, so I won’t be back at the dorm.”

“Ha-yeonie’s coming?” Yoon-a’s eyes went wide with delight. “I haven’t seen her in FOREVER. Eonni, I’ll come with you tonight! We haven’t shared a bed in ages~”

“NO!” — at reflex speed.

Yoon-a instantly assembled a wounded, puzzled face.

“Why not? Eonni’s never at the dorm anymore. I MISS you.”

Taeyeon’s brain sprinted. Tonight was BOYFRIEND night; the last thing it needed was this human beanpole in the bed.

“Ha-yeon needs me for something important. Family matters. Next time — next time I’ll bring you, promise.” She reached up and ruffled Yoon-a’s hair, deploying maximum-eonni soothing energy.

“Ahhh~ so THAT’S how it is.” Yoon-a stretched the syllables like taffy, nodding slowly, and a small unreadable, wicked smile surfaced that made Taeyeon’s scalp prickle.

Taeyeon changed the subject with the subtlety of a lane change at speed.

“Right — the Alps shoot! Did it go well? Anything fun happen?”

She worked hard to make it sound like ordinary sister-talk. The slight forward lean and the over-focused eyes filed a dissenting opinion.

And Yoon-a, looking at this transparently fishing, transparently smitten eonni — ears up, whiskers trembling, so carefully casual — suddenly found her unbearably cute. The stored grievance from Chamonix, the little revenge fantasy of “accidentally” telling tales and putting one drop of poison in that infuriating man’s wine — all of it quietly dissolved. She looked at the hope hiding in Taeyeon’s eyes, and decided not to break any of it.

“Fun things, hmm~” Head tilted, memory theatrically consulted — and then she gave a full color commentary: the French director’s peculiar demands, the frozen-stiff blooper reel, the crew’s language-barrier comedy, the postcard prettiness of Chamonix — every anecdote vivid, and every single one edited to route around a certain co-star, who appeared solely as “the colleague.”

Taeyeon listened hard, laughed in the right places, asked for details — and Yoon-a could feel her waiting, the whole time, for more information about one particular person, and could watch the small complicated flicker — relief braided with disappointment — each time the story steered wide.

By the end Taeyeon seemed satisfied — or confirmed — in whatever she’d needed, and patted Yoon-a’s shoulder, fully relaxed.

“Sounds exhausting. Rest well now that you’re back. I’m heading out early tonight — Ha-yeon.”

Yoon-a watched the light-footed exit, chin in her hand, and sighed.

Eonni’s completely gone, isn’t she?

And that man— The sunset profile on the snowfield. The grip that had yanked her off the rocks. The steady breathing under her on a kilometer of forest track. The heat of careful hands on an injured ankle. And that final, infuriating, upside-down “verdict.”

SO annoying.

She wound a strand of hair around one finger, pulled it straight, let it spring back.

This account is unsettleable. …For now.
