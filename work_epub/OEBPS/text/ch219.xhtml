Chapter Two Hundred Nineteen

When Life Goes Your Way, Drink Deep

Evening → deep night The seaside pension, Jeju · terrace, main bedroom, guest room black pork, seafood hot pot, soju — and a phone call from Seoul that no one at the table can afford

The sea wind poured in through the open terrace doors, salt-damp and constant, the surf keeping its old rhythm below. Dusk had settled fully; the far water was a single line of deep blue dissolving into the ink of the sky.

Under the warm terrace lights, the table was a happy riot.

At its center, the seafood hot pot burbled away — milky broth rolling with scarlet octopus legs, fat abalone, shellfish gaping open, blue crab split down the middle, the sweetness of it climbing straight up with the steam. On the charcoal grill beside it, thick-cut Jeju black pork belly hissed and spat, fat dripping into the coals in little starbursts of flame, char-smoke and meat-smell braiding together in the air.

Ji-ho lounged in the rattan chair, wind stirring the hair off his forehead, entirely at his ease.

Choi Sulli knelt on the cushion at his side, scissoring the grilled pork into bite-size pieces. She picked out one with a crisp edge and a blush-pink center, wrapped it carefully in a bright green leaf of wild chive, dabbed on a little of the salty anchovy sauce, and lifted it to his lips.

“Oppa, aaah—”

She tilted her small face up, eyes glittering, sweetness pooling at the corners of her mouth.

He ate from her hand. The pork was rich and charred and perfect, and the being-waited-on-by-a-beauty of it wasn’t bad either. He narrowed his eyes in contentment and chased it with a sip of soju.

On his other side, Tiffany leaned in as well.

She had steadied since the afternoon, though her gaze still drifted, and she didn’t hold Sulli’s eyes for long. Watching the “little sister” and the “oppa” so cozy together, a thin vinegar-sting of jealousy rose in her anyway.

She fished the plumpest abalone out of the pot with the long chopsticks, dipped it, blew on it with care, and offered it across.

“Ji-ho, try this one. It’s so fresh.”

Her voice was softer than usual — deliberately, placatingly soft.

He turned and took the abalone from her hand. Perfectly cooked: springy, slick, the sweetness of it flooding his mouth with the salt of the dipping sauce. Top-shelf sea and no argument.

He chewed, and looked at the two of them. Sulli in her loose pale knit, the wind lifting the fine hairs at her ear, the small white lobe and the clean line of her neck, wrapping the next bite of pork with the whole-hearted focus of a neighborhood kid set on pleasing her big brother. Tiffany, changed into a silk house dress, chestnut waves pinned loosely up off a bright forehead and a long neck, laying out food and pouring drinks with lowered lashes and careful hands — making up for something, or shoring something up, or both.

Sulli saw his glass was empty and had the chilled soju bottle up in an instant, filling it attentively. Not to be outdone, Tiffany lifted the freshest shellfish out of the pot, dressed it in sauce, and brought it to his lips.

Sea wind, surf-sound, good food, cold liquor — and flanking him, two beautiful women of entirely different flavors, both, tonight, bent on pleasing him. The char of the pork, the sweetness of the sea, the cold bite of the soju, the warm mingled drift of perfume and skin — languid, extravagant. He raised the refilled glass, looked past it at the dark seam of the horizon, then back at the two lovely, scheming, lamplit faces. Power and appetite, love and beauty — all of it condensed, for one evening, into taste on the tongue and warmth within arm’s reach. Some ancient poet had gotten it exactly right: when life goes your way, drink deep — never leave your gold cup empty to face the moon alone.

He tipped his head back and drained the glass to its cold, clean bottom.

Tiffany, watching Sulli’s diligent attentions, quietly shook her head at her own suspicion. No — surely not. If Sulli were really his, she could never face me this calmly. The same way I can’t face Taeyeon.

✦ ✦ ✦

Dinner wound down. The three of them cleared the table together, then killed an hour over flower cards, laughing the food down.

When the night had thickened, Ji-ho curled an arm around Tiffany and they withdrew to the main bedroom.

She had grown up in California, and now that the last wall between them had come down, she was openly, unembarrassedly affectionate in a way that still surprised him. Taeyeon and Irene both went shy at the very idea of sharing a bath; Tiffany simply took his hand and walked him into the steam as if it were the most natural thing in the world — lovely in that particular in-between way of hers, no longer a girl, not yet fully settled into womanhood, and altogether disarming.

Perhaps the evening’s needling had lit something in her, because tonight she held nothing back at all — as if every unreserved moment were its own declaration, pitched at an audience of one and aimed at someone else entirely: LOOK how much he loves me.

The steam clouded the glass. The rest of the night belonged to the two of them.

In the guest room down the hall, Choi Sulli lay in the dark with a pillow clamped over her head, thoroughly out of temper. Unbelievable. That vixen was doing it ON PURPOSE. Hmph — Americans. Not one shred of modesty in the whole country!

Ji-ho, for his part, had no complaints whatsoever about Tiffany’s attitude. She was generous and quick and needed only half a signal — off stage, the poised and dimpled idol; alone with him, wholly, artfully devoted. He’d verified the goods personally, as it were, and could confirm: the old “Secretary Hwang” nickname had been prophetic.

Afterward he gathered her, boneless and warm, back against him and murmured the soft things a woman listens for in the dark. He knew perfectly well how much the after mattered more than the during; his voice stayed low and sweet until her breathing went slow.

And then, into that tangled warmth, her phone rang.

Caller ID: Taeyeon.

Tiffany went white. She looked at him, pleading.

“Could you… step out for a minute?”

His mouth opened. He wanted to hear what Taeyeon would do — had half a mind, even, to tell Tiffany right now that Taeyeon already knew. But he hesitated a beat too long for courage, and nodded instead.

“I’ll take a walk. If you get sleepy, don’t wait up.”

“Mm. Thank you.”

She answered, and her voice snapped into its old easy intimacy without a visible seam.

Voice Call · Daedae

“Yeoboseyo, Daedae — miss me that much already?”

On the far end, Taeyeon stood hidden on the dorm balcony, fist clenched around the phone, listening to that warm, familiar tone. Tiffany. How could you do this to me? Right now— are you lying comfortably in my Ji-ho’s arms? MY Ji-ho!

Voice Call · continued

“Yeoboseyo? Daedae, is the signal bad? Can you hear me?”

“Oh— I can hear you, Fany-yah.” — a voice filed carefully smooth. “Why can I hear waves on your end?”

“Is— is there? Must be the Han River.” — a laugh waved past it, a little too quickly. “Oh right, Taeyeon — I bought some health supplements. Let’s take a trip down to Jeonju together soon.”

“Hm?”

“I haven’t seen your parents or Ha-yeon in forever. And let’s take your parents in for full checkups while we’re there — my treat. They’ve always been so good to me. I should pay some of that back.”

Pay THEM back? — or pay ME off, because the guilt is eating you alive? Taeyeon’s first instinct was to refuse. Then the second thought arrived, colder: a trip home would be the perfect place to test her. So she made her voice laugh.

Voice Call · continued

“Sure. I’ve been wanting to get Mom and Dad in for checkups anyway — they always dodge it, say it’s a waste of money. If YOU ask, they won’t be able to say no.”

Tiffany, over-bright and eager to please, chattered on, and for a while the two of them sounded exactly like they always had.

✦ ✦ ✦

Ji-ho dressed and slipped out of the room.

A door cracked open down the hall on the click of the latch, and Choi Sulli’s head appeared, followed by the rest of her — tiptoeing, eyes lighting up when she saw him digging through his things.

“Oppa, you’re going out?”

He turned — and got an eyeful. She was wearing very little of anything, and pale skin gathered every scrap of light in the dim hallway. He lunged for the curtains, yanked them shut, and glared, voice pinned to a whisper.

“Are you insane? Back to your room!”

She advanced instead, two soft steps, voice dropping into something low and pliant.

“No~ Not unless you carry me back~”

“Behave. Or you’ll get a spanking.”

“Go on then! Do it!” She spun around, small waist arched, entirely unrepentant. “Do it~ hurry up~”

He genuinely had no defense against her. The girl got bolder by the week. He gave up on the mask and cap, bent down, and swung her up sideways into his arms.

He carried her back on soundless feet and set her down on the guest-room bed — and her arms snapped around his neck like a sprung trap, dragging him down into the warm, scented dark of her.

“Yah— stop that—”

“I don’t care!” Kisses landed anywhere they could reach — his jaw, his throat — her voice sweet and urgent at once. “That bad woman did all of that on PURPOSE tonight!”

“Sulli, don’t—”

“Oppa.” Her voice went suddenly soft, eyes shining up through the dark. “Be gentle, then… I promise I won’t make a sound.”

Ji-ho was not a good man. He was a man of large and specific appetites, and against this particular girl, in this particular midnight, with a locked door and a dare hanging in the air, he had never once won. If I die, he thought, with the last operational corner of his brain, it will be a woman that kills me.

The door clicked shut. The night kept the rest.

And Choi Sulli smiled into the dark, unhurried. Men. So easy.
