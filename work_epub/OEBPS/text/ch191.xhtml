Chapter One Hundred Ninety-One

Ordinary Days with Taeyeon

May 19–20, 2013 Her apartment one lamp, one pronunciation bootcamp, one contract worry — and a plan taking shape in the dark

Deep night in Seoul. In Taeyeon’s apartment only the bedside lamp burned, its dim gold laying overlapped shadows up the wall.

She lay boneless in his arms, without the strength to lift a finger — cheeks flushed, bangs damp, breath still finding its way back down.

“Ji-ho-ya…” The murmur came out soft and sticky. “You’re… too much…”

He laughed low, fingers combing gently through her sweat-damp fringe. “And who started it? Hm?”

She recalled her own opening declaration — the full if I don’t conquer you tonight I’m changing my family name energy of it — and went a shade redder. Eyes closed, she performed a small tactical relocation and settled deeper into his arms, a satisfied cat adjusting its sunbeam.

He looked down at her — the leader of Girls’ Generation, the woman half the country called its eternal first light — lying against him with every guard down.

One year. In one year he had Taeyeon. He had Joo-hyun. He had Sulli. Ji-eun stood with one foot across the threshold. And once LOEN fell — the “nation’s little sister” who single-handedly fed an entire company would find her orbit bending toward his, too.

He understood it better every month: for a man, the root of everything is his own gravity.

Money, talent, position — these were only gravity’s visible spectrum. Stack the halos high enough, and women arrived the way moths argue with a flame.

She lay curled in the crook of him, feeling that satisfaction that starts in the marrow and radiates outward. Today’s whole inventory — the arm around her shoulders in front of IU, planted like a flag; the two songs that had stunned the room; and the hours just past — all of it had run her once around the border of heaven and back.

So THIS is why some women throw the whole map away for one man.

When the same man secures your heart, backs your career, and delivers — certain other things — at a bone-deep register, the heart ties itself to him without asking permission. Total obedience was an exaggeration. The tenderness, the leaning — those were real. A woman was, in the final ledger, a creature of feeling.

“Nuna. Sleep?”

“Mm~” The reply came fully nasal, a child bargaining. “Ji-ho-ya, I’m so tired~”

“Tired people still shower.”

A few seconds of silence. Then, limp and shameless: “Carry me, then~”

“Call me Oppa. Beg.”

She rolled her eyes at the ceiling of her own skull. Truly a boyfriend of refined hobbies. But “yeobo” and “Oppa” were both already on the record; the shame budget was long spent. She switched on the voice — the soft, nasal, weapons-grade one.

“I’m begging you~ Ji-ho Oppa~”

Her ordinary speech was already a soundboard’s dream; THIS register ran straight down his spine. He gathered her up, entirely defeated, and carried her to the bath.

Morning arrived through the curtain gap; they surfaced near ten. She hung on him koala-fashion, profoundly asleep, and he extracted himself limb by limb to build breakfast — seaweed soup, fried eggs, kimchi, toast still ticking from the toaster.

“Wah — Ji-ho-ya, amazing!” She padded out rubbing her eyes, saw the table, and paid for it with a kiss on his cheek at a small run.

“Go brush your teeth.” He swatted her lightly onward.

After breakfast they burrowed into the sofa — he with the laptop and a queue of work mail, she stretched across his lap with the “Shallow” lyric book, drilling sounds.

“Shallow—” Her brow knotted. “This ‘sh’ is impossible—”

“Tongue up toward the palate, air out along the sides.” He didn’t look up; the keys kept clattering. “Like — the shape of a whistle.”

Three failed attempts later she simply rolled over, flattened herself against his chest, and aimed her eyes up at him. “Teach me again~”

He looked down into two clear eyes with PLEASE INSTRUCT written across them in block letters, laughed, closed the laptop, and taught it again. And again.

“THAT’S it! Exactly that!”

When the sound finally landed she shot upright, seized his face in both hands, and kissed him. A real one. They came apart short of breath, her face pink and her eyes glittering.

“That’s the tuition~”

“Then I’ll be teaching this syllable several more times.”

“Greedy~”

The whole day went like that. She drilled; he worked; wrong sounds were corrected; correct sounds were taxed. When they tired, there was a film half-watched from inside one blanket, and long unhurried stretches that needed no name.

A homebody’s holiday: music, warmth, one locked door. She had never in her life been this precise kind of happy. Her boyfriend appeared to know everything — not just music but the machinery of English vowels, the cultural sediment under a lyric — and could hand any of it to her in words that fit her hands.

Time holds still for no one. But happy time, she noticed, cheats — it runs double-speed.

✦   ✦   ✦

That evening, dinner cleared, they sat shoulder to shoulder gaming instead of working. She belonged to the species of player defined by the trinity: terrible, devoted, and heavily monetized. Her character wore full gold-glowing premium armor and was currently being chased around the entire dungeon map by entry-level mobs.

“AAAH! Ji-ho-ya, SAVE me!”

His character waded in and cleared the screen in one practiced combo.

“Ji-ho is the BEST!” A smacking kiss landed on his cheek, mid-victory-lap.

Past nine they washed up and lay down — no urgency in it, just the long quiet holding, temperature and heartbeat exchanged at zero words per minute.

“Ji-ho-ya.” She spoke into the dark. “A few days ago — the company raised my re-signing.”

He didn’t interrupt. His hand moved slowly on her back: listening.

“They said they’d draft me a personally tailored contract, replacing the debut-era standard one.” A pause. “No details yet — just the opening move. But… I’m worried.”

“About what?”

“That they’ll make material out of us.” Her voice dropped. “You know how it goes. An idol dating — the LEADER dating—”

“Don’t worry, nuna.” He pressed a kiss to her forehead. “Negotiate on your terms, exactly as you want them. SM will not use us against you.”

By the time Girls’ Generation formally re-signed, LOEN would already be his. And SM did not pick fights with the man holding Melon.

She had never once doubted a sentence he said in that tone. She nuzzled into his chest and redistributed herself more comfortably.

“They’ll be calling the members in one by one, probably.” A sigh. “After the world tour — that’s when the headaches start.”

This round wasn’t a group contract but individual ones: nine separate negotiations, nine pressure campaigns — dating clauses, schedules, resource splits — a long and complicated season.

He kneaded her back gently and went away behind his eyes.

In his first life’s memory: full-team re-signing, announced September 15. And then, not two weeks later — the September 30th storm. Jessica out. And around the same season, “Girls’ Generation” turning into “Dating Generation,” couple news detonating one member at a time.

But this time the board had a new piece on it. Taeyeon was his. Tiffany was — unresolved. And Jessica… when exactly had she met that man, the one the fans would call shoehorn-face?

What he didn’t yet know: the meeting was already dead. A bank notification with eleven zeros had killed it.

And there was Yoon-a. Right — when did Yoon-a’s relationship start, in the old timeline?

A small electric jolt ran through him. He arranged his voice into perfect idleness. “Nuna — are the members all busy lately?”

“Mm. Two rest days, then full tour prep. It gets brutal from here.”

“Right — what’s Yoon-a nuna up to these days?”

“Yoon-a?” Her head tilted up off his chest, eyes narrowing one degree. “Why are you asking about Yoon-a?”

“We’re both EIDER endorsers. There’s a launch event next month — we’re appearing together.”

Delivered with flawless naturalness. She settled back down. “She hasn’t got anything special on, I think. Next month is the tour anyway.” A pause; her voice dimmed. “The tour… we won’t see each other for so long again.”

Her arms tightened around him, hard, like a person holding something the tide wanted.

“Hah. Yeah.” He exhaled. The artist’s tax: love in installments, paid mostly apart.

“Nuna — besides us, and Soo-young nuna, there aren’t any other couples in the team, right?”

“There are~ Hyo-yeon’s seeing someone too.” She thought about it. “The others… no, I don’t think so.”

Then the Yoon-a file needed moving up the queue. A small urgency lit somewhere in his planning cortex.

“Then—”

“No more other people. Please.”

She cut him off — and sat up, rising over him, knees bracketing his waist. Her hands found the buttons of the sleep shirt; silk slid from one shoulder, then further, baring pale skin and the fine architecture of her collarbones. She leaned down; her hair fell in a curtain and swept his cheek.

“In a few weeks I’m gone for months.”

“Ji-ho-ya. Love me properly.”

He filed every scheme in his head under TOMORROW and drew her down.

The rest belongs to the two of them, and to the small hours.

She poured everything into it — the whole pre-departure ache of it, loving hard enough to memorize: his temperature, his breath, the weight of his arms. These were the provisions she was laying in. On tour, on the far side of an ocean, she would live off these exact minutes, one remembered night at a time.

Long after she’d fallen asleep, he lay in the dark with her soft weight against him, wide awake, running the future like a spreadsheet.

LOEN. Melon. And how to hold the initiative through the whole grand opera of Girls’ Generation’s re-signing season.

Because ₩461.5 billion was never meant to stay a number in an account.

It was meant to become power. Leverage. And — for the woman asleep on his chest, and the others he intended to keep — the hardest guarantee money could pour.
