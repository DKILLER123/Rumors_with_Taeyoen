Chapter One Hundred Eighty-Three

A Love Proven in Deep Water

Late night Ha-ra’s living room the story of the sea, told to three women who each hear it differently

Jiyeon drifted back into the living room in a haze, fingertips grazing lips that were still running a low fever. The room’s attention was elsewhere; nobody clocked her state.

Ha-ra stood with arms crossed, brows drawn hard.

“So you’re telling me — you KNOW he’s dating Taeyeon, and you’re still willing to stay beside him with no name, no status, NOTHING?”

Sulli lounged against the sofa arm and flipped her hair with unbearable languor.

“What do you mean, no name? ‘Little sister’ is the BIGGEST name there is.”

“Is THAT what you call a little sister? Peaches!!” Ji-eun’s voice climbed. “That is a MISTRESS with extra steps!”

“A sister AND a sweetheart, then.” The smile curled, entirely untroubled. “Girlfriends break up. A little sister is for life. I think the arrangement’s excellent.”

“Yah, Sulli-yah!” Ha-ra took her hands, urgent now, pleading. “WHY? You’re this talented, this beautiful, this loved — why would you shortchange yourself like this?”

“Eonni. I’m not shortchanged. Truly.” Sulli squeezed back, and the play drained out of her face, leaving something level and quiet. “You all know about Choi Jae, right? The man I almost dated.”

Three nods. Three grim faces.

“The investigation’s still running.” Her voice stayed even; the chill got in anyway. “What they’ve found — drugs in drinks, videos for blackmail, and things I… genuinely can’t say out loud. If Oppa hadn’t blundered into my path exactly when he did, I don’t let myself imagine what would have happened to me.”

“Yes — he saved you,” Ha-ra pressed, “and I’m grateful to him for it. Why do you think I invite him to everything? Why do you think I plug his albums in interviews and tell every journalist he’s a good person? But REPAYMENT isn’t THIS, Sulli-yah. Love is supposed to be singular. All of you, for all of them, one person.”

Sulli slid her hands free, lifted the bomb-shot glass from the table, and took a slow sip. The burn drew her brows together briefly.

“Love. Right. That’s the word.” She set the glass down, and her eyes had gone somewhere soft-focused and absolutely certain at once. “Eonni — there is no one after him. Not for me. That’s finished. My whole heart’s already spent.”

“Sulli-yah, that’s because you haven’t MET enough people yet.” Ha-ra gentled. “Yes, he’s exceptional — handsome, writes hits, sings, apparently writes FILMS now, carries you into one—” she caught herself mid-inventory, appalled, and yanked the wheel back. “—but that’s the filter talking. When I was with Jun-hyung I also thought: best man alive, no one else, ever. And? Look how that ended. With your conditions, someone better WILL come.”

“No one will love me more than he does. That’s not a filter. That’s a measurement.”

“You’re TWENTY. How can you possibly be sure? Let eonni introduce you to a few—”

“Eonni.” The temperature of the word dropped ten degrees. “Say that again and I’m actually going to be angry. What Oppa and I have was tested on Gapado. Fully.”

The room went still. Even Ji-yeon surfaced from her own weather and turned.

Sulli took one long breath.

“On the island — I confessed to him. And he told me the truth. That he had a girlfriend. That it was Taeyeon eonni.”

Ji-eun’s fingers curled slowly into her palm.

Hah. Compared to how he’s handled ME — with Sulli, at least, the scummery came clearly labeled.

“In that one second, the sky came down.” Sulli’s gaze had gone long-distance. “I just wanted to be gone — as far as legs could carry. So I ran. He came after me. There’s a stretch of coast there — a cliff over the water.”

She paused. The tremor got into her voice anyway.

“He was afraid to crowd me. Kept his distance. Kept his voice soft. And then I — one careless step. My foot slipped.”

“Into the sea. At night. Off a height.”

“MWORAGO?!”

“Oh my god—!”

“SULLI!”

Three women came off their seats in unison.

“It’s over now. I’m fine.” The reassuring smile ran a shade pale. “That drop was maybe three meters. Past midnight — dark like a hand in front of your face doesn’t exist. And the water—” a breath, “—cold in a way I didn’t know cold could be.”

Her voice lowered, and the room went down into that night with her.

“I was thrashing. Pure terror, pure dark. And do you know what I still can’t get over? He didn’t hesitate. Not one second. Not HALF a second. I heard the splash before I understood it — he’d already jumped.”

Ji-eun’s hand had come up over her mouth. Ha-ra’s eyes were fixed and wide. Ji-yeon felt a shiver travel through her chest.

“He couldn’t see anything. Didn’t know the depth, the rocks, ANYTHING. And he jumped anyway.” The rims of her eyes had gone red. “Maybe heaven was watching. Maybe my splashing gave him a bearing. His hand found me.”

Her gaze traveled across her three friends, slowly.

“Can you picture it? Open sea, at night. Black and cold on every side, no lit shore, no one to call, nothing anywhere except the sound of waves and your own heart going like a drum.”

Ji-eun’s fingers had knotted into her shirt hem. Ha-ra was hugging her own arms. Ji-yeon felt the cold crawl up her back — imagination alone was more than sufficient.

“He towed me. Looking for anywhere to land.” The words came thicker now. “At first I could still kick. Then the cold just — took everything. My arms stopped answering. And he pulled me anyway, and I could feel his strength going, stroke by stroke, and I couldn’t help him at ALL. There was a moment — I actually thought — so tired. Maybe staying here forever, with Oppa, wouldn’t be the worst thing.”

“But he wouldn’t allow it.” The tears finally went over. “He kept TALKING to me. Kept swimming. Dragged us both through that water for — I don’t know how long. And right at the end of everything he had, there was sand. A beach. We made it.”

She wiped her face with the back of her hand and looked up, and her eyes were burning.

“If that beach had been ten meters further — no. FIVE. Five meters, and both of us stay in that sea forever.”

“And he never let go of me. Not once. Not for one second.”

The room held nothing but her unsteady breathing.

“Ji-eun. Ji-yeon. Ha-ra eonni. Tell me honestly — how many men jump, without thinking, into THAT? And how many, at the actual border of dying, still don’t open their hand?”

No one answered. There wasn’t an answer on the market.

“Meeting one man like that is already the whole allowance of luck for a lifetime. How am I supposed to give him up?” And then she smiled — genuinely, brilliantly. “Yes. He’s a philanderer. It’s also his ONLY defect. Look around this industry — around this WORLD — how many men aren’t? For most of them it’s the SMALLEST thing wrong with them.”

“So I’ll stay beside him as his little sister. For good. The oppa who dotes on me — and the man I love. And he lifts my career while he’s at it. Find me the arrangement that beats it.”

Ha-ra opened her mouth. Nothing came out.

She thought of herself and Jun-hyung — a love that couldn’t even hold out against SCHEDULES — set against a love with a night sea in it. And the question asked itself, unwelcome: if a man had done that for HER… would fickleness really be past forgiving?

Ji-eun stared at the floor, feelings in full collision.

The wager. If he actually pulls it off—? And even if he doesn’t — look at what he is. If it were me in that water… I’d have sunk exactly like Peaches did. The morality’s still screaming. It’s just… quieter than it was an hour ago.

Ji-yeon’s mind replayed a doorstep kiss, and a level voice saying I’ll be waiting.

Can he really lift T-ara out of the pit? He said he likes me. If I go to him for help and he names a price — do I even… want to refuse? — What am I THINKING.

“Okay.” Sulli broke it, reaching for the bottle and refilling four glasses with a steady hand. “Enough heavy things. It’s my choice, and I don’t regret it. Now — drink.”

Four glasses touched. The sound was small and clear.

And four minds were each somewhere else entirely.

✦   ✦   ✦

Ha-ra sipped slowly, eyes unfocused. She thought of the sweetest months with Jun-hyung, and how they’d lost — not to betrayal, just to time and distance, the two most boring assassins. Sulli at least owned a moment carved into the bone. What did SHE have to hold up next to that? Not one life-or-death anything. The envy arrived first, and the sadness right behind it.

Ji-eun rotated her glass and watched the amber turn. Three years, the wager said. LOEN. It remained, by every sane measure, a fantasy — unless he found capital willing to carry him, and even then he’d be a minority partner at best. But he’d never struck her as a man who wagered on fantasies. Which meant he was holding something. And if he WON—

Panic, appropriately. And underneath the panic, faint and completely inadmissible: something that was not panic at all.

Ji-yeon’s thoughts ran in a loop with four stations: T-ara’s silence; the kiss; I like you quite a bit, actually; and a girl in black water with a hand that never let go. If he’d gone into the sea for HER — would she still be sitting here hesitating?

“Sometimes I wonder—” Ha-ra’s voice drifted up out of nowhere, half to the room, half to the window. “What love is even supposed to LOOK like. The faithful kind? Or the kind that doesn’t count costs?”

“For me it’s simple.” Sulli shook her head slowly. “It’s knowing the road is bad — and still choosing to walk it with him.”

“But love shouldn’t hurt other people—” Ji-eun said it quietly, and mostly to herself.

“I know.” Sulli lowered her head, and whatever was in her eyes, she kept it. “That’s why I’ll settle for ‘little sister.’ That way… at least Taeyeon eonni is never hurt to her face.”

Another silence, of a different weight.

Ji-yeon stood abruptly. “Bathroom.”

She shut the door, leaned her back against it, and let out a very long breath. In the mirror, her lips still looked — she turned the tap on hard and slapped cold water against her face.

“Ji-yeon-ah.” She said it to the girl in the glass, barely audible. “What are you going to do?”

✦   ✦   ✦

In the living room somebody had changed the music to slow jazz. Three girls drank quietly while the melody carried their respective cargo. Nobody argued anymore. Nobody counseled anybody. Some choices only make sense from inside them — and some loves, once they’ve been through the water, stop answering to ordinary arithmetic at all.

Sulli looked out at the city — ten thousand windows burning — and thought of one black night sea, and two hands that would not come apart.

He is my light.

And she was willing to be the shadow that light cast — provided no one else ever got the job.

Let them all believe she’d settled for sister. The hour they all believed it was the hour she became harmless. And the one still standing at the end—

She smiled into her glass.

—would be her.
