Chapter One Hundred Eighty-Seven

Short on Feelings, Never on Gifts

The same evening D.A private dining room a royal hot pot, a foot beneath the table, and one red sports car

“Sister-in-law~!”

The second deployment came even sweeter than the first. Sulli crossed the room in quick steps and hooked herself onto Taeyeon’s arm with the intimacy of a returning family member.

“It’s been SO long~”

Taeyeon blinked at the girl attached to her elbow — then laughed despite herself and poked Sulli’s cheek. “Silly girl. Where did THAT come from? By rights you should be calling him brother-in-law.”

Which was, on paper, perfectly correct. Sulli had entered SM in fifth grade; for years she, Taeyeon and Tiffany had shared one small dorm room. Taeyeon had mothered her like blood — tucking blankets at night, boiling soup when she was sick, covering for her in practice. That bond outranked any ordinary sunbae-hoobae math. In Taeyeon’s accounting, Sulli naturally stood on HER side of the ledger — hence: brother-in-law.

But Sulli only tilted her head.

I’m Oppa’s good little sister. Obviously it’s sister-in-law.

She leaned to Taeyeon’s ear, eyes enormous and liquid, voice dropped to a conspirator’s syrup. “Eonni, you don’t understand. I’m still hoping brother-in-law writes me songs and casts me in things. Oppa’s petty, you know — nothing like your generosity. What if he gets jealous of the wrong word and stops taking my calls?”

The crooked logic of it undid Taeyeon completely. She dissolved, eyes crescenting, and squeezed Sulli’s wrist with helpless fondness. “You. Always running your little calculations.”

And privately — quietly — she accepted the title. Sister-in-law. It sounded, frankly… rather nice.

They walked in arm in arm — and Sulli suddenly stopped, frowning, and looked Taeyeon up and down. Then, discreetly, measured the height difference with her eyes.

“Eonni. Are you wearing — extremely tall heels today?”

Sulli stood one-seventy barefoot, famously tall for the industry. Historically, conversation with Taeyeon had required a downward angle. Tonight the angle was… wrong. Substantially wrong. Which also meant her entire display-the-long-legs strategy had just been quietly bankrupted.

Taeyeon, long accustomed to the question, shook her head with a small, insufferable smile. “Silly child. Don’t you read the news? Second puberty~ I’m 163 now.”

“Mwo???”

Round-eyed disbelief. Sulli leaned in close and looked properly — and it was true. Not just the height. The whole aura had shifted: the childlike sweetness had shed a layer, and what stood there now had a woman’s softness folded into it — a faint, unhurried allure in the eyes, skin with that rose-fed inner light, waist and legs balanced into something that read, from any angle, as expensive.

…A genuine rival, she noted — then caught herself. Wait. No. I’M the good little sister. The one with the headache here isn’t me~

The grin bounced straight back into place — and then her gaze snagged on Taeyeon’s neckline, and her eyes went wide.

“WAH — eonni, that necklace is GORGEOUS! It sparkles! It’s SO you!”

Taeyeon’s hand rose to the onyx blossom at her throat, and the smile escaped every containment effort. “Pretty, right? Birthday present. From your Oppa.”

Something flickered through Sulli’s eyes — one quick, stifled beat of grievance and envy. MY birthday, he gave me perfume. She’d loved it. Truly. But a girl is a girl, and sparkling stones are sparkling stones, and some small competitive ledger in her chest logged the difference anyway.

Hmph. The girlfriend tier. The benefits really ARE different.

He read her weather instantly and flashed her a look across the room — later; compensation; promise — and her face flipped back to full sunshine on receipt. She snuggled closer to Taeyeon and cooed, “Oppa’s taste is amazing though — it matches you PERFECTLY, eonni.”

And then, with tremendous unconcern, she raised one porcelain wrist into the light — where a delicate watch caught the lamp glow — and gave it a small, idle turn. “Eonni, look at mine~”

The bait worked instantly. “Oh? Sulli, that’s lovely — the design’s so fine! Where did you buy it?”

“Not bought~” A soft, satin smile. “It was a gift. From the wrap party.”

Half the truth, precisely measured. The watch was one of a pair — won together, worn in matching. Her eyes flicked to his wrist: the partner watch had, of course, been removed for the evening.

Coward, she noted, privately delighted.

The food began arriving. A gleaming brass sinseollo pot took the center of the table: seasoned radish threads at the bottom, then layer on careful layer — raw beef slices, fish jeon, liver jeon, fine-cut water dropwort and mushroom and carrot, walnuts and ginkgo and pine nuts tucked between, and on the crown, red clams, sea cucumber, glistening shrimp. Charcoal underneath; the broth muttering up to a simmer; the smell of meat and mushrooms unfolding into the room until the whole table sat inside a cloud of warmth.

Taeyeon lifted her chopsticks toward a gold-fried fish jeon for Sulli — and was beaten to the draw: a slice of impossibly tender beef landed in HER bowl first.

“Eonni eats first~ the beef’s at its best right now, quick-cooked. Careful, it’s hot.”

And in the same motion, a chopstick-load of his favorite mushrooms crossed to his plate — and in the half-second Taeyeon’s eyes were on her bowl, a flying kiss crossed with it, launched and landed faster than weather. By the time anyone could have looked, the sweet, demure little sister was back in her chair, beaming at her sister-in-law.

Taeyeon, thoroughly warmed, drank her soup and missed all of it.

The dinner rolled on in two channels. In the visible one, Sulli narrated set stories — Lee Kwang-soo’s twenty NGs, the script that nearly went into the sea — and Taeyeon laughed until she had to put her spoon down. In the other channel: chopsticks that kept “accidentally” clicking against his; a small stockinged foot that grazed his calf beneath the table at irregular, unguessable intervals.

He sat opposite, itched at by the guerrilla campaign, unable to respond, unwilling to escalate — finding the whole thing, to his private disgrace, extremely exciting. He tried a quelling stare. She answered it with a face. He administered one warning tap of his shoe to her shin. She counter-pressed her foot, unhurried, across the top of his — and turned, radiantly innocent, to Taeyeon:

“Eonni, the shrimp is incredibly fresh — shall I peel one for you?”

“No no, I’ve got it.” A pause; a fond sigh. “Our Sulli’s really grown up. Taking care of people now.”

“Of course~” The eyes curved shut with the smile. “I’m Oppa and eonni’s best-behaved little sister.”

And then, while Taeyeon bent over her shrimp — Sulli’s drifting gaze crossed her collar. Stopped. Sharpened by one degree at the eye’s corner.

Below the neckline, on fair skin: one faint red mark. A kiss mark. Unmistakably.

A small flame of vinegar lit at once. The conversation upstairs continued flawlessly; downstairs, one shoe came silently off, and a small, warm foot in a thin sock traveled up his calf — toes hooking the hem of his trousers first, then gliding upward along the fabric in slow, feather-light passes engineered for maximum interference.

His body locked. The chopsticks stopped. His eyes came up with a full formal warning in them.

She met the warning with a look of luminous innocence — and winked — and hooked her ankle around his leg for good measure.

“Ji-ho-ya, try this fish jeon,” Taeyeon said, oblivious, transferring it lovingly. “Sulli says it’s the best thing here.”

“…Right.”

He pressed everything down, produced a functional smile, and ate the fish jeon. His glare, delivered under new cover, was received by the young lady opposite and returned to sender, unopened, with a mischievous curl at the mouth’s corner.

✦   ✦   ✦

Two-thirds through the meal, Taeyeon set down her chopsticks and dabbed her mouth. “Restroom. You two keep eating.”

The door closed behind her.

His chopsticks went down. The look he leveled across the table came with pressure behind it, voice low. “Stop playing.”

She was entirely unbothered. She rose instead, rounded the table, planted both hands on the arms of his chair, and leaned in until their noses nearly touched — and then closed the distance and kissed him, hard, before his tactical assessment had finished loading.

Soft lips; strawberry balm; the mingled savor of the meal. Fast, fierce, and — he registered it clearly — carrying a payload of genuine grievance.

When it broke, she stayed close, forehead to his, breath uneven, eyes fogged with a thin bright layer, voice soft and imperious at once.

“I saw the mark on eonni. Oppa — I’m jealous.”

A beat; the eye-corner tilted up; the terms arrived. “It takes a kiss to fix. Otherwise — otherwise I’ll keep making trouble, and you and sister-in-law won’t get one peaceful bite of this dinner.”

He looked at her — the pout, the leverage, the shameless jurisdictional confidence — and the exasperation melted where it stood, as it always did. He reached up and pinched her cheek, soft and fine-grained under his fingers.

“You—”

A low sigh of surrender, and he closed the distance and kissed her properly.

Gentle, this time. Unhurried. Her small storm was smoothed out edge by edge until her eyes had bent into crescents and her arms had found his neck and she was nuzzling his cheek like a satisfied cat. She counted the clock in her head — eonni would be back soon — and released him with visible reluctance.

“Oppa. You’re truly, TRULY terrible.” Her fingers trailed his cheek, contradicting every word. “Certified philanderer.”

“Hmph.”

“And you’ve corrupted ME.”

“Yes, yes. I’m the villain. I corrupted you.” He was already reaching for the napkins, dabbing the transferred lipstick from her mouth’s corner, then his own, with the thoroughness of a man erasing a crime scene.

She returned to her seat, took up her chopsticks, and resumed the picture of composure — with a smile leaking out of every seam.

Roughly one minute later the door opened; Taeyeon sat back down, glowing. “Why has everyone stopped? Eat! The pot’s at its best now.”

“Eonni, quick—” a fresh-cooked meatball was already landing in her bowl, “—this one’s SO tender. I just cooked it.”

The remainder of the dinner passed in exemplary peace. The guerrilla ceased operations; the little sister was a portrait of devotion; and only occasionally, when her eyes crossed his, did something sweet and sly flash at the bottom of them — a cat who’d gotten the cream and filed the receipt.

Past nine, the plates were cleared.

“Sulli-yah, how are you getting home? Is manager eonni picking you up?”

“I’ll just grab a taxi~ it’s easy.”

“Absolutely not. What if someone recognizes you?” Taeyeon frowned with full guardian authority. “Come with us — Ji-ho will drop you first.”

“Oh—! Then… okay.” A precisely calibrated hesitation. “Sorry for the trouble, eonni. Oppa.”

“Since when are you a guest with us?”

He gathered them up. “Let’s go. I’ll take you both.”

Masks, caps; Sulli settled the bill; the elevator dropped them to the garage. The two women took the back seat and fell into easy, giggling traffic of stories.

He watched Taeyeon’s laughing face in the mirror, and the guilt rose on schedule.

In her eyes, I’m her only. In mine — she isn’t.

And not an hour ago he’d been trading stolen kisses with the girl beside her.

But the guilt — as it always did — was shortly overrun by the other thing: the dense, disgraceful satisfaction of holding Taeyeon, Sulli and Joo-hyun all at once. The feelings were real, every one. This wasn’t sport. Career, life, future — he could and would hand each of them the best of everything.

Only — the hiding had a clock on it now. He turned it over as he drove. After LOEN. Take the company, then tell Taeyeon everything. Kneel, take the beating, take whatever comes — everything except a breakup. And from then on: no more concealment. This is who I am and what I have. Those who can accept it — everything I have is theirs. Those who can’t — better for them to never come near.

Sulli was delivered to the dorm entrance. She bade them goodbye with impeccable manners.

“Bye, eonni~ bye, Oppa~ drive safe.”

“Mm. Rest early. Good night.”

No paparazzi ping from the system. He waved. “Good night, Sulli.”

“Good night, Oppa.”

She slipped out and waved through the window until the car pulled away.

“Sulli’s really grown up,” Taeyeon said softly, watching her go. “So much more mature now.”

The corner of his mouth twitched with heroic control. “Yeah. Grown up.”

The girl running foot campaigns under dinner tables. The girl who gets jealous of a necklace. The girl who taxes stolen minutes at one kiss apiece.

Grown up indeed — into a version of adulthood documented by exactly two people, sweet and dangerous in equal parts.

The night wind moved; Seoul’s neon shivered along the river.

“Let’s go home.”

“Mm. Home.”

Tomorrow.

She always had so many tomorrows to look forward to now. Because she was Oppa’s best-behaved little sister — at least, wherever “sister-in-law” could see.

And across the city, an order had already been placed: one red BMW sports coupe. Her temperament would love a sports car — she’d gotten the license dreaming of one, back when the savings were gone and the debts weren’t. He signed off on the delivery details and set the phone down.

Where the heart shortchanges her, the hands don’t get to.
