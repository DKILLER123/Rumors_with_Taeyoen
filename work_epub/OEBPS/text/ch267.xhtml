Chapter Two Hundred Sixty-Seven

Eonni, Your Man Is Making His Move on Me

Gwangju · Hara’s childhood bedroom a relationship becomes official, a bracelet carries warmth, and LOEN finds its next flagship drama

Song Ji-ho narrowed his eyes in the dark and exhaled, slowly, the kind of exhale that carries the whole night with it.

Sweat traced a line down his chest. He reached over, gathered the boneless weight of Goo Ha-rara against his side.

“Want some water?”

“No… I’m sleepy…”

The words came out glued together, eyes already closed.

“Mm. Sleep.”

She did — immediately, completely, arms locked around his waist, breathing dropping to the slow, even tempo of someone who has stopped negotiating with consciousness.

He was, by contrast, entirely awake. His attention went to the system panel.

Crowd Ignition, combined with Jiyeon’s Stage Valor, would strengthen Ji-ho’s own command of live performance.

The bracelet mattered more.

Sulli and Hara carried different versions of the same childhood wound. Sulli’s jade pendant had helped her through depressive spirals; Starlight Warmth might give Hara a similar anchor when loneliness turned inward.

A second system notice appeared.

Children were not a reward Ji-ho could decide alone. He could provide for them; that did not give him ownership over another person’s body or future.

One more skill arrived from the congratulatory package.

PASSIVE SKILL ACQUIRED · CONCEPTION BARRIER

Inactive stateWhen Ji-ho does not intend conception, supernatural protection prevents pregnancy.

Active stateNormal fertility resumes only when he consciously permits it.

The barrier does not replace mutual agreement, STI prevention, medical care, or a partner’s independent contraception choices.

Ji-ho stared at the description.

Powerful, certainly.

Also dangerous if treated as permission instead of protection.

He stored the skill and returned his attention to the woman asleep beside him.

Morning Hara’s grandmother’s apartment side dishes for Seoul

Hara refused every attempt to linger.

“We both have schedules. And you are not testing any mysterious new system ability on me.”

Ji-ho lifted both hands.

“Understood.”

“Good. Yesterday was emotionally exhausting enough.”

They washed, dressed, and prepared to return to Seoul.

Hara’s grandmother filled bag after bag with food.

“Ji-ho-ah, take these side dishes. The seasoned vegetables are good with rice.”

“Thank you, halmeonim.”

“And the kimchi. Tell me when it runs out.”

“I will.”

“Hara, bring him back. Don’t make me wait until winter.”

“I know, halmeoni. Please go inside.”

“This child…”

They bowed goodbye and carried the food downstairs.

In the back seat, Ji-ho opened a small case.

Hara’s eyes widened at the bracelet.

“It’s beautiful.”

“Give me your wrist.”

He fastened the silver chain carefully. The mermaid charm rested above the pulse point and moved in the window light.

Hara examined it from three angles, unable to hide her smile.

“Thank you.”

She kissed his cheek.

Dong-hwan kept his eyes on the road with the discipline of a man who had learned selective deafness as a professional skill.

✦ ✦ ✦
LOEN Entertainment · chairman’s office the alien drama enters development

Back at LOEN, Ji-ho summoned Cho Young-chul.

“Hoejang-nim?”

“I met screenwriter Park Ji-eun by chance. She has a drama I want LOEN to develop.”

Cho opened his notebook.

“Title?”

“My Love from the Star. LOEN will evaluate the budget, rights, network path, and production package.”

“She has two opening episodes. Begin preliminary staffing when four are ready, but no binding production start until legal and finance approve the package.”

“Except for the principal leads, casting proceeds through auditions.”

“Understood.”

The following day, Cho returned with the recovered opening scripts.

“Writer Park expects to have eight episodes in strong draft by December.”

December fit Ji-ho’s schedule. The English album was planned for October; promotion would dominate the months that followed. By late December, he could shift attention toward drama production.

“Prepare for a late-December production launch, contingent on delivery.”

“Ne. She also asked for your notes on the first two episodes.”

“Leave them.”

After Cho left, Ji-ho began reading.

MY LOVE FROM THE STAR · OPENING EPISODES

A Joseon-era record describes a strange object descending from the sky.

A fifteen-year-old widow-to-be, Cheon Song-yi, travels toward a household she has not yet entered after the sudden death of her intended husband.

Violent wind tears her palanquin from the road and sends it toward a cliff.

At the final instant, an impossible stranger stops the fall.

Do Min-joon · arrived with the unidentified object · four hundred years on Earth begin

The opening remained close to Ji-ho’s memory of the drama.

The harder question was casting.

The original star was not available to his plan. Son Ye-jin, Han Hyo-joo, and Song Hye-kyo each possessed enormous presence, but their established screen personas leaned away from Cheon Song-yi’s dangerous balance: arrogant yet lovable, ridiculous yet dignified, apparently fearless while privately fragile.

Why limit the search to obvious names?

Ji-ho had the Encyclopedia of the Perfect Actor. With enough preparation, someone close to him could grow into the role.

Sulli. Ji-eun.

And Yoon-a.

Yoon-a stayed in his thoughts.

September was close. If he intended to test her seriously as an actress, delay would only narrow the window around Girls’ Generation’s tour and year-end schedules.

He called.

VOICE CALL · IM YOON-A

“Yeoboseyo?”

“Sister-in-law, busy?”

“Yah. I’m your nuna. What do you want?”

“I had a strong script and thought I might offer my sister-in-law an opportunity. But your attitude suggests I should call someone else.”

“A script? Whose?”

“Park Ji-eun.”

“Wait.”

“Too late. I’ve changed my mind.”

“Don’t. Oppa—no, brother-in-law. Let me see it.”

“You want it?”

“I want the chance. Please.”

“Then dinner tonight. You’re buying.”

“Fine. I’ll buy.”

Yoon-a ended the call with excitement bright enough to make her forget the hallway.

Park Ji-eun.

One of Korea’s most commercially trusted writers.

Even at the height of her idol career, scripts from writers at that level did not arrive casually. Yoon-a knew that none of those four careers would automatically intersect with hers simply because she was famous.

Then the second thought arrived.

Why was Ji-ho offering it?

Taeyeon and Tiffany had both failed to maintain distance from him. His methods were not mysterious. Music for singers. Roles for actresses. Time, attention, and exactly the opportunity a person had trained herself to want.

Was he making his move on her?

Reject the meeting?

But this was Park Ji-eun’s script.

Accepting dinner was not accepting anything else.

At least, that was what Yoon-a told herself.

“Yoon-a?” Taeyeon called from the practice-room doorway. “Break is over.”

“Coming.”

Yoon-a returned to rehearsal with the phone still warm in her hand.

Taeyeon watched her for half a beat.

Eonni, your man is making his move on me.
