Chapter Two Hundred Thirty-Two

Red Velvet

August 3, 2013 · evening Mosu, Cheongdam-dong · the only three-Michelin-star table on the peninsula five trainees, one wager called due — and a group that doesn’t exist yet gets its first dinner

Evening of August 3, the sky westward banked in brocade sunset.

Mosu, folded discreetly into a converted old Cheongdam house, was the peninsula’s only Michelin three-star — anchovy-broth herb dumplings, the sea-urchin Louise, a beef Wellington people planned anniversaries around. Quiet money, floor to ceiling.

“Wah… daebak.” Seulgi had a firm grip on Irene’s sleeve, voice dropped to a reverent whisper. “Eonni. This place is expensive, right?”

“I… don’t actually know either.”

Joo-hyun looked precisely as comfortable as she felt, which was not very.

Of the five of them — Irene, Seulgi, Wendy, Joy, Kim Ye-rim — Seung-wan came from the most comfortable family and was correspondingly the least terrified. She took up tour-guide duty voluntarily.

“Mosu’s the only three-star in the country. Dinner runs about two million won a head.”

“How much? TWO MILLION??”

Seul-gi’s eyes went to full aperture. A month of part-time shifts — for ONE dinner? Soo-young’s tongue made a brief, unauthorized appearance.

“Do you have a reservation?”

“Yes—” Joo-hyun stepped up quickly. “Ji-ho.”

“Of course. This way, please — Mr. Song has already arrived.”

The server led them to a private room and knocked.

“Mr. Song, your guests are here.”

“Come in.”

Five girls filed through; the server followed.

“You can begin service.”

“Yes, Mr. Song.”

The door closed, and the room went quiet in the particular way rooms do when everyone is recalculating a relationship.

Joo-hyun stood in an agony of protocol — how did one introduce WHAT, exactly, to one’s little sisters? The others were simply nervous in the ordinary way. And Wendy, who had once traded snacks and midnight complaints with this man as an equal, found herself stiff as a new shoe. He was more handsome than ever, more finished — and he was the CHAIRMAN of LOEN now, while she remained a trainee with a practice-room key card. Could they even still be… friends?

Ji-ho looked at the row of carefully arranged faces, laughed, and stood.

“Well, if it isn’t the menace herself, Wendy. Since when do YOU stand around like a startled rabbit with your head down?”

The old tone. It worked like a key in a lock.

“That’s because there’s a big bad wolf in the room. Obviously the rabbit’s scared.”

“She’s making jokes — full recovery.” He crossed to stand by Joo-hyun. “Everyone sit. Tonight I’m not a chairman of anything. I’m the same Ji-ho you know.”

They sat — Seung-wan at his left, Joo-hyun at his right — and the three youngest remained approximately as relaxed as quail. So Seung-wan, constitutionally incapable of enduring silence, broke it.

“So… Ji-ho. Fancy restaurant, all five of us. There’s an agenda, right?”

He didn’t bother with the scenic route.

“Seung-wan-ah. Remember our bet? I have my own company now. And you — still not debuted.”

She blinked. Of course she remembered. A year ago: if he ever founded a company before she debuted, she’d join it — and owe him one unconditional favor besides. She’d filed it under ‘adorable delusion’ at the time. And here they sat, in a room his card would pay for.

“I remember, obviously. But — one YEAR. One year and you have a company. How? Seriously, what are you, some hidden-chaebol heir in witness protection?”

The other three leaned in with identical hungry curiosity.

“Long story. Someday, over something less expensive.” He waved it off. “Right now — the bet. I held up my end. Time to collect.”

“Wait— you’re SERIOUS? You actually want me at your company?”

“Naturally. I’m famously loyal to my people. Now that I’ve made something of myself, was I going to forget a friend? Look at me, pulling you up the mountain. Are you moved? Be honest.”

“Moved, sure, very moved—” she said, smiling with visible strain, “—but I’m under contract to SM.”

“The contract is my problem. I’ll solve it.” No flicker of doubt anywhere on him. “You only have to tell me whether you WANT to come.”

He wasn’t joking. She put away the grin and actually thought. The company was rumbling about a new girl group, yes — but she’d trained barely a year; selection was a coin toss at best. Whereas at Ji-ho’s company, with their history, with what he could DO now… And everyone — EVERYONE — knew by now what a Ji-ho song was worth on a chart.

“Okay. I’ll come to LOEN.”

“Wise choice.” He put out his hand. “You won’t regret it.”

“I believe you.”

Her small pale hand met his.

He turned to Seul-gi next.

“And Seul-gi nuna?”

“N…nuna?”

The word threw her — a man in his position, still calling her that. So he DID still consider her a friend. Something in her chest went warm, and some of the tension drained off. But… moving to LOEN? SM would fight to keep her. And the new group — she believed, honestly, that she’d make the cut. Besides: LOEN ran soloists. Would she get a solo? She thought of her injured vocal cords and tasted something bitter. Solo was no longer the safe bet — which meant a group — and did LOEN have even one gram of girl-group experience? None. Leave the most experienced idol factory on the peninsula for that?

She wrestled with it — and shook her head.

He didn’t press. He looked to Joy, who smiled apologetically: a secret SM trainee since middle school, half her life already invested; she couldn’t imagine leaving. Then his eyes moved to Yeri, who froze like a spotlighted deer.

“Me? You’d take ME too?”

“Of course. Why else would I invite you to dinner?”

Her face went scarlet with excitement. She idolized him — she was literally a paying member of Solbeam.

“I— of course I want to! Only, I’d have to talk to my parents—”

“Leave your parents to me. I can be very persuasive.”

“Then I’m in!”

He looked around the table and laid the whole hand face-up.

“LOEN’s roster, at the level that matters, is currently two names: me and IU. I invited exactly five people tonight because I think all five of you are the real thing. So here’s my offer, friend to friends: come to LOEN, and I GUARANTEE you debut as a group next year.”

“Really?”

“You’re serious??”

“Completely. And your debut song — I write it. Personally. And I stay close on every album after.”

A guaranteed debut. And Ji-ho songs.

Ye-rim and Seung-wan lit up like signage. And Seul-gi and Soo-young — wavered. Staying at SM meant a CHANCE at selection, and even selection guaranteed nothing after. Meanwhile the man across the table was the composer half the industry was begging at the door of — look at Apink, resurrected by ONE song, six music-show wins and climbing; look at what he’d written for the Girls’ Generation sunbaes. If they went… his songs would never be far away.

Seul-gi’s eyes slid, on their own, to the one person who hadn’t spoken: her closest eonni, silent all evening. EONNI. SAY SOMETHING.

But Irene’s mind wasn’t on contracts at all. It was on the careful, courteous distance he’d kept from her all evening — ordinary friends, nothing more — while she’d been quietly agonizing over how to introduce him. He seemed entirely untroubled by it. And that untroubledness sat in her chest like a stone.

“Eonni. Eonni.”

“Hm?” She surfaced, blinking.

“Are you okay?”

“Fine. What is it?”

“Eonni — would YOU go to LOEN?”

Joo-hyun looked across the table at Ji-ho. Their eyes met. She nodded without a flicker of hesitation.

“I’ll go.”

“Mwo?”

“Seriously??”

The table detonated softly. Everyone knew what Irene was to SM: the next group’s near-certain pick, likely dead center. And she’d said yes like agreeing to lunch.

She gathered her thoughts and laid them out in her quiet, orderly way.

“A year ago, Ji-ho was a trainee, same as us. Look where he stands now. I believe in his ability.”

“And tonight he came HIMSELF, with this much sincerity, to invite us — which means he’s serious about this plan, and serious about us. He isn’t a frivolous person. So, I’m willing. I’ll join LOEN.”

And that was the honest whole of it — not the private history, but the plain arithmetic: when someone this capable offers to walk in front of you, you go. Luck like that isn’t refused.

Seul-gi thought of trainee days — his relentlessness, the seriousness under the jokes. And, as eonni said: trainee to chairman in one calendar year. If you didn’t follow THAT, who exactly were you waiting for? He’d guaranteed the debut himself. What was left to weigh? BET.

“Okay. I’m in too.”

Every head swiveled to the last holdout. Joy stalled, wriggled — and asked the question that mattered.

“If we join… it’s the five of US? As the group?”

“Correct.” He nodded. “LOEN’s trainee system is a generation behind and I don’t know their trainees. I know YOU. You fit.”

Guaranteed debut. His songs. This exact lineup.

She bit down, and jumped.

“If you can get SM to release me — I’ll join LOEN.”

And there it was: the whole five. Red Velvet — secured.

“Don’t worry about the contracts.” His mood had climbed several floors. “Even if SM won’t release you gracefully — your penalty fees are, for me, not a large problem.”

So THIS is what money sounds like, Seul-gi thought. And she had to admit: saying that sentence, he looked infuriatingly good.

“I, Ji-ho, promise it here: none of you will regret tonight.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Business settled, dinner began — exquisite, architectural, and portioned for songbirds.

Three stars, he reflected, mostly purchased ambience. The flavors were fine. The square footage per plate was theatrical. Nobody left a three-star FULL.

Outside afterward, he pulled on cap and mask and became, in two gestures, an anonymous tall person.

“You all heading back to the company?”

“Aniyo — I want to go home first. I need to talk this over with my family.”

“Right, right — me too.”

“Then we split here. Within a week, I’ll be at SM to settle the contracts myself.”

“Mm. Bye~”

“Bye—”

“Eonni, you’re not coming?” Seul-gi looked back at Joo-hyun.

“Ah— go ahead. I have a few things to ask Ji-ho.”

“…Okay. Got it.”

✦ ✦ ✦

He’d driven himself tonight, and hadn’t touched the wine. The car pulled toward her apartment through the early dark, and the silence in the cabin had texture.

“Nuna.” He broke it first. “Are you angry that I hid us from them tonight?”

“Hm? Ani. I’m not angry.” A beat. “If I debut in a group with Seul-gi and the others, an open relationship complicates the whole team. I understand why you did it. I didn’t want to announce it in front of them either.”

“That was my thinking, yes. …Then why do you still look unhappy?”

“Understanding it and liking it are different things. They don’t conflict.” She looked at him levelly. “Also, you were COLD to me all night. What — one week as chairman and you’re already tired of me?”

“What nonsense is that?” He freed one hand from the wheel and closed it over hers. “You have no idea how badly I wanted to hold your hand in that restaurant.”

“Forget it.” She slid her hand out from under his. “It’s late. Drive properly. I’m angry. Take me back to the dorm.”

She turned her face to the window, profile set to winter.

He knew with total certainty that actually driving her to the dorm right now would be a catastrophic tactical error. He kept the original heading — and passing through a shopping street, saw a lit storefront and had an idea. He parked, adjusted the disguise.

“Mianhae — restroom. Two minutes.”

She didn’t answer. He slipped out, walked straight past the restrooms, and bought a box from the apple-pie shop — her favorite. Next door, a florist’s window glowed; he went in. The owner, fifties, appraised the tall masked customer with one professional glance and smiled.

“For a girlfriend, young man?”

“Ne. I… made her angry, a little.”

“Imo — in this situation, what flowers do I want?”

“Hahaha! Pink roses. Number one apology flower in the nation. The meaning runs: I’m sorry — I was wrong — I like you — forgive me.”

“Ne, one bouquet of pink roses, please. …And a card. I’ll write it myself.”

He came back to the car with an armful of dew-bright pink and a warm paper box, and Irene turned — and stalled, surprise flickering through the winter.

“I passed these and thought they looked like you.”

“Hmph. Don’t think THIS earns forgiveness.”

But her hands accepted the bouquet, and somewhere behind her face, weather was improving rapidly.

“And this — in case you get hungry tonight.”

“Wah, apple pie—” The eyes curved before she could stop them; then, hastily, dignity was reinstated. “Ahem. I’m dieting.”

“Then I’ll throw it away.”

“Ya ya ya— wasting food is wrong. Give it here.”

He handed it over, grinning. She hugged the pie, bent her head, and pressed a small kiss into the roses — and then noticed the card tucked among the stems.

Card · tucked among the pink roses — his handwriting

I’m sorry. I was wrong. I like you. Forgive me.

“Pfft— mwo-ya.” The laugh escaped whole. “Hahahaha— it’s so CHEESY.”

“Haha. That bad, really?”

“Terminally.” She rolled her eyes at him — and tucked the card away with extreme care.

“So… tonight. Still the dorm?”

“Just drive your car.”

The corner of her mouth had given up entirely.

“Understood.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Her apartment had become a home in her hands — soft lamplight at the door, art she’d chosen on the walls, the gray sofa with its riot of cushions.

She set the pie and the roses on the table, and he drew her in, and the evening’s last remaining distance closed to zero.

The lamps went low. The moon kept the rest of the night’s confidences.
