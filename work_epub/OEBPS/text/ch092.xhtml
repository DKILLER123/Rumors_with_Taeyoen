Chapter Ninety-Two

The Whole Internet, On Fire

Taeyeon’s fansites and forum boards became a single continuous scream — shock, heartbreak, betrayal, refreshing the page in real time.

Taeyeon Gallery · Live Board

#1 What am I looking at??? Taeyeon-ah, WHY???

#2 Who even IS that guy? Ji-ho? A trainee nobody’s heard of? On what grounds?!

#3 You don’t know Ji-ho? The Korean-American trainee who saved Choi Sulli. Fine, decent guy — but he’s a TRAINEE. Taeyeon-ah, why!

#4 Nuna-dongsaeng romance? With a company hoobae?? Eonni, wake UP! What can he give you right now? He’ll only drag you down!

#5 Dating at the peak of her rise? With a trainee? I genuinely can’t understand this. So disappointed.

#6 Is the dating ban decorative?? What is the company DOING? Why wasn’t this stopped?

Withdrawal declarations began appearing — and multiplying. The veteran fansite masters, the archive-keepers, and the greater mass of fans held their breath and waited: waited for SM, and for Taeyeon, to say something official.

The extreme wing didn’t wait. Years of lovingly produced fancams and photo edits were mass-deleted; posts tagged with her name filled up with venom. Across DC Inside galleries, Theqoo threads, and Twitter, the boards split into open warfare. “Taeyeon fans” and “Ji-ho” became a linked search — and any account that spoke one sentence in his defense got swarmed.

The quake crossed the ocean, too. On international K-pop forums and Twitter timelines, translated posts and reposted photos stacked up arguments hundreds of replies deep, no less vicious for being in English.

Organized street protest hadn’t materialized yet — but radicalized fans were already carpet-flooding SM’s official fancafé and SNS accounts: give us an answer. Protect the artist. Punish this properly. Others began organizing letter campaigns to SM’s mailroom demanding Ji-ho’s removal from the debut team and “severe discipline for artists who break company rules.” Small but very loud corners of the net floated boycott tags — her endorsements, her broadcasts.

Not everything in the roar was hostile. A minority tried reason:

Theqoo · Scattered Voices

#1 The photo’s blurry. What if it’s a misunderstanding? Could literally be him catching her mid-fall?

#2 Even if they ARE dating — idols are human beings. Why is love a crime?

#3 The real issue isn’t the dating, it’s the photo. Stalking someone to their front door at midnight is the disgusting part.

And one strain of support stood out, visible and unashamed: f(x) fans — Sulli’s fans above all. To them, the trainee who had once saved their girl started from a very different ledger.

f(x) Gallery · Live Board

#1 I remember this trainee. He’s a good person — he saved Sulli. If it’s real, I’m blessing it. He’s better than half the men in this industry.

#2 He saved Sulli AND made the pre-debut team in three months of training. If Taeyeon likes him, she has her reasons.

#3 Support. On the Sulli thing alone — full support, forever.

But those milder voices were pebbles against a landslide, and every one of them drew instant return fire — blessing it? bless your own bias into unemployment! don’t drag OUR Taeyeon down with you! a trainee saves someone once and that’s a dating license now?!

Fans, anti-fans, and bystanders brawled across every platform at once. Rumors, speculation, personal attacks, history-digging — though Ji-ho’s history offered almost nothing to dig — braided together and drove the temperature higher until the whole thing left the rails entirely.

Six months after his first storm, the name Ji-ho swept the entire internet again inside half a day — this time as the “scandal male lead” chained to the leader of the nation’s top girl group.

Explosive fame. Also: dangerous, and dripping with malice. His background went under the magnifying glass and every detail became a fresh angle of attack — the American passport, the trainee status, the debut that hadn’t even happened yet.

The scandal had become a tsunami with a hashtag.

The Same Afternoon TaeTiSeo “Twinkle” Fan-Sign Event the storm reaches ground level

The queue wound through the venue, albums hugged to chests, faces bright with anticipation.

Taeyeon sat behind the long table, makeup immaculate, receiving each album with a smile, signing with practiced ease, looking up now and then for the little exchanges — stay healthy, okay? — thank you for coming. Everything ran on rails. The air was warm.

Until, somewhere in the middle of the queue, a ripple started that was not warm at all.

First just a few heads bent together over phone screens, expressions tightening. Then the whispers hardened into words, and the words came up the queue like stones skipped across still water.

“Eonni! Is it true?” A young girl’s voice, trembling, far too loud.

“Taeyeon eonni, break up with that trainee — please!”

“Don’t date! Eonni, think of your SONEs!”

“Break up! Break up!”

Scattered at first. Then the fuse caught, and everything pent-up went off at once. More and more fans joined the calling and the questioning; the queue’s discipline dissolved; they pressed up against the front of the table holding their phones out — that same blurred photo on every screen — faces flushed, some with reddened eyes.

“Eonni, we supported you through everything — why NOW? Why HIM?”

“Give us an answer!”

“Break up — or I’m done as your fan!”

Taeyeon’s signing hand stopped in midair.

The smile froze on her face, then slowly drained away, leaving pure incomprehension behind. She blinked, gaze skimming the agitated, angry, unfamiliar faces in front of her, ears filling with shrapnel — break up — trainee — dating — while her brain packed itself with cotton and refused to connect any of it to the room, to this table, to her own two hands.

Break up? With whom? A trainee? …Ji-ho? Last night — the photo—?

The fragments collided and would not assemble. There was no time to understand how, overnight, the people who came smiling to support her had become this wall of fury shouting these words at her face.

Instinctively she looked for a familiar pair of eyes — Tiffany? Seohyun? — but everywhere she turned there were only surging silhouettes and mouths opening, closing, ejecting sharp-edged sounds.

She went blank.

Completely blank.

Her heart slammed inside her ribs, struck square by this sudden, malicious public tribunal — cold flooding in, panic behind it, and beneath both the specific helplessness of being executed in the open.

Her lips parted. Nothing came out.

It’s like the Black Ocean. It’s exactly like that day.

“We apologize! Everyone, please stay orderly! The fan-sign is temporarily suspended!”

Her manager had been posted at her flank the whole time, and he moved fast — one hard step, his body a screen between Taeyeon and the crowd, his hand already signaling staff. Two assistants closed in and wedged back the pressing front rank.

Tiffany and Seohyun had sensed it and stopped signing, faces turned toward her with open worry.

“Taeyeon, Tiffany, Seohyun — we’re leaving. Now.”

The manager made the call without hesitating — no more explaining, no more soothing a crowd that was past hearing. Safety first, always, and doubly so with an artist visibly hit below the waterline.

Down the narrow corridor that staff and security barely held open, the manager half-shielded, half-pulled a still-dazed Taeyeon away from the table, her steps floating and wrong. Tiffany and Seohyun rose and followed hard behind.

There was no time to gather the pens. No time to apologize to the hundreds still holding their albums in line. In a churn of shouting, some fans in tears, most simply stunned, three members of TaeTiSeo vanished into the backstage corridor —

— leaving behind a wrecked fan-sign, a hive-hum of voices, and, already being uploaded from a hundred phones, the next round of headlines: Taeyeon Flees Fan-Sign After Fans Demand Breakup.

Her face in those photos — white, lost — became one of the most-shared images of the day.

The scandal had officially burned off the screen and into the world, and it was scorching everyone it touched.

✦   ✦   ✦

The car ride back to SM was very quiet.

The manager had already laid it out in brief: one photo, one tabloid, one wildfire.

Tiffany and Seohyun traded a look and relaxed considerably — they’d been there for all of it last night. They knew it was an accident.

“Don’t worry,” Tiffany murmured. “We just explain what happened. We literally have the dashcam.”

Taeyeon didn’t answer.

She watched the streets pour past the window, fingers knotting themselves in her lap.

She knew it was an accident. But some deep corner of her trembled at the other thing — at how close the photo had come to being true.

She had never been able to refuse his feelings, and never been able to make herself answer them — precisely because she had rehearsed this exact catastrophe in her head a hundred times. And in a hundred rehearsals, she had never once imagined it would arrive this suddenly.

The fan-sign kept replaying. The faces. The heat coming off them. The word break up, chanted. Her chest kept going cold.

Where do we even go from here — me and Ji-ho?

Seohyun’s eyes flicked to the mirror, reading her eonni with that quiet precision of hers.

“Eonni — the building cameras can confirm we all left the company together. The facts are clean. The company will handle it.”

A small “mm.” Nothing else. The window still had all of her.

The car dropped into SM’s underground lot. The elevator climbed, and the air thickened with every floor.

When they were shown into the wide, airless office of the sajang-nim, Taeyeon’s heart sank through the floor.

Kim Young-min, face like standing water before a storm. Lee Soo-man seated to the side, fingertip ticking against the armrest, unreadable. PR department head Lee In-hun stationed by the wall, grim.

And against the far wall — Ji-ho.

Simple practice clothes, spine straight. He turned at the sound of the door, and his eyes went to her first thing: concern, and a question.

She looked away on reflex, heart cinching tight.

“Sajang-nim. Director.” The manager stepped forward and quietly reported the fan-sign collapse.

Kim Young-min’s frown ratcheted tighter. Lee Soo-man gestured for Lee In-hun to begin.

“Taeyeon. The situation in the photograph — explain it truthfully. Are you and trainee Ji-ho in a relationship?”

She pulled in a breath, forced her chin up, voice trembling at the edges.

“No. We are not.”

“Last night — I had something to say to him privately, about trainee evaluations. I got out of the car too fast and tripped. He caught me. That’s all. Tiffany and Seohyun were both in the car — they can confirm it. We left the company together; the parking garage cameras will show it.”

“It’s true. We were all there. It was an accident.” Tiffany, instantly.

“Our dashcam should have the full audio as well. It can be pulled if needed.” Seohyun, cool and exact.

Kim Young-min and Lee Soo-man exchanged a look with nothing in it — no belief, no disbelief.

“Bujang-nim. Tiffany, Seohyun. And the manager. Step outside.”

A beat of hesitation — no one argued. One by one they withdrew.

The door clicked softly shut, and the huge room held exactly four people: Kim Young-min, Lee Soo-man, Ji-ho, Taeyeon.

The air set like concrete.

Lee Soo-man adjusted his glasses. His gaze traveled slowly from Ji-ho to Taeyeon and back, and when he spoke his voice was lower than usual, carrying an invisible weight.

“There are no outsiders in this room now. Taeyeon. Ji-ho. I want an honest answer. Are the two of you — or are you not — seeing each other?” A pause, and the words came down like a gavel: “Do not lie. For the company’s sake and your own — do not lie.”

Her throat had gone to sandpaper.

She looked at Ji-ho, and found him already looking at her — something deep in his eyes, something he was trying to hand her without words. She couldn’t read all of it. She read the concern. And she read the other thing — the gravity.

Something’s wrong. Why does seonsaeng-nim keep pressing? Tiffany and Seohyun already testified. The dashcam, the garage cameras—

And then, as her eyes met Ji-ho’s, he shook his head — a movement so small it barely existed — and the warning in it went through her like cold current.

He thinks something’s wrong too. WHAT does he know?

Her thoughts sheared apart. The fan-sign shrieking, the tidal wave presumably still building online, the company rulebook, and the thing between her and Ji-ho — that window paper, pressed so many times, never quite torn—

We never officially started. That is the truth.

But the heartbeats? The kisses? The missing him like a physical illness? What is the word for those?
