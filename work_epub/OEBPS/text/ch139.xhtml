Chapter One Hundred Thirty-Nine

“Next Time Take Me Too — I Want the Milk”

Ji-ho woke with the Idol Aura’s usual gift — zero grogginess, full charge — and pulled the sheer curtain on a view worth the trip: the Xiang River winding silver through a half-awake city, morning mist thinning around the long island midstream, old lanes and glass towers interleaved. A city accelerating in its sleep.

Down in the second-floor buffet — pale wood, white light, a scattering of business travelers — the effect of ABSOLUTE FOCUS ran its quiet experiment: two servers whispering and tracking him across the room; a newspaper lowered two seconds longer than necessary; a woman in a suit frozen mid-tongs until her colleague nudged her. He nodded pleasantly in the general direction of the attention and loaded a tray like a man with a real appetite: two fat steamed pork buns, pickles, rice porridge, two tea eggs.

“Up this early?”

IU, behind him: white knit, light-blue jeans, low loose ponytail, clean light makeup — and one faint bruise-blue shadow under the eyes that concealer hadn’t fully argued away.

“Nuna didn’t sleep much either, I see.” His gaze rested on her face exactly one beat too long. “Rough night?”

Color shot up from her collar; she shot him a glare with several conflicting things in it — last night’s footage replaying uninvited; the sound of her own retreating footsteps at the very last moment; the tossing-until-3AM afterward — and answered by not answering, picking up a tray and drifting half a step closer to him than yesterday’s calibration would have allowed.

“Wah. What a spread. It’s going to be delicious.”

Her plate assembled itself in fluent Female Artist: half a bowl of plain porridge, one yogurt, a scatter of blueberries and banana, one egg white. She eyed his tray sideways.

“You eat all that and keep THAT body?”

“At my height the furnace runs hot — this is maintenance.” He added a slice of watermelon on principle. “You’re the one who’ll be hungry mid-shopping. Don’t come begging.”

“As IF.”

Managers arrived; schedules were read out — press availability at two, a three-hundred-seat fan event at the lakeside arts center at seven, morning free. IU sipped her yogurt, glanced at him over the rim, and weighed a thought that had been forming since the airport: a strange city, side by side in the morning crowd, snacks off a griddle, an hour of being an ordinary couple in a place where nobody’s watching for them — that would make a good memory, wouldn’t it?

“Then—” fork down, eyes curving, “—let’s go out? I want to try the things you mentioned.”

Dong-hwan approved between mouthfuls of noodle soup — hats, masks, he’d shadow them — and by nine they were in the old quarter: flagstone lanes, food stalls steaming, the whole street’s smell built from chili, spice, and morning fog.

He bought the famous stinky tofu first — black cubes fried crisp, drowned in chili sauce and garlic and coriander — speared one on a toothpick and held it to her lips.

“Dare you.”

One second of visible negotiation with herself; then she leaned in and took it — and her eyes went wide as the trick of it unfolded: crisp shell, custard center, the notorious smell alchemized into something outrageously savory.

“Mm—! It’s actually GOOD!”

They worked down the lane — the fried sugar-glutinous rounds next, crackling outside, molten inside, dusted white — and when sugar powder ended up at the corner of her mouth, he reached over and thumbed it away without ceremony, and she let him without ceremony, because the ledger between them had long since passed this entry. Dong-hwan trailed at guard distance; the crowd flowed around two conspicuous silhouettes with only the occasional double-take. An hour and a half, a bag of trinkets, and back to the hotel for the afternoon’s work.

The Same Hour · The Peninsula A Corporate Event — Backstage & Stage the 160 Club’s newest member takes questions

Girls’ Generation had just finished a corporate performance, and under the lights Taeyeon was doing something new: standing in formation NOT as the smallest one. One hundred sixty-one centimeters now, proportions visibly re-drawn, presence at full wattage.

The MC was Kim Shin-young — an old friend of the group’s — and she came at the topic like a customs officer.

“Aigoo — Taeyeon-ah. Confess. HOW high are those heels today?” A hand measured the gap between their heads. “This differential is WRONG.”

“Five centimeters, eonni!” — delivered with a smug little toe-bounce and a display of the white heels.

“MWO? Five?” She crouched, audited the heel at close range, straightened, and circled her subject slowly. “Then why has your entire BODY been… lengthened? You wore five-centimeter heels BEFORE and this did not happen.”

Murmurs from the fan section — the veterans had noticed weeks ago.

“Ne!” Taeyeon’s eyes curved to crescents around the mic. “I don’t know why either — it seems like… a second growth phase? I’m really 161 now!”

“YAH! Taeyeon! You’re TWENTY-FOUR, not FOURTEEN! Second growth phase?!”

“It’s true, eonni. She really grew.” Tiffany, notarizing. The members piled on: “We measured multiple times.” “Same shoe size. Just — taller.” “We’re all DYING of jealousy.”

Kim Shin-young accepted the unscientific facts with visible suffering, leaned in, and lowered her voice to a stage whisper:

“Honestly. Secret formula? Procedure?? Introduce your eonni. I ALSO wish to grow.”

Taeyeon laughed herself sideways — and thought, involuntarily, of a particular rich-tasting milk and the faint warmth after each glass — and said only:

“Maybe I’m just… developing late?”

“Eonni, if that procedure existed,” Sunny volunteered, stamping one still-tiny foot, “would I LOOK like this? I’d have been first in line!”

“Ah,” Kim Shin-young concluded, mock-grieving, “I finally understand why Taeyeon always looks so YOUNG. Her entire growth schedule is running several beats behind the rest of us!”

In the van afterward, the interrogation continued until Taeyeon, cornered and pinched from three directions, surrendered a partial truth:

“It’s NOTHING. I just… drink milk regularly.”

The van erupted — brand? additive? dosage? MILK ALONE? — until she covered her ears: “Ordinary milk! My body’s just special!” — and the members, extracting nothing further, subsided.

She settled by the window and opened her phone to the trending board, smug as a cat in a sunbeam.

The smile drained off her face by degrees. She stared at the wiping photo for several seconds, then locked the screen with a flat click.

Something sat on her chest — dull, tight, sour. She KNEW he was there for promo. She KNEW IU had the joint schedule; same company; endless collaborations; proximity was structural. But a photo is not a schedule. A photo is an ATMOSPHERE — and the atmosphere in that frame was specific. And IU… Taeyeon’s suspicion of that particular immaculate little face had been filed months ago, under PREDATOR, PATIENT.

“Taeyeon-ah~?”

Sunny had wedged herself in against her arm at some point, rocking it.

“The MILK. What brand IS it~? Tell me, eonni~ I want to be tall too~”

Jessica, mid-shove-Sunny-away, slowed measurably at the topic and angled one ear in.

“I— I don’t actually know the brand.” — recovered, barely.

“You DRINK it constantly and don’t know the brand?” Sunny escalated to full-body aegyo. “Taeyeon~ eonni~ GOOD eonni~ tell me~ I want to grow taaaall~”

“It’s— someone gives it to me, alright?” She turned her face away; her ears had gone warm. “Next time. Next time I’ll ask for you.”

“SOMEONE GIVES IT TO YOU?” Sunny’s eyes went to full aperture, keyword captured. “Who?? Who’s that considerate??”

“Stop ASKING—”

“Then next time TAKE ME! I want to meet the milk angel! I want the milk too! I want to grow!!”

“Eonni, eonni — me too!” Yoon-a’s head appeared over the seatback, hand raised, grinning. “I’m already tall but if YOU keep growing I’ll feel threatened. Three more centimeters for me as well, please.”

“YAH! Yoon-a!” Sunny lunged for her cheeks. “What is THAT supposed to mean!”

“AAH! Eonni I was wrong! MERCY!”

The van dissolved into shrieking chaos, engine noise fully defeated. Taeyeon looked back out the window, thumb rubbing the phone’s dark edge, the photo still printed on the inside of her eyes. She breathed in, slow, and shook it off her shoulders. Work wasn’t finished. Schedules remained. She was Girls’ Generation’s leader, Taeyeon —

— and certain questions, certain feelings, would keep until she was alone with them.
