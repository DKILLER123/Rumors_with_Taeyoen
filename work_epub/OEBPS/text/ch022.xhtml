Chapter Twenty-Two

My Heart Will Go On

He nearly walked straight into Kim Mi-yeon at the door.

“Seonsaeng-nim! Good evening.” The smile came easy, and so did what followed — because it happened to be true. “You know, in vocal class this afternoon — the moment you stepped up to the front, it was like the lights changed. That passion you carry for this work… honestly, it’s magnetic. I keep thinking: if I can still love music the way you do when I’m your age, I’ll count my life a success.”

“Hahaha! Is that mouth of yours dipped in honey?” She laughed from the belly, visibly delighted with this student of hers. Then she clocked the bag on his shoulder. “What’s this? Something on tonight? Heading home already?”

“Mm. Going to hunt down a part-time job, actually.”

Trainees from thin wallets working nights between practices — in this industry, that story was as old as the industry. She lit up at once, ever the fixer.

“I know half the café owners in this district — want an introduction?”

Cafés were the classic trainee gig, and a bad fit twice over: modest pay, daytime hours. He shook his head with proper courtesy.

“Thank you, truly — but I’m only free after nine at night. I was thinking of a singing residency. Bar work.”

And there was real logic in it, beyond the better pay: a bar stage, with the crowd close enough to touch, was live-fire training — crowd control, audience feel, the thousand small skills of holding a room. All of it banked directly toward the career.

She turned it over for a moment.

“Bars can be rough water — all sorts washing through. Tell you what. There’s a quiet lounge I can vouch for. Take the subway to Itaewon Station, exit four, turn right, about five hundred meters — 28 Itaewon 11-gil, place called Whisper Bar. Ask for Seo-ju unni and tell her I sent you.”

Whisper Bar? Something warm turned over in his chest. He knew that lounge — had sat in it more than once, in another life, and loved it every time. Of all the doors she could have pointed him to.

“Thank you — seriously, thank you, seonsaeng-nim!”

She patted his shoulder, and her voice gentled into something almost maternal.

“You want to thank me? Train hard and debut fast. You know — when Taeyeon first came to this company, I sat on that panel. All these years since, exactly one person has given me that same jolt. You. So — go be worth it.”

“I will. I promise.”

He bowed low and properly, and went. Behind him, Kim Mi-yeon already had her phone out, dialing from memory.

Outgoing call · “Unni ♡”

“Unni! How’ve you been?”

“Me? Same as ever. Free this weekend? Let’s go shopping.”

“Ah — well, actually, there is one little thing… fine, I’ll just say it. There’s a student of mine — my absolute favorite — looking for night work, and I sent him your way. Unni, I’m telling you, when this kid sings…”

✦   ✦   ✦
22:00 · That Night Whisper Bar — 28 Itaewon 11-gil ten meters of ceiling, a private sky

The subway delivered him to Itaewon without incident, and the lounge opened around him exactly as memory promised: a soaring ten-meter void of a ceiling where hundreds of custom LED points glimmered in constellation, a private galaxy pressed into one room. Below, booth seating lay scattered in comfortable disorder — deep brown leather sofas curled around agate-topped tables, each with its own soft-glow wall lamp, warmth and privacy in equal measure.

Exactly the way I remembered it. The thought arrived with an ache attached.

“Good evening — table for one?”

“I’m here to see Seo-ju unni. Kim Mi-yeon seonsaeng-nim sent me.”

“You’d be Ji-ho, then?” The server looked him up and down once, then beckoned him toward the back. “This way. Seo-ju unni’s tied up at the moment, but she left instructions. Your hours are ten p.m. to two a.m. — four hours, split into two sets of forty-five minutes each. Pay is ten thousand won an hour.”

“Two rest days a week.”

Ten thousand an hour — top of the market for this kind of work, and then some. Whatever Kim Mi-yeon’s relationship with this place was, it wasn’t casual.

“Thank you. Understood.”

“It’s nine-thirty now.” A glance at the clock. “Want to run a warm-up number first? Get the feel of the stage before the real set?”

Trial songs existed so singers wouldn’t come apart from nerves at showtime. For Ji-ho, walking into a bar was walking into his own living room — but sing a free song? Always.

“Gladly.”

The server led him to the performance corner by the main bar and reached for the microphone to introduce him. Ji-ho touched his arm gently.

“Sunbae — let me do that part myself.”

A beat of surprise; then the server stepped back, ceding the floor.

Ji-ho stepped up unhurried, adjusted the mic stand with hands that had done this ten thousand times, and switched it live. His voice rolled out into the room, warm and low and magnetic.

“Good evening, everyone. I’m ‘Ji’ — the new resident singer. Tonight I’d like to offer you ‘My Heart Will Go On’ — and my wish that everyone who found their way into Whisper Bar tonight finds a little romance waiting for them here too.”

Performance

— the intro rises: a tin whistle in E major, keening and sweet, an ocean of a love story folded into eight bars — Strings pool in beneath it, soft as tidewater. The room’s chatter dims. Glasses pause halfway. Every night in my dreams — I see you, I feel you — That is how I know you go on…

His voice poured through the melody like warm spring water finding its channel — straight past the ribs, no permission asked.
