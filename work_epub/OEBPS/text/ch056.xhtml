Chapter Fifty-Six

A Reason to Marry Sunny

Being examined head to toe by four sets of gleaming eyes was profoundly uncomfortable — and Ji-ho was in exactly that position when Taeyeon sensed the disturbance and crossed the room, placing herself — quite unconsciously — half in front of him.

“Yah — what are you four DOING?”

Oh? Shielding him already? Four pairs of eyes met, and the case file thickened in perfect silence.

Sunny’s face assembled itself into pure sugar.

“Ji-ho-ya~ watching us dance just now — you were completely mesmerized, weren’t you?”

“Huh? I just didn’t want to interrupt your practice, so I waited by the door—”

“Aigoo, no need to explain,” Yoon-a purred. “NINE great beauties in one room — being stunned is only natural. But now I’m curious…” the smile sharpened, “…which one of us do you like BEST?”

Taeyeon felt the trap forming and lunged for the wheel.

“Ji-ho! Weren’t you going to tell me about your evaluation? How did it—”

“Aish, who cares about a first evaluation score.” Choi Soo-young steamrolled straight over her, hooked Taeyeon’s arm, and towed her aside. “As of this moment, your speaking privileges are revoked. Sit quietly.”

Taeyeon blinked, freshly stripped of parliamentary rights, entirely unclear how it had happened.

“I—” he began.

“Don’t tell me it’s TAEYEON?” Sunny pounced. “Look at him — evaluation ends and he sprints straight here to tell HER the score in person. Tsk tsk tsk~”

And there it was. Taeyeon had leaked something, somewhere, and this pack of bloodhounds had caught the scent — now they were hunting as a unit. Ji-ho’s mind turned exactly one gear, and the corner of his mouth rose.

“The one I like best? Obviously Sunny nuna. Honestly — if I could marry Sunny nuna someday, that would be the dream.”

Yuri and Jessica — who had been enjoying the show from the sidelines — piled on instantly.

“Oooooh~ so it’s SUNNY! Straight to MARRIAGE, no less! This is serious!”

The word landed on Taeyeon like a dropped hammer — a genuine spike of hurt lancing through her chest, her expression cracking before she could catch it. Yoon-a’s sharp eyes registered it in real time, and her stomach dropped. Oh no. Taeyeon eonni is actually serious about this one. We’ve overplayed it. Tiffany’s face wasn’t doing much better — and somewhere underneath, one more small splinter: he called Taeyeon. Not me.

Sunny, meanwhile, had gone tomato red.

“YAH! You have a death wish, kid?! Teasing ME?!”

“But nuna asked?” Hands spread, the picture of wounded honesty. “I simply answered truthfully.”

She choked, regrouped, and demanded:

“…Fine. WHY me?”

“Aren’t you Lee Soo-man seonsaeng-nim’s niece?” Total sincerity, eyes clear as mountain water. “If I married you, I’d get SM shares, wouldn’t I?”

“MWO?!” Sunny’s soul briefly left her body. “You’d marry me for SM SHARES?!”

“…Why else?”

And the look that accompanied it — the faint, unmistakable distaste — communicated the rest with brutal clarity: if not for the shares, then for… you?

“HAHAHAHAHA!”

“For the SHARES!”

“Sunny-yah, this is your big chance! A boy THIS handsome, willing to marry you!”

“So this is what money does? I’m so jealous, hahaha—”

The room went down like dominoes — and somewhere in the roar, the splinter in Taeyeon’s chest dissolved completely; she laughed until she couldn’t stand straight.

Sunny — mild, sunny, famously slow to anger Sunny — stood vibrating amid the jackal chorus of her own members, forehead vein pulsing, teeth audibly grinding. Then the fuse burned out entirely and she launched like a small cheetah.

“YAH! You little— you are DEAD today!”

He was, of course, already running, long legs opening the gap effortlessly, apologies streaming behind him:

“Nuna, I withdraw the proposal! The wedding’s off! I renounce the shares!”

“STAND STILL! SONG JI-HO! STOP RUNNING!”

Around and around the practice room they went — a full pursuit spectacle, Sunny’s short legs churning at maximum output and mathematically incapable of closing. Which gave Ji-ho his idea: he cut a corner, dove behind Taeyeon, and deployed maximum piteousness.

“Nuna — SAVE me!”

Whereupon Taeyeon spun around and locked both arms around his — pinning him — and hollered:

“Sunny-yah! Quick! I’ve got him!”

“NUNA?!” Betrayal, total and disbelieving.

She shot him one speaking glance: Idiot boy. This IS the rescue. You lit Sunny’s fuse — let her land two token hits and vent, or she’ll be plotting your destruction for a month.

Eyes met; understanding passed. He blinked twice: message received, my merciful nuna.

“Taeyeon-ah — BEAUTIFULLY done!”

Sunny arrived at speed, small fists hammering down amid a running commentary—

“MWO? Marry me for the SHARES? You little— I’ll teach you MANNERS—”

—and, physiology being what it is, the blows landed soft as thrown marshmallows. He supplied two theatrical howls anyway, as etiquette required. Taeyeon released him and reeled Sunny in.

“Okay, okay — anger causes wrinkles.”

“Taeyeon-ah, he said he’d marry me for the SM SHARES!”

Yu-ri looked back and forth between the two of them, then delivered her finding with judicial gravity:

“I mean… if it’s Ji-ho? Even shares-motivated, you’re not exactly losing on the deal.”

“But I don’t HAVE any SM shares!!”

…Not wanted for herself AND holding no shares. That is, admittedly, rather bleak.

The members’ laughter went up another octave, and Sunny’s suffering deepened proportionally. Taeyeon — laughed dry, but with a captain’s residual conscience — surveyed Sunny’s thousand-yard stare and mercifully changed the subject.

“Ji-ho. The actual news. Out with it.”

“Right — how DID you score?” Tiffany’s smile had recovered too. “Calling Taeyeon specially — must’ve gone well?”

A chance to flex in front of all of Girls’ Generation. Inwardly: fireworks. Outwardly: still water.

“Mm. Scraped out first place, somehow.”

“MWO? FIRST?”

Tiffany’s eyes went saucer-wide. She’d taken monthly firsts herself — but she also remembered her own first evaluation, back when her Korean could barely order lunch. First place, first month, wasn’t a thing that happened. The whole room wore the same expression: every woman here had won firsts, and none of them had ever heard of a comprehensive first in month one.

Taeyeon alone was unsurprised — she’d heard the song. But hearing it confirmed still lit her up from the inside.

“Congratulations.”

“Nuna, you’re suspiciously calm. I was expecting shock. Maybe gasping.”

She shrugged, all mischief.

“With your ability? I’d only gasp if you LOST.”

“Wait — you KNEW he’d take first?” Jessica’s curiosity sharpened.

“Mm.” A nod. “He wrote a song. He borrowed a production room through me. If you’d heard it, you’d understand — this first place is entirely deserved.”

“Wrote a song? Words AND music? Independently?” Choi Soo-young’s eyes had gone wide and stayed there.

Another nod — and Soo-young began, visibly, to re-run every prior calculation about Ji-ho. She wrote herself; her lyrics for “How Great Is Your Love” had made the third album. Yu-ri wrote. Seohyun and Tiffany wrote. All lyrics, all of them — because composition was the wall. Everyone tried; almost everyone’s attempts died quietly in a producer’s inbox. She knew exactly what full independent writing meant, and the trainee she’d dismissed as “not matching Taeyeon” had just changed categories entirely.

Similar recalculations ran down the line. Yoon-a had always preferred mature, settled men — the nuna-dongsaeng thing wasn’t her lane. But if he’s talented AND looks like that… age is just— she caught herself. As expected of Taeyeon eonni. Fast hands.

And Tiffany sat with a feeling she couldn’t quite name — the growing certainty that something had happened between those two that she wasn’t privy to, that Taeyeon now stood closer to him than she did. But I met him first. It felt, she decided, exactly like watching someone else casually use the outrageously expensive lipstick you’d been saving.

Yu-ri — mid-arrangement-studies herself — opened her mouth to ask about the song, and never got the chance. Taeyeon was suddenly propelling Ji-ho doorward with both hands.

“Yah — news delivered, off you go, we have PRACTICE.”

Out in the corridor, she looked up at him, brisk and slightly pink.

“Busy right now. We’ll talk tonight on Kakao.”

“Nuna, I—”

“Go. Go-go-go.”

And thus was the month’s first-place trainee summarily ejected.

She turned back into the practice room — into eight pairs of eyes, all pointed directly at her — and deployed the captain’s voice.

“What are you all looking at? Back to practice.”

“Aigoo,” Soo-young sighed to the ceiling, “someone’s guarding her food bowl. Practicing, practicing.”

“Honestly, Taeyeon,” Yu-ri added with a luxurious eye-roll, “nobody’s going to STEAL him.”

“QUIET! ‘The Boys.’ From the top!”

The formation assembled, and Taeyeon exhaled. Ji-ho is in MY bowl. Whether I eat is my business — but the bowl gets guarded. Because these members of mine are STARVING, and yes, nobody steals a teammate’s boyfriend — but he isn’t my boyfriend yet, is he?
