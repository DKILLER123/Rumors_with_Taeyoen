Chapter Two Hundred Sixty

The Family Fund

Ji-ho’s hillside villa · afternoon a necklace, two signatures, and the word neither Taeyeon nor Tiffany can ignore

Tiffany returned to the villa and slowly surveyed the living room.

Ji-ho had said something was waiting for her.

On the coffee table lay a Tiffany-blue jewelry box tied with satin ribbon. A kraft-paper document envelope rested beside it.

She sat and opened the box first.

An aquamarine waterfall necklace lay against black velvet. The principal stone—listed on the certificate at 49.59 carats—held the pale clarity of seawater. Fine diamonds descended around it in articulated lines, catching the room’s light with every movement.

TIFFANY & CO. · AQUAMARINE WATERFALL GIFT

Center stone · aquamarine · 49.59 carats

Setting · cascading diamond framework on dark presentation velvet

Declared value · approximately US$200,000 in the story timeline

Message · not subtle, not inexpensive, and impossible to mistake for a casual apology

Tiffany watched the gemstone for a long time before touching its cool surface.

“You certainly know how to spend.”

Her voice vanished into the empty room.

What could she do?

She had barely survived confessing to Taeyeon and asking for a place that would not destroy their friendship. The existence of Ji-eun changed nothing fundamental. Leaving Ji-ho had never become easier merely because the situation grew more crowded.

“Song Ji-ho, you really are…”

She let the unfinished accusation become a sigh.

Then she opened the document envelope.

“Dream Family Fund?”

DREAM FAMILY FUND · PARTICIPATION OFFER
Eligibility

The fund is restricted to individuals whom Song Ji-ho formally designates as members of his private family circle.

Qualified participant

Hwang Mi-young, professionally known as Tiffany, is approved to subscribe capital.

Return mechanism

System-backed target return: 50–100% annually within the novel’s supernatural framework. This is not a real-world investment representation or ordinary market guarantee.

Restrictions

No borrowed capital, margin debt, leveraged subscription, or third-party nominee funds.

Liquidity and disclosure

Redemption terms, tax treatment, and legal reporting remain subject to the fund agreement and applicable jurisdiction.

Tiffany’s fingers tightened around the paper.

Fifty to one hundred percent a year.

She knew almost nothing about investing. Her earnings had spent years sitting in bank accounts because the alternatives frightened her: fraud, loss, or a persuasive stranger converting a lifetime of work into nothing.

Ji-ho was different.

His rise from unknown Korean-American trainee to LOEN chairman had required extraordinary judgment—or an extraordinary secret. Either way, his record was difficult to argue with.

But the return was not the phrase that held her.

Family fund.

Tiffany read those words twice.

Then she smiled.

So this was how he intended to tell her she belonged somewhere permanent.

After reviewing the restrictions and payment instructions, she signed and transferred three billion won in uncommitted savings.

“Family,” she murmured. “You really chose that word.”

✦ ✦ ✦
Mountain campsite · the same day Taeyeon reads the same word differently

“A family fund?”

Taeyeon sat tucked against Ji-ho with the agreement in her hands.

“An investment vehicle I established,” he said. “If you don’t already have a plan for your savings, you can consider it.”

Her attention remained on the title.

“What does ‘family’ mean?”

Ji-ho kissed her gently before answering.

“People I love and intend to protect over the long term.”

Taeyeon looked down at the document again.

“How many participants?”

Ji-ho decided honesty was now the only survivable policy.

“You were approved first. Tiffany was second.”

Tiffany.

The corner of Taeyeon’s mouth moved.

“And Ji-eun?”

Ji-ho had other plans for Ji-eun. Sulli and Irene had little idle capital; Jessica’s savings were committed to the company; Jiyeon had recently paid contract costs, supported her family, and purchased property.

“For now, participation is open only to you and Tiffany.”

“Is that so?”

Taeyeon’s eyes curved. The satisfaction was small, petty, and impossible to suppress.

Ji-eun still had distance to cover.

She read the restrictions more carefully than the source version of herself would have admitted, then turned to the signature page.

“I’ll transfer the capital after we return.”

“That quickly?”

“I trust you with more than money.”

Her expression became serious.

“Don’t make that trust another thing I regret.”

Ji-ho’s brow tightened.

“I won’t.”

Taeyeon held his gaze long enough to make the promise uncomfortable.

Then she placed the papers aside and leaned into him.

The afternoon became private. Whatever closeness followed remained beyond the page—chosen, mutual, and unable to erase the difficult conversation still waiting outside the campsite.

✦ ✦ ✦
SM Entertainment · styling floor after the campsite

When Taeyeon returned to work, she carried the fresh brightness of someone who had slept well, cried enough, and spent two days away from fluorescent corridors.

Her makeup artist studied her skin while applying a light finishing layer.

“Taeyeon eonni, your complexion is incredible today. Did you change products?”

“Ani.” Taeyeon touched her cheek and tried not to smile. “The usual.”

The usual products had never produced this particular result.

She remembered Ji-ho’s face, the campfire, and the words you were approved first. Sweetness gathered behind her ribs.

After the touch-up, she entered the lounge. Sunny occupied the sofa with a bag of chips.

One look at Taeyeon made Sunny’s eyebrows rise.

“What miraculous spring water did you drink? You’re glowing.”

Taeyeon dropped onto the sofa.

“Eat your chips.”

“Two days ago, you looked prepared to bury a man. Today you look as though spring arrived personally.”

Taeyeon refused to answer. Her mouth refused to stop smiling.

“Fany was cheerful this morning too,” Sunny added. “She brought coffee for everyone.”

Taeyeon’s fingers paused in her hair.

Tiffany.

The family fund had admitted her second.

There was jealousy in that knowledge. Surprisingly, there was also reassurance. Ji-ho had not treated Tiffany as a temporary mistake. He had placed her inside a structure meant to last.

And Taeyeon had been first.

Her smile returned.

He had damaged her standards so badly that even hierarchy could feel comforting.

“What are you thinking about?” Sunny asked. “That expression is suspicious.”

“Nothing. I’m buying dinner tonight. Korean beef. Invite everyone.”

Sunny stared.

“Did you win the lottery?”

“Better mood than that.” Taeyeon stood. “Are you coming or not?”

“Of course I’m coming.” Sunny followed, then lowered her voice. “But the two of you should be more careful.”

The two of you.

Taeyeon slowed for half a step before continuing.

“I know.”

Tiffany approached from the opposite end of the corridor in a pale pink cardigan. Her whole face brightened when she saw Taeyeon.

“DaeDae!”

“Dinner tonight. Korean beef.”

“I’m in.” Tiffany moved closer and lowered her voice. “Did you receive it too?”

“Mm.”

“I transferred my idle savings.”

“So did I.”

They looked at each other and sighed at exactly the same time.

“What do you think he means by it?” Tiffany asked.

Taeyeon tasted the words before answering.

“Family fund. He said I was first.”

Tiffany’s eyes curved.

“Then I’m second.”

“Why are you smiling? You accepted this awfully quickly.”

Taeyeon poked her lightly in the side.

“What is the alternative?” Tiffany asked. “He won’t belong only to us. You know that better than I do.”

“So?”

Tiffany leaned close.

“So we make sure we’re still standing together at the end.”

Taeyeon stared, then laughed.

“Fany, you’ve become dangerous.”

“Perfectly nice women don’t survive this kind of relationship.”

Tiffany offered her arm. Taeyeon linked theirs.

Perhaps this arrangement was not peace. It was, at least, an alliance with the person she trusted most.

At the corridor corner, Yoon-a watched them pass with narrowed eyes.

Song Ji-ho was frightening.

Two days earlier, Taeyeon and Tiffany had looked like the aftermath of his exposure. Now they walked arm in arm, carrying matching secrets and smiling again.

✦ ✦ ✦
LOEN Entertainment · executive reception room a CJ proposal arrives in person

Ji-ho had barely reached the office when his secretary approached.

“Hoejang-nim, a guest is waiting in the reception room.”

“Who?”

“Executive Director Lee Kyung-hoo of CJ Entertainment.”

Ji-ho paused.

“Lee Kyung-hoo?”

“Ne. Chairman Lee Jae-hyun’s eldest daughter. She recently returned from the United States.”

CJ Group.

Ji-ho considered the timing.

“Prepare two cups of tea. Use the Jukro blend from my cabinet.”

In the reception room, Lee Kyung-hoo sat with her back straight and both hands composed in her lap. The posture was flawless. The tension in her eyes was not.

She had been home for more than a week. Taking responsibility for the CCM problem was her first serious assignment after returning: a subsidiary in deficit, a damaged flagship group, and an asset CJ increasingly regarded as burdensome.

Today’s meeting served both duty and private curiosity.

Waiting stretched longer than the clock suggested.

Kyung-hoo looked down. Her hand had closed into a fist, and moisture had gathered in her palm.

She frowned at herself.

Raised as a successor, she had learned to maintain calm in rooms filled with older men who expected her to fail. Yet her heartbeat now resembled the first piano recital of her adolescence.

A knock sounded.

The latch turned.

Ji-ho entered against the corridor light in a white casual shirt and black trousers. The simplicity only sharpened the impression of him: clean lines, clear eyes, and an ease no camera had captured properly.

Kyung-hoo forgot her prepared opening.

Photographs often exaggerated celebrities. Ji-ho’s had failed in the opposite direction.

“Annyeong haseyo. Song Ji-ho.”

His voice and smile arrived together.

Kyung-hoo stood too quickly and offered her hand.

“Annyeong haseyo. Lee Kyung-hoo, CJ Entertainment.”

The touch lasted only a moment, but his palm was dry and warm while hers betrayed every degree of nerves.

Ji-ho noticed and spared her the embarrassment of mentioning it directly. He offered a tissue.

“Director Lee, would you like the air-conditioning lowered?”

Heat reached the tips of her ears.

“No, thank you. I’ve only just returned from the United States. I’m still adjusting to the climate.”

Ji-ho sat opposite her.

“Of course. Did you return to begin taking responsibility within the group?”

The neutral question helped Kyung-hoo recover. After several minutes of formal conversation, she opened her case and pushed a document across the table.

“Hoejang-nim, CJ has finalized the theatrical settlement for The Girl in the Sun.”

Ji-ho reviewed the figures.

He closed the statement and met her eyes.

“CJ sending its executive director personally with a settlement is an extraordinary courtesy.”

A trace of amusement entered his expression.

“But the chairman’s eldest daughter did not cross Seoul merely to deliver accounting.”

Kyung-hoo accepted that the opening had failed.

“Then I’ll be direct.”

She set a second file on the table.

“Are you interested in Core Contents Media?”

Ji-ho’s gaze became still.

“What happens if I am?”

“CJ sells CCM to LOEN.”

“And if I’m not?”

“Then our practical buyer is Kim Kwang-soo.”

Silence settled over the reception room.

Ji-ho lifted his tea and let the clean, green fragrance rest on his tongue before setting the cup down.

“His offer must be substantial.”

Kyung-hoo did not evade the numbers.

CCM DISPOSAL · COMPETING STRUCTURE
Indicative enterprise valuation

₩120 billion

Kim Kwang-soo proposal

₩68 billion for a controlling position

CJ strategic alternative

Transfer CCM to LOEN in exchange for equity rather than accepting a purely cash-based sale.

Ji-ho smiled.

“So you came for a higher bid.”

Kyung-hoo shook her head.

“Not a higher cash price.”

She leaned forward, composure finally aligned with her purpose.

“I want something more valuable than sixty-eight billion won.”

Ji-ho raised one eyebrow.

“I’m listening.”

“CCM in exchange for fifteen percent of LOEN Entertainment.”

The tea cooled untouched between them.
