We earnestly ask fans and the public to refrain from spreading unverified information, and to trust that justice will be pursued through legal channels. To all who have given us their trust and support, we once again extend our deepest gratitude.

Beyond the guilt-stricken, suddenly protective SONE bloc, the affair drew in a wave of new-generation fans — the talent-worshippers, the face-appreciators — who, together with a contingent of Sulli’s fans, spontaneously founded his first fan support station.

Its founder: the girl from the subway. Kang Seo-ju.

Field Notes

Why does SM keep playing the burn-then-reverse game? Because it works. The textbook case: Girls’ Generation’s Black Ocean era — a crisis that genuinely escaped control, which the group’s sheer strength and SM’s marketing machine then transmuted into legend. Manufacture (or inherit) a containable crisis; command the reversal narrative through overwhelming PR; convert catastrophic risk into emotional capital, name recognition, and narrative control.

Hero → nationwide villain → reversal. When people realize they were tricked into wounding an innocent, guilt converts into doubled, compensatory support — and everyone who lived through the reversal owns a piece of the story forever.

Ji-ho had no bandwidth for the internet. Waking in a hotel bed, his first act was hiring professional cleaners: the garbage carted off, the graffiti stripped, the corridor walls repainted to blank innocence.

His second was the hospital.

He stood in the doorway with a simple fruit basket and knocked lightly on the open door.

On the bed, the young woman was straining toward a water cup on the side table, the bandaging on her knee making the maneuver clumsy. Fair, clean-featured, with round, faintly doleful eyes — the classic “white rabbit” face, harmless and fresh, the girl-next-door who probably didn’t know she was anyone’s first crush.

She turned at the sound. A few scrapes and bruises stood out on the delicate face, and her expression was blank with non-recognition.

“I’m sorry — who…?”

He came in and set the basket on the cabinet.

“Apartment 801. Ji-ho. We met last night.”

“I’m sorry you were dragged into it — and hurt this badly.”

“801?” Her eyes widened as she studied him, and the chaos of the previous night resolved into sequence: the man who was attacked. The thug who shoved her and ran. And, at the very end — a warm back, carrying her.

“Ah— it’s YOU.” Then her expression turned subtly complicated. “You— you’re that Ji-ho? From the news? With Taeyeon—”

“Ne.” No further explanation offered. “How are your injuries? What did the doctor say?”

The subject steered itself gently back to her, and she relaxed. His voice is so gentle. The curiosity could wait.

“I’m okay. Mostly contusions and scrapes — the knee needed a few stitches. The doctor says it can’t bear weight for a while, so walking’s going to be awkward.” She smiled ruefully and gestured at the braced leg. “I hate hospital smell. Disinfectant makes me nervous. And… staying admitted must be expensive.”

Her voice dropped on the last words. Then she looked up at him, suddenly earnest.

“Um — Ji-ho-ssi. The emergency fees and the admission, you covered those, right? Can I see the bill? I’ll pay you back. It might just… take a little time.” As if afraid he wouldn’t believe her, she hurried on: “My income’s irregular — part-time jobs and acting classes — but I’ll keep track and pay in installments. Is that okay?”

He shook his head and took the bedside chair.

“This happened because of me. You were completely innocent. Every won of the medical costs is mine to front.”

He caught the protest gathering in her eyes and kept going.

“Besides — I’ve filed with the police. They’re tracking him. When he’s caught, all of this gets recovered from HIM in damages. So this is genuinely not yours to worry about.”

“But—” She held out a second longer, then pressed her lips together and changed the subject, partly to dissolve the awkwardness. “I can’t believe we’re… roommates. I do classes and part-time work all day — I feel like I’ve never once run into you.”

“Same. Trainee hours are chaos. We must have missed each other perfectly.”

“Right — I still don’t know your name.”

She smiled.

“Bae Soo-bin.”

“Bae Soo-bin?” A flicker of open surprise crossed his face. Not Chae Soo-bin?

“Ne.” She tilted her head at his expression. “Is something wrong?”

“Ah — no.” A small shake of the head. “It’s a lovely name.”

Quiet and introverted off her feet, she ducked her head at the compliment from an alarmingly handsome man.

“Thank you.”

So the stage name came later — a debut-era name clash, probably. He moved on.

“You mentioned acting classes. You’re an actress?”

“Mm — in the… reserves, let’s say.” At the topic her eyes brightened, and some of the sickroom wilt fell away. “I fell in love with performing, and decided to walk this road properly.” A pause, faintly sheepish. “My parents are both teachers — quite traditional. They didn’t approve at first. It took getting into Konkuk University’s film program for them to respect the choice.”

“A scholar, then.” One eyebrow rose — that, he hadn’t expected.

“Hardly.” She waved it off. “Once I actually got to university I realized coursework and real acting practice don’t coexist. Not if you want to prepare properly for stage work or auditions. So — I took a leave of absence, rented a room, and now it’s professional acting workshops plus part-time jobs to stay fed.”

Her eyes, saying it, were very steady — the particular toughness of someone who knows exactly what she wants and what it costs.

“I had a class today, actually. Didn’t exactly plan for… this.” A wry glance at her knee.

“Taking a leave to chase it — that’s brave.” He meant it; the shape of it rhymed with his own road. “So the main problem now is mobility?”

“Yes.” She nodded fast, watching him with a flicker of hope. “The doctor said this morning I was lucky — nothing serious, just keep the dressings changed and avoid infection. So I want to discharge. Lying here makes me anxious, and it’s burning money.”

He considered. For someone this sensitive who needed quiet to heal, home probably beat a ward.

“If you’re sure you’re up to it, I’ll handle the paperwork.” Decision made. “But — alone at home, stairs, groceries, daily life. How’s that supposed to work?”

She swung an arm, game.

“This little injury? I’ll manage.”

He looked at her — hurt because of his mess, chasing a dream alone in Seoul — and thought for a moment.

“Here’s the arrangement. We discharge you now — the corridor should already be cleaned and repainted. And until that knee is properly healed: errands, heavy bags, dressing changes, anything — you knock on my door or you call. Any hour.”

He took out his phone.

“Let’s trade numbers… roommate.”

She looked at him with open gratitude. He carried me to the ER. Fronted the bills. He was the victim, and he still didn’t look away. He’s… someone who carries things.

She produced her phone.

“Then — I’ll be in your care, Ji-ho-ssi. And,” she added, suddenly strict, “even if the attacker pays in the end — you show me that bill. Otherwise I won’t be able to live with it.”

“Deal. You’ll see it.”

This time he didn’t refuse outright — a step down the mountain, graciously provided.

Outside the window the sun had strengthened. One senseless disaster had introduced two strangers who had shared a wall for three months — properly, at last.

While he handled the discharge desk, Bae Soo-bin sneaked out her phone and checked the news.

Oh — it’s already been cleared up?

She read the whole cycle, wide-eyed. Fastest trainee ever into SM’s debut team. A songwriting prodigy. So… he’s actually AMAZING?

The discharge total came to ₩1,130,000. He showed her the bill as promised; she took one look and stealthily bit her tongue. So expensive!

“Come on. I’ll take you home.”

“Ne… thank you.”

She thanked him quietly and tried shifting her weight onto the injured leg.

The instant her toes touched the floor, the knee answered with a needle of pain; a startled, muffled sound escaped her and her balance went sideways—

—and an arm was already around her shoulders, a hand already steadying her elbow, a quiet strength gathering her back from the edge of a very undignified fall.

“Careful.”

The voice arrived from very close range — like a well-made cello bowed just beside her ear, resonating somewhere considerably south of the ear itself.

She looked up, pulse scrambled, and met his downturned eyes. The supporting posture had closed the distance to almost nothing: close enough to see the grain of his skin, the straight line of his nose, the clean angle of his jaw. He was frowning — at her leg, in focused concern.

“Are you all right?”

Why is his voice like that? Why is his skin as good as MINE? I’m the girl here—

At this range the face hit with enough force to make her briefly forget both breathing and the knee. Last night’s chaos had left no room to look; now, in full daylight, she finally saw her “scandal-center” roommate clearly. Not the standard chiseled peninsula handsomeness — something cleaner, quieter, gentleman-mannered, warm-voiced.

Her heart skipped a beat for no reason she was prepared to examine.

As expected of the flower garden that is SM…

“I’m— I’m fine.”

“Then let’s go.”

He steadied her as they walked, his breath brushing the fine hair at her forehead — faintly cool, faintly mint. Where his palm held her shoulder and arm, a small illicit current seemed to travel straight through the fabric. She could feel the warmth and the structural certainty of his arm and chest: a firm, entirely male solidity, a category of touch her life had not previously catalogued.

“Can you— can you still walk?” He’d felt her go rigid and mistaken it for pain.

“Huh? Yes— yes I can!” She surfaced with a jolt, cheeks catching up to the situation and igniting. She wrenched her gaze away from the unreasonably good face and the entirely-too-close lips, and tried to stand on her own — only to find her body had gone inconveniently soft, as if that one moment of proximity had drained the strength out of her.

“Don’t force it.” He didn’t let go — just adjusted his support so more of her weight settled onto him, carefully avoiding every bruise. “Take a second to adapt. We’ll go slow.”

Steady, level, and far too pleasant — every syllable landing on her overstrung nerves like a fingertip on a fret.

She managed a tiny “mm,” fixed her eyes on the floor, and let herself be half-supported, half-carried out of the hospital — her entire sensorium apparently relocated to the exact points where they touched, and to the clean, cool scent that would not stop following her.

A small voice in her head was shrieking: Bae Soo-bin, WHY are you nervous?! He’s just helping you walk!
