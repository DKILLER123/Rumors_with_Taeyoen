Chapter Sixty-One

A Little Bit Famous

Late May, 2012 Seoul Metro — Line 4 clouds drift, time flows, a month slips past

“Yah— yah! Seo-ju, LOOK!”

A high-school girl yanked her friend’s sleeve, pointing across the car.

“That guy! Doesn’t he look EXACTLY like the piano boy from Taeyeon eonni’s MV? Ji-ho?”

Lee Ju-yeon was a Taeyeon fan of the committed variety — she’d bought the TTS album the day it dropped and evangelized it since. Kang Seo-ju, by contrast, didn’t chase idols at all, and having studied until two in the morning, mostly chased sleep. But friendship had obligations. She lifted her heavy eyes and followed the pointing finger.

One look — and her breath snagged, and the drowsiness burned off like morning fog.

…I think I just fell in love.

It wasn’t the aggressive kind of handsome. It was the first grass-scented breeze of a spring afternoon crossing your face — the kind of presence that made your whole chest feel swept and lit. And the eyes were the trap: not the usual deep black or brown but something between pale gold and burnished copper — a clear, translucent amber. Calm. Warm. When her gaze accidentally brushed his, it was like looking out over open water; her whole mood lifted without her permission.

“It IS him! It’s him!”

While Seo-ju drifted, Ju-yeon had her phone out, “Twinkle” MV already queued, scrubbing to the piano scene.

“The first time I saw this part I couldn’t LOOK AWAY — and he’s even more handsome in person?! The camera is UNDERSELLING him — how is that possible — omo omo—”

Seo-ju didn’t follow idols — but even she knew about the Korean-American trainee who’d saved Sulli. Is it really him? She simply stood up and walked over.

“Yah — Seo-ju?!”

Deaf to her friend, Seo-ju planted herself in front of him — opened her mouth — and discovered that facing this face at close range, her vocabulary had resigned. Her palms began to sweat on her behalf.

The train hummed through the city. Ji-ho regarded the frozen student and smiled.

“Hi there. Something I can help with?”

His SMILE is nice too— In that instant, Kang Seo-ju finally understood her best friend’s entire relationship with Taeyeon.

“Excuse me — are… are you Ji-ho?”

Now THAT startled him. Being approached by girls was weather; being approached by name was news.

“That’s me. And you are?”

“I— I’m Kang Seo-ju! Second year, Cheongdam Girls’ High School!”

“Kang… Seo-ju. Seo-ju. That’s a lovely name.”

She promptly lost the ability to make eye contact. And Ji-ho rubbed his nose, privately mortified — the company had spent two days drilling idol-to-fan conversation scripts into the trainees, and apparently the training had installed itself directly into his reflexes.

Speaking of fans…

“Seo-ju-ssi — how do you know me? Wait.” His voice rose, hope audibly inflating. “Are you… my FAN?”

“Ne!” The nod nearly took her head off. She swallowed the part about having become a fan roughly ninety seconds ago on the basis of his face. “I first saw you in the SBS interview. What you did for Sulli-ssi — I thought it was so brave. Really amazing.”

“An actual fan!” He was on his feet. His first street recognition — and the feeling was everything vanity had promised it would be. “Thank you. Really — thank you for the support!”

At close range the face’s destructive radius only widened; Seo-ju could no longer look anywhere near it. The train chose that moment to reach her station — salvation. She snapped into a bow.

“When you debut I’ll support you the whole way! Bye!!”

And she seized Ju-yeon and hauled her bodily out the doors — Ju-yeon, who never got her own hello in edgewise:

“Yah, Kang Seo-ju! We had two more STOPS—”

The doors closed on them. Ji-ho laughed to himself all the way to Apgujeong.

First-week streaming record for a 2012 girl-group sub-unit — outselling most second-tier groups’ full-member albums. An explosion on arrival.

And riding the blast wave: the piano boy. Between the MV segment quietly going viral and SBS airing the joint interview with Sulli, Ji-ho now owned a genuine sliver of national name recognition. He simply never left the company long enough to feel it — the morning commute was his one public appearance, and today it had finally paid out. The hunger to be recognized, carried through all the unfamous years, had just been handed its first taste. His mood was excellent.

✦   ✦   ✦

At the company: thirty minutes of gym, then the day’s schedule — which today included the most cheerful oddity in the curriculum, tennis class. Nearly every trainee took it; nothing trained footwork agility for complex choreography quite like chasing a ball around a court. Exhausting curriculum, occasional delight.

Five o’clock arrived fast. He found Irene.

“Nuna — something’s come up tonight. I’ll have to skip dance practice.”

A blink; a small nod.

“See you, nuna~”

He left SM for his usual gym, showered off the day’s sweat, and changed: light gray long-sleeve tee, matching drawstring lounge trousers — one unified pale tone, radically simple, letting the 182-centimeter frame and the face do all the talking. The technical term was clean handsome.

Style Index: 70 · First-impression bonus: Clean · Comfortable

Nearly a month had passed since he’d burned the Opportunity Card on Career, and nothing special had surfaced — which was exactly correct. Opportunity cards needed triggering conditions; burn one and then hibernate at home, and it would sleep forever. He’d spent enough of them in the game to know the discipline: you built the runway, and the card landed the plane.

Field Notes

The strategic read on SM, June 2012:

EXO debuted last month, which slams the boy-group window shut for roughly four years — next roster likely assembling around 2014. The company may well pencil Ji-ho in as that group’s core. But the group route was never on his map. Solo — only solo.

SM was a group factory, yes, but it kept solo acts: BoA the queen, Kangta — and one Chinese solo vocalist, Zhang Liyin. His assets: heavyweight vocals and full self-production. His deficit: a training résumé measured in weeks.

And in this market, a solo debut was the hardest road there was. Moving SM would take a bigger chip. The chip he chose: “Some.”
