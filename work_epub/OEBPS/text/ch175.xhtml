Chapter One Hundred Seventy-Five

Into the Sea

The same night Gapado — the breakwater, the black water, a rented island house a call on speaker, a slap, a fall — and a rescue that changes everything

“Sulli? Sulli-yah!”

He felt her lock rigid against him and sat up fast, hands finding her shoulders, shaking gently. “Can you hear me?”

She came back like a diver surfacing from a nightmare — one violent gasp, then a fit of coughing. When she lifted her head he couldn’t see her face in the dark, but he could feel the ragged breathing, the tremor running through her frame. Her hand whipped around and clamped his wrist with everything she had, nails biting skin, voice scraped dry.

“Who is it? Since when? Oppa — you’re lying, right? It’s fine if you don’t like me — really, I’d be fine — just say you’re lying—”

The words tumbled, out of order, a mind trying to anesthetize itself: if he’d only say the sentence back, it could be unheard.

He didn’t answer with words. His free hand closed around her cold, shaking one and held it hard.

Then he acted.

He reached into the pocket of his own jacket — still hanging off her shoulders — and drew out his phone. The screen bloomed in the dark, lighting the hard line of his jaw and her paper-white face. He scrolled to the contact saved with a small heart — Taeyeon — and, with no hesitation at all, dialed. And pressed speaker.

Beep— …beep—

The ring tone stood out huge and flat against the silent shoreline, each repetition a hammer on her sternum. She stared at the glowing name, at the speaker icon, lips pressed into a bloodless line, nails deeper in his wrist, shaking and unable to stop.

Taeyeon eonni? So the scandal back then — the whole time — was never just a scandal?

Voice Call · speaker on

“Yeoboseyo? Ji-ho-ya?” — a lazy, nasal warmth, and behind it a racket: girls’ laughter, faint music, a hotel room mid-riot.

(background, clear and indignant) “Yah! Eonni! That’s Taeyeon eonni’s chicken leg! Put it DOWN!”

(background, an unmistakable boyish register) “Shhh! Quiet! Taeyeon eonni isn’t here — if you don’t tell her, how would she ever know who ate it?”

“YAH! IM YOON-A! I heard ALL of that! If that chicken leg is gone when I get back you are DEAD!” — a beat, then hushed, urgent, back into the phone: “It’s over, it’s over. Ji-ho-ya, quick, what is it? Any slower and the wolves divide my supper!”

(background, muffled, wronged) “Taeyeon eonni, it’s Seohyun-ie! She was stealing it and I caught—” “—mmph!! mm-mmph!!”

He felt the grip on his wrist clench tighter. He squeezed back — a uselessly small anchor — and kept his voice exactly as it always was with her: level, warm, unhurried.

“Nuna. Nothing special — just wanted to tell you the film wrapped today. How’s the tour? When do you fly home?”

Voice Call · continued

“Really?! Chukhahae!!” — the voice leaping, purely, uncomplicatedly glad. “One show left — we fly back next week! Oh — the film song! I’ll record it when I’m back, that’s not too late, right?” — a thread of careful earnestness under the words; his film mattered to her.

“Not late at all. Post-production takes ages. We’ll record as soon as you land — there’s plenty of time.”

“Good~ okay okay, I have to go — any longer and that Yoon-a demolishes my chicken leg for REAL. Bye-bye~”

“Mm. Good night, nuna.”

“Night, Ji-ho-ya~ saranghae~ mua!” — a flying kiss, and the line cut, brisk and sweet.

The dial tone purred twice and died. The screen went dark. The last light in the world went out with it, and the shore fell back into thick, dead black.

He put the phone away in silence.

“Ha— haha—”

A short, scraped laugh, fully corrosive, entirely at her own expense. Then, as if the jacket still holding his warmth — and now Taeyeon’s voice — had begun to scald her skin, she tore it off her shoulders and hurled it into the dark.

“WHY?!” Her voice went up like something breaking, tears crowding it, ringing off the empty shore. “Ji-ho! Why do you have a girlfriend?! Why HER? Why did no one TELL me?!”

The dam was gone; everything came through at once.

“Because I FELT it — in the way you kissed me, I felt it! You’re not indifferent to me! You care about me, you take care of me, you teach me, you steered me around every filthy person and thing in this industry — you wrote a whole film and named ME for the lead! You gave me a second life!”

The tears were pouring now, her voice climbing and cracking.

“Fate — the kisses — love — all those destined, redeeming things you WROTE — was every bit of it fake?! Something you performed for the movie, or to keep me happy, or — or to feed some ridiculous savior complex of yours?!”

“ANSWER ME! Ji-ho! Answer me!!”

The scream tore the night open.

Something invisible had his heart in its fist. He reached out through her struggling and pulled the shaking, sobbing girl hard against his chest, and held her there.

“None of it was fake, Sulli.” His voice landed low against her ear. “None of it. Wanting you safe. Being glad every single time you laughed. And—” a breath, “—what I felt when I kissed you. All of it real.”

“Then WHY—” She was crying too hard to finish.

“Because I’m — rotten.” His eyes closed on the word, and what came out next was the truth he despised himself for. “Because I’m greedy. Because I can’t make myself give up — any of you.”

The second life, and the system riding shotgun on it, had handed him everything a man dreams about at the bottom: talent at the top shelf, fame arriving in floods, money compounding in a sealed machine. For someone who had scraped through a first life’s full course of ordinariness and helplessness, that sudden all-powerful altitude didn’t leave a personality untouched. It had tilted him — quietly, thoroughly. And the tilt showed most exactly here: facing brilliant, dazzling women who, under this peninsula’s particular machinery, still had to claw for their footing. He was young. The mirror was kind. The talent was, in their eyes, blinding. And because he’d been an ordinary man at the core, he could do the one thing the chaebol heirs and the aging kingmakers never bothered to do — respect them as equals, understand the exact shape of their predicaments, and actually, materially help. A little sincerity, a little real support — and doors opened that power and money alone had never opened. All of it braided together into something complicated, contradictory, and magnetic: a man capable of a philanderer’s conduct and, inside the same chest, an honest, individual love for every single one of them. A greedy composite.

“You—”

She’d heard the whole low confession — half repentance, half warped manifesto — and pushed up out of his arms. He couldn’t see her face. He could feel the stare: shock, disappointment, and a new, thin filament of fear.

CRACK—!

The slap rang off the water, shockingly loud. She had put her entire body into it.

He took it without flinching — didn’t dodge, didn’t even turn his head. Heat bloomed fast across his cheek.

The follow-through left her frozen, palm buzzing, suddenly flustered by her own hand. “Why — why didn’t you dodge?”

“That one, I’d earned. But Sulli — I don’t regret it.”

“You don’t REGRET it?” Her voice went thin and sharp as wire. “Playing with people’s hearts? Hurting me and Taeyeon eonni in the same motion? Ji-ho, you are — such a disappointment!”

She stared at this man — the most familiar person in her world and a total stranger — while everything inside her thrashed: love, hate, disappointment, refusal, and the residue of devotion that would not rinse out.

“I don’t want to SEE you anymore!”

She shoved him — both hands, full force, catching him entirely unguarded — and bolted into the dark.

“Sulli!”

He staggered, went down hard on the grass, scrambled up, snatched the jacket and flashlight — and her silhouette was already gone, twenty meters into the black, nothing left but scattering footfalls under the surf.

“Choi Sulli! STOP! Come back!”

His chest clamped. He ran, beam swinging wild. The night shoreline was a minefield — loose rock, wet grass, drops with no railings — and she was sprinting it blind and out of her mind.

The shouts behind her only made her faster. Her athletic wiring had always been superb; grief burned like fuel; she tore along the coast path — and then, hearing him close, she swerved OFF it, hands and feet finding holds, scrambling up the side of a steep breakwater embankment.

“Sulli! NOT UP THERE! It’s dangerous!”

Beyond that crest there was nothing but a sheer drop to black, cold, rock-toothed water. He poured on speed, but she moved like a spooked animal, choosing the worst ground on purpose, and gained the edge — one more step and there was only the sea, meters down, churning.

“Sulli! I’m not coming closer! Look — I’ve stopped!” He froze several meters off, hands out, terrified of tipping her one inch further. “Just come down. Please. It’s too dangerous up there. We’ll talk — properly. Okay?”

She stood on the rim of the dark, wind hauling at her hair and her thin clothes, silhouette swaying against the last gray of the sky. She turned back to him, tears and fury in ruins on her face.

“Talk PROPERLY? About what? About how skillfully you orbit between me and Taeyeon eonni? Or about what I am to you — a fun little ‘dongsaeng’ you can flirt with whenever you like and never owe a thing?”

“That’s not what this is!” The words came fast and raw. “Hiding it at the start — that was wrong, and it was selfish. I know. Back then I had nothing, and I was greedy for every possible light — I was terrified that if any of you knew, you’d choose someone else, and I wouldn’t even have had the standing to fight for you!”

“And NOW?” A cold laugh through streaming tears. “Now that you’re rich and famous — now you’ve got the standing to play two hearts at once?”

He was quiet a moment. And then — because at this point she deserved the unvarnished version — he gave her the unvarnished version.

“Now — I trust myself to do right by you in everything except exclusivity. Respect. Support. Protection. Every resource I have. A future with real security in it. Everything except the one thing.” A breath. “Idols live apart from the people they love anyway — schedules see to that. Think of it as — a boyfriend who loves you completely, backs you in every possible way, and simply can’t be there every hour… or be only yours.”

It was his truest belief and his most shameless one, said in the same breath. Strip out the fidelity question, he told himself, and what man she was ever likely to meet would beat him — in talent, in means, in understanding, in the sheer will to help her? He knew exactly how the sentence sounded. He said it anyway.

“Ha— haha—”

She laughed like something breaking in half, shoulders shaking, tears running faster than the wind could take them.

“Ji-ho. You really are — completely shameless. And completely honest. What a combination.”

She shook her head slowly, eyes emptying out, and began to step back. “Don’t follow me. Right now — I can’t look at you.”

She needed space. Time. Somewhere to put a world that had inverted in the space of one sentence.

But grief had eaten her sense of the ground. The stepping foot found air.

“Ah—!!”

Weightlessness took her mid-cry — a short, cut-off shriek — and she went over backward, tumbling down the steep grass face of the embankment and out of sight.

“SULLI!!!”

His pupils slammed shut. His heart stopped outright. He dropped everything in his hands and ran faster than he had ever moved in either life.

SPLASH.

The sound came up dull and final, then her voice — choking, animal with fear:

“Oppa! Help— help me— kkgh—”

One great burst of white where she’d gone in. Then the waves closed over it.

“SULLI!! I’M COMING!”

No calculation, no hesitation — he went off the edge after her, straight down into black, freezing, bottomless water.

✦   ✦   ✦

The cold hit like a full-body electric shock, the sea squeezing in from every side. He fought to the surface, teeth already hammering, lungs burning, scraped the water from his eyes and swept the dark.

“Sulli! CHOI SULLI! WHERE ARE YOU?!”

The shout came out small against the open water. No answer. Only waves on rock.

Fear wrapped his heart like a snake with a body temperature of zero. He forced the panic down, fixed the entry point in his mind, filled his lungs, and dove.

Underwater visibility: none. The cold stabbed at his eyes. He searched by hand, by instinct, forearms scoring open on rock without registering it. Air gone — surface — breathe — down again—

On the third dive, his fingers met drifting hair, then soft fabric. He lunged along it and closed his hand around her arm.

She had stopped struggling. Her body was slack, loose-limbed; the only motion at her mouth was a thin trickle of bubbles.

He hauled her up with everything left in him. They broke the surface together; he coughed until his vision sparked, locked an arm around her ice-cold body, and kicked for the nearest shelf of rock. The sea was far rougher inside it than it had looked from above — the undertow dragging at their legs like hands, one arm pinning her against the current, the other arm and both legs fighting for every meter while the cold strip-mined his body heat and his limbs went wooden.

Then his foot struck stone. He half-dragged, half-carried her onto the flattest rock available and collapsed beside her, chest heaving, black spots swarming.

No time. Check her. NOW.

Trembling fingers under her nose: breath, barely there. Carotid: a pulse, slow and weak. Lips gone violet, face white as paper.

He knelt over her, jaw clenched against the cold and the exhaustion, and went to work. Airway clear. Head tilted, chin lifted. Nose pinched. Breath — into cold lips. Then hands stacked on her sternum, arms locked, compressions.

“One — two — three — four — Sulli, wake up. Please, PLEASE wake up—”

He worked the cycle mechanically and begged hoarsely on top of it. Cold. Spent. Pitch dark. No help coming. And under his hands, a life quietly draining.

In that moment the system, the fame, the money — Taeyeon, Ji-eun, Tiffany, Yoon-a — all of it receded to some far, gray, irrelevant distance. His skull held exactly one thought: SAVE HER. She does not die. He could not carry the other outcome. Not for one hour of whatever life came after.

“Kghh— khh—”

He had nearly reached the far edge of hope when her body convulsed — and she was coughing, hard, seawater coming up in spasms.

Relief hit harder than the cold had. He got her upright against him, turned her to her side, let her empty her lungs. “Sulli! Sulli — can you hear me?”

Her eyes dragged open, unfocused, hunting a long moment before they found his frantic face. Her lips moved. The voice was a thread.

“Oppa… c-cold… so cold…”

Her body was ice, still spasming in small waves.

Hypothermia. Rewarm her now, or lose her anyway.

He scanned their position: a bare rock shelf, ten meters of black, current-laced water between them and the beach. His own tank was deep in reserve. There was no other option on the menu.

“Sulli. Hold on to me. We have to swim back.” He wrapped her against his chest, trying to press what heat he had left into her. “Trust me. We’re going to make it back.”

Some piece of her consciousness surfaced. Through the shaking, she looked at the steadiness that hadn’t left his eyes even now — and nodded, small.

He drew the deepest breath of his life, took her in a rescue hold from behind, and slid them both back into the freezing water.

The crossing was its own circle of hell. His strength was going fast; the cold had disconnected half his muscles; every stroke weighed triple. The current shoved them off-line like a hand on a chessboard. Her weight — and the small, involuntary spasms that broke his rhythm — turned each meter into a negotiation. More than once he felt himself start to go under; brine tore at his airway; his lungs were two bags of needles. Against his chest, her breathing kept getting smaller.

No. Not like this.

He bit through the tip of his tongue and let the pain restart his nerves.

Faces strobed behind his eyes — a sweet good-night down a phone line; cool, clear eyes under practice-room light; a prickly girl counting her tuition grievances; a coffee truck with a winking reply; stars and ribbons and an unanswered message — and every one of them dissolved into the same place: the white, fragile face against his chest.

If I can’t even save the one in my arms — then all of it, every last thing I’ve stacked up, is worth nothing.

Will arrived from somewhere below the empty tank. He reset his grip, locked her tighter to his side, and swam — mechanical, economical, animal — through cold and current and dark.

Time congealed. Every second had to be paid for separately.

And then — when his consciousness had begun to fray at the edges and the current had nearly talked him into drifting — his foot came down on something that wasn’t rock.

Sand.

The joy of it burned through the cold like a struck flare. He got his legs under him, hauled them both through the shallows on hands and knees, and dragged her up onto dry beach — where they both went down flat.

He retched seawater into the sand, coughing, lungs on fire, no single part of him unhurt or warm. And he still couldn’t rest. He forced himself up and crawled to her. Worse than him — much worse: breath shallow, pulse sluggish, skin cold-slick, consciousness slipping again.

“Sulli. Stay with me.”

He got her onto his back and stood, and every step landed like a blade. But there was heat and shelter only in one place, and stopping was not on the menu. The discipline of years of training — and a physique the system itself had reinforced — cashed out in full tonight. Staggering, lungs sawing, navigating by memory and stubbornness, he carried her through the cold and the dark for the better part of ten minutes, and reached the rented house.

✦   ✦   ✦

Gapado’s houses ran scattered and few; there was no hotel. Most of the crew had spent the fortnight in tents on the island’s one flat field; the director, the leads, and a handful of principals had rented the empty homes. His house he shared only with his manager, Ryu Dong-hwan — not home when he shouldered the door open.

He laid her on the bed with exaggerated care, close to collapse himself, and kept moving on willpower alone.

Everything soaked and freezing came off her, down to the skin — his hands quick, clinical, without one gram of anything else in them. A fast check of her skin: no rigid patches, no dead-white frostbite blooms — but the hypothermia was written everywhere. He toweled her fully dry, then cocooned her in the thickest quilts in the house — head wrapped, neck sealed, extra layers pressed to the core lines of her body — and laid her flat.

Only then did he strip his own soaked clothes off. No hot shower — rapid vasodilation on a cold-stressed system could drop his pressure through the floor — just a rough toweling, dry clothes, and two tall glasses of warm water drunk standing, feeling the cold retreat one degree at a time.

✦   ✦   ✦

Ryu Dong-hwan had spent a chunk of the evening unable to raise his artist by phone — and had crossed paths with Sulli’s manager, who’d been told by HER artist that “there was something to take care of” before the girl vanished too. Two principals off the grid at once — layered on top of two months of on-set atmosphere that anyone with functioning eyes had noticed — assembled its own explanation. The industry had seen the pattern a hundred times: co-stars fall in during a shoot, fall out after wrap. Nobody said anything. Managers who liked their jobs — and everybody on this team liked working for this particular generous star — knew when to trade one look and go quiet.

What he hadn’t expected, walking back, was light in the star’s window.

He knocked. The door opened on a face he decided not to read too closely.

“Hyung. I need a favor.”

“…Name it.”

“Find Sulli’s manager. Tell her Sulli’s not coming back tonight. And—” evenly, “—see that she’s compensated for her discretion.”

Dong-hwan’s eyes sharpened one notch. So the girl WAS here. “She’ll have guessed where Sulli is. She won’t talk.” A pause, and then, delicately: “Are you two dating? If it’s dating, I’ll square it with her properly — and we should file it with the company.”

“Don’t file anything with the company yet. Sulli and I are… complicated.”

Ah. Complicated. In manager dialect that translated to: a location thing, over at wrap. For an agency, frankly, the best of all possible outcomes. He nodded, all understanding. “Got it. I’ll go talk to her. The company won’t hear a word — settle it between yourselves.”

He turned and went, mission in hand.

The door shut on a faintly baffled star. What exactly do you think you understood just now? SM already knew about Taeyeon. If Sulli’s manager filed a second report upstream, the resulting paperwork would be his obituary. …Still. The company not knowing was the correct outcome, whatever route it took.

✦   ✦   ✦

He came back to the bedside. Inside the cocoon of quilts her face was still bloodless, her breathing still too small, and his heart would not come down off its ledge.

Passive insulation wasn’t going to be enough. Not at her depth of cold.

The hesitation lasted exactly one second.

He lifted the corner of the quilt, shed the dry clothes he’d just put on, and slid in — gathering her chilled body against his own re-warmed skin, sealing the blankets around them both. Body-to-body heat transfer: the safest, most effective field treatment for hypothermia that existed. That was the whole reason, and tonight it was reason enough.

The movement stirred her out of her half-dark. Lashes trembled; her eyes opened, foggy, and found his face inches away.

“Oppa… you—”

“Don’t move, Sulli.” Gentle, and completely steady. “Your core temperature’s too low. This is the fastest safe way to bring it back. Sleep. I’ll be right here.”

Understanding surfaced. A tint of color — the first in hours — crossed the white of her face. She didn’t fight it; there was nothing in her to fight with anyway. Like a small cat locating the one heat source in a frozen world, she burrowed deeper into the warmth of him on pure instinct, and laid her icy cheek against his chest.

Under her ear: the heartbeat. Steady, strong, unhurried. Doom— doom— doom— each beat traveling through the cold shell of her and knocking, patiently, on everything that had nearly stopped inside.

The cold gave ground by degrees. Warmth spread from every line where skin met skin — and with it something deeper and older than warmth, the kind of thing that only settles into a person on the far side of almost dying.

Oppa. You jumped. Into THAT water — that black, that cold — you didn’t hesitate for one second. You were scared too. You were exhausted too. And you never once let go. You could have stayed up there. You could have just shouted for help. Instead you went into the dark and the cold WITH me — and dragged me back out of death’s hands with your own.

Why?

She didn’t need to ask it out loud anymore. Some answers never arrive in words. They arrive in the moment a body launches itself off a breakwater without checking the depth; in arms that would not unlock in freezing water; in a heartbeat pressed against your ear at the end of the worst night of your life.

The tears came again — but not the earlier kind. These carried pain and gratitude and a strange, wide-open clarity, all at once.

Taeyeon eonni… so it’s real. Fine. It’s real. But an oppa like this — a debt like this — a bond that cannot be cut out of me now no matter what I do… Sulli, how exactly are you supposed to let go of it? I’m not fearless. I know what exclusivity is worth. But when someone has priced your life above his own — the rules everyone else lives by go a little blurry.

She freed one still-cool arm, wrapped it around his waist, and pressed her face deeper into his chest, as if to hear more clearly what each heartbeat was saying.

Fight whoever holds him for him? …Maybe. Or maybe what I want is just this — this warmth, right now, and everything he CAN give, minus the one thing he can’t.

In the dark, her eyes slid closed, and the corner of her mouth curved — very slightly.

Oppa. You can’t run anymore. From the second you jumped, you were responsible for me. That’s the law now.

And he lay holding the slowly warming girl and her fiercely tightening arms, feeling everything at once: the after-shock relief of a life not lost; guilt and tenderness in a ratio he couldn’t compute; and a low, distinct premonition that the map of his complicated life had just been redrawn — permanently.

Whether he liked it or not, Choi Sulli — the girl he’d pulled out of the mud with his own hands, and very nearly followed to the bottom of the sea — was now carved into the track of his life at a depth that no wrap party, no schedule, and no rule was ever going to reach.
