Chapter Two Hundred Sixty-Three

The Young Secretary

Seoul · late evening an alien drama, a provisional casting agreement, and an unexpected solution to Ji-ho’s staffing problem

The proposition took shape almost immediately in Park Ji-eun’s mind.

LOEN had just produced The Girl in the Sun, one of the year’s largest films. Ji-ho had become chairman without any apparent intention of leaving acting or music behind.

If he agreed to star in her drama…

His face could carry fantasy. His performance in the film had proven he could act. His popularity guaranteed attention, and his money solved the most common early obstacle: finding someone willing to finance ambition.

Most importantly, her hero was an alien who had fallen to Joseon four centuries earlier and survived into modern Korea with impossible abilities.

At twenty, Ji-ho had become an artist, screenwriter, producer, actor, and chairman.

Alien casting, Park decided, did not get more convincing.

“Chairman Song, I know a quiet bar nearby. We could discuss the drama properly.”

“Lead the way, Writer Park.”

Soo-bin lifted one hand timidly.

“Then I should go home.”

Ji-ho looked at her.

“Why? You’re an actress, and one of Korea’s strongest writers is standing in front of you.”

Park Ji-eun laughed.

“With Chairman Song beside her, she hardly needs favors from me. But she does have a memorable face. If the right supporting role appears, I’d be willing to audition her.”

Soo-bin’s eyes brightened. She bowed deeply.

“Thank you. I’ll prepare seriously.”

✦ ✦ ✦
Private lounge bar script development in the outer room; a career offer behind the partition

The bar announced its prices before anyone entered: imported cars lined the curb, brass framed the entrance, and a host greeted Park Ji-eun by name.

She guided them to a private room with a smaller partitioned lounge attached.

Dong-hwan glanced toward Ji-ho.

“Hoejang-nim, Soo-bin-ssi and I can wait in the side room while you discuss the script.”

“Come inside,” Park Ji-eun said. “The partition is there for exactly that.”

Soo-bin and Dong-hwan settled in the adjoining space. Ji-ho and the writer took the main table.

Park Ji-eun poured the first drink.

“From the title alone, I assume the male lead comes from somewhere beyond Earth.”

She stared at him, then laughed.

“No wonder you wrote a hit film. Yes—he is an alien.”

“He arrived during Joseon, survived for four hundred years, and watched generations repeat the same mistakes.”

“That would make him emotionally detached,” Ji-ho said. “Not because he lacks feeling, but because attachment has always ended in grief.”

Park Ji-eun’s eyes lit.

The conversation accelerated.

DEVELOPMENT SESSION · MY LOVE FROM THE STAR

Core dramatic collision

HeroA four-hundred-year-old outsider: controlled, observant, exhausted by impermanence.

HeroineA top actress: arrogant in public, ridiculous in private, emotionally fearless where he is guarded.

EngineImmortality meets celebrity; secrecy meets a woman whose life is watched by everyone.

ToneRomantic comedy sharpened by mystery, danger, and the certainty of separation.

Opening scripts are in progress. Final investment, network, director, casting, schedule, and completion terms remain subject to written agreements.

Ji-ho had future knowledge of the finished drama, but he also understood screenwriting well enough to discuss motivation, episode engines, and emotional rhythm without parroting scenes that had not yet been written.

The deeper they went, the more Park Ji-eun felt she had found someone who understood both commerce and story.

✦ ✦ ✦

In the side room, Dong-hwan lifted a glass of ice water.

“I’m driving later, so this will have to count. To your next role.”

Soo-bin touched her small glass to his. She drank sparingly, more from nerves than enthusiasm.

Conversation without Ji-ho present felt awkward. Dong-hwan relieved it with a question.

“What have you appeared in so far?”

Soo-bin blinked.

Was she truly that invisible?

“The Girl in the Sun.”

“Really?”

Her expression became wounded.

“A company employee.”

“Ah. The woman in glasses.”

Dong-hwan scratched his head. One of the year’s biggest films had still failed to make audiences remember her name.

He reconsidered the assistant vacancy.

“May I ask something direct?”

Soo-bin straightened.

“Do you trust Chairman Song?”

“Of course.”

The speed of the answer told him enough. He did not interrogate whatever feeling sat behind it.

“LOEN has been searching for a personal executive assistant. Someone discreet, organized, and able to work around both corporate and artist schedules.”

“The candidates so far either want access more than a job or lack the confidentiality the position requires.”

Soo-bin’s attention sharpened.

“He doesn’t have a permanent assistant?”

“Not one he trusts with everything. He’s balancing LOEN, performances, production, acquisitions, and film development. The temporary secretarial coverage handles office traffic, not his full schedule.”

She thought of the faint shadows beneath Ji-ho’s eyes.

Success had made him look untouchable. She had never considered how much administration might be accumulating behind it.

“What would the assistant do?”

“Calendar control, calls, route confirmation, briefing packets, document tracking, and coordination with security and artist management.”

Dong-hwan kept his tone professional.

“It would begin as a paid trial. Training included. Your acting classes continue, and audition leave would be written into the agreement.”

“You would not be giving up acting.”

Soo-bin looked down at her hands.

She had spent months distributing flyers, serving barbecue, and waiting for auditions that never arrived. An assistant position would offer stable income and place her near LOEN’s new screen division.

It would also place her near Ji-ho every day.

That thought frightened her because it mattered too much.

“I’m under contract with Starship.”

“Then nothing happens without written clearance. We’ll speak to the agency, and you’ll review the employment terms sober, with advice if you want it.”

Dong-hwan leaned back.

“LOEN’s screen division is new. One or two years from now, if you want to return to acting full-time, you may have a stronger network and more training—not less.”

“But that future cannot be guaranteed. The job must make sense on its own.”

His honesty made the offer more persuasive.

“Could I think about it?”

“You should. I’ll send a written description tomorrow. If you remain interested, we schedule a formal interview.”

Soo-bin nodded slowly.

“I’m interested.”

Not agreement. Not yet.

But the possibility had entered the room.

No binding offer is made while alcohol is being served.

✦ ✦ ✦

In the outer room, Park Ji-eun leaned over the table with growing enthusiasm.

“So the hero has lived four hundred years and believes he has already seen every form of human foolishness.”

“And the heroine is a top actress,” Ji-ho said, “confident enough to irritate him and vulnerable enough to expose the lie beneath his detachment.”

“Exactly.”

Soo-bin paused behind the partition door.

“The heroine will be difficult,” Ji-ho continued. “If the performance leans only arrogant, viewers will reject her. The comedy has to reveal loneliness without begging for sympathy.”

Park Ji-eun set down her glass.

“Then I’ll be direct. I want you for the male lead.”

Ji-ho raised one eyebrow.

“Your appearance, restraint, and acting fit him. You can also invest, and LOEN needs a flagship drama for its new screen division.”

Ji-ho did not accept immediately.

“Finish a production-ready package. I want the first eight episodes, series treatment, rights chain, budget range, and proposed network path reviewed by LOEN.”

“If the material holds, LOEN invests and I take the lead.”

Park Ji-eun smiled.

“Fair.”

“One more condition. I want consultation rights on the female lead.”

The writer hesitated.

“Consultation, yes. Final casting must remain a production decision, not a private appointment.”

“Agreed. I won’t gamble a project to reward someone.”

Female lead

Next delivery

Recovered opening scripts · treatment · episodes through eight · preliminary budget and network strategy

Soo-bin’s eyes stung behind the partition.

Ji-ho was negotiating for the project, yet he had still remembered to mention her to one of Korea’s most successful writers.

When she entered the outer room again, Park Ji-eun noticed the redness around her eyes.

“Are you all right?”

“Ne. Just tired.”

Ji-ho glanced at her but did not expose the emotion.

The meeting continued until the bottles on the table had multiplied. Ji-ho drank more than usual while discussing production strategy; Park Ji-eun matched the pace with the cheerful stamina of someone who had found a promising partner.

Dong-hwan eventually checked the main room.

“That’s enough for both of you.”

Park Ji-eun had folded her arms on the table. Ji-ho leaned against the sofa with his eyes closed.

“Writer Park?”

She lifted her head with difficulty.

“Call a designated driver. And make sure Chairman Song gets home safely.”

“We will.”

Dong-hwan arranged her car and verified the driver before returning.

Soo-bin sat beside Ji-ho while she waited. He opened his eyes after a moment and focused slowly on her face.

“Soo-bin?”

“I’m here.”

“Why?”

“I was in the side room the whole time.”

He nodded and closed his eyes again.

Then he reopened them.

“Were you crying?”

Soo-bin’s face warmed.

“No. I was… moved.”

“By what?”

“You asked Writer Park to remember me.”

“Mm.”

His eyes closed again.

Soo-bin watched the faint line between his brows and remembered the neighbor who once carried her down concrete stairs before he had power, wealth, or a company.

Dong-hwan returned.

“Let’s go, Hoejang-nim.”

He helped Ji-ho stand. Soo-bin followed.

✦ ✦ ✦

Night air cleared Ji-ho’s head slightly.

“Writer Park?”

“Her designated driver has departed. I confirmed the vehicle and destination.”

Ji-ho nodded.

“We’ll take Soo-bin home.”

She wanted to refuse out of politeness. She wanted more strongly to remain near him for another twenty minutes.

“Thank you.”

Dong-hwan drove. Ji-ho and Soo-bin sat in the back.

Streetlights passed over his profile in intervals. Soo-bin held her bag in both hands and tried not to stare.

“Writer Park said she’ll consider you for suitable supporting roles,” Ji-ho said without opening his eyes.

“But a lead role must be earned. No introduction can perform for you.”

Soo-bin turned toward the window before he could see her eyes fill again.

“I understand. Thank you.”

At her building, she stepped out and bent to look through the open door.

“Thank you for the ride. And for speaking to Writer Park.”

“Sleep early.”

“Ne. Good night.”

Soo-bin remained on the curb until the car vanished.

The air was cool. Her palms were warm.

The assistant position returned to her mind—not only because it would place her beside Ji-ho, but because it might finally give her stable ground from which to keep pursuing acting.

She would read the offer carefully.

And if the terms were fair, she would try.

✦ ✦ ✦

In the car, Dong-hwan checked the rearview mirror.

“Hoejang-nim, I spoke to Soo-bin-ssi about the assistant vacancy.”

“Mm.”

“Her situation is difficult. Few auditions, little agency support, and several part-time jobs.”

Ji-ho remained quiet.

“I suggested a paid trial as your executive assistant, subject to Starship approval. Written duties, audition leave, and no promise that the role becomes an acting contract.”

Ji-ho opened his eyes.

“What did she say?”

“Interested. I told her nothing is decided until she receives the written description and interviews sober.”

Ji-ho studied him.

“You’ve become remarkably proactive.”

Dong-hwan laughed nervously.

“Trying to reduce your workload.”

“Only my workload?”

Dong-hwan chose silence.

Ji-ho leaned back and remembered Soo-bin’s shy smile, the clear eyes that still belonged to the neighbor from before his life became unrecognizable.

“Let her interview.”

Relief crossed Dong-hwan’s face.

“I’ll arrange it.”

“Where are we going now?”

Ji-ho closed his eyes again.

“Bangbae-dong, Seocho.”

Ji-eun’s villa waited there.
