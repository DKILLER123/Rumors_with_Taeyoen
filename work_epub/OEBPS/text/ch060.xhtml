Chapter Sixty

Kindred Spirits

His thumbs moved lightly over the keyboard.

KakaoTalk · Taeyeon nuna
Ji-ho
You’re the captain of Girls’ Generation, nuna. Of course I have to work this hard.

She stared at the sentence, and her heart missed one clean beat. Why does my being Girls’ Generation make HIM work harder? Is it so that — someday — he can stand next to me without flinching? Heat crept up her ears; her face went a little deeper into the pillow.

Before she could finish savoring the implication, the second message landed:

KakaoTalk · Taeyeon nuna
Ji-ho
As your hoobae-disciple, I can’t be out here embarrassing you, right?

The upward curve of her mouth froze mid-air, then sagged. A flicker of genuine vexation crossed the bright eyes.

“…That’s IT?”

She jabbed his avatar with one accusing fingertip. Is he actually made of wood, or is he playing dumb with me ON PURPOSE? The little balloon of expectation he’d inflated deflated with a squeak — and then, refusing to stay deflated, swelled back up out of pure spite. She pressed her lips together and typed fast, probing slipped back between the lines:

KakaoTalk · Taeyeon nuna
Taeyeon nuna
Oh~ so it was only about not embarrassing me. And here I thought SOMEONE had grander ambitions. [sticker: cold.jpg]

He read the emotional hairpin turn in it and his eyes warmed. This version of her — small-tempered, testing him around corners — was vivid beyond all her stage selves. He was mid-reply when the phone buzzed: a second thread sliding in. Tiffany.

KakaoTalk · Tiffany nuna
Tiffany nuna
Hey — when you wrote that song, why classical piano as the core instrument? It feels so different from the electronic elements around it.

Ji-ho
Classical piano has this clear, grainy texture — it carries its own faint room-reverb. Laying a simple single-note line underneath neutralizes the coldness of the electronics and creates this newborn kind of rawness. Quiet loneliness, basically.

Tiffany nuna
OHHH. That’s it exactly!! And the bass — that slap section felt SO good. How did you treat it?

Ji-ho
Slap technique over solid root motion, tone rounded off, locked tight to the drum pulse. Its whole job is filling and anchoring the low end — it closes the rhythm loop with the drums so the piano and synths can float on top without the track tipping over.

Tiffany nuna
DAEBAK. Ji-ho, you think about this stuff SO clearly. Then the strings in the hook — those short pizzicatos and the long pads — that’s pushing the loneliness even deeper, right??

Ji-ho
Exactly right. Your ear is genuinely sharp, nuna — one hint and you hear the whole architecture. Impressive.

Tiffany nuna
OBVIOUSLY! I’m Tiffany! Teach me more from now on!!

He could see her through the screen — the eye-smile at full crescent, the chin tilted up in that proud, guileless way. The gemstone of Girls’ Generation ran on the most direct wiring of them all, and was, truthfully… very easy to please.

Taeyeon’s new message slid in on cue. One thumb-swipe, and he was back in her thread, picking up the charged conversation exactly where its static had been left humming — unhurried, ambidextrous. With Taeyeon: warmth with a current in it, probe and counter-probe. With Tiffany: genuine admiration, correct distance, impeccably legitimate subject matter. Every boundary crisply maintained; every interaction defensible in open court. It was, after all, only music theory.

Time dissolved into the message stream — until the phone had gone warm in his hand and the clock read 23:28. He surfaced with a start and typed fast:

KakaoTalk · Taeyeon nuna
Ji-ho
Nuna — something’s come up at the practice room, I have to log off.
Tonight was really fun. Felt like the old days of running into you at the company. 🙂
Next time — will you tell me the legendary “horror stories” from your trainee days? [sticker: curious]

Taeyeon nuna
Fine, fine, busy man. Go.
As for the stories… depends entirely on SOMEONE’s future behavior. [sticker: tsundere]

Regret and affection, folded into one sign-off. He switched threads.

KakaoTalk · Tiffany nuna
Ji-ho
Nuna, let’s go deeper on the arrangement stuff next time — you genuinely have ideas.
Oh — just remembered: there’s a song whose string writing feels EXACTLY like what we discussed. Sharing the playlist link — might spark something for you.
Great talk today. Thanks for the questions! Off to prep the next practice block — talk soon.

Tiffany nuna
REALLY?? Amazing, I’m listening RIGHT NOW! [sticker: excited]
Go go, good luck with practice! Next time I’m asking you EVERYTHING!

Phone away. He headed for the appointed room.

Irene, Seulgi, Wendy, Joy — all present. Joo-hyun walked to the center of the floor.

“Ji-ho. Seung-wan. Soo-young. Behind me — two meters apart. Follow my movements.”

Three bodies arranged themselves in a line at her back.

“One, two—” Arms sweeping flat, crossing in at the chest, left foot stepping through. “Three — hips rotate forward, ONCE. Four, five — turn. Six, seven — arms SOFTER here, don’t muscle it. The hips swing on their own arc — draw the curve, don’t force the curve. Again. Ready — three, four, five, TURN. Twist from the waist — lift through the hip~”

The two rookies drilled with total sincerity. Soo-young was less green than she looked — a non-public trainee who’d been through SM’s training long before her official 2012 entry. Joo-hyun taught; Seul-gi prowled the line, catching and correcting.

And somewhere in the teaching, Irene discovered something unexpected: she was enjoying herself.

This wasn’t the usual quick fix-your-arm correction — this was real instruction, and real instruction turned out to be a different discipline entirely from dancing itself. Skills she’d assumed she fully possessed revealed themselves, the moment she tried to transmit them, as loose piles of fuzzy instinct. To teach these three, she had to forge instinct into language — clear words, ordered logic, a scaffold running from first principles to the hard parts. The process forcibly reorganized her own understanding, exposing gaps she’d stepped over unknowing for years. And their questions — Ji-ho’s especially — kept arriving from angles she’d never once thought to look from, refracting familiar technique into new light.

The satisfaction of it was startling. She taught harder.

Hour folded into hour, and deep past midnight, the five kindred spirits became — as usual — the last trainees to leave SM.

“Ji-ho — see you tomorrow!”

“Nuna. Seung-wan, Soo-young, Seul-gi. Tomorrow.”

“Bye-bye~”

He turned toward home; the four of them toward the dorms.

Above them all, Seoul’s neon burned on, indifferent and gorgeous — lighting the roads of countless souls exactly like theirs, running or briefly resting along the twin rails of the dream and the life.
