Chapter Two Hundred Twenty-Nine

Not Unwilling

August 2, 2013 · night A private dining room · Riverhill, Hannam-dong — 270 degrees of the Han River a question with no good answer, a zipper that stops halfway — and a bargain that turns into something else

Twenty-one-year-old Jiyeon could not answer right away.

It was true — on the question of how to handle the troublemaker, the members had split. So-yeon eonni had wavered; Bo-ram eonni and Q-ri eonni had opinions but no appetite for saying them out loud; Eun-jung eonni had argued for sitting down and talking it through. Only she and Hyo-min eonni had refused to swallow it, and gone in head-first. But she also remembered the sweet years — being the maknae everyone spoiled, “ruling” the dorm on borrowed indulgence. That warmth had never left her. And besides: could she ever really cut the label T-ARA out of her own skin? Impossible. To go anywhere, the bullying tag had to come off first; the truth had to come out. The public would only take back Jiyeon after it took back T-ara. So — the group’s redemption, or her own success? It had never been a choice at all.

“Try the braised cod.” His voice pulled her back; a plump, glistening piece was already landing on her plate. “Chef’s signature here.”

“Thank you.” Color touched her cheeks. She took a careful bite — the reduced sauce over the delicate flesh, the clean sweetness of radish underneath — and her eyes went bright before she could stop them, voice jumping half an octave. “Wah, daebak — it’s actually SO good!”

“No work talk while we eat.” He lifted his glass. “Ji-yeon. The wine’s good here too. Try it.”

“Mm. Cheers.”

The wine went down cool and easy, and a faint flush rose in her pale face, which suited her better than the tension had. They left work where it lay and drifted into easier country — interests, small absurd stories. He was funny in a dry, precise way, and she kept losing composure into actual laughter.

Set aside the wandering heart, she thought, and he was very nearly a perfect man. Which was, of course, the problem.

After dinner she excused herself to the washroom, rinsed with the small bottle of mouthwash in her bag, and repainted her lips a brighter shade.

He took her to Riverhill in Hannam-dong — a room with a 270-degree window on the whole run of the Han, reservation-only, couples only, empty tonight of anyone but them. Wine and dessert, low light, the river laid out below like spilled jewelry.

The privacy of it put her pulse up; her palms had gone faintly damp.

He settled comfortably into the wide sofa and poured two glasses.

“Ji-yeon. Not sitting?”

She looked at the space beside him, bit her lip, slipped off her heels, and curled herself onto the couch sideways. A soft floral scent came with her, sweet without weight.

He took a sip.

“So. Tell me what you’re thinking.”

She emptied her glass in one motion and turned wet, steady eyes on him.

“Your two options — for me, they were never separate questions.”

“T-ara did not bully her. She couldn’t take the schedule, she skipped team commitments over and over with no reason, and her temper was ugly — every staffer who worked with us knows it. I will not accept a world where WE became the villains everyone spits on while she plays the victim.”

“T-ara gets cleared and starts again. AND I succeed. Both.”

His gaze moved off her set, stubborn face and out to the river.

“You know yourself how hard those two things are. Both of them.”

The wine had loosened something; her voice came up hot.

“You SAID you could do it.”

“I can.” He turned back to her. “But Ji-yeon — you have to give me a reason to.”

A reason.

A thin, wintry smile touched her mouth. So that was it after all. What else did she have that a man like this could want? Beauty was the shortcut — and the toll road. Fine.

“If you deliver on your promise—” she rose slowly to her feet, and her fingers went to the zipper at her back, “—then tonight, I’m yours.”

The pale blue dress slid off her shoulders and pooled at her feet.

She stood in the low light, chin high, enduring his frank gaze while humiliation burned up her neck — the first time she had ever stood before a man like this. Years of dance had carved her clean and strong; she knew exactly what she looked like. Her hands shook anyway as they reached back for the last clasp.

Click. The clasp gave.

One release of her fingers now, and the last of her dignity went with it.

But what Ji-ho wanted was the whole of Jiyeon — not a body with the person evacuated from it.

He was on his feet at once. He stepped behind her, and re-fastened the clasp with steady hands. Then he bent, gathered the dress from the floor, and drew it gently back up over her shoulders.

She twisted to stare at him, lost.

“What is this? Are you HUMILIATING me?”

“I— Jiyeon — strip down in front of you and you’re not even INTERESTED?”

“If you’d finished, of course I’d be interested,” he said, perfectly even. “But you didn’t finish, did you.”

“You—! BASTARD!” Her hand swung; he caught the wrist, light as catching a moth.

“In such a hurry to become my woman?”

“Who’s in a hurry?! Isn’t this what you WANTED?”

“And if I want, you just give?”

“You— let GO of me! Ji-ho!” She fought the grip. “You bastard — you’ll only help me if I pay with my body, and now you stand there acting NOBLE? Hypocrite!”

“I do want you.” He held her eyes and didn’t blink. “But I want the Jiyeon who comes with her heart attached. Get you tonight, and I own you for an hour. That’s not the deal I’m interested in.”

“An HOUR? Listen to yourself!” Cornered, she went on the attack with a fistful of internet-acquired wisdom. “Look at you — you’d fold in ten minutes, tops!”

“…You know a surprising amount.”

“I know EVERYTHING!” She pressed the advantage, chin out. “I’m standing here half-dressed and you didn’t even react — three minutes! You’re a three-minute man!”

“You—!”

He was, for one blinding second, genuinely tempted to submit evidence.

“Angry?” And now — maddeningly — she leaned INTO him, all provocation. “Do something about it then! If I so much as whisper ‘it hurts,’ I’ll change my name!”

He looked at the fight in her — and at the rims of her eyes, which had gone red some sentences ago — and the exasperation folded itself away into something much softer.

“Enough.” The word came down flat and final. His hands closed on her shoulders and held her still. “Is that really what you think? That the only thing T-ara has left worth putting on a table — is your body?”

“Your songs. Your stages. Six careers of work. All of it worthless now?”

She went still. The fight drained out of her by degrees.

“What… do you mean?”

“T-ara still has commercial value. THAT’S what I’m buying.” His voice had gone level and serious. “And I need your help to unlock it.”

“Commercial value?” Her brows knotted. “What value? Where? We’re blacklisted at home and stalling in Japan—”

“Which leaves one market you’ve never tried.”

“…Where?”

“China.”

“China?” The knot deepened. “Of course we’ve thought about it. But—”

She didn’t finish. She didn’t need to — the political weather between the two countries said the rest.

“Relax. A window is coming — sooner than you think.” He said it with the calm of a man reading tomorrow’s paper. “I grew up in LA Koreatown — half my childhood friends spoke Mandarin before English, and I know exactly how big that market is and how starved it is. I’m not leaving it on the table. I’m planning to establish a company there. And when it exists — I want T-ara under contract to it.”

“When the timing ripens, I’ll spend a stretch working that market myself — including an album made FOR it. You’d come with me. The attention takes care of itself.”

“Even if all of that is true—” she was still squinting at him, half-believing, “—our company signing us over to YOURS won’t be simple. Kim Kwang-soo will want the biggest partner he can get.”

“Which is why,” — the smile came back, unhurried — “before any of that, I’ll go to your company holding the one thing they can’t buy anywhere else: I clear T-ara’s name. THAT’S my offer, and they’ll take it.”

“…So that’s the ‘reason’ you wanted?”

Not entirely, of course. But there is an art to which truths go first.

He neither confirmed nor denied.

“It’s a road we can walk together and both win on. It just isn’t a SHORT road.”

“Then—” her brain snagged on something and refused to move past it, “—why did you let me get as far as the zipper before you stopped me?”

“Because I wanted to look.” He said it with disarming, criminal honesty. “You’re beautiful. I enjoyed it.”

“YOU—!” Fury and flattery collided somewhere in her chest and canceled to a blush.

“Then why did you stop me at all?”

“Because I don’t want you to hate me. And—”

“And WHAT?”

“And I already saw everything the first time we met. The curiosity’s gone.”

“WHAT?”

The memory arrived like a slap — Ji-eun’s house, fresh out of the shower, nothing under the robe, and HIM in the hallway. Her whole face went furnace. THE CURIOSITY’S GONE? What did that MEAN? Gone HOW? Not attractive enough to stay curious about?! Where exactly was she lacking! Everything was round and high and exactly where it should be — she’d checked! PERSONALLY! This absolute—

“SONG JI-HO! I’ll KILL you!”

She launched herself at him, all nails and fury.

“What do you mean the curiosity’s GONE! What am I missing?! I’m a C! Do you understand?! A! C!”

The struggle tipped them over onto the sofa in a tangle. She ended up on top, braced on her arms, glaring down at him — and made the tactical error of actually looking at his face. Straight brows. Deep eyes with something like starlight at the bottom. The nose, the jaw, all of it unfairly assembled.

Her heart picked up speed without consulting her.

He was, objectively, measurably, infuriatingly handsome. And she — she knew what she was too: the sharp-boned, cold-glamour face that photographed like a weapon, the long fox eyes, the full under-eye warmth that softened all of it. Two very dangerous faces, very close together.

He rolled, reversing them, and the clock somewhere in the room slid past midnight.

He came down toward her slowly.

He’s going to kiss me. Refuse? Don’t refuse? The debate convened, deadlocked—

He didn’t leave the committee time to vote. His mouth settled over hers, warm and unhurried — and she jolted like a live wire had touched her, eyes flying wide, then falling shut by degrees. Clean scent. Careful weight. Nothing in her recoiled; everything in her leaned. Her arms came up around his neck on their own.

He was, by now, fluent in this particular language — and she had no defenses that held. The whole evening, she realized dimly, her feelings had been conducted like an orchestra: shyness, shock, fury, delight, hope — every section cued on time. She should have minded. She didn’t mind nearly enough.

A long time later he lifted his head and looked down at her, gentle now.

“Ji-yeon. What was that, just now — a transaction you resented? Or something with feeling under it?”

She stared up at the face that made rational thought difficult, replayed the voltage of the last minute, and found she couldn’t produce the lie.

“…Not resented. I— I liked it.”

“Good.” He smiled — the signature folded-crescent smile — and she lost her train of thought entirely.

“Ji-yeon. I don’t mind that you came to me because you needed help. So extend me some tolerance too, hm? I’ll carry your career. You enjoy everything that comes with me. Your problems — hand them over; I’ll solve them. The price is your heart. Given, not rented.”

She would admit it to no one: she was, constitutionally, hopelessly, a romantic. And in this moment, her heart moved. The talent that blocked out the sky. The wealth and standing that could shelter anyone standing behind it. The face that belonged on the ideal-type charts she’d filled out as a trainee. Only — the wandering. Could she live with the wandering? She didn’t know. What she knew was that she was TIRED. Tired from years of being compared, mocked, dragged; tired of climbing out of one pit into the next; tired of crying into pillows and receiving, at best, competent sympathy. What she wanted — wanted so badly it embarrassed her — was someone who would pull her in and say: Ji-yeon. Hand it to me. Your problems are mine now. She needed that person badly enough that perhaps — perhaps — the wandering could be a line item she chose not to audit. At least he was honest about it. At least the whole ledger was face-up on the table.

She closed her eyes, and nodded — a small, complete surrender.

He kissed her again, slow and thorough, and the river lights ran on below the window while two heartbeats negotiated themselves into one rhythm.

His hand found the hem of the dress and traveled; she trembled once and let him, breath going deep and slow.

“Mm— didn’t you say you’d already seen everything… and the curiosity was gone?”

“Seen, yes.” The laugh arrived low against her ear. “Never TOUCHED. …Ji-yeon. I take back my earlier assessment entirely.”

“Shut up— Ji-ho. Keep kissing me.”

The night deepened. The river carried the lights away. The rest stayed between them.

✦ ✦ ✦

He would admit — privately, to the audit committee of one — that methods had been used tonight. Let her believe the price was her body, so the first step was hers to take. Refuse the payment, and watch the shock rearrange her. Then produce the real plan, and let hope and gratitude arrive together. Then catch the feelings as they fell. Emotional control, applied at the moment the armor opened. No — he was not, by any strict accounting, a good man. But feelings were never one clean thing: values, temper, money, capability, station, face, figure — every one a load-bearing wall. And a woman who KNEW about the others, walking in anyway — for her, these harmless maneuvers, this bounded and honest sort of bad, weren’t a betrayal of the thing. They were the courtship the situation demanded. The women who threw themselves at the title with nothing underneath — THOSE he wouldn’t touch with gloves on.

Later, smoothing her dress with a face still full of heat, Jiyeon ran her own audit and reached her own finding: she had, somewhere tonight, been thoroughly outplayed — and her head was a mess, and none of it felt like a loss. The objectives were achieved: her career, T-ara’s rehabilitation, an actual road map. She’d walked in intending a one-night transaction and walked out with — apparently — a boyfriend. A wandering, impossible, infuriating boyfriend.

Hm. Somehow… not a bad trade at all.
