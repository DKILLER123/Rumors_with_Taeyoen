Chapter Eighty-Three

The Missing Memory

“Ji-ho — I’ve been wanting to meet the hero who saved Sulli for AGES, and here you finally are.” Ji-yeon hoisted a glass. “Drink one with me.”

“Sure. Ji-yeon nuna — geonbae.”

One glass down. She refilled his immediately, frowning at the honorific.

“‘Nuna’ sounds so stiff. We’re chingu-adjacent — just call me Ji-yeon.”

Unsolicited warmth is never free. He eyed the suddenly gracious Jiyeon with mild professional suspicion, and played along.

“…All right. Ji-yeon it is.”

She beamed and raised her glass again.

“Chingu-yah! Another round~”

“Chingu-yah! Sulli says you WRITE songs? Daebak. One glass of respect~”

“Chingu-yah! Do you like T-ara? You DO? Amazing — that’s worth three glasses minimum!”

She’s running a filibuster of toasts. He clocked the freshly changed, aggressively modest pajamas, and the whole campaign decoded itself at once. Ah. She figured it out. And now she needs to know what I know — via interrogation under the influence.

Mildly embarrassed on her behalf, he declined nothing. Bottles emptied at speed.

Ji-yeon watched him drain glass after glass and celebrated internally. Hah! My tolerance was FORGED by Hyomin eonni herself. Tonight you go DOWN, Ji-ho.

“Straight drinking’s boring.” She snagged an empty bottle, radiating confidence. “Let’s play a game. Spin the bottle.”

“Sure. Why not?”

Her arm strength was genuine; her spin-the-bottle record, respectable. Unfortunately, the man across the table had spent over a decade of a previous life in bars where drinking games were the local currency.

He smiled — and began, gently, to farm her.

Ten rounds: she lost seven. And that was WITH him throwing rounds out of courtesy.

The night deepened; bottles emptied and were replaced; Ji-yeon’s eyes took on the red gleam of a gambler chasing losses, the world going pleasantly soft at the edges, her entire consciousness narrowed to a single objective: drink him under the table, then extract the answer.

IU, Hara, and Sulli — who had been huddled in their own quiet corner of conversation — drifted over, drawn by the noise. IU played a few cautious rounds and retired early to protect her remaining sobriety, cheering from the sidelines instead.

✦   ✦   ✦

Morning climbed up the sky, insolently bright.

Jiyeon woke in the master bedroom with a skull full of hot gravel. She peeled herself upright, shuffled to the bathroom, and ran the tap warm — splashing her face until something resembling consciousness reported for duty. Hands braced on the sink, she rode out the hangover’s opening wave of nausea, raked her hair back into a ponytail, and began the slow ritual of washing up: warm water, cleanser worked to foam, careful small circles, towel.

And then she saw it.

On the fair skin just below her collarbone: a small red mark.

“…What is THAT?”

She tugged the collar aside and prodded it with a fingertip. No pain. No itch.

Seafood allergy?

…Wait. That color. That SHAPE. That’s what the eonnis always described as a—

“You’ve lost your mind. LOST it, Jiyeon.” She lectured her own reflection. “Aigoo — no boyfriend to your name, and you’re diagnosing yourself with THAT? Tsk tsk. It’s an allergy. Obviously.”

(A mark of THAT kind required kissing to have occurred — and between T-ara’s 2012 schedule, which barely budgeted for sleep, and her complete absence of romantic prospects, the hypothesis failed peer review on the spot.)

“Ji-yeon! PARK Ji-yeon!” IU’s voice floated up the stairs. “If you’re awake, food’s ready — come DOWN!”

Food?! She snatched her toothbrush at combat speed.

“Give me two minutes~!”

On the subject of eating, Jiyeon’s enthusiasm had never once wavered in her life. She pattered downstairs to find IU alone at the table.

“As expected — only FOOD gets you vertical.”

“Says WHO? I was already washing up when you called.” A rapid seat was taken. “Look — nobody else is even up yet.”

“Up? They’re all GONE.” IU ladled out hangover soup. “Hara eonni had a schedule — left with Ji-ho after nine. The peach was going to stay for breakfast, but Victoria eonni called and she shot out the door.”

Ji-ho LEFT? Did I… did I ever actually ask him last night? She scratched her head. The game — she remembered the game starting. After that: static. Nothing.

She was still excavating when IU set down her spoon, expression shifting to something between wonder and gossip.

“You know — Ji-ho turned out to be genuinely SCARY.”

Scary? Him? The excavation was instantly abandoned.

“Scary… how?”

“This morning I accidentally overheard him on a call.” IU leaned in, voice dropping to secret-sharing register. “I think he’s DEBUTING.”

“WHAT? Debuting?! Isn’t he like — three months into training?!”

“Three months, yes. And I asked the peach about him.” The delight of privileged information sparkled openly now. “She says his singing is on another level — The One sunbae-nim took him as a FORMAL disciple.”

“MWO? The One sunbae-nim? Then— that’s the same as Taeyeon sunbae?! He’s THAT good?”

“That’s what the peach says. And there’s more — his FIRST monthly evaluation, he performed a song he wrote HIMSELF and took first place overall. Lee Soo-man seonsaeng-nim was on the panel — and loved him.”

“He WRITES? And took FIRST at SM — his first month?!” Her eyes had gone perfectly round.

“And THEN—” IU was fully enjoying herself now, “—he wrote ANOTHER one. Word is it got picked for BoA sunbae-nim’s new album — a producer walked into the practice room personally to ask for it.”

BoA sunbae-nim. THE BoA. Ji-yeon’s processor began to smoke quietly.

“And after they asked?”

“He REFUSED.” IU stroked her chin like a detective summing up. “Put that next to the debut call this morning, and… he’s keeping it for himself. SM just launched EXO, so it can’t be a boy group. Which leaves SOLO. And for SM to greenlight a solo plan for a foreign rookie — that tells you everything about what they think of him.”

She elbowed Ji-yeon, eyes gleaming.

“Yah — if he really debuts on his own songs, his writing is the real thing. Someday WE might be commissioning tracks from him.”

Debut. Music prodigy. Ji-ho?

The handsome face surfaced in her mind’s eye — followed immediately, treacherously, by the memory of a certain neckline geometry — and her face began to heat. And then, without warning, a single frame of lost footage flickered up out of the static: her own two hands, fisted in somebody’s collar, her own voice, sloppy with soju—

“You… you SAW, didn’t you? Admit it—”

“Ji-yeon-ah? Why are you suddenly RED?” IU’s concern cut the film reel. “Still queasy? Drink the soup while it’s hot.”

“…It’s nothing.” She kneaded her temples, lifted the bowl, and drank half of it straight down, warmth flooding through her. “Hoo. Alive again.”

Then, sighing:

“Variety recording this afternoon. Half a rest day per MONTH. When do we ever get to actually LIVE?”

“If they gave you real time off you’d hate it,” IU laughed. “Fine — trade with me. I’ll join T-ara as the maknae, you go solo.”

A joke — but T-ara’s 2012 was genuinely on fire, and two or three hours of sleep a night was Ji-yeon’s standing reality. Exhausted and happy, she grinned.

“Absolutely not!”

“Aigoo. Stingy.” IU’s lips curled. “Oh right — that parachuted-in princess of yours. How’s that situation?”

“Hwayoung?” Ji-yeon’s face fell like a curtain. “Don’t get me started. We’re all half-dead busy, and she keeps skipping dance practice. Stomachache. Foot ache. Headache. All theater. One time she begged off practice for a ‘foot injury’ — and we came back to the dorm just as she waltzed in loaded with SHOPPING BAGS. Tell me that’s not infuriating! Why did the company even TAKE her?”

“Favors changing hands, obviously.” IU shrugged. “Some people just have the right father.”
