Chapter One Hundred Sixty-Two

Asia’s Number One Group

Mid-January 2013 SBS — the Inkigayo stage a comeback in full flight, and one quiet needle

While two people hid from the snow in Hannam, Taeyeon was living inside an entirely different tempo.

Backstage at SBS Inkigayo, the light ran dim and the mirror ran honest. Her makeup was immaculate — and under the heaviest concealer money could buy, the faint blue shadows still ghosted through. A week of stacked schedules — music shows, radio, fan-signings — had compressed sleep to under four hours a night.

Her body wanted a bed. Her blood wanted the stage.

“Girls’ Generation — standby!”

“Coming!”

One deep breath, and the leader’s brightness came on like stage power. Nine of them filed out into the light.

The opening bars of “I Got a Boy” detonated the hall.

The fan chant came back like a tide taking a beach — name after name, then the creed itself: Girls’ Generation now, Girls’ Generation later, Girls’ Generation forever. In this era they closed every broadcast they appeared on, for the simple mercy of the other groups: no one wanted to perform after SONE finished shaking a building.

Nine figures cut through the shifting light, every move exact, every angle loaded — the absolute control that had earned the throne and kept it.

First place went where everyone knew it would.

Trophy in hand, still catching her breath, Taeyeon stepped to the microphone.

“Thank you to SBS, to the company, to our tireless staff — and to our dearest, dearest SONE!”

A pause. Her gaze swept the pink ocean below — and then, as if through the lens itself, found some particular direction, and her voice softened a degree she didn’t quite catch herself.

“And finally — special thanks to the one who wrote this song for us. Ji-ho, my hoobae. Truly — thank you.”

The hall went up an octave. His name in the industry now functioned as a dual guarantee — quality and heat — and stacked against Girls’ Generation’s own wattage, the chemistry had produced numbers that read like typos.

“I GOT A BOY” · COMEBACK SCORECARD

01 Jan 1 — Fourth studio album released

02 Jan 4 — MV passes 10M views in 35 hours — fastest K-group MV to the mark

03 Jan 10 — M Countdown: 10,000 — first perfect score in show history

04 Physical — 250,000+ in two weeks · digital 1,350,000+

05 Global — Billboard World Albums #1 · World Digital Songs #1 · Oricon Albums #2 · Gaon monthly #1

06 Press — Billboard: “a song that defines the decade”

Spoken honestly, as pure songcraft “I Got a Boy” was a genre collision that not everyone would call flawless. But celebrity is its own alchemy. The writer was the ten-years-once genius who had shut out an entire digital top ten with one album; the performers were the empresses of K-pop. Anyone who found the song strange was, per the fandoms, simply not equipped for it — and the press releases arrived pre-agreed: “boundary-breaking,” “genre-defining,” “the genius meets the empresses.” The Billboard line got quoted until it wore grooves.

✦   ✦   ✦

Encore done, the noise fell away by backstage degrees.

Tiffany walked beside Taeyeon without a word, eyes drifting to the tired, luminous profile — and the pride and softness she’d just heard in “my hoobae” slid in somewhere under her ribs like a fine needle.

“He really is incredible, isn’t he?” Taeyeon hadn’t noticed the silence beside her; her eyes had curved into crescents on their own. “Honestly, I didn’t even like the song at first. But it was HIS — so I figured it had to work.”

“Mm. He’s always been a genius.”

The smile Tiffany produced took real effort, and her voice came out dry around it.

Hong Kong arrived without being invited — whiskey warmth, the softness of it, all of it. She’d filed that night under “accident,” under “alcohol,” under “one moment of weakness,” and buried the file. But burial never worked on this one. His stunned eyes. Her own runaway pulse. Who leaned in. Who didn’t stop it. And every night since — the tangled shame-and-wanting she refused to name in daylight.

And now his name lived next to words like “miracle.” Now he wrote songs for Taeyeon — with care, with intent — and stood in his own blaze of light, and the thought crept up the trellis like something poisonous and green: ALMOST. It had almost been her.

If she’d been more direct that night. If she’d said a different sentence. Would the person he treated that carefully — the one who got to say his name from a winner’s microphone — be standing where Taeyeon stood?

“Tiffany?” Taeyeon’s voice tugged her back. “You okay? You’ve gone pale.”

“Ah — fine. Just tired, probably.” The brighter smile went up like scaffolding. “Go change. We’ve got the interview.”

She watched Taeyeon walk toward the dressing room — light-footed, carried by a career and a love both running at full sail — and felt the dark things stir in their damp corner: envy, vanity, comparison. Moss species. They grew without sun.

She knew exactly how wrong it was. That was her teammate. Her closest friend. And knowing changed nothing about the itch of “almost mine” — because hearts are contrary machines, and the harder you forbid a thought, the more precisely it learns your address.

She pressed it all down, followed Taeyeon into the bright noise of the schedule, and let the evening look normal.

But the thorn named “comparison” had already seated itself in the softest tissue available — waiting, patient, for one careless touch. Because collapsing a resolve rarely requires force. Only a well-timed opening, a plausible excuse, or one weak evening.
