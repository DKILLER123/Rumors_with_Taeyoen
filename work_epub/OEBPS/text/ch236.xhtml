Chapter Two Hundred Thirty-Six

Laying Out the Future, Again

August 5, 2013 · night Grand Walkerhill · the master suite, Moonlight wing one question asked in a bathrobe — and a chess game that reaches years past the answer

The suite was top-resort extravagant, and Taeyeon walked into it like a child entering a toy store.

“Wah!”

She let go of Ji-ho’s arm and set off on inspection — a slow lap of the living room, touching things; a gasp at the heated soaking pool; a detour through the bathroom; and finally the bedroom, where she pressed one hand into the enormous bed and sighed from the soul.

“Wah~ so SOFT~”

She ended at the window. The night outside was ink-quiet, thick enough to swallow every worry whole. Just the two of them now — and she felt every muscle in her body unclench at once.

“Nuna. What are you looking at?”

Warm breath arrived at her nape a half-second before his arms did, folding her small frame back against him. His lips found the pale curve of her neck.

“Mm—”

A low hum escaped her; goosebumps chased themselves across her skin. His hands traced the narrow line of her waist.

Of all of her, this was what undid him most — the snow of her skin, and a waist his two hands could almost circle.

Her breathing shortened. She caught his hand, cheeks going pink.

“Wait— bath first—”

“Okay.”

He scooped her up without effort — a startled “Ah~” — and her arms looped his neck, and then, at some private thought, she burst out laughing, bright and helpless.

He looked down at the girl in his arms. The fine oval face; the clear, light-catching eyes; the black hair like poured silk. The single small dimple — not the standard-issue kind, but her own: low on the right cheek, appearing only when she grinned wide, and devastating when it did. This was his woman. Leader of the first girl group of her generation. The core of the “empresses” of the industry. Chart queen. One of the faces of K-pop’s arrival on the world’s stage.

At the pool’s edge she hopped down, landed light, and began — slowly, one button at a time — to make the evening’s intentions unambiguous. She turned, let the fabric slip from one round pale shoulder, and looked back at him.

He closed the distance and gathered her from behind, hand finding the smooth warmth of her waist.

She leaned back into him — and caught his wandering hand — and asked it, quietly, into the quiet room:

“Ji-ho-ya. Do you… still love me?”

The room went perfectly still.

He did not answer at once.

Listening properly meant four separate operations: hear what was said; hear what wasn’t; work out why it was said; work out why it was said TO YOU. There were two things a man should never do quickly, and answering this question was one of them.

She knew about Tiffany. She had neither confronted him nor staged a scene nor interrogated either of them. Which told him everything: after the first raw pain of it, she had weighed the whole board — and found she could not survive losing him AND Fany at once; could not risk the team fracturing over a best-friend war in the exact season of the renewal negotiations. She asked DO YOU STILL LOVE ME because her heart was lost in its own house — terrified this wasn’t appetite but defection; that he was leaving her FOR Fany. A woman standing in front of a straying man this carefully, this humbly — that was a woman who loved him down to the marrow. And the sight of it put a thin blade through him.

He also understood, coldly and clearly, what he owed Sulli. She’d been right: the wound was always going to open; earlier was survivable, later was not. And she had accidentally handed him the one version of discovery that Taeyeon could metabolize — because the rival wasn’t a stranger, it was FANY. Taeyeon the homebody kept few people close; Fany’s weight in her life rivaled his own. So she had swallowed it, and instead begun quietly signaling — LOOK AWAY, GIVE UP, GO — hoping to keep the friend and the man both, and pretend none of it had ever happened. Naive. A little foolish. And exactly, heartbreakingly her: the best plan a girl who refused to lose anyone could build.

His silence stretched one second too long. Fear started climbing her face; her body gave a small tremor; the questions began stampeding — SHOULD I NOT HAVE ASKED? DID I RUIN IT? — colliding with the stubborn, wounded counter-voice: I’M HIS GIRLFRIEND. I’M ALLOWED TO ASK. AND WHY AM I THE ONE AFRAID?

She twisted around, eyes gone scattered.

“Why— why aren’t you answering?”

He pulled her hard against him and held her eyes.

“Because I was thinking.”

“About how to tell you — that you’re the woman I love most.”

Light, entering a dark room.

“I’m… the woman you love most?”

“Of course you are.”

He bent and kissed the snow of her shoulder; she shivered and locked her arms around him.

Human closeness came in gradations — that was simply true — and on his private map, Taeyeon stood where she had always stood: first.

And while his lips traveled her collarbone, the strategist in him kept working. Sulli, Joo-hyun, Fany — each had received a home; material fact was the great tranquilizer. But women could not be treated identically — differentiation was the entire discipline. The better-treated felt chosen; the lesser-treated felt spurred. So: apartments for Sulli and Joo-hyun; a VILLA for Fany. If Fany someday learned of the others, the arithmetic of the gift would whisper HE HOLDS YOU HIGHER. If Joo-hyun learned and burned, he’d have the makings of the apology — and if she didn’t burn but sharpened instead, better still. As long as nobody left, hierarchy wasn’t cruelty. It was architecture. And Taeyeon — the girlfriend, the first, the one whose position was not negotiable — required something in a category of her own.

His mouth found hers; the kiss went long and slow. She was going soft and warm against him when he said it, conversationally, against her ear:

“Nuna.”

“Mm~”

“I’m going to acquire a stake in SM. When I do — I’ll register part of it in your name, held on my behalf.”

“Mwo?”

She surfaced from the haze instantly, staring at him.

“You’re buying into SM??”

“Mm. Not now. Next year.”

Next year — when three members of a certain boy group would collide with SM and file to terminate, and the stock would go through the floor. He kept that part to himself.

Her brow creased, working at it.

“Why SM, suddenly? Is it… for me?”

The eternal divergence of male and female arithmetic: a woman’s first hypothesis for any male action was HERSELF. Though in fairness — half of it WAS her. More precisely: it was every woman in Girls’ Generation he had grown greedy about. The other half was the future.

“Mm.” A nod. “But not only. After LOEN, I’m already JYP’s second-largest shareholder. Add a position in SM, and of the big three agencies — I can reach two.”

Delight arrived, then cooled into an unexpected frown.

“Wouldn’t that trigger an antitrust review?”

“Hahaha—” He flicked the tip of her nose, delighted. “Look at you. ANTITRUST.”

“Ya! I finished high school, thank you!” — teeth briefly bared, maximally cute.

“Review only triggers when you CONTROL one company and hold major influence in another — both stakes above the threshold. LOEN sits second at JYP, but Park Jin-young keeps control. And in SM I won’t cross five percent. Nothing to worry about.”

She understood roughly none of the mechanics and all of the music: second-largest at JYP, reaching into SM — her Ji-ho, again, playing a board she couldn’t even see the edges of. The admiration rose up her chest like heat. She wet her lips, and her voice came out low.

“I don’t understand any of it. But I believe you. Whatever you need me to do — I’ll do it. I’m yours.”

“Now stop talking, Ji-ho-ya. I missed you.”

“Love me~”

A request like that, from a face like that, has never once been refused in history.

The warm water rose around them; the rest belonged to the two of them, and the pool’s still surface did not stay still.

✦ ✦ ✦

Afterward, she lay boneless against his chest, unwilling to move so much as a finger. And he — mind rinsed clear and running fast — went back to the board.

The SM position had one more layer he hadn’t spoken aloud: years out, when the founder exited and giants fought over the company’s valuation, a quiet early stake would matter enormously. And Tencent’s money moving through the sector would lift the price regardless — even as a pure trade, it was a win. But the deeper play wasn’t SM at all. It was Kakao.

PRIVATE MEMO · THE KAKAO DOOR — DO NOT WRITE DOWN

Because he knew how the original story ended: Kakao, grown enormous, comes for LOEN — and if the owner refuses, Kakao simply builds a rival platform on top of its monopoly messenger and grinds you down, the Tencent playbook exactly. If you can’t beat the flood, own part of the river. Buy the door before anyone knows it’s a door; when the merger converts his Daum paper into Kakao equity, push cooperation, maybe cross-holding — and his position on the peninsula climbs to a floor no one in this industry had ever stood on. In a country that had chosen culture as its export engine, the music pipes were not something he intended to rent.

Power. And beauty. The two oldest currencies—

A pale finger was drawing slow circles on his chest.

“Sweetheart. What are you thinking about?”

“Nothing much.”

“Oh.”

IS HE THINKING ABOUT FANY? It IS her birthday. Hmph. Watch me empty you of the strength to think about anyone.

She executed a maneuver of unambiguous intent — a slow, deliberate shift against him. Nothing. His eyes were on the middle distance, somewhere around a stock chart. She tried again. Still nothing.

She was genuinely offended now. THIS SIGNAL HAD NEVER FAILED. Historically, one wriggle and he was fully present and reporting for duty.

Splash.

She stood up in the water and hauled on his arm.

“Up.”

“Hm?”

Bewildered, he let himself be arranged — seated on the pool’s edge, dripping, while she looked up at him with an expression that mixed challenge and sweetness in dangerous ratios.

Distracted, are we. Let’s see the stock market compete with THIS.

The water rocked, ring after slow ring.

Kakao, he decided, closing his eyes, could wait until tomorrow.
