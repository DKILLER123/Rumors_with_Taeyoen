Saturday · 7:30 PM A Gangnam Office Tower — Elevator Bank the “some” moments, off the charts and into the world

The doors opened on a cargo of weekend-overtime faces. In the silence, someone’s phone leaked sound — the familiar piano intro, thin and tinny and instantly recognized — the verse about last night’s drama rerunning on the TV while a phone stays silent until sleep.

Shoulders shifted, minutely, in unison. Someone’s head nodded a few millimeters. Nobody spoke — but an ah, you too passed through the car like weather. Out on the street, earbuds went in, and more than one stride lightened to an internal tempo.

Good music does that. It holds people up.

Hongdae, a convenience store, deep in the night: the part-time girl behind the register had the lyric video propped against the till, humming along, fingers keeping time on the counter. A young man off late overtime came in for a samgak-gimbap, caught the sound, and something tired in his face eased.

“Great song, right?”

“Ne. Tenth listen today, probably.”

“Same.”

He pushed out into the dark, and what he was humming, unprompted, was the line about suddenly hating the words just friends.

A taxi, somewhere in the city: the driver’s radio landed on the song. In the back seat, a young couple who had been separately deep in their phones looked up at the same moment.

“This one’s everywhere lately,” the driver offered.

“Mm. It’s good.” The girl stole a sideways glance at her boyfriend.

He said nothing — and took her hand.

The song had just reached the line about holding someone in front of everyone like something worth showing off; neon poured across the windows, and the silence in the cab went sweet and strange.
