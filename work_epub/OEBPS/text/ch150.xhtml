Chapter One Hundred Fifty

Heart to Heart

The same night · later Hong Kong — a hotel room, high floor consolation, group theory, and a phone call to Tiffany

Knock, knock.

Click.

The door opened; he slipped inside.

Taeyeon stood in plain white sleepwear, freshly washed, cheeks soft and faintly full — like a little rice-flour dumpling someone had dressed for bed.

He pulled off the cap and mask and craned a look deeper into the room.

“Nuna — where are Tiffany nuna and Seohyun nuna?”

She answered by walking straight into his chest, burying her face there, and breathing in — long and deep, the familiar scent settling her from the inside out.

“They went to the afterparty.”

Her small body fit against him soft and warm, freshly bathed — jasmine in the damp hair, a clean thread of white tea underneath. Gentle, and quietly magnificent. Like moonlight over a southern river town.

“Nuna. No trophy tonight — does it sting?”

“A little.” Her voice came muffled against his shirt. “The TTS album… we really did prepare so much for it. It’s a shame.”

He tightened his hold and lifted — shifting his grip to the bend of her knees, half-carrying, half-cradling, the way you’d scoop up a sulking child — so that she hung above him, looking down.

Her arms circled his neck on reflex. Loose damp hair swung down and brushed his forehead.

He carried her into the room, talking as he went.

“‘Twinkle’ is a great song. But it felt like it was missing one thing.”

“Missing what?”

“SM’s full effort.” He settled into the armchair and let her straddle his lap, hands loose around her waist. “That album ran on your name more than their push.”

She went quiet, red lips pressed together. TTS’s design brief was to spotlight Tiffany and Seohyun — carried on Taeyeon’s drawing power. That was simply true. And she had walked into it with her eyes open.

“I know.” It came out soft and unresisting. “For a girl group to last, you need balance. Checks and counterweights.”

“Otherwise the resentment inside the team stops being solvable. And then everything comes apart.”

She stayed folded against him, and the talk unspooled — and piece by piece he was shown the actual operating system of the nation’s girl group: equilibrium, maintained at all costs.

Industry Dossier · Strictly Between Two People

The Balance Doctrine · Girls’ Generation

As told to Ji-ho, one tired midnight in Hong Kong — leader’s edition, unabridged.

The Two Poles Taeyeon and Yoon-a carry the group’s popularity; Jessica runs third. Every strategy begins from this fact.

The Counterweight “The Boys” era deliberately elevated Jessica — signature styling, key vocal sections, set-apart stage treatment — an ice-princess axis to balance the two leads. Jessica, in turn, is balanced by Tiffany and the maknae line.

Case Files Sunny’s privilege was the landing itself, nothing after. Yuri had the visual-line push and let it drift. Japan pushed Sooyoung; America pushed the American, Jessica; and 2012’s TTS spotlighted Tiffany and Seohyun — carried on Taeyeon’s name — while Jessica took drama and stage-musical lanes as compensation.

The Price of Peace Variety slots, OSTs, endorsements, stage positions, line distribution — the arguments behind closed doors get loud, and sometimes go cold for weeks. The doctrine holds anyway, because the group gives each member more than any of them could take alone.

One Reader’s Conclusion Never debut in a team.

The two towering popularity poles were Taeyeon and Yoon-a, with Jessica running third. So when “The Boys” cycled, the company deliberately elevated Jessica — the iconic styling moments, the signature vocal sections and hook lines, the stage treatments that set her visibly apart — because an ice-princess image with rock-solid professional skills could counterweight the two leads, holding a fragile three-way summit in place. And Jessica, in turn, was counterweighted by Tiffany and the maknae line. The early core five formed a self-balancing structure that kept the whole edifice from tipping.

Sunny’s real privilege had been the landing itself — after that, no special treatment, but never neglect either. Yuri had the face and the early push to contest the visual line, but her loose, unbothered temperament, the absent body-management, the no-appetite-for-competition — the company eventually just let her drift at her own altitude. When they pivoted to Japan, they pushed Sooyoung, who’d debuted there once already and whose look the market favored. In 2011, for America, they pushed the American: Jessica. And in 2012 — because Tiffany had never had a proper turn, and Seohyun’s variety-couple popularity had spiked — TTS went to those two, drafting off Taeyeon’s gravity. Jessica got drama and stage-musical lanes as compensation.

And the leads did their part without being asked: Yoon-a carried the group’s name into every schedule she ran and put team calendars above her own; Taeyeon — Taeyeon had no life outside singing and Girls’ Generation at all. The soul of the group, and the reason the diehards stayed diehard. Everyone gave more than they took, because the group gave them more than anyone could alone. Until — someday, eventually — it wouldn’t.

Of course the real ledger was endlessly more tangled — variety slots, drama offers, OSTs, endorsements, stage positions, line distribution — and the arguments behind closed doors got loud, and sometimes went cold for weeks. Hearing it all only hardened his own old resolve: never debut in a team. Far, far too much friction.

When the last of it was out of her, she went boneless against him, cheek on his shoulder, breathing slow. This, she thought. This is what a boyfriend is for. One person who just listens — so nothing has to stay locked in your chest. The quiet inside her right now was worth more than any trophy tonight had been.

Feeling her fully relax, he stole a glance at his watch. Over an hour gone. About time.

“Nuna.” He eased her upright. “It’s getting late. I should head back.”

“You’re just going to LEAVE?” Open disbelief.

“Mm.” A kiss dropped on her forehead. “If Tiffany nuna and Seohyun nuna come back and catch us in here—”

“I don’t mind. But you, nuna?”

A three-woman room on tour. Being discovered by Tiffany and Seohyun — she only had to imagine it for one second to feel her entire social existence end.

But letting him walk out unspent — that was its own injustice. Her houseguest of a monthly visitor had just left, and appetite, once learned, keeps its own calendar.

“Wait.”

She rolled off him, pattered to the bed, grabbed her phone, and dialed.

It rang a long time before connecting — and the far end was all noise and music.

Voice Call

“Tiffany-ah. What are you all doing?”

“Karaoke~! Why, what’s up?”

“How long until you’re back?”

“Mmm — late! Probably past two! Why~?”

“Nothing, nothing. Just asking. Have fun — don’t drink too much.”

“Arasseo~ saranghae!”

“Mm. Nado saranghae.”

She tossed the phone onto the duvet and bounced back into his arms, eyes enormous and glittering, voice gone soft as warm taffy.

“They won’t be back until almost dawn.”

“So. Are you REALLY just going to walk out that door?”

She weighed almost nothing. Round, springy thighs locked at his waist; his hands full of soft warmth; gardenia and dark wood drifting up from her skin like it had grown there. A powerful argument against all exits.

But — the other side of the harbor. The clock. The OTHER clock.

He clung to the last clear inch of his mind and produced, with difficulty:

“Nuna. I don’t have… that. With me.”

“Then don’t use one~”

Her voice had gone to melted snow, and the almond eyes had flooded with a lambent, pulling light — and somewhere inside him a fuse simply burned through. There is no man alive who refuses that sentence.

He tossed her onto the bed with more force than manners.

White. Impossibly, luminously white.

“Ya— not on the bed. Carry me to the bathroom.”

He was occupied with the side of her neck and declined to respond.

She pushed at his shoulder, urgent and laughing at once.

“Quickly — if the girls come back tonight and the bed’s soaked through, how am I supposed to SLEEP here? Go, go, go!”

“Understood.”

He gathered her up — she clung like ivy — and carried her through the bathroom door.

The shower came on; warm water folded over them both; and the little room slowly filled with white mist, until the mirror held nothing at all.
