Registration wasn’t hard; it was merely tedious. Application forms, copies of the works, identification, a real-name declaration — assemble the stack, submit at the counter or through the Commission’s website, done.

By the time it was all stamped and he reached the SM building, the clock read one in the afternoon.

“HEY!”

Two small hands landed on his shoulders from behind.

“AISH—!”

He jumped clean out of his skin — spun — and found Seulgi wearing the radiant, unrepentant grin of a successful prankster, with Irene beside her, lips pressed together, eyes curved with suppressed laughter.

“Seul-gi-yah. You DO know people can be scared to death, right? Actual death.”

“Guilty conscience much?” Her eyes narrowed to gleeful slits. “That’s what you get for tricking Seung-wan into calling you oppa.” Then a flicker of suspicion: “Wait — is THAT why you’re in so late? You’re hiding from her, aren’t you!”

“Me? Afraid of HER? Besides, I’m genuinely older — where’s the trick?” Airy, unbothered, magnificent. “I had business this morning.”

“Oh, really?” Her eyes lit up. She tilted her head, looked past his shoulder, and hollered at volume: “SEUNG-WAN! You made it!”

Ji-ho’s face changed instantly — Wendy’s small avenging fists flashing through his memory in vivid detail.

“Just remembered something urgent. Bye Seul-gi, bye Joo-hyun nuna—”

And he was gone.

The moment his back disappeared around the corner, Seul-gi and Joo-hyun looked at each other and dissolved — full, undignified, wall-leaning laughter.

Around said corner, Ji-ho pulled up short and — with tremendous care — leaned back out for a reconnaissance peek.

A small body hit his back like a guided missile, slim arms locking around his neck, an indignant voice detonating by his ear:

“YAH, Ji-ho! Caught you AGAIN!”

Seung-wan?! How— Half a second later the answer arrived: Seulgi had played him twice with one shout.

Honesty compelled certain observations: Seung-wan weighed maybe forty-five kilos, nothing at all across his back — a warm, buoyant, faintly sweet-smelling nothing that was, objectively, rather pleasant. But if an instructor rounded the corner right now, they’d both be sitting through a very long talk about trainee conduct. He gave a small shrug of his shoulders and deployed his gravest voice.

“Seung-wan-ah. Be honest with me. Have you… fallen for me?”

She dismounted like the piggyback had caught fire.

“Dream ON! Am I INSANE? Who would like YOU?”

“If you don’t like me, why do you launch yourself at me on sight?” Solemn as a judge. “This has to stop. My girlfriend will get jealous.”

Something twinged in her chest, quick and unwelcome, and the question was out before she could stop it.

“…You have a girlfriend?”

“Not at present. HOWEVER.” The turn came with a full chin-lift of confidence. “With this face? Every beautiful artist in this company will be lining up eventually. Which makes them all my future girlfriends. So when you’re hanging off me like that and one of them sees you and assumes you’re the girlfriend and cancels her pursuit — can you take responsibility for my loss?”

She stared at him with the expression of a scientist observing a new and shameless species.

“You’re saying Yoon-a sunbae. Sica sunbae. Taeyeon sunbae. Joo-hyun eonni. ALL of them. Will chase YOU.”

“Naturally.”

The laugh escaped her without permission — she had never in her life met anyone with this exact caliber of nerve, and somehow her whole mood had lifted anyway. She looked him up and down, shaking her head slowly, mouth fighting a smile.

“Handsome, sure. Tragically insane, though. I’m going to find Soo-young. Enjoy your daydream, weirdo.”

And off she pattered.

He watched her go, smile tugging loose on its own. Wendy was pure voltage — around her, the eighteen-year-old body finally felt like it came with an eighteen-year-old life attached. Which was, of course, exactly why he couldn’t resist teasing her.

Official trainees carried a curriculum far beyond vocals, dance, arranging, acting, rap, and body work. The special courses were where the industry showed its true face.

Field Notes

The hidden curriculum of the idol factory:

Flash training. A debuted idol walks into walls of camera flashes from dozens of outlets — night turned to noon in strobes. The requirement: not one flinch, not one blink. Nearly every artist alive needed drilling to face it with a serene face.

Expression management. Which smile reads warmest; which angles flatter; what face to hold when the stage goes wrong in front of ten thousand people.

And the rest: etiquette class, scam-prevention class, talent-expansion class, the infamous “360 media trap questions” seminar, camera-finding drills — the full armor-plating of a public life.

The trainees’ days dissolved into that grid, hour by scheduled hour. Today Ji-ho trained straight through to six — then headed home to shower and change. Because tonight was his first formal lesson with The One, and if he timed it right, the session would land squarely inside the God of Learning’s eight-o’clock window. Compounding interest, in its purest form.
