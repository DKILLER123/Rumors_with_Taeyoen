Chapter Seventy-Nine

Hara

Busan-born, Seoul-resident — but on the subject of where to eat in this city, Sulli’s authority remained absolute. Dusk was settling, her disguise was holding, and she led him confidently to a haenyeo restaurant from her memories.

“Imo — is the upstairs sea-view room open?” The Busan dialect came out of her thick and native.

“Open, open!” The busy proprietress looked up — and her eyes promptly lit. “Aigoo — heavens. What a HANDSOME young man.”

The Busan accent was mostly a matter of melody; he followed it fine, and bowed politely.

“Thank you. Good evening, imo-nim.”

“Good, good, good — quickly now, upstairs with you!”

She installed them in the private room with great ceremony, handed over menus, and — while Sulli deliberated — beamed at Ji-ho with her whole face.

“Aigoo, genuinely handsome. Young man — tonight imo is gifting you a seafood pancake. On the house.”

The face literally pays for meals. He thanked her hastily. Sulli, laughing, rattled off the order like a returning regular: the signature sea-urchin nurungji rice, the raw marinated seafood platter, sea-urchin gim rolls, soy-marinated crab, grilled eel, grilled scallops, seafood soup —

“—and soju and beer, imo!”

“Aigoo, is our young lady a regular? She orders like a professional.” The proprietress squinted at her curiously. “And what’s with the mask, child? Aren’t you warm?”

Sulli glanced at Ji-ho, smiled, and pulled the mask down.

“Imo. Remember me?”

“Ah— AH!” Recognition detonated. “The— Soo-jung’s girl! Yes?”

At her mother’s name, the smile flickered — held.

“Ne. Sulli.”

“Hahaha, of course — Jin-ri!” Delighted clapping. “Aigoo, pretty as ever — prettier! They said you went to Seoul and became a big star!”

Sulli held up two fingers a centimeter apart.

“A tiny bit famous.”

“HAHAHA!” The proprietress roared, then slid her gaze to Ji-ho, smile turning richly conspiratorial. “And this young man is…?”

Sulli peeked at him sideways, suddenly shy.

“My… chingu.”

“Aigoo — I understand, I understand COMPLETELY.” The full auntie-smile deployed. “Don’t you worry. My lips are sealed.”

“Imo! You’ve misunderstood — we really are just—”

“I know, I know~” Eyes crinkled to slits, entirely unconvinced. “This room has the best sea view. Enjoy the scenery — imo will see to the food.”

At the door she turned back once, took in the pair of them — and sighed with her whole chest.

“Aigoo. To be young. To be YOUNG.”

The door closed. The room went abruptly, conspicuously quiet.

♪ Do it do it chu~ ♪

Her new phone burst into song — rescue.

“Oh — it’s Hara eonni!” She pounced on it. “Hello, EONNI~~”

Goo Hara’s year had been living mostly in Japan; this trip home covered exactly two fan meetings — one Seoul, one Busan. She had just wrapped the Busan stage, boarded the van for the ride north, finally checked her phone — found Victoria’s messages about Sulli going dark — and called in genuine alarm. At the sound of that bright voice, the alarm finally let go of her; she sank back into the seat.

Call · Goo Hara → Sulli

“Sulli-yah — Victoria eonni said no one could reach you! What HAPPENED?”

“It’s… a long story, eonni. I’ll tell you when I see you.”

“…Okay.” The weight in Sulli’s voice registered; she bit her lip. “Then where are you right now?”

“Busan, actually!”

“REALLY? I’m in Busan too! Where — I’m coming to you!”

Sulli hesitated, glancing at Ji-ho.

“What is it?”

“Hara eonni’s in Busan — she wants to come find me…”

“Of course. I don’t mind at all.”

Down the line, Hara had caught the male voice.

“Sulli-yah… is someone WITH you?”

“Ne — Ji-ho oppa. If eonni doesn’t mind, I’ll send the address!”

Unreachable all day… and she’s with Ji-ho. A grin spread across Goo Hara’s face. Are they DATING?

“Yah — what would I mind? Although… you two, alone? Wouldn’t I be intruding~?”

“EONNI! It’s not like that! Not— not currently! Just come, I’m sending the address!”

She hung up in a fluster and sent the pin. In the van, Hara read the address and turned the phrasing over with delight. Not CURRENTLY like that. Meaning: possibly, eventually, exactly like that. A hero-saves-the-beauty story… how romantic.

She pressed her smile flat, put the phone away, and — when her manager appeared — produced a folded wad of bills with an apologetic bow of her head.

“Eonni… could you take the express bus back to Seoul tonight?”

“Hara. Are you going to see a boyfriend?”

I WISH. Mine’s as busy as I am. She shook her head.

“No — Sulli’s in Busan. I’m going to her.”

At Sulli’s name the manager relaxed, took the money and surrendered the car keys.

“Fine. Back at the company by noon tomorrow. Promise.”

“Thank you, eonni~”

✦   ✦   ✦

At the haenyeo restaurant, a thoroughly disguised Goo Hara knocked at the private-room door.

“That’s DEFINITELY Hara eonni!” Sulli bounced up and pattered to the door; Ji-ho rose behind her.

Click.

Hara bounded in, kicked the door shut behind her—

“AAAH!! EONNI~”

“SULLI-YAHHH~”

—and the two of them collided into a hug and jumped up and down in place like the floor was a trampoline.

“Weren’t you in Japan?! When did you get back?!”

“Yesterday! Two fan meetings — I flew back specially.” She pulled off the mask and sunglasses, then extended one slim fair hand past Sulli’s shoulder. “Ji-ho. We meet again.”

“Hara nuna. Good evening.” A light, courteous shake. “Sit — the food just landed.”

Her eyes swept the loaded table and ignited.

“DAEBAK. And I skipped dinner!”

They settled in, and Sulli went to work with a bartender’s fluency: beer to two-thirds, the small glass of soju submarined into it, a tissue capped over the mouth of the glass, palm sealed on top — one decisive shake.

Somaek. Soju and beer, roughly 8.5 degrees combined — modest on paper, notoriously fast to the head.

“A-ssa~ Sulli-brand somaek, first serving!” She set the inaugural glass in front of Hara.

“I drove here. Can’t.”

The glass migrated, with visible mourning, to Ji-ho’s placemat.

“Ahhh~ tragic,” Sulli sighed with feeling. “Raw marinated seafood is WASTED without a drink.”
