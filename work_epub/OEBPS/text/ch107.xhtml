Chapter One Hundred Seven

The Fan Club

The encore ended; Ji-ho and IU bowed and walked off hand-raised into the wings — and straight into the media gauntlet: a rapid-fire interview, backstage candid shots, the going-home video with its ritual “please keep loving us!” delivered into a handheld lens. Every second pre-allocated to tonight’s broadcast or tomorrow’s feeds.

As the last camera lowered, Sulli materialized and attached herself to IU’s arm.

“Oppa! Congratulations on first place!”

“Yah, Peach. You’re hugging MY arm and congratulating only Ji-ho?”

“Aigoo — Ji-eun, you’ve won a MILLION of these. It’s oppa’s FIRST~” Sulli’s eyes curved into crescents.

A transparently fraudulent excuse — which IU accepted anyway, tapping Sulli’s forehead.

“…Fine. You pass.”

“Sulli-yah — we need to go~”

“Coming, V-eomma.”

Idol time ran tight; f(x) had another schedule. A quick three-way hug —

“When there’s a free day — dinner. All of us.”

— one long look from Sulli at Ji-ho, a wave to IU, and she was gone with her team.

✦   ✦   ✦

The makeup room smelled of cleanser and hairspray. Ji-ho sat with his eyes closed while a makeup artist worked the stage face off him; beside him, IU emerged from under her own layers, studied his bare skin for two full seconds, and laughed.

“Ji-ho. Your SKIN. You just sang under broiler lights and there’s no oil, no redness — what’s the management routine? Genuinely jealous.”

“That’s my line, nuna. Yours is like white jade — I can’t find a single pore. Certified luminous.”

“Are we doing corporate mutual flattery right now?” The smile deepened. “…Thank you, though.”

The banter carried them through cleanser and clothing changes, and then her schedule swallowed her into the night, and his manager’s van carried him back through neon-streaked glass toward the company, eyes shut, storing up.

It only gets busier from here.

✦   ✦   ✦

The conference room lights were still on; coffee stood ready on the table; the team sat ringed around schedules, analytics, and sentiment briefs. Ji-ho downed half a bottle of water and dove into tomorrow’s itinerary.

Manager Ryu Dong-hwan dragged a finger along a curve on his tablet, barely containing himself.

“Tonight’s stage reaction is superb — discussion clustering on your visuals, the vocal, and the eye contact with IU. Beyond music shows, the company’s lining up a couple of variety spots for you two—”

Near the end, he turned the tablet around.

“One more thing. Your fan club. Time to formally meet them.”

“The early core fans self-organized under the name ‘Solbeam’ — light, from Sol. We’ve decided to keep it, and build the full structure around it.”

“Now it’s time for you to walk in and say hello. Register an account, give me the ID — I’ll get you verified.”

A flicker of genuine startled delight crossed Ji-ho’s face as he took the tablet. The club’s mark was simple and warm — light in motion. He thought of the stage lights; of every voice that had shouted his name at a camera.

Account registered. Verification stamped. He touched Join.

An outrageous number. A typical rookie opened with a few thousand paid members — sometimes a few hundred. Only the flagship groups of the great houses, riding a breakout release, cracked twenty thousand out of the gate.

And as the paid base grows — raise the tier, add a limited membership — this becomes a seven-figure-dollar after-tax stream on its own. Every year.

Money taken meant work owed: the maintenance wasn’t his problem, but the contract was — at least two appearances a week in the cafe, plus the cooperation the platform needed: signatures, posters, photobooks, offline meets.

And there it was, the arithmetic of going solo: Girls’ Generation split with SM fifty-fifty too — and then split the remainder nine ways.

A soloist kept the whole half.
