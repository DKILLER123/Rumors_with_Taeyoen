Chapter One Hundred Sixty-One

The Film’s Theme Songs

January 14, 2013 · Morning Hannam — the new apartment a system unlock, and two songs for the film

Early the next morning, the interface chimed before he was fully awake.

⟨ DING ⟩ PARTNER DEVELOPMENT unlocked. Development quests generated for partner Bae Joo-hyun — see the PARTNER center.

The Endorsement Queen.

He turned the track over in his mind and found he liked it. Endorsements might not carry the raw name-recognition of acting or singing, but they were real money on a lighter workload — and with her face, everything short of quests three and five was very achievable. That face was not a face the camera argued with.

✦   ✦   ✦

Joo-hyun surfaced slowly from sleep, reached across the bed on instinct, and found it empty. Her whole body ached in the manner of furniture that had been disassembled and reassembled by enthusiastic movers — the price of a first night, paid in full despite all his gentleness.

The bathroom door clicked open and he emerged in a cloud of steam, toweling his hair, entirely unburdened by clothing.

She went scarlet, clapped a small hand over her eyes, and burst out laughing anyway.

“Mwo-ya — why aren’t you WEARING anything?”

“Ani.” Genuine puzzlement. “After last night, what exactly is left to be shy about, nuna?”

She considered this, conceded the logic, lowered her hand, and looked at him with great dignity and a face like a peach blossom.

“If you’re awake, come shower. It’s ten already — aren’t you hungry?”

“Find me clothes first. I’ll wear yours — mine can go in the wash.”

She tried to move, discovered her limbs had resigned, and retreated under the duvet like a cat declining the day, only two large eyes showing.

He picked out the smallest things he owned, turned around, took in the situation, dropped the clothes on the bed, and walked over. One hand under her knees, one at her waist, and she was airborne.

“Honestly. A whole child. Can’t even bathe without help.”

“MWO? Ya! Who’s a child! Put me DOWN! Ji-ho!!”

Struggle proving useless, she deployed the signature move — teeth, applied directly to his shoulder.

“OW— nuna. I wasn’t this rough with YOU.”

A tactical error. The bite intensified.

He remembered, too late, that her competitive streak yielded only to surrender, never to argument.

“Sss— it HURTS, nuna. I was wrong. I was completely wrong.”

The jaws relented at once. Then — apparently embarrassed by her own ferocity — she gave the bite mark one small apologetic lick, which achieved considerably more voltage than the bite had.

“Nuna, do that ag—”

“Don’t even THINK about it!”

And then she was laughing again at her own absurdity, helpless and loud.

Holding her, he ran the private comparison: like Taeyeon, she was small and snow-pale with a completely inexplicable sense of humor and a laugh like a delighted auntie. The difference was sequencing — Taeyeon ran soft with occasional bursts of chaos; Joo-hyun started soft and grew bolder by the hour, her competitiveness surfacing in the strangest places. He was not complaining.

Warm water, soft foam, and shyness burning off like morning fog — she washed his back with a gentleness that was simply her nature coming through.

“Nuna — if anyone ever gives you trouble at the company, call me. Or go to Taeyeon nuna or Tiffany nuna. One’s my sunbae from the studio days; the other owes me a favor — both are close to me. They’d help you. Or find The One seonsaeng-nim — I’m his favorite student. He’d look after you.”

She paused, and hummed acknowledgment. Proud and private, she had no intention of ever asking Taeyeon or Tiffany for anything — but the intention behind it landed. And something else landed with it: he wouldn’t dare say those names to her unless the relationships were clean. The little knot of worry about him and Taeyeon loosened another turn. Her hands on his back went gentler still.

✦   ✦   ✦

Lunch was simple and unhurried. Outside, the snow still owned the roads — and SM’s perennial honor student found that requesting a day off required no reason at all when you had her attendance record.

They folded into the sofa: she with the training plan open, frowning and nodding by turns; he with staff paper, starting on the film’s music.

Composition 80 — more than equal to a film’s soundtrack. And the assignments had been settling themselves in his head all morning. Taeyeon — first, and deepest — deserved the centerpiece. A duet with her for the theme, and a solo insert for the film’s brightest scene.

The first melody that surfaced from the other life was “Love Me Like You Do” — dream-lit electro-pop, a love declared without hedging, a future chart-conqueror across seventy countries. Perfect for the film’s heroine and her single-minded devotion.

But not for Taeyeon’s color. The song fit Tiffany like tailoring — California-raised, native English, a brightness the track was practically built around. And… he owed Tiffany something after Hong Kong. A song like this might be the bridge — the thing that let her make peace with an arrangement that included Taeyeon. Give it to Tiffany, then.

Which left the duet — and there had only ever been one candidate. “Shallow”: the slow-burn conversation that climbs from murmur to full-throated release, a future Oscar-winner and a No. 1 single across twenty markets, one of the great duets of its decade. The build suited Taeyeon’s instrument exactly; the imagery of shallow water and deep ends suited the film; and a duet with her would finally repay the old debt of “Some.” The song would likely outgross the movie — and both would tow his second-half plans across the Pacific. Taeyeon’s career lifted, Tiffany soothed, the box office pulled by the soundtrack, and the Western waters tested a full season early. Four birds. One stone.

He bent over the paper and began writing fast.

The motion woke Joo-hyun from her studying; she leaned in, eyes bright.

“Inspiration?”

“Mm.”

She studied the thicket of notation, understood a tenth of it, and needed no more than that.

“It’s going to be beautiful.”

“I think so too. Which is why, sweetheart — practice that vocal plan. Songs are coming with your name on them.”

“…Sweetheart?”

Something in her chest went warm and fizzy all at once.

She leaned over and kissed his cheek, soft as punctuation.

“Okay~ sweetheart. Whatever you say.”

The afternoon settled into parallel quiet — one writing, one studying. Now and then she selected a chocolate from the snack plate with a curator’s care and held it to his lips; he bit it, then caught her fingertips too, and she pulled back with a small indignant sound and cheeks like sunrise.

He licked his lips, unrepentant, and tucked a strand of hair behind her ear, fingertips tracing her cheek on the way down. She tipped her face up; the distance closed on its own.

A long moment later they parted, and her eyes had gone soft-focus and happy.

“Nuna. You’re sweeter than the chocolate.”

“Eww~ so CHEESY~”

She burrowed into his chest, laughing, and he closed his arms around her, and the snow kept the world outside exactly where it belonged.
