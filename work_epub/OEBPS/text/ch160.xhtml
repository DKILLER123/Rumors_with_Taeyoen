Chapter One Hundred Sixty

Peach Blossoms in Bloom

The same evening · snowfall Hannam — the new apartment a plan for the future, and a snowed-in road

Joo-hyun hurried into the bathroom, cheeks glowing like sunset.

The closeness of the afternoon had left her flustered enough that pouring the milk for dessert had gone badly — splashed fingertips, a wet wrist. Small disasters, treated with the full crisis response.

She ran the water and washed her hands twice over with soap, working the lather with methodical care until the clean sweet scent finally settled her. She dried her hands, patted her cheeks once, and headed back — colliding squarely with him in the hallway.

Her eyes went everywhere except his face. A blush slid from her ear down the side of her neck and vanished into her collar.

“I— I should go home tonight after all.”

He blinked, surprise and disappointment arriving together.

“Nuna won’t stay?”

She gave a small nod. She had come today half-hoping and fully terrified, and somewhere in the evening the terror had taken the wheel. Right now she wanted her own small familiar room and its manageable heartbeats.

A moment of quiet. Then his voice, warm and unbothered:

“Okay. Let me shower and change, and I’ll drive you.”

Her mouth opened — and he closed the loophole before it formed.

“It’s past seven and below freezing. You are not taking a taxi alone. That part isn’t negotiable.”

Her heart went soft against her will. She nodded.

He pressed a light kiss to her forehead and went to get his things.

✦   ✦   ✦

Shower sounds from down the hall. She drifted back to the living room to gather herself and her scattered composure, cheeks cooling by degrees, pulse insisting on its own schedule.

Before long he emerged — light grey turtleneck, dark overcoat, hair still damp.

“Let’s go.” He picked up the keys and took her hand as naturally as breathing, her fingers disappearing into the warmth of his.

He opened the door — and winter came in sideways.

The world had gone white while they weren’t watching. Goose-feather snow drifted down in slow spirals, banked on the eaves and the branches, and a few flakes landed, impertinent, in Joo-hyun’s outstretched palm.

“It’s beautiful—” she breathed.

He was smiling, and it was not a subtle smile.

“Beautiful, yes. But nuna—” his voice had a bounce in it he wasn’t even trying to hide, “—you may have a problem. You can’t leave.”

“Eh?”

She blinked up at him, lashes catching snow-dust, the picture of innocent bafflement.

“The hill road down from here is steep,” he explained with tremendous patience. “This much snow, no chains — driving it is genuinely dangerous. It can’t be done. It seems—” a beat of pure theater “—you’re staying tonight.”

She stared at him, at the joy he was entirely failing to conceal, and her heart took off like a startled rabbit.

“Then— then I’ll sleep in the guest room,” she said, very quietly.

“Of course.” He agreed with perfect grace, took her hand, and led her back inside. “Dinner first.”

✦   ✦   ✦

A simple dinner, then washing up side by side. Afterward he pulled a thick cashmere blanket from the cabinet — cloud-soft — and spread it over the sofa by the floor-to-ceiling window.

Outside, the snow kept falling without a sound. Inside, the warmth held.

She settled against him, head finding the hollow of his shoulder on its own, the faint florals of her hair drifting up.

“Ji-ho-ya—”

“Mm?”

“Thank you for today.”

His arm tightened, settling her closer.

“I’m the one who should say it. Thank you for being next to me.”

They sat inside the quiet a while, letting it be enough.

Then he seemed to remember something, eased free, and stood.

“Wait one minute.”

He came back from the bedroom with a bound folder and set it in her hands. The cover read: Irene — Vocal & Acting Systematic Development Plan.

She opened it slowly.

Not vague encouragement — engineering.

Private Curriculum · Irene · Acting Track

Systematic Development — Performance Craft

HabitHer specific performance habits, addressed one by one — the guarded smile, the held shoulders, the beauty that resists expression.

EmotionEmotional progressions broken into workable stages — from a flicker of micro-expression up through full body language.

VoiceVocal exercises designed around the exact character of her instrument, notation precise to single notes; breath training stepped to her measured capacity.

StudyReference clips and a reading list attached — because of course there was a reading list.

Method source: The Perfect Actor’s Encyclopedia — an individualized regimen, a real ceiling, an honest timeline.

“When… when did you make this?” She looked up, startled wet-eyed.

“Bit by bit, lately.” He sat back down beside her, serious now. “Nuna — your voice is unmistakable the moment it starts, and your acting has real bones. What’s missing is only systematic training.”

“Work through this at your own pace, and it will climb. Step by step.”

He paused, then said the rest plainly.

“After you debut — I’ll write your solo songs. And if you ever want to act, I’ll write you the script myself. I want your road to run long, nuna. Longer than a pretty face gets you. Though the face alone would get you quite far.”

Her fingers pressed hard into the folder’s edge. The small cold fear she’d been carrying for weeks — too short, too quiet, too replaceable for a man rising this fast — loosened, and let go. He wasn’t a man who said beautiful things. He was a man laying track for a future with her in it.

“Why are you… this good to me?” Her voice caught on the middle of it.

He reached over and traced her cheek, slow.

“Everyone has flaws. I have plenty.”

“I’m scared you’ll find them and leave. So I work very hard at being good to you.”

“I know your temper — gentle, careful. The better I treat you, the more you’ll forgive me, and then you’ll never break up with me at all.”

“Aren’t I awful?”

Deep in love and fathoms past objectivity, she heard none of the confession inside the joke. She only looked at him, eyes misting over.

“No. You’re not awful. You’re the best person I’ve ever met.” A wet, helpless smile. “How would I ever break up with you?”

The snow-light through the window silvered her eyes like lake water. She leaned in and pressed her face into his chest, and his heartbeat came through steady and unhurried — the safest sound she knew.

✦   ✦   ✦

The night deepened; the snow went on.

Under the blanket, warmth gathered slowly, the way trust does. They stayed like that a long time — the window full of falling white, the room full of small kisses and smaller promises, nothing rushed, every line of hers respected without her having to draw it twice. When her eyes finally would not stay open, he tucked the blanket to her chin and gathered her in.

“Sleep,” he said softly. “I’m not going anywhere.”

She nodded into the edge of the dream, cheeks faintly flushed — peach blossoms in bloom, out of season and unbothered by it.

The last thing she heard was pressed gently into her hair:

“Nuna. I’ll be beside you. Always.”

We measure love with clocks and call it fast or slow — but when the heart moves, the second hand melts, and time is re-poured from scratch.
