Chapter One Hundred Thirty

Taeyeon’s Solo Dance

8:00 PM Banpo-dong — The Apartment two surprises, one room

Taeyeon keyed in the code with a skip in her fingers and pushed open her own front door — onto darkness and silence.

“Still working, Ji-ho-ya?” she murmured, feeling for the entryway light, an elegant little paper gift bag hanging from one hand. She toed into her slippers. “Good — I’ll change first. Surprise HIM for once.”

Two skipping steps toward the bedroom — and a shape detached itself from the dark and folded her into its arms.

“AH—!”

The shriek lasted half a second before a warm, familiar, entirely shameless kiss sealed it off. The clean scent came down over her like weather; her panicked heartbeat identified its assailant and immediately re-tasked itself — twice the speed, entirely different reason. The paper bag hit the floor with a soft whump.

She closed her eyes, wound her arms up around his neck, and answered the ambush with full fluency — half a month’s worth of missing him igniting on contact, burning both of them warm.

Somewhere in the rising temperature, her last thread of executive function reasserted itself. She patted his shoulder, breathless.

“Wait— wait. Put me down first. I have a surprise for you.”

A surprise? He set her down, curiosity visibly lit.

She retrieved the bag, hugged it to her chest, cheeks blazing, and issued instructions in a voice that wobbled adorably at the edges:

“Don’t come in until I call you. PROMISE.”

He nodded, smiling. She kicked off the slippers and pattered barefoot into the bedroom at speed. The door clicked shut.

Roughly ten minutes later, it opened exactly one hand’s width. A slim white arm emerged and beckoned.

“Come here. Quickly.”

The conspiratorial theater of it put an extra half-beat in his pulse. He crossed to the door. It swung slowly open.

She stood just inside, wrapped in the warm lamp-haze — in a black sequined slip dress of frankly daring design: cut close through the body, hem at mid-thigh over sheer black stockings, and — the detail that emptied his head — a clean sweep of bare skin at the waist, held together by nothing but a few fine straps, rising and falling minutely with her breathing, glowing like pearl under the amber light.

He stopped in the doorway. His breath audibly snagged; his throat moved on its own.

“Well?” She tilted her head, winding a strand of chestnut hair around one finger, catching her lower lip in her teeth — shy and lethal in the same motion. “Does it look good?”

“‘Good’ is not remotely the word—” His voice had dropped half an octave.

She felt the heat of the look and spun lightly on the spot to show him the back — bare to the waist but for two crossed ties, the delicate machinery of her shoulder blades shifting under lamplight.

“I bought it… specifically for you.” A breath. “Do you like it?”

He stepped in and shut the door behind him with the back of one hand. The room smelled of her sweet fruit-note tangled with a thread of lit sandalwood — private, warm, low-lit.

“And what’s the occasion—”

He came close enough that the air between them carried heat in both directions. She tipped her face up, eyes bright.

“Don’t you notice anything DIFFERENT about me today?” A small nervous bite of the lip — pure mischief wrapped in pure nerves. “Guess right… and tonight, I do whatever you say.”

His fingertips touched, at last, the bare curve of her waist — warm, impossibly smooth. She shivered once and leaned in rather than away.

He read her the way she wanted to be read: the smoke-deepened eyes; the vintage red on her lips; black sequins detonating against milk-pale skin; the sheer-wrapped legs; bare feet on the floorboards. Not tall — perfectly made. And for her to stage all of this, unprompted, could have exactly one trigger: the milk had worked, and she knew it.

“Nuna,” he said quietly, fingertips tracing the warm skin at her side, “…did you get a little taller?”

Her eyes flew wide; a hand came up over her mouth.

“You— how did you KNOW?”

He straightened to his full height, level-eyed.

“Haven’t you noticed? The height difference between us — it’s barely changed.”

She measured it, pupils tracking up. True. She’d grown — 161 now, by the wall chart — and yet here she stood, still exactly one chin’s-height below him. Which meant—

“You— you grew TOO?”

Understanding landed, and behind it, a strange warm tide.

“Love is a two-person thing. Isn’t it?”

The sentence went through her like a struck bell.

Love is two people, running toward each other. She loved this man. This man loved her. And the universe — hormones, dreams, centimeters — kept co-signing. If this wasn’t written somewhere, what was?

“You guessed right.” She reached up and laid her fingertips along his cheek, eyes gone soft all the way through. “So tonight… I do whatever you say.”

And underneath, a smaller voice, for internal circulation only: love me hard, Ji-ho.

He closed her small hand inside his.

“Then, nuna — in this dress… dance for me.”

“Dance?”

“Mm.” He nodded. “Not Girls’ Generation’s leader Taeyeon. MY nuna. One dance that belongs to me and no one else. Will you dance it?”

She held his gaze for a long moment — then smiled, slow and sure, and nodded. She stepped back, picked up her phone, and tapped twice. A low electronic pulse rolled out into the room, rhythm-heavy, faintly hazy at the edges — and the whole space changed climate.

And she began to move.

Performance

A dance with no stage in it.

None of the drilled precision of ten thousand practice-room hours — every motion loose, instinctive, and aimed. Sequins scatter lamplight with each turn; the sheer-dark line of her legs draws and crosses; and at every rotation the bare sweep of her waist catches the light, fine muscle moving under pearl-smooth skin — a picture engineered, frame by frame, to be impossible to look away from.

Her eyes never leave him. Inside them, taking turns: mischief, challenge, tenderness — braided into something with a pull like gravity. He leans against the wall and forgets to move, while the air in the room climbs degree by degree and thins like altitude.

The track surges — drums stacking — and on one clean turn she closes the distance entirely: hands landing on his shoulders, body following, the last bars danced at zero distance.

The music stops. The room holds nothing but two accelerated breathing patterns, interleaving.

“Now—” she tipped her head back to look up at him, faintly breathless, one fingertip idly working the top button of his shirt, “—your turn.”

Her lips, under the vintage red, were ripe to the point of provocation.

His eyes had gone dark and deep, currents moving at the bottom. He raised a hand — traced the flush of her cheek with the pad of his thumb — let it drift to the soft curve of her lips, and murmured:

“Nuna—”

“I’d like to test that famous voice of yours.”
