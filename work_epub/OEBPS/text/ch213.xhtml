Chapter Two Hundred Thirteen

Rewriting Fate

July 2013 SM’s top floor · Cube’s war room · a Jeju photo shoot · one dorm gone quiet two chairmen who can’t smile at good news, one girl group resurrected — and a Top 3 sweep

July’s heat rolled over Seoul — but on SM Entertainment’s top floor, the temperature in the chairman’s office ran strangely mixed.

Lee Soo-man and Kim Young-min sat across the wide sofa set, a tablet between them showing the latest American chart data and the jubilant domestic headlines stacked on top of it.

“Billboard Hot 100. Twenty-seventh and thirtieth—”

Kim Young-min adjusted his glasses, and there was remarkably little joy in the voice. “Tiffany and Taeyeon. This time they’ve — genuinely given us an enormous surprise.”

“A surprise? Or a shock?” Lee Soo-man’s face ran complicated. “This is a fortune poured out of the sky, landing squarely in SM’s courtyard. Except — the luck that carried it here belongs, in the main, to someone else.”

Neither needed the someone named.

The joy was real. The flagship’s members standing inside the world’s main chart — the symbolism, the brand accretion — no ledger could price it. The stock had ticked visibly upward within hours; PR was drowning in foreign inquiries; and Tiffany and Taeyeon, the vocal line’s core, had just vaulted onto an entirely new tier of individual value: no longer only members of Girls’ Generation, but singers with Billboard Top 30 records to their names — the clearest beachhead SM had ever held for a Western campaign.

And the grief was just as real, sitting in both their throats like a fishbone. The creative halo, the industry prestige, the long-tail revenue of the biggest Korean-market songs of the year — all of it orbiting a young man who had once been THEIRS to debut.

“Back then — with Ji-ho — did we bite down too hard on the splits?”

Kim Young-min finally said it out loud.

Lee Soo-man sighed.

The talent had been obvious inside these walls from the first month. He’d been willing to solo-debut the boy himself. But the company had priced the contract the way the company always priced contracts — hard terms on publishing, hard terms on ownership — and the signing had collapsed. Add the Taeyeon relationship, and the exit had written itself. Men like Park Jin-young, men like Ji-ho, were never going to live inside SM’s rulebook — and SM did not break its rulebook for individuals. If Taeyeon weren’t the boy’s girlfriend, even the OST collaboration would never have happened. And who could have modeled THIS: two songs from a summer romance film, delivering the answer sheet no optimist had dared write. The songs were now several times more famous than the film. And SM’s role in the miracle: a vocal-supply platform. Meat on the table, chopsticks in someone else’s hand — for two men accustomed to holding every pair of chopsticks in the industry, a uniquely indigestible sensation.

Worse still: EXO’s comeback stood on the calendar, and nothing in the vault sang better than “Growl.” If they wanted a Ji-ho song NOW — they’d be paying Ji-ho’s current market rate for it. Which had just gone vertical.

“It’s late to say any of it.” Lee Soo-man’s voice recovered its working calm. “What matters is now. Taeyeon and Tiffany’s renewal negotiations — the original plan is dead. All of it gets redrawn.”

And that was the real, immediate problem. The first Girls’ Generation renewal had always been a war with many fronts: each member repriced on seven years of contribution, future popularity, solo potential. Taeyeon — leader, first vocal, enormous solo upside. Tiffany — image, fluent English, the obvious international lane. Both were already priority retentions with leverage. NOW they held a Billboard hit apiece — nuclear-grade chips — and behind them, teams whose confidence had never been higher. They’d want more than money: resource priority, solo autonomy, a voice in team scheduling. And seven other members would be watching every concession.

“They’ll lead with it at the table. Obviously.” Kim Young-min rubbed the bridge of his nose, face heavy. “Signing bonus, splits, resource tilt, solo control — and if we move too far for those two, the balance across nine becomes unmanageable.”

Lee Soo-man rose, paced to the window, and stood with his back to the room, watching the ant-stream of traffic below.

“Yes. Balance.”

He turned back, and the eyes had resharpened.

“For now — we consider how to extract maximum value from the accident. One: PR and artist management, coordinated — ‘Girls’ Generation’s vocalists conquer the Billboard,’ saturation coverage, push the team and company brand to the ceiling. Two: renewal strategy adjusts — for Taeyeon and Tiffany, sweeter individual-development commitments: concrete solo timetables, overseas support. But the core holds: no equity incentives, no shortening of the long bind. Three—”

A pause; slower:

“Make contact with Ji-ho’s side. Congratulations first — the film, the songs. Goodwill on the record. Perhaps — a future door. After all: Taeyeon is his girlfriend.”

Kim Young-min nodded. The only rational play. This industry had no enemies and no friends — only interest, compounding.

“And one more,” Lee Soo-man added, with a thin edge of chill. “Probe Taeyeon’s current depth of feeling for him. If necessary — the relationship itself is leverage. I know her temperament: if it’s real, she will not risk exposure damaging HIM. As for Tiffany — she needs reminding that this success is an unrepeatable accident. Ji-ho is TAEYEON’S boyfriend, not hers. Her career rests on this platform, and on the team’s halo. She should hold tight to what actually holds her up.”

Grace in one hand, pressure in the other. The house style, unchanged since 1995.

✦   ✦   ✦

July 20, 2013. Cube Entertainment’s Apink — after a member departure and a year and a half of silence — returned with the mini-album Secret Garden, and the title track “NoNoNo”: signature freshness, healing melody, a spring of clear water dropped into a summer chart ruled by body heat.

Cutting a road through with PURITY, of all weapons.

First hour: “NoNoNo” airdropped into third place across Melon, Genie, Bugs. And above it, unmovable for nearly twenty days, the two songs that had been suffocating the industry all month.

One writer. Three styles. Three artists. The entire real-time podium.

The network detonated on schedule. Seven of NAVER’s top ten live searches carried his name.

Communities · the Top 3 sweep

#1 INSANE. Actually insane! The whole podium! A solo OST, a duet, a girl-group title — all three!!

#2 Last week we were discussing him conquering the Billboard. This week he came home to rule the peninsula. Dimensional strike.

#3 The rule now is “trust-listen Ji-ho.” If Ji-ho made it, it’s certified.

#4 My husband makes EVERYONE he touches famous!

#5 What are the SM and Cube chairmen feeling right now? One regretting his life choices, one laughing himself awake at 3 a.m.?

#6 Apink hit the JACKPOT. Commissioning Ji-ho was the best decision that company ever made. “NoNoNo” is pure brain-glue!

That last comment was the whole story. For Apink, “NoNoNo” was never just a hit — after the member departure and the long silence, this comeback had been a back-to-the-river battle: lose it, and the once-anointed rookies-of-the-year sank for good. Instead: the “healing anthem” label inside a week, the old fandom re-secured, a flood of general listeners craving something clean — and above all, the name on the credits, which in July 2013 was itself the loudest guarantee in the industry. The song delivered against the impossible expectation, joined its two siblings on the podium — and Apink’s name was welded to the center of the “Ji-ho phenomenon.” Explosive attention on a scale they had never once known.

THIS was rewriting fate.

Inside Cube, the mood ran opposite to SM’s top floor in every particular: the chairman’s phones ringing from dawn — advertisers, program bookers, overseas partners — every promotional resource in the building re-aimed at Apink at maximum tilt, a once-in-a-decade window and everyone in the company knew it. Online, the group’s story flipped overnight: no longer the team facing dissolution, but the lucky ones Ji-ho CHOSE — Cho-rong’s milk-soft voice, Bo-mi’s variety spark, Eun-ji’s pipes, Na-eun’s face, Nam-joo and Ha-young’s energy, all suddenly re-examined under floodlights. They had caught fire.

The critics, meanwhile, had run out of vocabulary. Seoul Sports ran the column under a headline that said everything: “A One-Man Dynasty: Ji-ho Defines the Summer 2013 Chart War.” — “We are watching something without precedent: one creator, in three registers — pop love-song, soul duet, healing fresh-pop — through three different performers — Tiffany, Taeyeon, Apink — holding the entire chart by himself. This is what the word genius is for.”

And the mid-size agencies began to circle, several of them arriving with numbers that could only be described as unhinged, all wanting one thing: his name on their artist’s next comeback.

✦   ✦   ✦

The eye of the storm, meanwhile, was on Jeju, shooting a pictorial with Sulli.

Break time; soft sea wind; unreasonable sunlight. She scrolled the news and finally couldn’t stand it, scooting close with her eyes glittering.

“Oppa. The entire internet is saying you’re the god of the music industry now.”

He gazed out at the blue horizon and smiled with tremendous serenity. “God, no god. The songs are good, people like them. It’s that simple.”

Tch. There it is. The performance. She knew her Oppa down to the studs: charitably, “humility” — accurately, he LOVED this bit. Strike the whatever-it-was-nothing pose, then stand there soaking in her admiration like a lizard on a warm rock. And she DID admire him, enormously — which was exactly why she declined to feed him today.

So she clasped her hands behind her back and began a slow, pausing retreat, nodding gravely.

“Ohh. I see~ It really IS simple, then.”

…That’s it? He watched the placid little face and experienced system error. Incorrect. This girl was supposed to be dizzy with awe by now, wrapped around his arm, cooing. Where was the cooing?

“Ahem. Peaches.” He cleared his throat. “Actually — this — it isn’t something just anyone could—”

“HEEHEEHEE—” She detonated into laughter. “Oppa! You couldn’t HOLD it! Hahahaha!”

“Oh, you DARE play me, little fox—”

“Nyeh nyeh nyeh~ Oppa, come catch me then! Hahaha!”

And just then his phone buzzed — Lee Hyun-woo, terse as ever:

[MESSAGE · Lee Hyun-woo]  Inquiries surging on all fronts, far past forecast. SM senior management has signaled, through private channels, a desire for “bygones-be-bygones, deep cooperation.” How shall I respond?

He read it, and typed back exactly one line: “Decline politely — cite the new album schedule.”

What arrives too easily is never valued. Let it season.

✦   ✦   ✦

The same week, Girls’ Generation wrapped two tour stops in Taipei and flew home.

Incheon International, arrivals hall — and both women’s steps actually checked at the wall of bodies waiting: flashes strobing, fans screaming, a scale beyond any homecoming they’d ever had. Lenses shoved nearly to their faces; hands thrusting letters and gifts; security forming a moving wall.

“Tiffany! Congratulations on breaking the Billboard Top 30 — how does it feel?!”

“Taeyeon-ssi! The collaboration’s being called ‘soul resonance’ — any studio stories?!”

“How do you rate producer Ji-ho’s talent — the man holding the entire Top 3?!”

“Word is SM wants deeper cooperation with Ji-ho-ssi — will you two bridge it?!”

“Taeyeon-ssi! There are rumors the songs came to you BECAUSE you’re dating — any comment?!”

Tiffany kept the full-sweetness smile up, thanked the fans, credited the songs and their maker, and slid past everything personal with a professional’s footwork. Taeyeon said nothing at all, head down, walking — no confirmation, no denial, no oxygen. Which, to every lens present, read as exactly one thing: the shy face of a woman whose relationship had just been named out loud. The flashes went hysterical over every micro-expression.

In the van, the manager was already gushing through the schedule explosion — solo endorsements, magazine covers, luxury-brand events, seventy-plus percent of the inbound queries naming only two members. The other seven were happy for them. The other seven were also human.

✦   ✦   ✦

Back at the dorm, the air ran delicate.

Yoon-a sat cross-legged on the rug, hugging a cushion, chewing snacks she couldn’t taste.

“Wah — genuinely, completely daebak,” Sunny said beside her, clicking her tongue. “This attention is basically ‘Gee’-era. No — bigger. This one’s internationally certified.”

“Beyond daebak.” Yuri leaned in, envy entirely uncamouflaged. “Fany and Taeyeon did SO well. …Ah, I’m jealous. That’s the BILLBOARD.” A beat; the practical wrinkle arrived. “Wait — with their solo schedules stacking up, how do we even coordinate full-team practice now?”

Soo-young came in from the kitchen with drinks and picked it up, voice dropping. “Scheduling’s the small problem. The real one — did you notice? The inbound offers are seventy, eighty percent for those two. And the company’s — tone has shifted.”

The living room went briefly quiet, and the girls traded glances with everything unsaid moving underneath. The envy was real — who doesn’t want the summit? It was the purest validation a singer could hold. But right behind it: the low-grade dread of comparison, the sense of a precision ship’s ballast shifting. There was a reason SM had always engineered popularity-balance across the nine. Jealousy wasn’t a character flaw; it was physics. Teams held together in famine and cracked in feast — resources, positions, line distribution, income splits, all of it.

And of the seven, two carried extra cargo. Sunny, who had stood in that study and mocked him to his face — a middling singer with delusions of her uncle’s altitude — and now watched his talent flatten every market it touched, watched Tiffany rise to a height no one in the team had ever reached, ON HIS ARM. The thing she’d condemned had quietly changed shape: no longer just an affair, but a fusion of feeling and colossal career gravity. Whatever else he was, his promises were no longer big talk. And Yoon-a — whose popularity had always run one-two with Taeyeon’s — now watching Tiffany’s Western surge reflect home until it edged past her own. All of it sourced from one man. The feeling of a best friend’s man being THIS good was its own strange vertigo: joy for her sisters, real and loud — and underneath it, unbidden, her private bar for an “ideal partner” ratcheting upward — talent, light, the power to lift the people beside him into the sky — and the twin irritations of having the thought and knowing she’d had it. Even her verdict on his two-boat conduct had gone quiet and complicated. At minimum: he had genuinely, massively delivered for both eonnis.

A good sister suffering, you ache for. A good sister thriving — thriving past you, thriving until you audit your own road and the person beside you on it — that feeling had no clean name. Neither of them spoke of the line Tiffany had crossed. It was a secret between best friends now — a seed of unknown species — and under the sunlight of this much success, it had begun to grow in both their chests at once. They were beginning to understand her choice: not love blinding the eyes, but a whole-life calculation, feelings and career weighed together.

Taeyeon was summoned back to the company by Kim Young-min that afternoon.

And Tiffany’s heart had already left the building entirely — flown south, replaying one steamed-over bathroom on a loop: pride and adoration compounding until they physically would not sit still inside her. She grabbed her bag.

“I’m going out. Don’t wait up tonight.”
