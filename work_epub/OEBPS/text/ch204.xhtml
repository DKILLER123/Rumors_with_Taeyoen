Chapter Two Hundred Four

For Now

June 4–5, 2013 · deep night The villa — every room of it room assignments, a steamed-up bathroom, and three whispered conversations in the dark

The same women who’d been throwing eye-daggers an hour ago were now piled into one weeping, singing, laughing heap — toasting the old days at full volume, fully feral.

This was the point in the evening where a wise man exits the frame.

He rose and slipped out without a sound.

Back in the study, he steadied himself and started sorting the second half of the year.

First: the film — June and July burned on promotion. Behind it, the comeback album: as the movie’s heat crested, pivot it into album pre-heat on the theme song’s tailwind. Planning manager Lee Hyun-woo had never stopped working the channels — running his socials and fan cafés, stoking the film and OST, and “accidentally” leaking that a new album was in the works, keeping the fandom’s kettle just below boil. August: recording, MV, parallel promo. September: release, music shows — and the OTHER thing. LOEN. Somewhere in November, if the machine held, he might exhale.

That was work. Then — the women. After LOEN: receive Red Velvet cleanly, keep Ji-eun steady on the branch. Yoon-a — proud, high standards; step one was quietly foreclosing the Lee Seung-gi timeline, then the long game. And Jiyeon—

He rubbed the bridge of his nose.

T-ara, post-scandal: center of gravity moved to Japan — the group reality show, Japanese singles, a tour, coasting on accumulated fuel. The Japanese market hadn’t blacklisted them the way home had, but the slope was still down: record sales sliding, corporate bookings thinning. The ordeal had ground most of the arrogance off the members — but “humbled” was not “at peace.” Having stood that close to the summit and been thrown off it, several of them burned hotter than ever. Jeon Bo-ram and Qri were fine — but Park Hyo-min, Ham Eun-jung, Jiyeon: the ambition in those three hadn’t died, it had compressed. And when Ji-yeon watched Apink detonate off one association with him— the window would open on its own.

The phone rang. Taeyeon.

Voice Call · Taeyeon ♥ · background: chaos, off-key singing

“Annyeonghaseyo~ Delivery please, more alcohol downstairs~ thank you-uu~” — maximally drunk, maximally sticky, tongue two sizes too big.

Click. She’d hung up on him. He looked at the phone and laughed.

He came back down to a battlefield. Jessica and Yoon-a were fused into one corner of the sofa, heads together, murmuring. Yuri was excavating for surviving bottles, rump skyward. Sunny sat cross-legged on the rug, grinning warmly at empty air. Tiffany hugged her knees in the armchair, gaze unplugged. And Taeyeon lay poured across the big sofa, pink from hairline to collarbone, watching the ceiling with her lips parted — expression best described as happily uninstalled.

Several gazes drifted toward him.

“Where’s the alcohol?” Yuri demanded.

“House is dry. You drank ALL of it.” He spread his hands. “No more bottles. Look at yourselves — sleep.”

The hour was late, the party half-dissolved; sending them home in this state was out of the question. The villa had rooms to spare — and exactly two sets of fresh guest bedding.

“Sica and Yoon-a — one room. Sunny and Yuri — the other.” He ran the assignments. “Taeyeon and Fany take the master. I’ll take the sofa.”

At the word “bed,” Taeyeon achieved verticality on the third attempt; Tiffany caught her, and the two of them staggered toward the master bedroom in a mutual-support formation. The rest dispersed, listing at assorted angles, toward their rooms.

He stayed and reset the living room. By the time the last bottle and bag were cleared and the floor wiped, the water sounds from the far rooms had stopped one by one.

He knocked at the master bedroom. Nothing.

Taeyeon’s tolerance was decorative; she’d have flatlined on contact with the mattress. Tiffany was probably in the shower. His sleepwear was in that closet — and the sofa wasn’t going to warm itself.

Two more knocks. Silence. He eased the door open.

One dim bedside lamp. Taeyeon lay on her side facing the door, scrubbed clean, dead asleep — skin milk-pale in the low light, lips their natural faint pink, slightly pursed, breath long and even with the smallest thread of a snore. Deep under.

He couldn’t help it: he crossed over and pressed the lightest pinch into her cheek. Warm, soft, ridiculous. She didn’t stir — nose twitched once, burrowed deeper into the pillow, gone.

He smiled without sound and turned toward the closet — and the bathroom door clicked open behind him.

Tiffany came out toweling her hair, wrapped in a bathrobe — HIS bathrobe, oversized on her, slipping loose at the collar. She startled hard at the shape in the room, clutched the lapels — recognized him, exhaled, and pulled the collar closed anyway.

“Mianhae. Came for my sleepwear — I did knock.”

He explained it plainly enough. His eyes, less plainly. The robe swallowed her, which somehow made the slightness of her more explicit; steam-flushed skin at the open collar; the trademark eye-smile currently wet-lashed, startled, and shy — a combination with measurable effects.

“It’s— it’s fine.”

She dropped her gaze, fingers worrying the robe’s belt.

The air went suddenly specific. On the bed: Taeyeon, defenseless, deep asleep. Two steps away: Tiffany, bath-warm, robe-wrapped, peach-flushed. He felt his throat tighten — and discovered he was in no hurry to leave.

He walked toward her. She read something in the approach and backed up — one step, two — until her shoulders met the cool wall, nowhere left to go.

“What— what are you doing?” Her voice shook slightly; her eyes flicked once, guiltily, to the sleeping figure on the bed. “Taeyeon’s RIGHT there—”

“Heh.” A low laugh. He stopped in front of her and set one hand against the wall beside her ear, boxing her neatly between arm and wall. “What could I possibly be doing?”

He leaned down, breath skating the flushed shell of her ear.

“Obviously — taking a shower.”

And he pushed off the wall, stepped past her, and walked into the bathroom.

She stayed against the wall, heart slamming. For one full second she’d been certain he was going to— She exhaled, relieved. And then, unbidden and unwanted, right behind the relief: one hollow drop of disappointment.

She sat down on the bed’s edge, looked at Taeyeon’s sweetly oblivious face, and the war resumed. After a moment she reached out and rocked the sleeping shoulder gently.

“Taeyeon? Taeyeon-ah?”

Nothing. Long, even breathing.

“Taeyeon-ah… mianhae—” Her voice dropped to a wire’s width, thick with guilt. “Jinjja mianhae. I know. I’ve wronged you. I’m a terrible woman.”

“But I really— I can’t refuse him.”

“And technically— I met him first, you know… and you KNOW what I’m like. I’m— a face-fanatic. It’s a medical condition.”

The joke convinced no one present, including the only conscious person in the room.

She was mid-confession to her unconscious best friend when a thought struck her; her face changed; she yanked the robe’s collar out and looked down inside it.

Empty. EMPTY. The clothes she’d changed out of — including one specific set of black lace — were sitting in the bathroom laundry basket. WITH HIM IN THERE. If he saw those—

Decorum lost to crisis management. She crossed to the bathroom and tapped the frosted glass, voice pressed flat and low.

“Ji-ho? Are you— have you started washing? Can I— come in for one second?”

Through the water: level, unbothered. “Come in.”

She bit her lip, turned the handle, and slipped in sideways.

Steam everywhere; the temperature several degrees of dangerous. He stood by the shower head in nothing but black briefs — the full clean architecture of him unhidden, wide shoulders, narrow waist, watching her come in with visible amusement.

The image had concussive force. Face: instantly scarlet. Mouth: instantly dry.

She kept her eyes bolted to the floor tile and speed-walked to the corner basket, snatched up the black lace set, balled it into her palm, turned—

A warm hand closed on her wrist.

Her whole body locked.

“Song— Ji-ho?”

She pulled. The hand didn’t move. He drew her in instead — one easy motion — and she collided with the heat of his chest with a small cry, the balled-up lace tumbling loose to the tile, black on white, screaming.

“Ah!”

She dove to retrieve it; his arm was already around her waist, fixing her in place against him.

“Nuna.” Low, at her ear, breath hot on the damp skin of her neck. “Mianhae. The LOEN work has eaten every hour lately. I’ve neglected you.”

Her knees filed for leave. The oversized robe had gone looser in the struggle — shoulder, collarbone, a long line of steam-pinked skin.

His hand slid in along the open collar — palm scorching against her cooler skin, moving slowly, deliberately, with unmistakable intent.

“The Fair Trade Commission requires SK to cut below twenty percent by September—” he narrated it against her ear, conversational, while his fingers traced her shoulder and she shivered in his grip. “So this window — I have to take it.”

She was melting and furious about it: business briefing as seduction, and it was WORKING.

“If it lands—” his lips brushed her earlobe, “—I’m planted in this industry at the root level. And then I can give you the life you actually want. Nuna. What do you think?”

“Ji-ho— don’t— not like this—”

The resistance was tissue-paper. She turned her wet, fogged eyes up at him, the crescent eyes swimming with shyness and struggle — and played her last card.

“Taeyeon— Taeyeon’s right outside—”

Which achieved the exact opposite of its stated purpose.

His eyes went darker. He bent and took her half-open mouth — and reached past her in the same motion and spun the shower valve.

Warm water crashed down over both of them — hair, faces, the thin fabric between skin and skin. The robe soaked through in seconds and clung, near-transparent.

The heat of the water, the heat of him, the leftover soju, and one sleeping best friend two walls away — all of it compounding into a vertigo neither of them steered.

Cold tile at her back; scalding warmth at her front. She twisted her mouth free, voice gone pleading and wet.

“Not— tomorrow, there’s tour rehearsal tomorrow— please—”

She caught his wandering arm with both hands, half a sob in it now. “After— after the tour, okay? I’m— I’m yours sooner or later anyway—”

His kisses had moved down the wet line of her neck to her collarbone. He lifted his head, breathing rough, and pressed one more question into her ear, relentless.

“What Yuri said earlier. Was it true? You and Taeyeon nuna — the film committee—”

“NO! It’s not— I never—!” The denial arrived at maximum velocity and maximum blush.

“Nuna.” He pinched the soft of her waist, mock-punitive. “Lying is naughty.”

“Mmh—” Her brows knit; her eyes fled to every corner of the steam; and finally, in a voice barely bigger than the water: “…Watched. Some.”

“Tonight I can leave you untouched,” he murmured — and then said several more words against her ear, in pure breath.

Her eyes flew open to full aperture. She glared at him — wet, scarlet, comprehensively outgunned; the glare carried all the deterrent force of a kitten’s. The steam had erased the mirror entirely. Only the shower kept talking.

She bit her lip, face at combustion temperature, braced her back against the slick tile — and, under his gaze, very slowly, very reluctantly, sank down—

The rest belongs to the water sounds, and to a mirror fogged too thick to testify. This night, sleep was a scarce resource in several rooms of the house.

The next-door guest room, meanwhile, held two open pairs of eyes in the dark.

“Eonni.” Yoon-a broke the silence softly. “What are you actually going to do next?”

A long pause; then Jessica’s sigh. “I don’t know. First — the tour. Properly.”

“Honestly— I understand you.” Yoon-a rolled onto her side to face her. “We all need our own plans. We can’t be ONLY Girls’ Generation forever. But eonni—” her voice gathered, careful and serious, “—everything we are today, the single most valuable label either of us owns, is still GIRLS’ GENERATION. We bought that name with our entire youth. All nine of us, together.”

Her hand found Jessica’s under the blanket and touched it lightly.

“Whatever you build — don’t lose the name doing it.”

Jessica said nothing. But her hand turned over and gripped Yoon-a’s, hard — two slender hands locked under the covers.

That’s everything a sister can say, Yoon-a thought, sighing inwardly. She’d come tonight to settle a birthday grudge — and instead been broken by Sunny’s opening question, mocked to her face about her acting, and then had the whole revenge agenda derailed by the Sica crisis. Vengeance: unexecuted. Dignity: net negative. Total defeat.

And still — the curiosity outweighed the grudge.

“Eonni.” She couldn’t help it. “Why HIM, though? Your brand is fashion — what can Ji-ho actually do for you?”

Behind Jessica’s eyes: eleven zeros, briefly luminous.

“Yoon-a-yah. He is — possibly the most successful person I know. And the one most capable of actually helping me.”

“He’s talented, sure — songs, scripts, all of it. But BUSINESS—”

“Yoon-a.” Jessica cut in, tone gone complicated in a way that resisted parsing. “I can’t say more. Just know this: he is far, far more formidable than you think. And — far more complicated.”

A pause. Quieter:

“Also — keep the relationship good, but keep your DISTANCE. He is… not a good man.”

Yoon-a’s brows drew in. Maintaining ties with a talent like that — obvious. But “not a good man” — from SICA? And the image resurfaced, unbidden: Fany eonni’s lips tonight, slightly swollen, color suspiciously fresh — the look of a kiss hastily repainted.

“Eonni. Do you — KNOW something?”

Behind Jessica’s eyes: a doorstep in the moonlight, an ambitious gaze, an invitation inside. Her voice dimmed.

“Nothing specific. Just — think about it. That face. That talent. Probably that MONEY, too. In THIS industry — do you honestly believe he sits home faithfully guarding only Taeyeon?”

“And Taeyeon’s a homebody with a simple heart — completely captured. She couldn’t police him if she tried.”

She turned her head and looked at Yoon-a’s lovely face in the dark.

“So: be careful around him. That’s all.”

At some point the water in the master bath finally stopped.

The door opened a crack; Tiffany’s head emerged first, scanning. Taeyeon lay exactly as before, sweetly, obliviously asleep.

The relief nearly buckled her. She turned, flashed a frantic SHHH at the man toweling off behind her, then bolted — bare-footed, wrapped in nothing but urgency — dove under the far edge of the blanket, cocooned herself with her back to the bathroom, and initiated an aggressive impersonation of sleep.

He came out in sleepwear, took in the rigid burrito at the bed’s edge and the starfish sprawl of Taeyeon occupying two-thirds of the mattress, and shook his head, smiling. He pulled a spare thin blanket from the closet.

Passing the bed, he bent to one flushed ear and murmured:

“I’ll dry your clothes and bring them in.”

The ear moved. Its color deepened by one full stop.

He laughed without sound, took his blanket, and let himself out.

✦   ✦   ✦

The living room was clean and quiet. He lay on the sofa, wide awake.

The currents inside Girls’ Generation ran better than he’d feared — nine years and a Black Ocean will weld people — with only Jessica’s course steepening toward the rocks. And his own board: the LOEN endgame, Ji-eun’s management, Yoon-a, Ji-yeon — no light lifting anywhere on it.

In the second guest room, two more voices in the dark.

“What do you think — what will Sica actually DO?” Yuri, small-voiced.

A sigh, threadbare. “You know her. Soft on the surface, mule underneath. Once she’s decided — nine oxen can’t drag it back.”

“Then what do WE do?”

“Us?” Sunny’s laugh had no humor in it. “What CAN we? Back her — and answer to the company with what? Block her — and spend nine years of sisterhood doing it? We walk one step, we watch one step. And hope she figures it out herself. Or that the road bends.”

“And if— if she really does break with the company? What happens to— to Girls’ Generation?”

The question was too heavy for the hour. The dark held it a long time.

“Sleep, Yuri. Stop thinking. Tomorrow—” a breath, “—we’ll see.”

Both of them knowing: sunrise dissolves no problems. It only lights them better.

In the master bedroom, Tiffany lay listening to Taeyeon’s long, even breathing beside her, her own thoughts a saltwater knot. She touched her lips — still faintly numb, faintly swollen, still carrying the ghost of— And then she turned her head and looked at the sleeping face an arm’s length away, and the guilt came down like cold surf, filling every cavity.

“Mianhae, Taeyeon—” she breathed at her sleeping friend, barely voiced. “But I— I really can’t control it— I like him so much—”

“And I really— can’t refuse him.”

And precisely then, Taeyeon smacked her lips in her sleep, mumbled a syllable belonging to no known language — and blindly, unconsciously, reached out, wrapped both arms around Tiffany’s arm, pressed her cheek against her shoulder, nuzzled once, and sank contentedly back under.

Tiffany went rigid — and then her eyes flooded so fast it physically hurt.

She didn’t pull the arm back. She leaned in closer instead, closed her eyes, and let the whole unresolvable tide roll through her chest in the dark.

Deep night. The villa finally, fully quiet — only wind in the courtyard leaves.

Moonlight crossed the floor with geological patience, silver climbing the wall, thinning, and folding at last into the larger dark.
