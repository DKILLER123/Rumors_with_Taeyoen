November Seoul, the first true cold sliding in off the river.

In the waiting room at M Countdown, he sat with his eyes closed and let the makeup artist work. The face in the mirror scarcely required the help — the high clean bridge of the nose, the cut line of the jaw.

“Ji-ho-ya. Nervous?” Dong-hwan came over with the day’s run sheet in hand.

He opened his eyes. The mirror returned a gaze clear and deep as well-water.

“Not nervous.”

The makeup artist paused, powder puff mid-air, unable to help herself.

“Ji-ho-ssi, your features are honestly— too perfect. The eyes and the nose especially. And the line of the lips—”

He answered with a small smile and let it pass.

“Five minutes!” A knock rattled the waiting-room door.

✦   ✦   ✦

The stage went dark. The screaming grew louder.

He stood on the lift platform, hand tight around the microphone. The intro began — solo piano, unaccompanied, clear mournful notes falling like rain — and the platform rose slowly into the light.

White shirt. Black trousers. The simplest costume in the building, made singular by the face above the collar.

The audience went silent for exactly one heartbeat. Then the screaming redoubled.

M Countdown · “Eyes, Nose, Lips” · comeback stage

Eyes closed, mic raised, he opens on the song’s brutal first plea — the one that begs the departing lover to skip the apologies and make the ending swift and merciful — and the hall goes stone silent on the first phrase.

What kind of voice IS that? Clear, gentle, torn at the edges — every turn, every switch of breath seamless, like grief that has studied perfect technique.

In the control room, the PD’s eyes go wide at the monitor wall. “Real-time ratings are climbing — 3.2… 3.5… 4.1 percent! Still rising!”

On stage he is somewhere else entirely. The performance is not singing so much as a controlled spill of feeling — three minutes and forty-nine seconds of it, until the last note thins into the air and is gone.

He bows, breathing hard. Three full seconds of silence. Then applause and screaming come down like a tsunami.

Social media detonated on schedule. #SongJiho_EyesNoseLips went straight to the top of the trending board. What did I just witness? Are that face and that voice allowed on the same person? That is top-tier vocalist technique, full stop.

The Solbeam chant nearly lifted the roof off the studio: “You are our morning sun — light up the world!”

Backstage, he waited out the count with patience. G-Dragon and Psy were serious opposition — but “Gangnam Style” was deep into the back end of its promotion cycle. He liked his odds.

When the announcement came, even the host’s voice carried a tremor.

“This week’s number one on M Countdown is—”

The numbers spun up the big screen and froze.

“Ji-ho — ‘Eyes, Nose, Lips’! Total score: 10,078!”

Nearly a thousand points clear of second place.

His eyes went wide — a flicker of what looked like genuine surprise — and that one small expression set off a fresh wall of screaming. He stepped forward and accepted the trophy with fingers that trembled slightly.

“Th-thank you—” The mic carried it out across the hall. “Thank you to everyone who supported me. Thank you to my fans, for everything. Thank you to the company. And thank you to… every single listener.”

Custom required an encore from the winner. He didn’t repeat the full arrangement — he simply nodded once to the band leader.

encore · “Eyes, Nose, Lips” · stripped rearrangement

No production. One piano. And precisely because there is nothing else on the stage, the voice carries the entire weight — improvised turns through the bridge, dynamics handled like glasswork, the song’s grief cut a full shade darker than the studio version.

The encore fancam passes a million views on YouTube within two hours.

Over the next six days, the peninsula’s music shows weathered what the boards had already named the Ji-ho Storm.

Friday, Music Bank. He appeared in a black velvet suit, and the ruined look in his eyes broke hearts on camera by the thousand. First place: 9,872 points — more than 1,300 clear of second.

Saturday, Show! Music Core. A stage stripped to a single spotlight and one white piano. He sang the whole song seated — and stood on the final chorus as every light in the rig came up at once, a visual detonation that pulled an audible gasp out of the hall. No first-place ranking at Music Core that season — pure promotion, pure statement.

Sunday, Inkigayo. The last of the three networks, triple-crown cap in force. He brought the week’s most carefully designed staging, and posted a first-place score of 10,523.

Every day the stage changed — different costume, different arrangement, different emotional angle. What never changed was the crushing margin, and the fact that nobody could look away.
