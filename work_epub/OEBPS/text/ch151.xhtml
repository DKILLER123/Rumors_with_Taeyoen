Chapter One Hundred Fifty-One

You’re Not a Hallucination — You’re Really Ji-ho?

The same night · Hong Kong A karaoke room → the hotel suite two women come home early

In the corner of the karaoke room, Seohyun’s small face had gone apple-red with drink, and she was leaning against Tiffany’s shoulder.

“Eonni… why do you seem so far away tonight?”

Tiffany’s eyes were half-lidded, languid, her answer arriving soft and slow.

“Hyun-ah. Let’s go back.”

Seohyun sat up straight, openly startled.

“Mwo? We’re skipping round three?”

“Skipping it.” Tiffany pushed herself upright and pressed two fingers to her temple. “Taeyeon called me half an hour ago — told us to have fun. But she’s alone at the hotel, and she’s definitely bored. I’m… worried about her.”

Taeyeon eonni, the nation’s champion homebody, bored? Unlikely. But they’d all had about enough to drink anyway — one more round and it would be a problem. Seohyun nodded.

The two of them rose to leave — and the disappointment around the table was immediate. It was only round two; the night was barely warmed up.

“Ya — it’s just getting started! Leaving already? You wound me.” Kim Hee-chul, half-joking, all theater.

Tiffany said nothing at all. She walked to the table, filled a glass with the strong stuff, tipped her head back, and drained it in one motion.

“Oppas — congratulations on your awards. And I’m sorry for tonight. Hyun and I can’t drink any more.”

Then she took Seohyun’s hand and headed for the door — leaving Hee-chul standing there with the distinct expression of a man whose joke had landed harder than intended. He hurried after them, apologetic.

“Tiffany, Seohyun — sorry, sorry, I was only teasing. Don’t take it to heart.”

She stopped, turned, and gave him a small smile.

“It’s fine, Hee-chul oppa. I really am just worried about Taeyeon being alone. Go have fun.”

In the car, the night wind slipping past the windows, Tiffany — who had drunk that last glass far too fast — sagged against Seohyun’s shoulder and mumbled into the dark: “Daedae… we’re coming back…” Seohyun sighed. That’s what you get for showing off with liquor that strong.

✦   ✦   ✦

In the suite’s bathroom, the steam was just settling. Two silhouettes separated.

He shivered once — November air found wet skin fast. Taeyeon managed to get the shower running again, and the warm water rinsed away the thin sweat and everything else the last hour had left behind.

Washed and dried and dressed again, she stood with her back to him, fingertips combing order back into her hair. Then she took a small bottle from her bag — the Secret Garden “Magnolia Poem” he’d given her — twisted the cap, and pressed two drops into her palm, quiet as a signature. White magnolia and soft milk warmth unfolded into the air.

He was already dressed. He wrapped his arms around her waist from behind, chin settling on her shoulder, breath warm at her ear.

“Nuna. I’m heading out.”

A light kiss landed on her earlobe.

She turned, raised the hand that carried the perfume, and laid her palm gently along his cheek — fingertips tracing the clean line of his jaw. Then she went up on her toes and kissed him, soft and deliberate.

He blinked once — and answered it. Her hands slid over the bare skin at the back of his neck, behind his ears, and locked there.

Ji-eun. This scent is my signature — mine, Taeyeon’s. I hope you understand what it means. And walk away on your own.

Beep—

The key-card reader chirped at the door.

Taeyeon jolted like a live wire, shoved him back, voice dropping to a panicked hiss.

“Quick! HIDE!”

His eyes swept the room once and locked onto the heavy floor-length curtains. One sideways step and he was gone. The fabric swayed — then stilled.

She took one deep breath, forced her face into order, and hurried to the door.

It swung open on Seohyun, laboring under the weight of a very unsteady Tiffany.

“Taeyeon eonni — help me, quickly.”

Seohyun’s pale cheeks were flushed with effort, a fine sweat at her temples.

“How did she get THIS drunk?”

Taeyeon rushed in to take the other arm, heart aching at how badly Tiffany was listing.

“Tiffany eonni was worried you’d be bored alone at the hotel — she wanted to come back early to keep you company. Hee-chul oppa got a little sulky about it, said it was a rare gathering — so eonni just downed a full glass of the strong stuff as an apology.”

“Mwo? Kim Hee-chul!” Her eyes went round with fury. “He is DEAD.”

They maneuvered Tiffany inside together — and passing the entryway, Taeyeon’s peripheral vision snagged on a pair of extremely conspicuous men’s sneakers. Her heart stopped. She angled her body between the shoes and Seohyun without breaking stride and steered them toward the bed. One fast scan of the room: curtains motionless. Nothing visible. She let out a breath only she could hear.

They settled Tiffany onto the bed.

“I’ll get water.” She crossed to the entryway at speed, yanked the shoe cabinet open in passing, buried the offending sneakers in its deepest shadow, and collected two bottles of water off the shelf like that had been the entire mission.

Back at the bedside she handed one bottle to Seohyun, cracked the other, and gently lifted Tiffany’s head.

“Tiffany. Tiffany-yah. Drink some water.”

“Mmm— it’s our Daedae~” The eyes opened halfway; the trademark eye-smile surfaced through the fog, voice gone soft and sticky. “I came back to keep you company~”

“I know. Good girl. Open.”

A few swallows in, Tiffany rolled half over, buried her cheek in the pillow, and mumbled:

“Where is this…?”

Taeyeon delivered one exasperated, affectionate swat to her rear.

“Where do you THINK? The hotel!”

“Ya — I TOLD you not to drink too much, and you come back in this state.”

“Ah!” A small yelp; a wriggle; and then, mumbled half into the pillow: “Because I was happy… because Ji-ho won the rookie award… because he said I was beautiful…”

The last words dissolved below hearing.

Taeyeon froze mid-motion, then crouched close.

“Mwo? What did you say?”

But Tiffany only giggled at nothing and said no more.

Taeyeon stood, hands on hips, a small crease between her brows. Priority one: extract Ji-ho safely. Her gaze fell on the thoroughly drunk Tiffany — and a plan assembled itself.

“Hyun-ah.” She caught Seohyun mid-sweat-wipe. “Come out with eonni to buy hangover medicine?”

“Mwo?” A blank blink. “Eonni, I JUST got back—”

“Aigoo — so you’d let me go out alone this late at night?” She tilted her small face up, pink skin near-translucent under the lamps, round eyes deployed at maximum innocence — every inch not the older one in this conversation.

Seohyun regarded this performance and sighed in surrender.

“Haa… understood, eonni. Let’s go.”

“Mm! Thank you, Hyun-ah!” Her volume rose noticeably, addressed to a certain somewhere in the room. “We’re going out for HANGOVER MEDICINE now!”

Caps, masks, careful disguises — and the two of them were gone.

Click.

✦   ✦   ✦

The door settled. From behind the curtain, a head emerged with great care.

He confirmed the room held only one very drunk Tiffany, then eased out on cat feet.

Nuna, ah, nuna. You trust me far too much. If I were even slightly a worse man— he shook the absurd thought off and crept toward the entryway.

Where he stopped dead. Where are my shoes?

He crouched, rummaging around the low cabinet. Ah. Found them.

On the bed, Tiffany rolled over. Through the fog, something dark seemed to move at the edge of the room. She struggled upright, bleary eyes sweeping the suite. Empty.

“…Ghost?”

She mumbled it, and climbed unsteadily off the bed. From the shoe cabinet came a soft creak. She wove her way over — three sways to a step — and found a figure just straightening up.

“Who… are… you?”

Her soft voice carried with unreasonable clarity in the quiet room. The figure went rigid. Then turned, slowly, wearing a rueful smile.

“Nuna.”

“Oh? It’s our Ji-ho~ hehe.”

Recognition landed; the eye-smile bloomed; she took one delighted step forward — and her ankle folded sideways.

He caught her by the waist before she hit the floor.

“But… why would Ji-ho be here?” She tipped her flushed face up at him, eyes swimming. “Is this… a hallucination?”

She laughed at herself, small and bitter, and went boneless against his chest, the words spilling out drunk and wounded.

“Hwang Mi-young, you really are hopeless… too scared to ask Daedae, too scared to reach for him… can’t forget him, can’t move first…”

“So now… the only place I get to see the person I want to see… is a hallucination?”

He listened in silence. From the outside, his rise had been impossibly fast — trainee to headliner in half a year. But from the inside, for him and for everyone whose hearts were tangled up in his, everything was still too slow. Too slow to let him hold Taeyeon with one hand and offer Tiffany the truth with the other.

“Why won’t you say anything?” The voice went smaller, more aggrieved; her fingers closed on his collar. “You only dare talk to me on Kakao? Because of… Daedae?”

“Ani—” She shook her head at her own question and buried her face in his chest. “Of course hallucinations don’t talk… ha. Look at me…”

A few seconds passed. Then her head came up suddenly, eyes rimmed red.

“Do you know how awful you are?”

“Why did you have to be that handsome? Why did you have to write songs like that? Why did you have to SAVE me?”

“Why — why — WHY!” Her grip on his collar tightened; her voice climbed. “Take away any ONE of those — just one — and I could have forgotten you!”

“Instead I get to watch you burn brighter and brighter — and weigh more and more in here—”

“Today, when you said I was beautiful — I was SO happy. Did you know that?”

She stared up at the face — the deep-set eyes, down the straight line of the nose, landing at last on the mouth that always seemed to carry the beginning of a smile. Too handsome. Want— And if this is a hallucination anyway… then one kiss doesn’t count. Does it? The thought caught like wildfire.

She hauled his collar down and kissed him.

Soft lips, tasting faintly of alcohol — and for one full second he stood frozen. Then the familiar sweetness of her wrapped around him — warm woody rose over something bright as California sun — and his eyes closed, and he answered it, nearly on instinct.

The kiss deepened by degrees — from a question to an argument to a surrender. His hands found her back and gathered her in; hers lost themselves entirely. She drifted until cold wall met her spine — cool tile at her back, warmth everywhere else, and no strength anywhere in her body to object with.

“Mm—”

The alternating shock of cold and heat shivered through her — and burned off half the alcohol in one pass. Her eyes flew open. She shoved him back hard.

“You— you’re not a hallucination— you’re really Ji-ho?!”

“It’s me, nuna.” And he leaned in again.

She turned her face away, the fog in her eyes clearing into shock.

“How are you HERE? Unless— you and Taeyeon—”

“Mm.” No denial at all. “Taeyeon nuna and I are together.”

“MWO?!”

Fully sober now. She had guessed — of course she had guessed — but hearing it confirmed out loud put a sudden ache at the bridge of her nose, and her eyes went hot without permission. And then, right behind the hurt: fury, and the sheer absurdity of it.

She shoved him again, voice shaking.

“You absolute bastard! You’re with Taeyeon — so why did you kiss me?!”

“Nuna kissed me first.” Calm as a court record.

She choked — then flared hotter.

“Fine, I kissed first — you could have PUSHED ME AWAY! Why did you kiss back?!”

He looked at her reddened eyes, her trembling mouth, and answered without a flicker.

“Why would I push you away? I like you too, nuna.”

“MWO?! Shibal—!” Her hand came up to slap him; his caught her wrist, steady.

“Nuna is this beautiful. This lovely.” Entirely unashamed. “Is liking you supposed to be wrong?”

“I—” Her thoughts scattered like startled birds. Beautiful? Lovely? Him liking her did sound… not-wrong… no, wait—

“But you’re DATING Taeyeon! How can you like me at the same time?!”

“Which is exactly why I’ve worked so hard to keep my distance from you.” A step closer; the wall at her back; his shadow over her. “Tonight, nuna — YOU crossed the line.”

I… crossed the line? She stared at him blankly — and apologized on pure reflex.

“…I’m sorry—”

“Don’t apologize.” Something that looked like real pain crossed his face; his voice dropped. “Nuna. I know better than anyone — the heart doesn’t take orders.”

“Don’t be too warm, or you startle them. Don’t be too cold, or you push them away. Keep the conversation to topics they love, and never one step further—”

“It’s like raising a wild animal inside your chest. You feed it every single day. And you never, ever let it make a sound.”

The heart… doesn’t take orders. Every word of it landed somewhere she already lived. Because the worst pain was never failing to win someone. It was surrendering the right to try — voluntarily, quietly, and every single day.

“Nuna. I’m sorry.” A bitter smile; he released her hand. “I’m going.”

He turned and walked to the door without looking back.

She stared at his back — at his hand closing on the door handle — and something in her chest tore open. The impulse surged up her arm: grab him. Call his name. Her hand rose. Her voice died in her throat.

Click.

The door shut, gently.

She slid slowly down the wall until she was sitting on the entryway floor, arms wrapped around herself, face buried against her knees. The sound of crying, small and strangled, rose into the empty suite.

✦   ✦   ✦

Outside, he did not leave right away.

He leaned back against the cold door, listening to the faint sound from inside, and closed his eyes.

From here, all he had to do was keep climbing — brighter, bigger, more undeniable. Tiffany would walk the rest of the road herself. Her best friend’s happiness; the perfect almost-hers man — envy, pride, the arithmetic of comparison. Forces like that moved people. Using them made him feel exactly like what he probably was: a thoroughgoing bastard. But who doesn’t love beautiful women? Who doesn’t want more than they’re allowed? Handed a second life — was he supposed to let go of everyone he loved who loved him back? No. He simply wasn’t standing high enough yet.

Time. It always came down to time. LOEN — next year’s window — HAD to be seized. Settlement money next month. Then the Wealth Robot goes live.

He drew one long breath. The corridor lights stretched his shadow out ahead of him. He straightened his collar and walked to the elevator, footsteps fading down the silent hallway until they were gone.

And inside the room, the crying went on for a long, long time.
