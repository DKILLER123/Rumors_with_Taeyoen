Chapter One Hundred Forty-Nine

Good Little Sister Sulli

The same night · after the final award HKCEC — backstage corridors & the parking level prize-splitting, exit strategies, and a master schedule

The 2012 Mnet Asian Music Awards was, when you stripped the lights off it, an exercise in carving the roast so that every table got a slice.

MAMA 2012 · WINNERS BOARD (SELECTED)

01 Best Male Artist — G-Dragon

02 Artist of the Year — BIGBANG

03 Song of the Year — Psy, “Gangnam Style”

04 Best Female Artist — IU

05 Album of the Year + Best Global Male Group — Super Junior

06 Best Global Female Group — KARA

07 Best Female Dance Group — f(x)

08 Best Female Group — SISTAR

09 Best OST — Seo In-guk & Jeong Eun-ji, “All for You” (Reply 1997)

10 Best New Male Artist + Best Collaboration — SONG JI-HO (“Some,” with IU)

Best Male Artist to G-Dragon; Artist of the Year to BIGBANG. BIGBANG was legitimately great — but measured by the sheer planetary reach of “Gangnam Style,” nobody could have argued if Psy had swept Artist AND Song of the Year. Instead, Psy left with Song only. Best Female Artist went to IU — and he was happy for her, truly, while privately noting that BoA remained the rightful heavyweight in that bracket; the gap was still real. But MAMA had company arithmetic to balance, and BoA no longer needed trophies to prove anything. So Super Junior took Album of the Year and Global Male Group. KARA took Global Female Group. f(x) took Female Dance Group; SISTAR took Female Group; the Reply 1997 duet took the OST.

Which meant Taeyeon and TTS went home from every single nominated category empty-handed. Grant that the TaeTiSeo sub-unit couldn’t match full-group gravity, and Global Female Group to KARA was defensible — but on Best Female Group, TTS’s album numbers should have carried it. Prize-carving, though, is prize-carving: a slice for you, a slice for me, everyone leaves technically fed.

Taeyeon’s smile had gone visibly effortful. A full night of applauding other people — with IU collecting the heavyweight Best Female Artist on top of it — sat in her chest like a swallowed stone.

✦   ✦   ✦

As the ceremony wound into its final countdown, he glanced left, glanced right, and began running scenarios like a man defusing ordnance.

Taeyeon: shut out all night, morale at the seabed, urgently in need of boyfriend-grade consolation — plus his rookie award deserved celebrating. IU: Best Female Artist in hand, mood at orbital altitude, and if tonight ended in a confirmed relationship, that was career and love harvested in one evening — a life peak. So: when the lights came up, what exactly was the plan?

His brain spun at redline. Near the finale, Jackie Chan appeared as the closing presenter alongside Han Chae-young to hand Artist of the Year to BIGBANG — the film legend was here principally to walk his Chinese Zodiac cast across the MAMA stage for the movie’s promotion. Hong Kong cinema royalty carried enormous weight on the peninsula; Leslie Cheung, Jackie Chan, Andy Lau — superstars of the first magnitude, all.

And looking at Jackie Chan, Ji-ho suddenly had his perfectly unimpeachable excuse.

✦   ✦   ✦

The curtain fell. Artists rose in waves, funneled by staff toward exits and afterparties. The backstage corridor thickened with congratulations, small talk, and lowered voices.

He held the rookie trophy and swept the crowded corridor once, fast.

Left: IU, ringed by well-wishers, glowing, her glance escaping toward him at regular intervals. Right, farther off: Taeyeon with Tiffany and Seohyun, smile fully retired now, tired and elsewhere — and every time her eyes crossed the celebration around IU, the corner of her mouth pulled down a millimeter before she caught it. Tiffany opened her mouth to comfort; a small headshake closed it.

Two weather systems, closing from two directions. The minutes right after a ceremony are the dangerous ones.

On cue, a staffer with a VIP lanyard approached with a small bow, addressing him, IU, and the Girls’ Generation members nearby.

“IU-ssi, Ji-ho-ssi, Taeyeon-ssi, Tiffany-ssi, Seohyun-ssi — there’s a private VIP celebration in the hotel’s Sky Lounge shortly. We’d be honored if you’d attend.”

IU’s eyes came straight to him, message legible at fifty meters: she did not want to go. Her plan for tonight’s summit did not involve a room full of executives.

She tugged his sleeve lightly, voice low and languid, weighted with implication.

“Let’s… skip that one? I’m a little tired.”

He ran the numbers at speed. The lounge would be wall-to-wall top-tier names and CJ leadership — networking gold. But if he went, IU would attach herself to him in front of everyone; and if Taeyeon also went— no. Private venue, uncontrolled variables, unacceptable risk profile.

He assembled a regretful expression, gave the staffer a courteous half-bow, then answered IU at deliberately increased volume — enough to drift, oh-so-incidentally, to a certain listener not far away.

“Honestly, I’m worn out too. And—” a beat; his gaze slid, apparently idly, toward the knot of people still orbiting Jackie Chan, “—I’ve been a fan of his films since I was a kid in LA. This chance won’t come again. I want to go pay my respects and ask for a photo. A formal party — I’d just stand there being stiff anyway. Let’s pass.”

Airtight. The man’s films had raised half the planet’s kids, Koreatown’s included.

IU weighed it — a flicker of disappointment at not extracting him immediately, but “no party” served her primary objective, and she could hardly stand between a fan and THE Jackie Chan.

“Then… all right. I’ll wait for you in the car?”

“Go on ahead and rest. I won’t be long.”

He said it easily — and in the same motion, half-turning, caught Taeyeon’s incoming glance mid-air. One blink. A signal dispatched: contact soon.

Her eyes shifted, fractionally. Message received.

She turned to Tiffany and Seohyun, who were still debating the lounge, and said, wearily:

“Let’s just skip it. I’m tired — I want to get back early.”

Step one: complete. He watched IU depart toward the parking level with her assistant — three backward glances in two hundred meters — while Taeyeon and the TTS members folded quietly into the departing stream on the other side. Pressure halved. Temporarily.

He did not go straight to Jackie Chan. Instead he found an unremarkable stretch of wall, pretended to straighten a cufflink, and watched the crowd around the star thin at its own pace. Patience. The alibi had to be real — which meant the alibi had to take TIME.

✦   ✦   ✦

The dedicated corridor to the parking level ran wide and cool and half-empty, the ceremony’s roar already a memory behind the concrete.

IU walked light and quick, the Best Female Artist trophy snug against her ribs, assistant half a step behind.

Around a corner — her step checked.

Ahead: Taeyeon, flanked by Tiffany and Seohyun, manager in tow, coming the same way. Expression at zero, head tilted toward whatever Tiffany was saying.

Two lines of sight met in the middle of the corridor.

IU’s smile brightened several stops. She quickened her pace, delight arranged perfectly across her face.

“Taeyeon eonni! Your gown tonight was SO beautiful!”

Beside her, Tiffany’s brow creased by a degree. The ceremony had just ended. Complimenting the DRESS, to a woman who’d gone home empty-handed — there was a blade folded into that silk.

Taeyeon raised her head. The smile she produced was procedurally correct.

“Thank you, IU-ssi.” Nothing readable in the voice. “And congratulations — Best Female Artist. Genuinely impressive.”

“Ah — this?” IU glanced down at the trophy as if noticing it for the first time, smile deepening, tone arranged into feather-light modesty. “Luck, mostly. I still have so much to learn from my sunbaes.”

“Especially you, eonni — Girls’ Generation’s record is the summit we hoobaes measure ourselves against.”

And as she said it, her gaze drifted — weightless, deniable — across Taeyeon’s empty hands.

Taeyeon appeared not to register the subtext at all. She nodded gently, and returned a needle wrapped in cotton of her own.

“Awards are a moment. What matters is making the music you love for a long, long time, and being remembered by the people who love it. Your popularity is tremendous right now, IU-ssi — the road ahead is long. I hope you can hold on to your first heart the whole way.”

Gentle as warm milk — with seniority and longevity folded quietly inside.

IU’s smile did not move except to sweeten.

“Ne, eonni’s exactly right. I’ll keep it in mind.”

She stepped in closer, as if to speak more warmly still—

—and a thread of scent rode the corridor’s air current straight to her: white magnolia over a soft milky warmth, lush and clean at once. Familiar. Distinctly, specifically familiar. Her pupils tightened by a fraction of a millimeter. A woman’s nose does not forget perfume — and she had caught this exact note before, faint, on the collar of Ji-ho.

Thought moved at light speed; the smile never flickered. She inhaled, casually appreciative.

“Eonni, your perfume tonight is so unusual — it’s lovely. What brand is it? I don’t think I’ve ever smelled it before.”

Taeyeon’s body stiffened for half a second, imperceptibly. It was the magnolia fragrance HE had given her — “suits your baby-faced elegance,” he’d said — worn tonight on purpose, meant to be noticed by exactly one person after the ceremony. She had not budgeted for THIS nose finding it first.

Recovery was swift. A strand of hair tucked behind one ear.

“A gift. From someone very important to me. It’s a small niche house — you wouldn’t know the name.”

“Is that so?” A blink; the gaze rested one extra beat on Taeyeon’s carefully still face. “Funny — I had the impression Ji-ho likes fresh scents in that same family. Ah — just thinking out loud.”

She let one hand rise, covering a small laugh, as though the name had slipped out by accident — while her eyes stayed locked on target.

“Are you eonnis heading straight back to the hotel? You’ve worked hard tonight. Rest well.”

Seamless and jagged at once: perfume, Ji-ho, and tonight’s “hard work” all threaded onto a single silk string and offered with a bow.

Something tightened under Taeyeon’s sternum. The smile held. Barely.

“Mm. Back to rest. You rest early too, IU-ssi — tonight must have been… very satisfying.”

“Ne. It’s all thanks to the fans that I could receive an award like this.”

“Well then — eonnis. Goodnight.”

IU dipped her head, smiling, finally released the eye-lock, and continued past with her assistant.

In the half-second of passing, that milky magnolia note wrapped close one more time — and she filed it away, sealed and dated.

Taeyeon stood where she was, watching the white gown recede, fingertips curling slowly into her palm.

“Taeyeon-ah…” Tiffany’s voice came carefully.

“Let’s go.” Level. Already walking.

“Hee-chul oppa’s calling us to the celebration.”

“Celebrate WHAT? Nobody won anything. I’m going back to the hotel.”

✦   ✦   ✦

Timing: about right.

He steadied his breathing and walked toward Jackie Chan — arriving just as the orbit around the star happened to thin to a pair of assistants.

He stopped at a respectful distance and bowed.

“Hello, seonsaeng-nim — sorry to interrupt. My name is Ji-ho.”

The film legend turned — and finding an unreasonably good-looking young man with a settled air and a sincere smile, gave him a genuinely warm one back. An assistant leaned in and murmured the briefing: this year’s MAMA Best New Male Artist; Korean-American; massive in both Korea and the Chinese market; the duet stage and the rookie award tonight both went viral in real time.

“Oh? Ji-ho! Hello, hello! I saw you from the floor earlier — full of energy! Congratulations on the award!”

“Thank you, seonsaeng-nim!” The deference was real, and so was the shine in his eyes. “I grew up in Los Angeles on your films — every VHS the video store had. Your movies were half my childhood. Meeting you at MAMA is an honor I didn’t expect.”

“Haha, too polite, too polite.” A wave of the hand, entirely at ease.

“And the new film — Chinese Zodiac.” The pivot came earnest and specific. “I don’t think it’s just an action-adventure. The story it tells about finding and protecting cultural heritage — bringing lost treasures home — that means something. I’ve been following the release news. I’m genuinely looking forward to it.”

That landed squarely home. The man had poured years and real conviction into that film — and here was an influential young artist who understood precisely what it was FOR. The smile widened into something personal; the look he gave the young singer acquired real warmth.

“I’m very glad you see it that way!” A hand clapped his shoulder, friendly and solid. “A lot of heart went into this one. I hope people connect with it.”

“It’s going to land. I’m certain.” Then, with a fan’s perfectly reasonable shyness: “Seonsaeng-nim — could I trouble you for one photo? I’d like to post it, so more of my friends and fans know the film is coming.”

Admiration expressed, promotion volunteered — an easy yes.

“Of course! Come!”

He bent immediately into a humble half-bow of a pose; the legend threw an arm around his shoulders like an uncle at a reunion. Phones and cameras came up on both staffs at once.

Chinese Zodiac’s box office would be what it would be — but the film’s larger campaign around repatriating looted heritage was real, and lending it a shoulder cost him nothing and meant something. This was promotion he was happy to do. And, incidentally: the meet-the-idol alibi had now consumed a fully documented, fully unimpeachable block of time. IU should be just about impatient. Taeyeon should be just about alone.

He straightened his jacket and walked toward the parking level, pace steady.

✦   ✦   ✦

The parking entrance ran dimmer than the corridor, air layered with gasoline, dust, and the far-off breath of the city’s night wind.

He was mid-calculation on run-of-show — which stop first, how long, exit lines for each — when a bright shape bounced out from behind a pillar and seized his wrist.

“Oppa! I’ve been waiting FOREVER — quick, quick, quick~”

Sulli — cheeks flushed pink with running and excitement, unfairly pretty in the half-light, delight entirely uncamouflaged.

“Sulli? You didn’t leave with the members?”

“Leaving is BORING!” She released his wrist, clasped both hands together at her chest, and tipped her face up into his space — berries and faint florals and pure girl-shaped trouble. Her signature smiling eyes crinkled to crescents, lit underneath with expectation. “You just won the rookie award, oppa. Shouldn’t we celebrate properly? Just us two!”

The last words dropped to a softer register — coaxing, sugared, aimed.

Looking down at that face — near enough to count lashes, engineered by nature for exactly this kind of ambush — something in his chest did go soft. His hand rose on autopilot and rumpled her hair. Excellent texture, as always.

“AH!! Oppa!” She sprang back a step, protesting at maximum cuteness, hands flying to repair the damage, mouth in a small pout. “I hate that! It took forever to style!”

“Really hate it?”

“…Aniya.” The voice dropped eight full tones; the cheeks went pinker. “I like it.”

She did like it — liked what it meant: that with him she was special, the treasured little sister. Or — not-only-sister. Preferably not-only.

She caught his sleeve again and swung it gently, voice gone soft as rice cake.

“So, oppa. Tonight. Let me be the one who celebrates with you. Yes? I know a place, it’s perfect—”

He nearly folded on the spot. But the mental split-screen intervened: IU’s triple backward glance en route to her car; Taeyeon’s held-together face. The clock was merciless and he could not be in three places at once—

—unless. A thought fired, fully formed: redirect. This particular front could be converted from a threat into a diversion IN ONE MOVE.

He let apology fill his face, turned his hand over, and patted the back of hers gently.

“Sulli-yah. Tonight I really can’t. I… already have an appointment.”

The light in her eyes visibly dimmed; the whole small face fell.

“Mwo-ya… with WHO? More important than me?”

The vinegar in it went entirely unnoticed by its own speaker.

“Someone important.” He left it vague — and then, watching the slump deepen, the soft spot won a concession, and his tone turned conspiratorial. “BUT. When we’re back in Seoul — oppa has a surprise prepared for you. Deal?”

“A surprise?” Attention: successfully redirected. The eyes re-lit. “What surprise? Tell me NOW, oppa!”

“If I tell you, is it still a surprise?” He shook his head, smiling — then, seeing the residual sulk, dropped the suggestion in like it had only just occurred to him. “The Hong Kong skyline’s right there, and you’re never here. Why not grab Ji-yeon and Hara and go play? Aren’t you three always saying you can never sync schedules? Tonight they’re all in one city.”

The idea struck home instantly. Right! Oppa was first choice, but the girls were the OTHER source of joy — Hara eonni, Ji-yeon, and Ji-eun, freshly crowned and absolutely requiring congratulation. All of them, one city, one night. It would be CHAOS. The good kind.

Gloom evaporated. The phone was already out.

“Oppa’s right! I’m calling them NOW!”

Goo Hara: free, in immediately. Jiyeon: free, in immediately. Next: Ji-eun.

Voice Call

“Sulli-yah? What is it?”

“Ji-eun-ah!! CONGRATULATIONS on Best Female Artist, you’re incredible!! We’re all going out to celebrate you — Hara eonni and Ji-yeon already said yes, you’re the only one left! Come! We’re in HONG KONG!”

“Ah… Peaches, I’m a little tired, I was going to rest early—”

“Nooo~ don’t be like that! Just a LITTLE while? We haven’t played together in forever! You won a HUGE award, how can you not celebrate? And me and Hara eonni got prizes too, so it’s three celebrations in one, pleeease, Ji-eun-ah~ you’re the best~”

In her dim van, IU pressed two fingers to her temple. Her entire evening had been architected around one man’s arrival and the precise calibration of how far tonight should go — straight to the summit, or one more scenic switchback first. And there was that perfume note, still itching. She wanted to refuse. But Peaches and Ji-yeon were her actual best friends… and — wait. She could bring Ji-ho ALONG. First exposure of the almost-boyfriend to the inner circle: a vaccination, administered early. Cost-benefit resolved itself in half a second.

“Honestly — what am I going to do with you… fine. Send me the address. I’ll come over in a bit.”

“YAY! Knew it — Ji-eun’s the BEST! Waiting for you!!”

Sulli hung up glowing.

✦   ✦   ✦

Shortly after, his phone rang. He stepped aside to take it.

“Ji-ho. Peaches just called — the girls are taking me out to celebrate.”

“Come with me.”

He loaded his voice with exactly the right surprise.

“You’re all celebrating together? That’s funny — I was JUST with her. She invited me first, and I turned her down. Because of our appointment.”

“Mwo?” A crease formed between IU’s brows. What was this sequencing? Sulli had called HIM first — and her second? Was she the backup plan? Did Peaches have designs on him too? …Well. Obviously the man she’d chosen was excellent; of course others would circle. And he HAD once saved Sulli outright. Still — Sulli-yah. You’re one step too late. This one is spoken for. — Meanwhile, the fact that he’d declined Sulli FOR THEIR APPOINTMENT was deeply satisfying. And the fact that she herself had failed to decline, out of best-friend obligation… guilt, small and prickly.

“Sorry. Sulli kept doing the voice. It’s unrefusable — you know the voice.”

“Should I cancel? Or — just tell her our thing fell through, and we BOTH go?”

“I just turned her down; walking in with you now would look strange,” he said — and then rerouted, tone upgraded to selfless. “Besides: it’s a girls’ night. One large male suddenly at the table is a whole atmosphere problem. You just won a career-defining prize — celebrate it with Hara and Sulli and Ji-yeon. That’s exactly who tonight belongs to. We have… plenty of time.”

“I’ll go see a friend meanwhile.”

Reasonable. Generous, even. And yet — her lips pressed together, and her gaze drifted through the tinted glass into the parking level’s dim recesses. A friend. Taeyeon’s van… had it not JUST pulled out? The thought pricked like a fine thorn. Fine, she decided, eyes cooling behind the sweet voice. Then let me verify.

“Understood~ then go do your things. Don’t drink too much. I’ll walk the harbor with Peaches and the girls — three hours, probably.”

She lowered the phone and spoke to the front seat.

“Oppa. Take me to the address Sulli sends.”

Sulli, meanwhile, had skipped off to regroup with f(x) first — Victoria-eomma required notification before any midnight adventures. A proper Hong Kong night with her best friends; the official afterparty could happen back in Seoul.

He pocketed his phone and squared his collar.

Three hours. Taeyeon first, then the rotation. He allowed himself one small congratulatory smirk at his own logistics.

What he did not allow for was this: no woman survives to the top of that industry by being simple. The Master of Time Management was, at that exact moment, cruising serenely toward the edge of a cliff.
