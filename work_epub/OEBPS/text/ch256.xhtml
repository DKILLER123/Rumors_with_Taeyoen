Chapter Two Hundred Fifty-Six

The Music Video Lead

Morning Ji-ho’s hillside villa breakfast for two, an acquisition rumor, and a role designed to turn rejection into attention

Jiyeon rinsed after brushing her teeth and discovered that Ji-ho had already left the bedroom.

She followed the smell of breakfast into the open kitchen: browned butter, crisp toast, and eggs just beginning to set at the edges.

Ji-ho stood behind the island transferring a fried egg to a plate. He had changed into a loose white T-shirt with the sleeves pushed to his elbows. Morning light laid a thin band of gold across his shoulders.

Jiyeon leaned against the doorway and watched.

A chairman who cooked his own breakfast.

He looked up and caught her staring.

“Another minute.”

“Mm.”

She took a stool at the island. The eggs had crisp edges and soft centers. Toast, cut fruit, warm milk, and coffee completed the table.

“Why don’t you hire a housekeeper?”

“I’m not accustomed to staff inside my home. Besides, I rarely eat here.”

Jiyeon bit into buttered toast. It was excellent.

After spending the night in his arms, however, eating across the island felt strangely formal.

Too far away.

She set down the toast, lifted her plate and milk, and walked around to his side.

Before Ji-ho could ask, she settled sideways on his lap and leaned against him with the solemn confidence of someone correcting a seating error.

“I’m eating here.”

Color touched her face, but she did not retreat.

Ji-ho laughed, circled one arm around her waist, and used her fork to offer a small piece of egg.

Jiyeon accepted it. The soft yolk melted over the crisp edge.

“Good.”

He followed with a slice of apple.

She raised the warm milk to his mouth in return. A pale trace remained on his upper lip. Jiyeon reached to wipe it away, and he caught her fingertip long enough to press a teasing kiss against it.

Her face reddened instantly.

She tore off a piece of toast and fed it to him in retaliation.

Breakfast became a slow exchange of bites and quiet laughter. By the time the plates were empty, nearly half an hour had passed.

Jiyeon still showed no intention of leaving his arms.

“Full?”

“Mm.”

Ji-ho carried her the short distance to the sofa rather than return her to the hard stool. She looped both arms around his neck on instinct.

“What are you doing?”

“Finding a more comfortable chair.”

He sat with Jiyeon curled against him. She tucked her face beside his neck and breathed in the familiar clean scent.

“You smell good.”

“Then stay there.”

She kissed the line of his jaw and immediately hid again.

“Why?”

“I wanted to.”

Ji-ho held her with one arm and reached for the investigator’s file with the other.

When Jiyeon saw it again, the first shock had passed. Anger remained, but it had become focused.

“You found the messages. When can we release them and clear the group?”

Ji-ho kissed her forehead.

“Not yet. The file answers one question and creates another.”

Jiyeon looked up.

“What question?”

“CCM almost certainly knew Ah-reum had been threatened. Even if the investigator’s organized-crime lead were true, Kim Kwang-soo had enough industry leverage and CJ support to contest it.”

“Publishing verified messages early could have disrupted the bullying narrative. Why did management choose delay, contradictory statements, and silence instead?”

Jiyeon frowned and searched her memory.

Kim Kwang-soo was not incompetent. His career had included major successes—from Turbo and Kim Jong-kook’s solo rise to building T-ara from an unstable concept into one of the most commercially dangerous girl groups outside the largest agencies.

Yet during the crisis, nearly every public decision had worsened the damage.

The Two Theories · And Why Neither Holds

Later generations would hand down two main conspiracy theories about this chapter of history.

The first: the Troll’s backing ran deep. The parachuted-in debut, the untouchable temper — both signs of a hand above her holding the umbrella.

The second: CJ had offended Madam Park, and Lee Mi-kyung had gone to ground in America to wait it out. CCM, as a subsidiary, became the nearest thing to kick.

Did the CJ feud ripple onto T-ara? Certainly. There would have been opportunists currying favor with the Blue House, and opportunists kicking a body already on the ground. But it could never have been Madam Park herself, or anyone in her orbit, aiming at T-ara specifically.

You think being a top girl group makes you untouchable? Are you bigger than Bong Joon-ho? Than Song Kang-ho?

Madam Park spoke one sentence to her Minister of Culture, and nine thousand names went onto a blacklist. Nine thousand. Bong Joon-ho and Song Kang-ho — men of that stature — could not get off it. Lee Mi-kyung was squeezed until she left her post.

If the Blue House had truly wanted T-ara destroyed, it would have needed no bullying scandal. One line on one list, and the group would not have been “boycotted” — it would have quietly ceased all activity, disbanded with bows, and gone off to honest work.

Instead: T-ara kept appearing on music programs. The group split in two — one unit dispatched to Japan, N4 kept at home — still releasing albums, still releasing new songs. No official pressure anywhere in sight.

And more: if a President were crushing T-ara, which SM artist would have had the guts to stand up publicly for them — again and again? TVXQ, Girls’ Generation, Super Junior, all of them repeatedly speaking up for T-ara while the Blue House ground it down? They would all have to be insane. So would SM’s board.

“Has anything unusual happened at the company recently?” Ji-ho asked. “Anything involving CJ?”

“Maybe.” Jiyeon thought carefully. “A few days ago, several people visited. The person leading them was a young woman, and staff said she was a CJ Entertainment executive.”

Ji-ho’s eyes sharpened.

“A CJ executive?”

“There are rumors CJ wants to sell CCM. Sajang-nim has been inviting CJ executives to dinner constantly.”

“He wants to buy the company?”

“That’s what everyone says.”

Something shifted into place.

Ji-ho stared past the file, assembling possibilities.

JI-HO’S WORKING HYPOTHESIS · NOT PROVEN FACT

Did the crisis lower CCM’s value for an internal acquisition?

Corporate pressureCJ’s political conflict creates uncertainty and encourages disposal of vulnerable entertainment assets.

Market hostilityThe T-ara controversy destroys revenue, weakens CCM’s financial statements, and reduces the subsidiary’s sale price.

Information asymmetryManagement may possess evidence capable of challenging the public narrative while outside buyers do not.

Potential incentiveAn insider who acquires CCM cheaply could later release evidence, restore part of T-ara’s value, and retain the recovery upside.

Alternative explanationsPanic, legal caution, poor crisis management, fear of external retaliation, internal division, or simple incompetence remain possible.

No document in Ji-ho’s possession proves deliberate sabotage. The theory must not be circulated or treated as fact without corroboration.

The political explanation alone had never satisfied him.

CJ’s conflict with the Park administration could certainly have made partners cautious and encouraged opportunists to attack. But if the government itself had chosen to blacklist T-ara, there would have been no need to rely on a fan-driven bullying scandal.

In the novel’s alternate political timeline, thousands of artists and filmmakers faced formal exclusion. Directors and actors far more powerful than T-ara were vulnerable. Direct retaliation would have looked like cancelled investment, withdrawn broadcasts, and an official wall—not a group continuing to appear on Music Bank, promote through subunits, and work in Japan.

That made the ownership rumor more important.

Ji-ho’s future memory supplied the rest of the pattern: by late 2013, Kim Kwang-soo would gain control of CCM within the story’s timeline. T-ara would quickly return with “Number 9,” tentative efforts to revisit the scandal would begin, and the group would later pursue China with extraordinary success.

Was that merely the recovery plan of an executive who finally obtained freedom?

Or had the collapse itself helped make the purchase possible?

Ji-ho looked down at Jiyeon.

“I have a theory. Only a theory.”

He explained it piece by piece: CJ’s instability, CCM’s falling value, the evidence management may have held, and the financial advantage available to an insider who could buy before a recovery.

Jiyeon listened with widening eyes.

“You think Sajang-nim let us be destroyed so he could buy CCM?”

“I think the incentive exists. That is not the same as proof.” Ji-ho’s voice remained firm. “Do not confront him. Do not mention this to staff. We verify ownership discussions, timing, internal communications, and who ordered the evidence withheld.”

Her gaze fell.

“We trusted him. We called him sajang-nim and treated him like an elder.”

Ji-ho stroked her hair.

“People are complicated. Some sacrifice anything once ambition teaches them to call it necessary.”

Jiyeon remained quiet, then raised eyes filled with new determination.

“I’m glad you came.”

“Jiyeon-ah—”

“Without you, we might never learn what happened. We could spend the rest of our lives remembered as bullies.”

Ji-ho pulled her closer.

After several breaths, Jiyeon recovered enough to smile.

“But now you’re here. So it’ll get better.”

“That much faith?”

“Of course. You find things no one else can find. You write songs everyone remembers. And…” Her cheeks warmed. “You’re good to me.”

Ji-ho kissed her forehead.

“Then I can’t afford to disappoint you.”

“You said the rumor helped you understand something.”

“It gave me the missing incentive.” He lightly pinched her nose. “That matters.”

Praise brightened her immediately.

“What do we do next?”

Ji-ho considered the album pages waiting in his studio.

“My second album is close enough to begin pre-production. I want you as the female lead in one of the main music videos.”

Jiyeon almost sprang out of his arms.

“Really?”

Then doubt replaced the excitement.

“But Korea is still boycotting me. What if I damage your release?”

Ji-ho laughed.

“The campaign is aimed at the United States and Europe. Domestic hostility is not the only market signal.”

ENGLISH ALBUM · LEAD MV STRATEGY
Male leadSong Ji-ho
Female leadPark Ji-yeon
Primary marketsUnited States · Europe · global digital platforms
Creative objectivePresent Jiyeon as an actress and performer outside the domestic scandal frame
Evidence strategyRelease independently verified material only after the investigation closes remaining gaps

International success cannot prove innocence. It can, however, force audiences to reconsider someone they had reduced to one scandal.

“If the album succeeds abroad,” Ji-ho continued, “Korean coverage will face a contradiction. Some outlets will continue attacking you. Others will recast you as part of a Korean success overseas.”

He disliked the cynicism of the next truth, but it remained true.

“Public pride often changes which facts people are willing to hear. If we release verified evidence while attention is at its highest, guilt and national pride may do what reason alone failed to do.”

Jiyeon pictured it: rejection at home, recognition abroad, then a return carrying results no one could easily dismiss.

Hyuna’s appearance in “Gangnam Style” had shown how one global video could redirect attention toward its female lead. If Ji-ho’s album reached the scale he expected, the same mechanism could operate again.

“And the MV role?” she asked. “What am I playing?”

“The female lead.”

Mischief entered her eyes.

“Is there a kiss scene?”

Ji-ho raised an eyebrow.

“Do you want one?”

Her face turned crimson.

“I’m only asking.”

“There is.”

“Really?”

Her excitement was so immediate that Ji-ho laughed.

“No. It’s a narrative performance, not an excuse to kiss on camera.”

Jiyeon glared.

“You tricked me.”

“We can revise the storyboard if the lead actress insists.”

“Who insists?”

She struck his chest lightly and had her wrist caught for the trouble.

“You’re truly taking this album West?” she asked, recovering. “Asian artists don’t always receive a fair chance there.”

“Nothing guarantees fairness. We prepare until the work is too strong to ignore, then create our own leverage.”

His confidence was not noisy. That made it more convincing.

Jiyeon formed one small fist.

“Then I’ll work hard. I won’t embarrass you.”

“I’ll be counting on you, Ms. Park.”

She kissed his cheek.

“A deposit.”

“When is the balance due?”

“After we succeed.”

“And what does success buy me?”

Jiyeon leaned close and whispered an answer intended for him alone.

Amusement flashed in his eyes.

“Then I’ll have to work harder.”

She looped her arms around his neck and stayed there while sunlight climbed across the room.

“Ji-ho.”

“Mm?”

“Why are you doing so much for me?”

He looked down at her with an expression gentle enough to undo the question.

“Because you’re beautiful.”

Jiyeon blinked.

“That’s your reason?”

“I’ve always been weak to beautiful people.”

She laughed, then found herself crying again.

Ji-ho wiped one tear away with his thumb.

“Don’t cry.”

“I’m not. I’m just happy.”

He held her until the feeling passed.

Later, another concern returned to him.

“Jiyeon-ah. I heard your father hasn’t been well.”

“Majayo. His teeth were bad years ago, so I paid for treatment after I began earning.”

Her expression grew more serious.

“Recently he tires easily and has little appetite. My brother took him for tests. They said his liver enzymes were abnormal and his spleen was enlarged. He was given medication and told to stop drinking and return for monitoring.”

“Then make sure he sees a hepatologist, not only a general clinic. Abnormal liver function plus splenomegaly can have several causes, and improvement after medicine doesn’t mean the underlying problem is gone.”

Jiyeon studied him curiously.

“Why did you suddenly ask about my father?”

“Am I not allowed to worry about my future father-in-law?”

“Future father-in-law?” She made a face. “You’re not marrying me.”

Ji-ho chose silence.

Jiyeon did not appear wounded. She had understood the shape of the relationship before accepting it.

Still, he repeated the medical point.

FAMILY HEALTH REMINDER · PARK HOUSEHOLD

Persistent liver abnormalities require specialist follow-up

Do not assume recoverySymptoms and laboratory values can improve temporarily while chronic disease continues.

Follow-upHepatology review, repeat liver tests, imaging as clinically indicated, medication review, and evaluation of splenomegaly.

LifestyleStrict avoidance of alcohol unless the treating physician says otherwise; no unapproved supplements or “liver detox” products.

The treating medical team—not Ji-ho—must determine diagnosis and medication.

Jiyeon promised she would speak to her brother.
