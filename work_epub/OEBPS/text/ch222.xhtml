Chapter Two Hundred Twenty-Two

Is That All You’ve Got?

July 31, 2013 The riverside apartment · LOEN Entertainment, 12th floor — Finance a payout goes “missing,” a finance director learns about recordings — and the whole building takes sides

Curtains drawn tight against the afternoon, the living room steeped in a warm orange half-light. Afterward, Irene lay curled and boneless against him, hair damp at the temples, entirely spent and entirely unwilling to move. The rest stayed behind the curtains, where it belonged.

“Get me your ID later. I’ll have the lawyers finish the paperwork.”

A small tremor went through her.

She hadn’t even debuted — and she owned a home. She knew perfectly well what accepting it meant: tolerating the other women whose existence she could no longer pretend away. And the refusal simply would not come out of her mouth. It wasn’t just the shock of a man handing over billions of won like loose change. It was the moment on her knees on that warm floor — the surrender that had happened somewhere deeper than the apartment could reach. She could not lose him. Not his brilliance, not the weight of his attention, not the scale of his generosity. Not him.

“Okay.”

Soft as breath.

Good, he thought. Irene was a clever woman — the yes meant she accepted the state of things as they stood. As long as no other woman was paraded in front of her, she would elect not to know. Which made it official: except for Taeyeon, every woman around him was settled. The self-satisfaction of that, stacked on top of tomorrow’s signing, had him practically incandescent.

“Nuna,” he murmured against her ear, “this weekend — call Seul-gi, Seung-wan, Soo-young, and Ye-rim. Dinner, all six of us.”

“Why?” Her eyes cracked open, puzzled. “Are we… telling them about us?”

“Don’t ask. Just do it exactly like I said.”

“Ya— appa—” a squeak, then instant melting, “gently— I’ll do it, I’ll do everything you say, okay~?”

“Call me Oppa.”

“Oppa~”

“Call me yeobo.”

“Yeobo…”

“Mm. Keep going.”

“Yeobo… yeobo…”

✦ ✦ ✦

Ji-ho returned to the company in an excellent mood — which lasted precisely until Ryu Dong-hwan pushed into the studio wearing an expression he almost never wore.

“Ji-ho. Vice President Yoo has… made his move.”

He settled into the desk chair, unbothered. “Go ahead.”

“One: the promo plan for the new album got rejected. Officially, ‘budget too high.’” A beat. “Two: your stylist, your makeup artist, and several of the staff who work your schedules are all being ‘reassigned’ in the next weeks.”

He nodded along. All expected. Choke the resources, break up the familiar team — page one of the standard playbook.

“And three—” Dong-hwan’s voice dropped further. “Your revenue share this month has been suspended. The stated reason: ‘omissions in settlement.’ It needs to be ‘recalculated.’”

The first two hadn’t moved his face. The third lifted one eyebrow.

“Vice President Yoo can reach into FINANCE now?”

“He and Finance Director Seo Dae-ha are both the CEO’s men — close for years.” A bitter smile. “Director Seo’s office… almost certainly got a quiet word.”

Ji-ho was silent a moment. Then he laughed.

Twin blessings. He’d been hunting for the right pretext, and the man had walked over and pressed the knife into his hand, handle first.

“Got it. Hyung, you can get back to work.”

He stood, squaring his shirt cuffs.

“Where are you going?” Dong-hwan’s premonition arrived fully formed.

“Finance.” The corner of his mouth curved, cold and clean. “To ask exactly how my money went ‘missing.’”

“Ji-ho! Don’t do anything—”

But he was already through the door and gone, long strides eating the corridor.

✦ ✦ ✦

Finance occupied the twelfth floor of the LOEN building.

The desk staff blinked at the sight of him, then assembled a professional smile.

“Ji-ho-ssi — how can we help you?”

“I’m here for Director Seo.”

“The director is in a meeting, if you’d like to—”

“I’ll wait in his office.”

He was already moving. The staffer shot up—“Ji-ho-ssi!”—too late; he had knocked once and pushed the door open without waiting for an answer.

Seo Dae-ha looked up from his documents, and his brows crashed together.

“Ji-ho-ssi. Do you not see that I’m busy? Out.”

Instead, Ji-ho crossed to the desk, planted both hands on it, and leaned in, eyes level.

“Director Seo. My revenue share was suspended this month. I’d like the specifics. What exactly was ‘omitted’ from settlement? And when, precisely, will the recalculation be complete?”

The pressure of it stalled Seo Dae-ha for a full second — and then the fury caught. An ARTIST. Warned once already, and instead of apologizing he barges into MY office to interrogate ME? Utterly lawless. Fame had clearly cooked the boy’s brain — did he think the company couldn’t survive without him? Already jangled for days by the looming ownership change, the director went off like a struck match, surging to his feet, finger out.

“And what exactly are YOU, to speak to me like that?!”

The room’s air froze solid.

Outside the door, two finance staffers’ heads appeared, then vanished at speed.

“Settlement procedure is internal company business! We owe explanations to an ARTIST now?! Do your job, and the money you’re owed won’t be one won short! Now get out — immediately!”

Ji-ho smiled.

A faint smile. And for reasons Seo Dae-ha could not have articulated, it dropped the temperature of his blood a degree.

“Director Seo.” He straightened, unhurried, voice gone terribly even. “Just now you asked what I am.”

He took one step forward. Seo Dae-ha stepped back on instinct and hit his own chair.

“I am an artist of LOEN Entertainment. I am a person who generates profit for this company. And you, Director — you are an employee who draws a salary to manage its finances. So between the two of us: who, exactly, owes courtesy to whom?”

“You—!!”

“My share is written into a contract in black and white. You suspended it on the word ‘omission’ — without so much as a formal notice.” His gaze flicked across the nameplate on the desk. “Director Seo Dae-ha. Is that abuse of authority — or misappropriation in office?”

“What are you— that’s slander!” The desk banged under a palm. “Ji-ho! I’m warning you — defamation carries LEGAL liability!”

“Does it?”

He drew out his phone and thumbed open the voice recorder, screen tilted just enough to be unmistakable.

“Then perhaps the director would repeat it for the record. Why exactly was my share suspended? Which line items were ‘omitted’? And how long does a recalculation take?”

Seo Dae-ha’s face went the color of wet paper.

He hadn’t imagined the boy would go THIS far — a recording, in this office. If that audio ever got out, CEO’s backing or not, the public-opinion storm alone would drown him. And of all times — NOW, mid-transition, with unknown new owners inbound.

“You— out. Go. I’ll have it recalculated immediately!”

“Recalculated?” The phone disappeared. The smile returned. “I’ll see the result before end of business.”

“You—” The director’s voice had gone to sandpaper. “What is it you’re actually after? Biting everyone in reach like a mad dog — what does it GET you?”

“After?” A soft laugh. “My own money, Director. Is that hard to understand?”

He turned for the door — and paused with it half open, looking back with eyes like a January window.

“It seems LOEN has grown some woodworms. Eating the company hollow, board by board.”

The door clicked shut, gentle as a verdict.

In the corridor, staffers snapped their eyes to urgent nothing on their monitors as he passed. He walked to the elevator without expression, mind already three moves ahead. Yoo Jae-ha in the open, Seo Dae-ha in the shadow — one flag apiece. Perfect.

Behind him, Seo Dae-ha had collapsed into his chair. Is the boy insane? …But do insane people build résumés like his? And if he’s sane — what’s his ANGLE? Unless— unless the mystery fund buying the company had already reached out to Ji-ho? Of course it would — he’s the golden goose; any acquirer would lock down IU and Ji-ho before signing anything. So the boy is… a KNIFE? An advance blade for the new owners?? Shibal — Yoo Jae-ha, you’ve buried me! Why did I let myself get dragged into this?!

“YA! Who authorized suspending Ji-ho’s settlement??”

“Hm?? Does nobody here want their job?!”

The finance staff exchanged looks of genuine wonder. Me? I suspended it? Did I really?

“What are you standing around for?! Recalculate it and RELEASE it! And draft a written notice — the previous ‘omission’ was an administrative error, with apologies to Ji-ho-ssi!!”

✦ ✦ ✦

The elevator opened; he stepped in and pressed his floor. The phone buzzed once against his palm.

Message · Lee Myung-ha, SHIN & KIM

4:47 PM

Mr. Song — the agreements are finalized. Tomorrow, 10:00 AM, SK Group headquarters conference floor. We’ll be honored to receive you.

He watched the screen dim, and the curve climbed his mouth slowly. Tomorrow. After tomorrow, everything is different.

The doors opened again — onto Ji-eun, who had evidently been waiting for exactly this elevator. She closed the distance fast, voice pitched low.

“You went to Finance? Are you okay?”

“I went to Director Kim Jun-myeon for you — but he was in meetings. Mianhae.”

He looked at the worry in her eyes — the real, unstaged kind — and something in his chest shifted a degree.

“I’m fine.” A pause. “Ji-eun-ah.”

“Hm?”

“After tomorrow,” — his eyes were very deep — “you’ll understand why I did all of this today.”

She stalled, another question already forming — but he was walking away toward his studio.

She stood watching his back, and the feeling arrived with total clarity: this man was standing in the center of a storm. And the storm was almost here. She believed — believed absolutely — that he could work miracles. And she intended to be riding the wind when he did.

✦ ✦ ✦

Within half an hour of Ji-ho leaving the twelfth floor, the story had sprouted wings and toured every corner of the LOEN building.

Trainee floor. The washroom.

“Did you HEAR? Ji-ho sunbae-nim just stormed Finance and went off on Director Seo — at his own desk!”

“Real or fake?” The lipstick hand wobbled. “Director SEO? The famously impossible one?”

“One hundred percent real! My friend’s ON twelve, she saw it! He walked straight into the office and demanded to know why his settlement was frozen — the director’s face went GREEN!”

“Wow… that’s insanely brave… isn’t he scared of getting buried?”

“Buried? With his numbers? The film’s almost at twelve million admissions, he’s got two songs sitting on Billboard — the company’s going to bury THAT?” A shrug. “If you ask me, sunbae-nim has exactly the leverage he thinks he has.”

“But making enemies upstairs… Vice President Yoo’s side won’t just—”

“That’s tomorrow’s problem.” The compact clicked shut, and there was something wistful in the voice. “At least he fights for what’s his. Unlike us — we get screamed at and bow and say thank you.”

Two doors down, the argument was already running among the male trainees.

“That’s what real leverage looks like! Those executives squeeze us dry every day of the week — sunbae just said what all of us can’t!”

“Said it for whom, exactly? HE vented; WE’RE the ones who’ll live under the crackdown when management tightens every screw in this building. That’s called collateral damage.”

“You’re just a coward!”

“And you don’t understand a single thing!”

✦ ✦ ✦

Third floor. The artists’ lounge, over cooling coffee.

“The kid’s genuinely not afraid to die, huh. Seo Dae-ha is an old fox you just don’t touch.”

“He’s at his peak — of course he’s not afraid.” A stir of the cup, a note of vinegar. “It’s the half-famous like us who have to read faces for a living.”

“Famous means untouchable now? Anyone remember Kim Min-seok, two years back? Red-hot, crossed management, and where is he today? Singing in a bar.”

“Ji-ho’s different, though… he’s got overseas reach. Those two English songs—”

“Overseas reach, in Korea, inside LOEN? A dragon from elsewhere doesn’t beat the local snake. He doesn’t know that?”

“I’d say he knows it perfectly well.”

The voice was the eldest in the room, and the room turned to it.

“Meaning what, hyung?”

A slow sip of coffee.

“Think about it. From debut to today — name one step he’s gotten wrong. The film. The OSTs. The English tracks. Every move has landed with frightening precision. Does a man like that suddenly storm a finance office on IMPULSE?”

They looked at each other.

“You’re saying… he’s holding cards.”

“I’m saying nobody who gets to where he is, is stupid. Watch. This thing—” the cup came down softly, “—isn’t over.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Administration floor. The tea room.

“He really kicked the hornet’s nest this time. I passed Director Seo’s office just now — he was flaying his whole team alive. Ji-ho got to him GOOD.”

“Serves him right! Sits on every expense report like it’s his personal money, all because he’s the CEO’s man — about time he hit a wall!”

“Keep your voice DOWN. If the director hears you, do you want to keep working here?”

“Let him hear. What’s he going to do, fire a nobody like me? The real question is how a man that publicly humiliated manages a department afterward.”

“True enough… but Ji-ho’s bold beyond bold. The vice president throttles his resources, and his answer is to raid FINANCE? That’s declaring war on the whole management floor.”

“I heard both of them — the VP and Director Seo — are the CEO’s people. He’s crossed two in one week. When the CEO gets back—”

“Hasn’t the CEO been practically living away from the building lately? Some enormous project…”

“However enormous, internal discipline still has to function. Mark me: this blows up, and the CEO personally steps in.”

“Or…” — a glance at the door, a drop in volume — “what if Ji-ho has someone BEHIND him?”

The tea room went quiet.

Because really — where DOES a newly famous artist find nerve on this scale? Unless—

“Stop guessing. Do your work. Executive-floor business is not for the likes of us.”

But every one of them carried the question back to their desks: was Ji-ho digging his own grave — or had he already finished digging someone else’s?

✦ ✦ ✦

Music production wing. The corridor outside Studio B.

Executive Director Kim Jun-myeon came out of his meeting, and his aide closed in with the afternoon’s file of whispers.

“Director — IU came by looking for you earlier. She seemed anxious.”

“Anxious? What now?”

“Finance suspended Ji-ho’s settlement. He walked into Seo Dae-ha’s office and confronted him to his face. The director couldn’t come down off the ceiling.”

“Heh. Interesting. GENUINELY interesting.”

“Director, should we— about Ji-ho—”

“I heard you.”

A wave of the hand, face unreadable. He drifted to the window and studied the street below for a long moment.

“What’s Yoo Jae-ha’s reaction?”

“Nothing visible yet. Though Director Seo has certainly called him by now.”

Kim Jun-myeon nodded, and something almost imperceptible curved his mouth.

“Storm’s coming, is it.”

“Director, do we want to—” the aide ventured.

“No.” He was already walking to his office. “We want good seats.”

The aide blinked, then hurried after him. Inside, Kim Jun-myeon settled at his desk, fingers drumming lightly.

Ji-ho. More interesting by the month. He’d had the young man filed under ‘talented artist’; the file clearly needed a thicker folder. Taking on Yoo Jae-ha and Seo Dae-ha SIMULTANEOUSLY meant either terminal stupidity — or a hand of cards nobody else had seen. And nothing about that young man suggested stupidity.

“Look into something for me.” The words came abruptly. “Aside from company business — who has Ji-ho been MEETING lately?”

“Yes, sir.” A hesitation. “Director… if Ji-ho actually—”

“If he actually knocks Yoo Jae-ha off his perch,” — a real smile now — “how exactly is that bad news for Music Production?”

The aide’s face cleared with comprehension. Yoo Jae-ha held publicity and licensing, and had spent years squeezing Production’s budgets through them. If someone was about to saw that particular branch…

“Understood. I’ll start immediately.”

✦ ✦ ✦

The executive floor.

Yoo Jae-ha stood outside the CEO’s office, face overcast, while the secretary apologized carefully into the middle distance.

“Vice President, the CEO truly isn’t in today — he’s at SK headquarters in meetings.”

“Back when?”

“He… didn’t say.”

Yoo Jae-ha inhaled slowly, jamming the fury down. The CEO living at SK headquarters meant one thing: the sale was closing. Which made disciplining Ji-ho MORE urgent, not less — if the new owners arrived to find a company that couldn’t even manage one artist, whose failure was that? His. The optics demanded a public correction, immediately.

“The moment he returns, I’m the first call.”

He dropped the sentence and left. Back in his own office, the door shut hard and a folder hit the desk harder.

Ji-ho. An ARTIST. First the OST, publicly thrown back in his face; now a raid on Finance. Clearly the boy needed to learn how weather is made — and who makes it.

“Think a few good numbers make you untouchable?” The cold laugh came out loud in the empty office. “I’ll show you who runs LOEN.”

He picked up the phone and dialed.

“It’s me. Ji-ho’s new album — every promo channel we control, kill it. Yes. ALL of them. And put a word in with our friendlier outlets. Some… material… can start moving.”

He hung up, eyes dark and satisfied.

You’re famous? I’ll make you famous in ways you haven’t imagined. Famous until you’re radioactive. Until you crawl in here on your knees and BEG me to clarify.

✦ ✦ ✦

Ji-ho’s studio.

Ryu Dong-hwan was orbiting the coffee table like an ant on a hot pan lid.

“Ji-ho-ya, Ji-ho-ya, this time you’ve really— AIGH!” A gale-force sigh. “The whole company is talking. Every version of the story. Yoo Jae-ha is NOT going to let this stand — what’s our next move?”

On the sofa, Ji-ho turned a magazine page at leisure.

“Hyung. Sit. Have some tea.”

“What MOOD am I in for tea!” He dropped onto the opposite seat anyway. “Your settlement — you really believe Seo Dae-ha will release it?”

“He will.” The page turned. He didn’t look up.

“How can you possibly be sure?”
