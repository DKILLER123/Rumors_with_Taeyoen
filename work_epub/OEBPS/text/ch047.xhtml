Chapter Forty-Seven

The Timing of Things

He held her gaze at that impossible distance — the curved eyes, the snow-fair skin, the rose-pink lips that frankly constituted a safety hazard — and made a decision.

He leaned in. Slowly. Deliberately slowly — every centimeter of the approach gift-wrapped with time enough to stop him.

Her pupils snapped tight. Her fists closed on nothing. One thought went off in her skull like a flare: he’s going to kiss me.

A deer began ricocheting off the walls of her chest. What do I do? WHAT do I do—

The honest inventory: she liked him. Today had only compounded it. And in this exact moment she felt no offense at all — what she felt was expectation, and a pull, and a small voice pleading its case: Taeyeon-ah. You’re twenty-four. It’s allowed. Let him — and find out what you actually feel.

But as he closed the last of the distance — his breath touchable now, her heartbeat in open rebellion, her face burning like a struck match — the shyness won by knockout.

She recoiled backward off the chair, words tumbling out in beautiful disorder:

“I just remembered — Fany’s waiting for me — I have to go—”

And she fled the production room like a startled lamb.

Ji-ho watched the door swing shut, and his face showed no disappointment whatsoever — because the lean had never been the objective. It had been a probe, and the probe had returned exactly the data he wanted: she didn’t refuse the idea. She refused the timing.

The cold arithmetic ran clean. Kiss her now, on their current foundation, and a relationship might not even survive its own launch — and whatever did survive would cost enormous maintenance. Worse: a trainee dating a top-tier idol entered the relationship kneeling. That imbalance wouldn’t fix itself quickly even after he rose. So for now, ambiguity wasn’t the consolation prize.

Ambiguity was the optimal play.

He turned back to the console and resumed polishing — hook contour, rhyme repairs, the evaluation bearing down.

Outside the door, Taeyeon had not, in fact, gone anywhere.

She stood flat against the corridor wall with one hand pressed over her heart, which was performing something in the percussion family. Excitement, elation, delight, mortification — the whole cocktail, shaken. Her face had gone peach-blossom pink from hairline to jaw, and one sentence looped in her head at full volume:

He likes me. Ji-ho likes ME.

Every repetition spiked her pulse all over again — and the spike itself was its own confession. Whatever this was, it ran both directions.

Was it dating-grade yet? She honestly couldn’t say. But the company’s dating ban ran until around next year anyway — which left more than half a year to find out properly.

As the month-end evaluation bore down, SM’s trainee floors tightened like a drawn bow. B-ranks hungered for A. A-ranks hungered for the pre-debut team. Practice rooms stayed lit past one a.m. as a matter of routine now, occupied by the voluntarily sleepless.

Ji-ho spent the stretch registering the copyright for his reworked song, then grinding rap technique in every self-directed hour — until the week quietly delivered him to the weekend, SM’s one mandated rest day.

He slept until his body finished, surfacing at nine. Washed, brushed, and planted before the open wardrobe, he confronted the eternal question. Lazy-weekend styling? Minimalist?

Style Index: 80 · First-impression bonus active: “Gentle Senior”

He nodded at the panel, satisfied, and caught the subway for Dongdaemun.

Today’s real agenda: assembling the Hip-Hop Kid outfit for the evaluation. Dongdaemun — one of Asia’s largest fashion clusters, wholesale and retail stacked into one riotous district, everything from accessories to full wardrobes at prices a stipend could survive — had been his clothing supplier for years. And Seung-wan, fresh off the plane from Maple Country with the district’s legend ringing in her ears, had been begging to explore it. Hence: the weekend appointment.

Line 1 to Dongdaemun Station, then the walk to Eulji-ro 6-ga — and the unmistakable silhouette of Dongdaemun Design Plaza. He spotted Seung-wan instantly.

And, to his genuine surprise, Irene beside her.

“Seung-wan! Joo-hyun nuna!”

Both heads turned at the shout — and both pairs of eyes did a small, unmistakable brightening.

He came toward them in a loose gray-blue oxford shirt, two buttons open at a clean collarbone, sleeves rolled easy to the forearm; ivory tapered trousers below, the soft palette washing his whole presence clean. On that frame, the lazy styling stopped being lazy — a senior walked out of some campus manhwa, heading straight for them.
