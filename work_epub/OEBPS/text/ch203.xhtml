Chapter Two Hundred Three

The Fight

June 4, 2013 · night The villa living room a delivery feast, a deadly party game — and the crack that runs through nine years of sisterhood

Could it be—?

Yoon-a strangled the thought in its crib.

No. Impossible. Taeyeon eonni and Fany eonni had been roommates since the trainee dorms — shared one bed, split single bowls of ramyeon, survived the worst years shoulder to shoulder, climbed to the absolute summit hand in hand. What stood between them wasn’t friendship; it was family, forged in fire. And Fany eonni — all soft smiles on the surface, but underneath ran a spine of steel and a code of principles thicker than most Bibles. A person like THAT, moving on her own sister’s man? Ji-ho’s face was, granted, criminally excessive, and he did NOT read like a man of simple appetites — but Fany eonni’s taste and discipline— Best friend steals boyfriend? That was eight-o’clock-drama sewage. Not Girls’ Generation. Never Girls’ Generation. Too horrifying. Stop THINKING it.

She shook her head hard, physically ejecting the thought, and washed it down with a gulp of stone-cold coffee.

Outside, the light had gone tangerine.

He checked the time, stood, produced his phone, and excavated a stack of delivery flyers from the coffee-table drawer.

“Nuna-deul — it’s getting late. What are we eating tonight? My treat.”

“Aigoo, hand it over — WE’LL order!”

Taeyeon rose immediately and reached, entirely naturally, for HIS phone.

His stomach dropped through the floor. The PHONE. Joo-hyun. Sulli. One preview banner at the wrong second — Joo-hyun especially, who fired off whatever crossed her mind whenever it crossed—

His arm settled around her shoulders, smooth as lake water. “Your voice is too recognizable — the delivery guy will clock you in one syllable. I can do voices. You all choose; I’ll call it in.”

She bought it without a flicker, surrendered the phone, seized the flyer stack instead, and plopped back down mid-sofa. The sisterhood converged instantly.

“Grilled sea eel!” Yuri’s hand shot up first, eyes glittering. “One portion for Ji-ho — I hear it’s GOOD for young men, especially since — tonight might be very strenuous for him~”

She dragged the last words out to triple length, eyebrows semaphoring at Taeyeon.

“YAH!!! KWON YURI!!” Taeyeon went red from throat to ear-tips in one pour and launched a cushion. “What are you SAYING! Grilled eel — VETOED!!”

“Mwo? Why vetoed?” Yuri caught the cushion, innocence weaponized. “Did I say something wrong? Is caring for brother-in-law’s stamina a crime now?”

“You—! Aish!!”

Taeyeon’s internal arithmetic, briefly legible on her face: he was ALREADY at the outer limit of what she could survive un-supplemented. Fortified? She had a TOUR in four days.

She attacked the flyers at speed and brightened. “Grilled pork cartilage and grilled mackerel instead! These! GOOD ones!”

“Eonni~” Yoon-a leaned in, finger on a stir-fried soondae flyer. “This one. One order? It’s been forever.”

“Spicy braised chicken feet!” Sunny licked her lips. “Chicken feet plus soju: undefeated in all of recorded history.”

“Kimchi and tofu. For balance.” A pause; Jessica’s tone acquired uncharacteristic urgency on food policy. “And tell them — the banchan must contain NO cucumber. None.”

The menu compounded fast: mussel soup for the morning after; spicy chicken gizzards, crispy; a mixed jeon platter; cool moon-snail salad—

“Grilled oysters,” Yuri added, out of nowhere.

“YAH! Oysters are ALSO vetoed!”

Taeyeon nearly levitated, a cat stepped on tail-first.

This time Yuri was authentically wronged. She blinked. “…Those were for ME. I like oysters. Ha — SERIOUSLY, Taeyeon, what is with you today? Why are you terrified of feeding this man anything restorative?”

She stroked her chin, ran a slow assessment over the man in question — the shirt, the shoulder geometry beneath it, the forearms — and nodded with dawning scholarly insight.

“Ohh~~~ could it be that Ji-ho’s hardware is simply so overqualified that supplementation risks a system overload? Fear of OVERCLOCKING, is it?”

“KWON! YU! RI!!”

Full-body blush achieved, Taeyeon grabbed a second cushion and closed to melee range, intent on physical censorship.

“You be QUIET! You’re still GIRLS’ GENERATION! Image! IMAGE!”

“ANI!” Yuri parried, cackling, retreating around the sofa. “Back in the dorm, you and Fany were the DEDICATED film-appreciation committee — with commentary tracks! Detailed ones—mmph!!”

The sentence died under four hands: Sunny — cheeks faintly pink herself — from the left, and a critically-hit Tiffany from the right, both diving to gag her. Three women went down in a laughing, shrieking heap.

“Yah! Quick! Keep her mouth SHUT! Aish! Honestly—!”

Taeyeon stamped her foot, wine-red to the collarbones.

He watched the pile of them — the discipline officer in full system failure, the leader glowing like a stop signal — and laughed out loud, shaking his head.

She wheeled on him, hands on hips, ferocity at maximum, effect negligible. “Ji-ho. WHAT are you laughing at?”

“At all of you. Ordering dinner shouldn’t be this entertaining.” He crossed over, dropped an arm around her shoulders. “Okay, okay — truce. Everything as listed. I’ll call it in.”

“The alcohol! Don’t forget the ALCOHOL!”

“Understood, Yuri nuna.”

✦   ✦   ✦

Food and bottles arrived; the party circled in. Warm light on seven faces; the air loose and easy.

Taeyeon raised the first glass, cheeks already carrying a faint pink. “Everyone — to Ji-ho’s housewarming!”

“TO THE HOUSEWARMING!”

Glass met glass in a bright scatter of sound. He raised his own with a helpless smile. He’d lived here for months. But — look at her eyes. Happy was happy. Let it be a housewarming.

Three rounds in, the plates were wreckage and Taeyeon was fully lit — skin flushed that translucent rose from throat to ear, eyes gone soft-focus, lips parted. She melted against his side, boneless, entirely content.

Yuri clapped twice, breaking the honeyed moment. “Just DRINKING is boring. Game?”

“YES! Let’s!”

Yoon-a materialized upright, doe eyes making one slow, predatory orbit that terminated on him. The corner of her mouth curled.

Revenge window: OPEN.

“Play what?” Sunny finished stripping a chicken foot and wiped her hands.

“‘Of Course’ — how about it?” Yoon-a proposed, sweetly.

“Of Course”: the classic peninsula blood sport. Two contestants; one fires the most vicious question conceivable; the other MUST answer “of course” without hesitating, laughing, or breaking. Fail — drink. Between close friends, the attack ceiling simply does not exist. Known side effect: friendships end.

The assembled madwomen nodded through it unanimously. He felt a distinct barometric drop — but the vote had closed before his mouth opened. His opinion, evidently, was decorative.

Clockwise: Sunny, Yoon-a, him, Taeyeon, Tiffany, Jessica, Yuri, back to Sunny. Free choice of opponent; winner hands off.

Sunny rolled her wrists until the knuckles crackled, surveying the room. Eyes dropped away from hers wholesale — everyone knew what Sunny was in a party game. Predator class.

Her gaze locked, smiling, on the game’s own proposer.

“Yoon-a-yah. If you’re walking down the street and your bangs grow a little longer and cover your face… will people think you’re walking backwards?”

Pfft— someone broke instantly.

Translation, as received by the target: front and back indistinguishable. Flat. FLAT. The nostrils flared; the cheeks inflated into small furious buns. She breathed. She could not — physically could not — say “of course.”

She seized her glass and drained it with hate.

Alcohol down, tongue across lips, eyes re-lit with vengeance. Ji-ho could WAIT. Sunny: you die first.

She turned on Sunny with the trademark honey-smile, voice pure syrup. “Eonni. You’re still in kindergarten… right?”

Sunny’s breathing thickened on schedule. Kindergarten = height report. She grasped her own nape and laughed, beaten.

“Yah, Yoon-a!”

One obedient penalty glass.

His turn. Naturally, he selected the resident menace.

“Yoon-a NUNA—” the honorific applied with tongs — “you actually can’t act at all… right?”

Acting. Mocked. To her FACE. The flush hit — half shame, half fury. Truth was the sharpest blade.

“Song! Ji! Ho!”

“Oh? Pressed?”

“WHO’S pressed?!”

She launched across the gap and rained soft-grade fists on him — cotton artillery, closer to aegyo than assault.

“Can’t handle the game?” — dodging, laughing.

“Hmph!” She disengaged, drained her glass with wounded dignity. “CONTINUE.”

Taeyeon’s turn. She picked Jessica — the TaeSica summit, at last.

“Sica—” head tilted, eyes swimming, smile barely contained. “Your favorite food… is cucumber, right?”

“Euughh~~!!”

Instant kill. Visible full-body revulsion. The one flavor in the universe her soul rejected on contact. Unanswerable. Fatality.

Jessica drank, shuddering. Tiffany’s turn: she chose Taeyeon.

“Taeyeon-ah—” crescent eyes blinking, angelic. “The body part you’re proudest of… it’s your eyebrows, right?”

Taeyeon froze — hand drifting unconsciously to the famously sparse brows — then folded sideways into his chest, laughing in surrender. Penalty accepted.

Then: Jessica’s turn.

And against every projection in the room, she chose him.

“Ji-ho.” The cool voice, the level gaze — fixed on him with unusual focus. “You do a lot of investing. And you’re good at it… right?”

The table blinked collectively. He stilled for half a second. He HAD sunk real hours into finance and corporate strategy — and under the God of Learning, the results had been… considerable.

He nodded. “Of course.”

His counter, then. He held her lovely eyes and asked, quietly:

“You haven’t given up… right?”

Zero hesitation. “Of course.”

“What riddle-game are you two playing?” Taeyeon sat up out of his chest, blinking through the soju fog. “I understand NONE of that.”

Jessica ignored the room entirely, eyes still on him. “If I bring you a plan that moves you — will you invest?”

He held the look for a moment. Then he lifted his glass and drank it down. Forfeit. Answer withheld — by choice.

Last time, she’d dangled her leverage and made him wait. So now: same coin, returned. The more she wanted this, the more his refusals would pull her forward on her own momentum.

Something flickered and dimmed at the bottom of her eyes. These weeks had buried them both — no stage on which to be charming, no opening to persuade. And now this.

“Sica. What EXACTLY is going on between you and Ji-ho?”

Taeyeon pressed, soju making the syllables round and soft, eyes wide with unguarded curiosity.

And Jessica — cornered between secrecy and the need for her members someday anyway — set down her glass, and laid the whole thing out. The brand. The plan. The future.

✦   ✦   ✦

When she finished, the living room had gone silent in a way no game could produce.

Every face ran its own weather. Support her? — the schedules. Oppose her? — nine years. And underneath, unspoken: if Sica diverted her energy to a brand, her share of resources fell to… whom?

Taeyeon poured another glass down, skin flushing deeper rose, and leaned on his shoulder, watching her friend. “Your brand— what does Ji-ho have to do with it?”

“Look around this villa.” Jessica gestured at the room itself as evidence. “Ji-ho doesn’t just EARN — his eye is real, his investments work. So I consulted him.”

A beat.

“And in his assessment, my plan was worth exactly nothing.”

The voice stayed level; the sediment underneath it did not.

“Disrupting team schedules. War with the company. Members resenting the use of the group’s name. Product and pricing that won’t sell. He says I fail. Guaranteed.”

She lifted her head and swept the circle of faces.

“I’ve thought about all of it. He’s right on every point. And I’m not giving up.”

“Idol work is a youth trade. We can’t eat from it forever. So I’m going to solve his objections one at a time — and I want your support.”

“Whatever concerns you have — say them now.” A pause; steel came into it. “But this business — I WILL build it.”

Taeyeon blinked, lost. WHY did it have to be a company? They were idols. Singers. Wasn’t doing THAT well the whole job?

Yuri set her glass down first, directness amplified by proof. “Sica. I’m not against you. But like the man said — the brand sells on ‘Girls’ Generation’s Jessica.’ And WE are a team. What happens when solo schedules collide with team schedules? When my drama shoot crossed a group schedule, the room director skinned me alive — and I STILL had to reschedule.”

The memory visibly still stung; her brows pulled hard.

Tiffany hugged a cushion, sipping, voice more serious than the room was used to. “And the company. You think they’ll approve a top-team member running an outside brand that competes with company resources? YOU get crushed in the middle — and the whole team’s resources take the hit with you.”

Yoon-a had been silent the whole stretch. She, of anyone, understood — she’d spent years walking the actress tightrope over the team’s calendar.

“As long as team schedules aren’t touched—” she offered softly, “—eonni building something isn’t a BAD thing—”

“A GOOD thing?” Tiffany’s voice snapped up an octave. “What’s good about it? What does she know how to do besides sing? Company operations? Product design?”

“Tiffany. What exactly do you mean by that?” Jessica’s voice went to ice. “Is planning for my own future a CRIME now?”

“Yes — I don’t know those things. So I’ll LEARN them. I’ll find partners!” The volume climbed with the color in her face. “Idol work is a youth trade — how many times have WE said it to each other at two in the morning? Every one of us deserves a road that isn’t just ‘Girls’ Generation.’ Don’t we?!”

Tiffany’s chest heaved. Partners. PARTNERS? The partner you’re hunting is MY man. With that face, that figure — you think the resident philanderer stays professional? You’re gift-wrapping yourself—

“And RIGHT NOW you’re demolishing the road we’re ON!” Yuri, louder. “World tour in FOUR DAYS. The most critical window of the year. Whatever you’re building — why can’t it wait for a quiet quarter? Why divide your focus NOW?”

“Because NOW — before re-signing — is the strongest negotiating position I will EVER hold!”

Word by word. The room stopped breathing.

Yuri raked both hands through her hair. “AISH! Why does everything have to be this complicated!”

Sunny, quiet until now, spoke into the wreckage, softly.

“Aren’t we all just — afraid?”

She looked at Jessica; then around the circle.

“Afraid of change. Afraid of someone stepping out first. Afraid that this circle — the one we huddle inside to stay warm — is showing its first crack.”

Direct hit. On every heart at that table. Girls’ Generation was nine. Was ONLY ever nine.

“YES! I’m afraid!” Taeyeon came upright out of his chest, rims of her eyes flooding red. “I’m afraid we stop being nine! Afraid of a concert with one voice missing! Afraid the name GIRLS’ GENERATION stops being whole!”

The leader’s oldest religion: the roster. And in re-signing season, with the company watching — if Sica’s brand detonated the negotiations—

“Sica—” her voice caught and tore. “Is this business worth more to you — than US?”

“How can you even WEIGH those?!” Jessica’s voice shook. “You’re my youth — my SISTERS! And the brand is my dream — my FUTURE! Why am I being forced to CHOOSE? Why can’t I have BOTH?”

“Because the world doesn’t HAND you both!” Tiffany, cold and hard. She jerked her chin toward the armchair. “You asked Ji-ho for investment. He drank instead of answering. Is that not CLEAR enough? Even an OUTSIDER can see the risk and the fault lines! You think this is designing a few pretty clothes?!”

“Did you swallow GUNPOWDER today? What is this hostility?” Jessica ignited fully. “And YOUR solo development was starved, Tiffany?! TTS — the company pushed your subunit an entire YEAR — of course YOU’RE content!”

“MWO? YAH! Jessica! The company never poured resources on YOU? ‘The Boys’ promotions — who got the dress while everyone else wore pants? Whose close-ups? The drama? The variety show?!”

“BECAUSE I EARNED IT!”

“ENOUGH!” Taeyeon’s palm cracked against the table as she surged up. “FIGHTING about camera time?! Is THAT where we are?!”

Sunny reached for arms. “Everyone breathe—”

“BREATHE?” Tiffany flung the hand off, past all governance now. “She saw Ji-ho has money and vision and she wants his INVESTMENT — who knows if the wine isn’t really about the—”

The sentence stopped itself one word too late.

The whole room froze solid.

Jessica had gone paper-white, lips trembling. “Tiffany. Say that again.”

Tiffany regretted it before the echo died — and had nowhere to retreat. “Did I say something false? Of ALL possible partners — him? Private consultations and everything?”

“What are you IMPLYING? If anyone here is—”

“THAT’S ENOUGH.” He set his glass down hard, voice dropping through the noise like a keel. “All of you. Stop there.”

Silence, ringing.

Jessica turned away and wiped her eyes with the back of her wrist, furious at the tears for existing. Tiffany’s rims went red; she looked at the wall. Yoon-a was crying quietly without advertising it. Yuri poured herself a glass and drank it purely as fuel. Sunny exhaled the sigh of the last sane sentry on the wall.

Jessica lifted her chin, voice refrozen. “Since it’s come to this, there’s no reason for me to stay.”

She reached for her jacket. “My business. I’ll build it MYSELF. No one needs to trouble themselves.”

“SICA.” Taeyeon caught her wrist. “A fight is a fight. Walking out is something else.”

“Nine years of fighting and we’re still here. SIT.”

And she hauled her back down onto the sofa — with the specific, non-negotiable gravity that only Taeyeon possessed.

What remained in the living room: suppressed sniffling, the clink of bottles, and the sound a family makes when it discovers its first real crack.

He drank in silence, watching the brightest women in the country cry in a circle. “Of Course” — spectacular on variety shows. Live-fire catastrophe at home. And no one at this table was wrong: Jessica’s stubbornness and long sight; Taeyeon’s animal defense of the whole; everyone else’s fear, which was only love wearing armor. No villains. Just positions — and timing, colliding at full speed.

Bottle after bottle emptied.

And the sisterhood that had been seamless for nine years sat at the crossroads of the present and the future, showing its first visible seam.
