Chapter Two Hundred Thirty-One

A Man’s Standing Is a Woman’s Glory

August 3, 2013 · morning Ji-eun’s villa · LOEN Entertainment, 25th floor a girl who once waited two hours in a lobby rides the chairman’s elevator

The next morning, Ji-eun woke a little before he did.

She turned onto her side by careful centimeters, pillowed her head on her own arm, and simply watched the sleeping face beside her.

“Seriously… so handsome.”

Her hand crept out on its own — fingertip tracing the air an inch above his brow bone, down the line of his nose, along his jaw — never touching, terrified of waking him. Then she drew her hand back, and the corner of her mouth curled up without permission. THIS MAN IS MINE. The thought rose from the bottom of her chest trailing a faint, tingling sweetness that spread everywhere it touched.

✦ ✦ ✦

He dressed for the day in charcoal gray — no tie, the top button of the shirt left casually open. Effortless, and completely unfair.

“Mm. I saw the list you sent. Good work.”

“JYP?”

“Understood. Leave that one with me.”

While he took his calls, Ji-eun stood in the dressing room, conducting a review of considerable strategic weight.

What to wear today. A dress? …No. She shook her head at her usual armory of dresses and, after three rounds of deliberation, chose a pale apricot blouse with a cinched waist — collar open just enough to show the pale line of her throat, the waist tailoring turning soft into slender. A charcoal pencil skirt below, smooth to the calf. Formal but not stiff; elegant without trying to be seen trying. …Still missing something. She swapped in the undergarment that the stylists privately called the ace of confidence, checked the neckline’s geometry with a professional’s eye, and — there. Perfect. She turned once before the mirror, skirt flaring shallowly, and studied the woman looking back: precise makeup, snow-fair skin, bright eyes. GOOD. Worthy of standing beside that man — without upstaging him. That balance was the entire art.

He ended his call just as she came out of the dressing room, and his gaze held on her for a two-count.

Wow, he thought. Ji-eun, dressed with intent, was already flickering into the silhouette of the top-tier artist she would someday be. Real presence.

She kept her eyes down and did not ask how she looked — though the answer mattered enormously — only let the corner of her mouth lift, shy and satisfied at once.

✦ ✦ ✦

Eight-thirty. They left together.

The black Maybach waited at the villa gate; Ryu Dong-hwan was already stationed beside it and moved the moment they appeared, opening the rear door with one hand cupped over the frame.

“Hoejang-nim. Ji-eun-ssi. Good morning.”

“Mm.”

Ji-ho nodded and stepped aside to let her in first.

She slid onto the seat, fingers tightening unconsciously in the fabric of her skirt. This road — she had traveled it a thousand times. Always in the artist van, behind dark privacy film, shuttling between schedules. LOEN’s flagship act, the company’s pillar, its most reliable revenue line — treated with courtesy everywhere and admitted, truly, nowhere. Never once inside the inner circle.

And now— She tilted her head and watched his profile as he frowned over something on his phone, long fingers moving on the glass, morning light falling across his shoulders like a conferred honor. Different now. Everything was different now.

The car slid down into LOEN’s garage and stopped at the private elevator. He entered first; she followed half a step behind, eyes lowered, watching their two reflections in the mirrored wall — his tall, hers small — overlapping into one picture.

Ding. The doors opened on the twenty-fifth floor.

The executive level was already busy — and when the corridor saw who had stepped out of the elevator, it froze mid-motion, conversation dying in the air.

“Hoejang-nim.”

The first to recover bent at the waist. Then the second, the third — dominoes down the length of the hallway, every back folding, greeting after greeting.

“Hoejang-nim, good morning.”

“Hoejang-nim—”

She walked behind him and did not look at anyone directly. But her peripheral vision collected every lowered head, every ninety-degree spine, and something complicated moved through her chest. She KNEW these people. The middle-aged man to his right, for instance: Choi Myung-hwi, head of Music Production — the man who held her album budgets in his fist. A man she had never once been able to afford offending.

And now the once-untouchable division head stood folded in half, voice pitched to velvet.

“Hoejang-nim — IU-ssi — the budget proposal for her album has been sent to your inbox.”

“Good work. We’ll take it up in the afternoon meeting.”

“Yes.”

Choi Myung-hwi held his bow until Ji-ho had passed three full steps, straightened cautiously — and then his gaze moved from the chairman’s back to Ji-eun’s face, stalled one beat, and bloomed into warmth.

“IU-ssi, you’re looking wonderful today. You’ve been working so hard lately — please make sure you’re resting.”

She curved her mouth into the exactly correct smile.

“Thank you, Bonbujang-nim.”

And she turned, light on her feet, and caught up to Ji-ho.

One step. Two. And her mind slid back, without warning, to a fifteen-year-old in plain clothes with a scuffed backpack, standing at the LOEN front desk whispering, “I’m here for the audition.” The polite receptionist. The waiting area. Two hours in that chair — the longest two hours of her life, arriving on the far side of a dozen failed auditions, holding the very last ticket she had. From invisible to indispensable, and every centimeter of that road paid for in full. And now she was walking the silenced carpet of the twenty-fifth floor, toward the office whose windows owned the best of the Seoul skyline. The summit of the building. The summit of everything. …And that office still lacked its mistress.

“Hoejang-nim — the overseas division’s quarterly report is at ten, and Legal’s contract review at eleven.” The interim secretary fell in beside them, brisk and precise. “The materials you requested yesterday are on your desk.”

He acknowledged it with a low hum and pushed open the office door; she followed him in.

“Ji-eun-ssi — coffee, or tea?”

“Tea, please. Thank you.”

The secretary smiled, withdrew, and shut the door on a sudden, expensive silence.

She let her gaze travel the room. The window-wall with the jewel-blue sky and its gauze of clouds. The plants on the sill, glossy with care. The bookshelf running the length of one wall, dense with proposals. And the man at the wide desk, head bent over documents, already elsewhere. She watched him and her thoughts drifted — briefly, warmly, tiredly — to the previous night. Her new curriculum had had… pronounced effects. Happiness was happiness; stamina was a separate line item.

“What are you thinking about?”

She looked up. He had left the desk at some point and was leaning against its edge, arms crossed, watching her.

She blinked, and something impish came into her eyes.

“Thinking…” — a deliberate pause, and then the smile broke, eyes curving to crescents — “that my man is incredible.”

Both readings intended. Career, and otherwise.

He raised an eyebrow, amused.

“That’s all?”

“Mm.” She propped her chin on both hands, eyes shining up at him, unashamedly worshipful. “Watching all those people who used to stand so far above me bow like that in front of you—” she let the syllables stretch, savoring, “—feels AMAZING.”

“Then get used to it.” He straightened and walked back behind the desk. “There’s going to be a great deal more of it.”

She tilted her head and watched him work — eyes down, sunlight on his shoulders, the cold clean profile, the pen moving in decisive strokes with never a hesitation. Twenty years old. TWENTY. She had debuted at fifteen and spent five years in this industry’s threshing machine; she had met every person ever labeled a genius. Not one was like this. Every time you decided he had reached his ceiling, he went through it in some direction you hadn’t known existed. Everything impossible, he simply did. You could never see the bottom of him. Mysterious. Magnetic. And the more you learned, the harder the lock turned.

A knock, right on schedule.

“Hoejang-nim, the ten o’clock materials are ready.”

“Come in.”

The overseas division head entered with two team leads behind him; all three bowed in unison.

“Hoejang-nim.”

Ji-ho nodded. The division head straightened — and his eyes crossed the sofa, caught, and lit with immediate professional sunshine.

“IU-ssi is here as well.” A small courteous dip. “Is the new album preparation going smoothly? Our division has been following your project closely — if there is anything at all we can support, please reach out at any time.”

She lifted the corner of her mouth into an elegant curve.

“Thank you, Bonbujang-nim.” A beat, soft as falling snow. “You’ve worked hard.”

His smile doubled in wattage; he protested that it was no hardship at all, no hardship whatsoever, while handing the materials to the chairman with both hands. She raised her tea, blew gently across the surface, lowered her eyes, and did not look at him again. THIS division head. Her second year in the company, at the annual dinner, she had crossed the room to pour him a respectful drink — and he had glanced at her once and turned back to his conversation. She had stood there holding her glass in front of everyone. She had never told anyone how much that had burned. Even after the charts, he had stayed lukewarm, always. She had kept the receipt. And now he bent at the waist and smiled with his whole face and said PLEASE REACH OUT AT ANY TIME. The tea went down warm, and something deeply satisfying went down with it.

Delicious.

The materials confirmed, the delegation withdrew with matched bows, and the door clicked home on quiet again.

She set down her cup, rose, and drifted to the window-wall, her back to him, looking out at the ranked towers.

“Ji-ho.”

“Hm?”

“You know… I used to think this building was so tall.” A pause, the memory sifting down. “Every time I stood at the bottom and looked up at this window, it felt—”

She stopped. Something crossed her face, half wistful.

“—so far.”

He said nothing. He listened.

She turned around slowly, and her eyes on him held a different kind of softness.

“Standing here now, looking DOWN — it’s still just as high.”

A small laugh, unburdened.

“But it’s not the same anymore.”

She walked back to the sofa and didn’t sit — just stood, looking at him.

“Before, I looked up at it with my head tipped back.”

“Now I look down at it — standing at my man’s side. Shoulder to shoulder.”

He didn’t answer that either. He reached out, drew her in, and closed his hand firmly around hers.

She looked down at their interlaced fingers — his long and strong, hers slender between them — and thought: thank you. For not correcting me when I said SHOULDER TO SHOULDER. For not saying ‘not yet’ or ‘you’ll have to wait.’ You just took my hand.

✦ ✦ ✦

She spent the whole morning in the chairman’s office.

Delegations came and went — Legal’s director, the finance office chief, a section head from Planning — and every one of them bowed to Ji-ho first, then discovered the woman having tea on the sofa, and warmed by several visible degrees. IU-ssi, it’s been too long. IU-ssi, you look lovely today. IU-ssi, fighting on the new album. She answered each with the same poised smile, posture never slipping, while the tea cooled in her hands to room temperature and she never once asked for a fresh cup.

Enough. It had been a full tasting of the chairman’s-wife experience — and it was intoxicating, exactly as advertised: everyone she had once approached with both hands and a bowed head now approached HER. A man’s standing, she thought, is a woman’s glory. The sweetness of this one morning would keep for a very, very long time.
