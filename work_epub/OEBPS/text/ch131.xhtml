Chapter One Hundred Thirty-One

Taeyeon’s Advice

Taeyeon gave him one long, luminous look — loving this man past all argument — and nodded.

Candy had always been her weakness. And the night, by all private accounts, was very sweet.

✦   ✦   ✦

Next morning, she woke slowly to an empty stretch of bed — still warm, still carrying his clean, cool scent. Recently vacated, then.

She padded barefoot to the shower, let the hot water take the night off her skin, and met herself in the mirror: a lazy, well-tended softness around the eyes, girlish sweetness and something newly bloomed sharing the same face. Dark-green fitted knit, pearl-white straight trousers, light clean makeup — and out.

Ji-ho was just emerging from the kitchen with two glasses of milk. His eyes brightened at the sight of her.

“Nuna. Breakfast.”

Her gaze landed on the milk-white glass in his hand, and certain memory fragments arrived uninvited; her cheeks pinked.

“…Understood.”

That one glance — sweetness and allure improbably fused — was genuinely unfair. He set one glass in front of her: month two of the Height-Reforging Milk, on schedule.

She sipped in small mouthfuls, and — habit, or flavor — her tongue darted out to catch a trace of milk from her lip. The unconscious gesture darkened his eyes measurably. She felt the temperature of the look, remembered exactly how mornings like this could derail, and coughed once, steering hard for safe waters.

“Right — Ji-ho-ya. Yoon-a mentioned something. You only have the team LOEN assigns you, don’t you? Nobody of your own?”

“Mm.” He bit into his buttered toast. “Dong-hwan hyung handles the coordination. For events the company sends a temp assistant and a stylist.”

“That won’t DO.” She set down her glass, assembling the full sunbae posture. “You need to build your own personal entourage. Soon.”

“Personal entourage?”

Field Notes

Professor Kim’s staffing seminar, delivered over toast: the exclusive personal manager comes first — the artist’s most trusted executor: every schedule, every company negotiation, even career-strategy decisions run through them; they filter the bad offers and fight for the good ones, and they often practically live with the artist. Then the hair stylist, makeup artist, fashion coordinator, personal assistant, work assistant, bodyguard, plus the long-term trainer, vocal coach, dance teacher — a top artist effectively runs a small company out of their own shadow.

For Ji-ho’s current stage, three positions were non-negotiable: an exclusive manager, at least one assistant, and a fixed stylist. The manager above all.

He turned it over. True — there was currently no one he trusted enough for the role. Dong-hwan was capable, but Dong-hwan was the COMPANY’S man; his first loyalty was structural. And the next decade’s cautionary tales were full of artists knifed by their own inner circle.

“No rush,” he said, drinking his milk. “Skills can be trained. Loyalty and trust come first. Better an empty seat than the wrong person in it.”

“Exactly right.” She nodded, satisfied. “Take your time — but keep it in mind.”

Breakfast done, he left first — Star Counter-Surveillance quiet, Utterly Unremarkable humming — and merged invisibly into the morning traffic toward the company.

That Morning LOEN Entertainment — Conference Room the first formal planning meeting for SUNYE

In attendance: Music Division head Shin Min-hyuk, production chief Kim Si-won, Artist Development chief Lee Ji-yeon, manager Ryu Dong-hwan, and the heads of promotion and marketing. The first half of the agenda: listening to the finished demos, and a first pass at the production budget.

Ji-ho connected the drive and pressed play — and the conference room slid, degree by degree, into a strange and total silence.

The last track faded. You could have heard a pin strike carpet.

Several seconds later, Kim Si-won inhaled sharply, voice actually trembling.

“These— you wrote ALL of these?”

Ji-ho nodded, calm.

“Genius. This is simply — GENIUS.” Color climbed the producer’s face. “Any ONE of these could carry an album as its title track. No — half of them are the career-defining songs that A-list singers spend decades praying for!”

“Ji-ho-ssi… we undervalued you.” Lee Ji-yeon wasn’t bothering to hide her excitement either. “This album’s quality is beyond anything we projected.”

Shin Min-hyuk’s finger ticked against the table, eyes bright with something close to shock. His expectations had already been high — and these songs had cleared them by a story. A veteran of decades, he knew exactly what he was hearing: not merely “good,” but the raw material of classics. Era-defining potential.

“SM must be regretting this into physical pain,” Dong-hwan murmured, glowing with proprietary pride.

The room caught fire. Then Kim Si-won, wrestling his excitement down, ventured carefully:

“Ji-ho-ssi — one request, if I may. The quality here is TOO high. Twelve of these on one disc is… extravagant, isn’t it? Consider splitting them — banking half for future albums? It guarantees sustained output, sustained heat—”

Heads nodded around the table. Commercially, it was the textbook play.

Ji-ho shook his head, and there was no give in it.

“Thank you — truly. But my idea of an album is this: the best, most complete musical statement I can make, delivered all at once. I don’t lack songs. There will be new ones. What I want to make is an album listeners will REMEMBER — one that stands for this exact season of my life, with nothing held back and nothing to regret.”

His gaze crossed the table, carrying that quiet certainty.

“If we do it — we do it all the way.”

Glances traded; tongues clicked at the sheer extravagance; and under it, everyone in the room felt the pull of the ambition. Shin Min-hyuk was silent a moment — then laughed, stood, walked to the window, and turned back with his eyes burning.

“GOOD. That spirit is exactly what this needs.” He rounded on the room, voice like a gavel: “The two billion won budgeted for this album — is no longer presentable.”

He lifted the tracklist like a rare artifact.

“Facing work like this, LOEN must match the ambition. We are not making a bestseller. We are making an album that attacks the year-end awards and enters K-pop history — a LEGENDARY album.”

“Bujang-nim, the budget—” Kim Si-won began.

“Leave the budget to me.” A cutting wave of the hand. “I’ll take these demos to the sajang-nim personally. He’ll hear what they’re worth.”

Hours later, word came down: the CEO had signed off personally.
