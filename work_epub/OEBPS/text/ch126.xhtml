Even a CM Song Can All-Kill

IU, it turned out, had real gifts as a lyricist. The music for the morning-glory song reached her — and the words came back fast, and came back good.

Then the recording booth: two voices that had already learned each other’s grain, polishing note by note, line by line. His magnetic low register wove through her clear sweetness, and the little candy-bright song was tracked in record time.

The CM shoot followed — crews dressing sets, gaffers chasing light, the two leads bent over storyboards in a corner. The story was simple; two days, wrapped.

Half a month later, LOEN’s, IU’s, and Ji-ho’s accounts posted the Elite collaboration teaser simultaneously — two fresh-faced figures in a wash of clean autumn light — and the comment sections ignited on contact.

THE FILM · a school in golden autumn light.

He is the basketball team’s ace, always on the court after class. She stands in the corner of the stands, eyes following him, never once brave enough to say it — always slipping away before the final whistle.

The library. Two silhouettes crossing in the stacks, missing each other by one shelf, again and again — until, unknowing, they trade places. She stretches on tiptoe for a book just past her fingers. About to give up — and a taller shadow arrives behind her, chest brushing her back, one long arm lifting the book down. Close-up: warm amber halo; held breath. She turns, pink-cheeked, and looks away. He hands her the book, expression calm, and turns to go. Her fingers twitch — the courage never comes.

But he doesn’t go far. He leans against the next shelf, watching her from the quiet. And the film dissolves into HIS memory: he has known her schedule for months. Every shelf she visits, he has visited after her. Their paths have been overlapping all along — she just always left too early for him to say it.

Years skip. Graduation. Working lives. And then — as if by shared instinct — both come back to the old school on the same day. He stops at the library steps. She walks alone to the edge of the basketball court. Then two figures follow the wall thick with blooming morning glories, one from each end, drawing closer through the swaying flowers — and meet at the corner. A held look. Two smiles. Two hands, finally joined, walking out of frame.

— fin.

✦   ✦   ✦

The All-Kill’s shockwave rolled straight out of music and into commerce — landing first on the CM’s true protagonist: the uniforms.

IU’s styled set — pale grey V-neck sweater, navy tartan pleats, fitted blazer — read collegiate and fresh at once; his classic stand-collar jacket did his shoulders every favor. The library gaze between them became, overnight, a canonical image of the “campus couple.” A same-item buying frenzy followed; #IU_SongJiho_Uniform and #Elite_MorningGlory trended for days; Elite stood up a same-look section on its site and ran the production lines hot.

The deeper effect landed in procurement season. Summer turning to autumn meant schools weighing uniform renewals — and the CM’s vision of campus life hit students where their ideals lived. At several private schools, student councils formally petitioned administrations, arguments fully drafted: contemporary design, proven fabric quality, an upgrade to the school’s whole image. For Elite it was a beachhead into accounts they’d never been able to enter. Sales spiked; the stock climbed; the marketing team that had signed the deal collected commendations.

And at the center of the wave: the singer who was also the composer and co-producer. The “talent” label gained another coat of shine — and Elite, feeling generous, raised the promised ₩200M bonus to ₩300M, bringing the total fee to ₩1.7 billion. Under his 70/30 split with the company, after taxes, Ji-ho’s take came to roughly ₩900 million — in the dollar math he still ran by reflex, north of three-quarters of a million. For a rookie’s first deep commercial collaboration, the yield — chart results, brand sales, cultural footprint — was close to unheard of.

The offers that followed jumped a full tier: from premium consumer goods to national brands, from simple endorsements to design collaborations. One well-built campaign had produced a hit song, an acclaimed film, a market tremor — and lifted a rookie to an altitude that made the whole industry squint.

That autumn, the name Ji-ho pressed itself into the public eye, wound through with morning glories and the flutter of being young.

Seoul turned crisp and dry. Half a month after the CM’s All-Kill, the album’s tracks were nearly locked — and the copyright registrations turned up one deeply awkward fact.

SUNYE · Tracklist Correction
FLAGGED“Beautiful Girl” — PRE-EXISTING WORK

the title track of a Jamaican-American singer’s 2007 debut — already released, already famous, in THIS timeline. caught at registration. lesson: from now on, CHECK FIRST.

REPLACED WITH“Spring Day” — solo rework

the 2017 masterpiece, rearranged for a single voice. triple-checked against the registry: clean. application mailed.

A close call — and a standing reminder that a regressor’s memory of “future” songs occasionally included songs that were, in fact, past ones. The vault had plenty of alternatives; the swap only made the album stronger.

With the rights application out, the path forward was clear: production meetings, recording, MV, promotion. And exactly then, a courier from France laid a new folder on the team’s conference table.

EIDER · Asia-Region Brand Ambassador — Proposal
The Brand

Founded 1962, Chamonix, at the foot of the French Alps; named for the eider — the Arctic duck whose down survives the polar cold. Through its partnership with the K2 Group, a national-tier premium outdoor brand on the peninsula.

The Ask

Asia-region brand ambassador. The current peninsula models’ contracts — Lee Min-ho and Im Yoon-a — are approaching expiry; headquarters and K2 seek a face for the next market strategy.

The Rationale

Demonstrated influence over young consumers (the Elite case) — and the candidate’s particular profile: an American-raised artist with genuine cross-border reach, whose overseas fan communities span the region and beyond; clean image; and a build tailor-made for technical outerwear.

— expansion context: EIDER’s Greater China operation, licensed through K2, opened its first flagship in Shanghai last month, with 15–20 stores planned across the region in 2–3 years. They need a face that builds recognition FAST.

“The French headquarters cares about brand soul,” Dong-hwan reported, barely containing himself. “Their internal note says you have — quote — ‘a quiet kind of resilience. Prepared toughness. Very eider.’ The K2 side and the regional operators are more practical: your core fans are urban 19-to-30s, which overlaps their city-outdoor line almost perfectly, and your fanbase’s spending power and word-of-mouth make you the highest-efficiency choice on the board.”

As for the outgoing Lee Min-ho — contract simply run to term, no renewal, mutual understanding: brands need different voices in different seasons. EIDER had form here; it had once decisively swapped a scandal-brushed model for a cleaner face before. “Safety” and “fit” were the whole religion now.

Details were settled; signatures dried. And in the quiet window before the announcement, Ji-ho and his team slipped out of the country — bound for the Alps, and the campaign shoot.
