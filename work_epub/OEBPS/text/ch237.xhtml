Chapter Two Hundred Thirty-Seven

The Frightening Yoon-a

August 5–6, 2013 · deep night Grand Walkerhill · corridors and quiet rooms of the Moonlight wing a ten-minute alibi, a hallway kiss witnessed — and a deer-eyed detective who sees far too much

Wrapped in his scent, wrapped in the warmth of his chest, Taeyeon floated in a happiness thick enough to drown in. So sleepy. But not yet — she told herself sternly — not yet. Hold on. Stay awake.

It was a losing battle. Between the squat rack, the ab bench, and certain systemic advantages, Ji-ho remained calmly unbeatable, and the evening’s small rebellion had ended the way all her rebellions ended.

Perhaps half an hour later, he nudged her gently.

“Taeyeon. Taeyeon-ah.”

The only answer was a small, piglet-soft snore.

He eased himself free, showered without a sound, dressed, slipped out — and knocked, very lightly, on Tiffany’s door.

It opened at once, as if she had been standing just inside it all along.

She had taken off her makeup — the first time she had ever let him see her bare-faced. A rounder, softer face than the cameras knew; skin not porcelain-pale but warm and healthy; the big eyes even more alive without the liner.

“Is it… ugly?”

Her hand went to her cheek on reflex, and something unconfident flickered through her eyes.

“Ani. It’s beautiful.”

He stepped in and shut the door softly behind him.

“Did you wait long?”

“Not long—”

“How long is ‘not long’?”

She looked up at his eyes — then pressed her lips together and lowered her head, and didn’t answer.

He sighed quietly and opened his arms, and she came into them, burying her face against his chest, breathing him in like something she’d been rationed.

She had missed him. Missed THIS — the specific harbor of it — more than she was ever going to say out loud.

They stood like that a while. Then he steered her to the sofa, sat, and settled her across his lap.

“Did you like the present?”

A hesitation, half a breath long.

“I like it. But—”

“No ‘but.’” He cut it off gently. “If I can’t give you the life you want, what right do I have to you?”

“And I CAN give you that life, Fany.” His fingers caught her chin and tipped her face up, unhurried and entirely certain. “All of it. Only — I hate mess. You understand me?”

Her heart gave one hard knock. She understood perfectly.

Accept the career help. Enjoy the life he furnished. Let his standing shelter her from every small ugliness of the industry. And the price: stay peacefully at his side, and want nothing that made noise. He had grown more imperious — and the strange thing, the thing she wouldn’t have admitted under oath, was that the force of it landed as SAFETY. She had always been drawn to strength; his had simply… consolidated. Even the villa — she disliked admitting this too — had done its work: the sheer concrete fact of it had settled her into her new place more firmly than any promise could have. Career, life, an impossible man — he had already given her more than she’d dared to budget for. Greed, from here, risked everything. So she nodded, obedient — and privately resolved to give way to Taeyeon from now on. She’d never wanted that war anyway. But if some OTHER woman existed out there — some third party beyond Taeyeon — well. Then California would show the peninsula what it looked like when the gloves came off.

And he, watching her settle, felt the old lesson confirm itself once more: money and power were rhetoric that needed no grammar. Said with feelings alone, that same speech would have earned him a slapped face and a shouted SCOUNDREL. Said with a villa behind it, it earned a nod. Though — to be fair to her and to himself — a woman who could be conquered by money ALONE wasn’t worth conquering; clubs were full of those. It was the feeling underneath that made this one worth keeping.

She tucked her head against his chest, listening to his heart.

“Where’s Taeyeon?”

“Asleep. Worn out.”

“Worn OUT?” She lifted her head and thumped him once, scolding. “Ya. That tiny body — how is she supposed to survive you?”

“You wrong me. Tonight SHE wouldn’t let ME go. Ordinarily I’m very gentle with her.”

“Wouldn’t let you—”

One turn of thought and she had it: Taeyeon was GUARDING him. From her. A helpless little laugh escaped. So exactly, completely Taeyeon. If it had been Sica — with that love-hard fight-hard temper — a stolen boyfriend would have earned a slap across the face by now, delivered in front of witnesses. Taeyeon just… swallowed things. And swallowed things. And the swallowed kind of pain, she knew, cut deepest going down.

After the world tour, she promised herself. Jeonju, the health checkups for his parents, and then — the truth, all of it, on her knees if her knees were what it took. Whatever beating, whatever words. Only one clause was not negotiable: DON’T MAKE ME LEAVE HIM. Taeyeon’s heart was soft; everyone who loved her knew where the softness lived. She would aim every apology straight at it, and she hated herself a little for knowing she would.

“All right. I should get back.”

He patted her lightly and stood.

“Rest early.”

“Arasseo. I’ll walk you out.”

At the door she held onto his hand a beat past reasonable.

“Good night.”

He did, in fact, miss the California weather of her — tonight was simply the wrong night. He thought a moment.

“These next weeks I’m on album prep — evenings are quiet. If you have time, call me.”

“Mm. I will.”

“Go on in.”

He turned for Taeyeon’s room.

“Ji-ho.”

He looked back — and she was already in motion, quick steps, up on her toes, and the kiss she gave him held nothing in reserve. Then she stepped back, eyes curling into their crescents.

“Saranghae. Go on.”

He smiled — and the moon-curve of his eyes caught her off guard all over again.

His smile-eyes look like mine, she thought, watching him go, faintly dazed. Is that what they say happens? People in love, growing to match?

✦ ✦ ✦

He let himself back into the dark suite without a sound and gathered Taeyeon in.

Half-asleep, her nose twitched once. Fany’s perfume. She ran the arithmetic behind closed eyes: gone roughly ten minutes. A hug, then. Maybe a kiss. Nothing further — still inside the borders of what she could bear. WISE OF YOU, she thought at the ceiling with grim satisfaction, and let sleep take her.

Down the hall, Tiffany sat back on her own bed and took the purchase contract out one more time. Fifty billion won. She sighed at herself, wry and quiet. “Well. Apparently I really can be bought.”

✦ ✦ ✦

“Mmph— Yoon-a, WHY are you dragging me—!”

Jessica pried at the hand clamped over her mouth, urgent and furious in a whisper.

“Did you NOT see that? Fany and Ji-ho were KISSING! How can they—”

“SHH!!” Yoon-a’s hand clamped tighter, voice dropping to a hiss. “Eonni. QUIETLY.”

Both of them were in swimsuits, dripping wet from the garden pool, hair still streaming — two accidental witnesses hauled back into a bedroom at speed.

The door shut. Jessica shook her off, planted a hand on her hip.

“Ya, Yoon-a! Look at your face. You KNEW already, didn’t you?”

Yoon-a sighed.

“Not just me. Sunny eonni knows too.”

“MWO? Sunny too?! Then why has nobody told TAEYEON? Why hasn’t anyone exposed him?!”

Jessica was genuinely furious now. Taeyeon was YEARS of sisterhood. She herself had schemed a little around Ji-ho’s usefulness, fine — but thinking about leverage and actually climbing into your best friend’s relationship were different continents. She’d assumed Fany’s feelings were a quiet, harmless crush. This was betrayal, the real kind. One of them had drifted in her HEAD; the other had ACTED. People grade themselves on intentions and everyone else on behavior — and right now she was grading hard.

“Eonni. Calm down first.” Yoon-a took her shoulders, level and serious. “This is not a thing WE get to stick our hands into. Ji-ho isn’t a trainee anymore. He isn’t even just an artist.”

“He is the chairman of LOEN. Don’t let the friendly manners fool you into thinking we’re free to pass judgment on his private life in public.”

Jessica opened her mouth. Closed it. Then:

“Even so— we can’t just WATCH this scoundrel play Fany and Taeyeon at the same time! At minimum Taeyeon deserves to KNOW. Doesn’t she?”

“Eonni.” A tired exhale. “Did Taeyeon eonni seem NORMAL to you today?”

Jessica frowned, rewinding the day. Now that it was said aloud — something HAD been off. She just couldn’t name where.

“Taeyeon eonni acts unhinged around US — the butt-slapping, the jokes — but that’s girls-only static. Underneath, she’s the quietest person in this group. Crowds embarrass her. Public affection HORRIFIES her.”

“So: kissing him in front of all nine of us? DRAGGING him to the bedroom by the hand, with commentary? Does that sound like her? That’s a Fany move. Or yours. You two grew up in America — you don’t feel it. She DOES.”

“So what you’re saying is…?”

Yoon-a looked at this beautiful, slightly buffering eonni and worried, sincerely, about her future vulnerability to con artists.

“I’ve thought about it carefully. I think Taeyeon eonni already KNOWS.”

“Today was a warning to Fany eonni. A flag planted in front of a witness list.”

“MWO?”

Jessica’s jaw actually dropped.

“The day we got back from Taipei — remember Fany eonni vanished for two days? ‘A friend’s house,’ she said.”

“Ne. So?”

“Ani.” A slow headshake. “Count the friends Fany eonni would actually STAY two days with. It’s us. It’s only ever been us.”

“…She lied?”

“That same day, I saw Taeyeon eonni end a phone call — and then she asked me, out of nowhere: ‘Yoon-a-yah, can you hear waves in Seoul?’ I told her Seoul’s inland, didn’t think twice. But from that day her whole state changed. Absent. Red-rimmed eyes in the morning, more than once. And when Fany eonni came back — Taeyeon eonni went even quieter.”

“My guess: she was on the phone WITH Fany eonni. And she heard the sea.”

Jessica’s eyes went wide as the pieces docked. “So Fany was somewhere with WAVES—”

“Plenty of coastline in this country,” Yoon-a said, with the patience of a tutor watching a student finally carry the one. “For example: Jeju. Where Ji-ho happened to be.”

“Jeju— that was the film promo. But then Sulli would have been there too! And Sulli’s close with BOTH of them — she’d never keep that from Taeyeon—”

“Eonni. Wrong again.” Another headshake, gentler. “Don’t forget: he SAVED Sulli’s life. Then handed her a lead role that made her a ten-million-admissions actress. Whatever Sulli is to him now — her testimony stopped being neutral a long time ago.”

Privately, Yoon-a’s suspicion ran further than she’d say: she did not believe for one second that a man like that — money, talent, appetite — had kept his hands entirely off a girl that beautiful who owed him her life. THE GIRL IN THE SUN’s chemistry hadn’t looked like acting because perhaps it wasn’t. And that was the part that genuinely frightened her. Rich men collecting women was ordinary physics. But Sulli, Fany, Taeyeon — none of these were foolish girls. Hiding one or two relationships was a skill. Being DISCOVERED and having every one of them stay quiet, stay orbiting, stay HIS — that was something else entirely, and Yoon-a, who considered herself the sharpest person in most rooms, could not reverse-engineer how it was done. And one more certainty, cold as pool water: he was watching HER too. He simply hadn’t moved yet.

Jessica stared at her dongsaeng and felt something adjacent to fear. Everyone knew Yoon-a and Sunny were the clever ones — but the girl in front of her right now, assembling a conspiracy from a wave-sound and a two-day alibi, was clever in a way that made the back of the neck prickle.
