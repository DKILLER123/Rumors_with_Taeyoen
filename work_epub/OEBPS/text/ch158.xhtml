Chapter One Hundred Fifty-Eight

Interviewing the Manager

January 2013 Gangnam — a quiet café the planner Dong-hwan promised

A quiet café in Gangnam, the kind chosen specifically for not being seen in.

Lee Hyun-woo — the planning manager Dong-hwan had vouched for — had cleared his slate and arranged the meeting the moment he was back in the country. When Ji-ho pushed through the door, the man was already on his feet: a well-cut dark-grey suit, no tie, an air of contained competence. Nothing about him read like the industry’s usual hungry operators.

“Ji-ho-ssi, hello. Lee Hyun-woo. Thank you for making the time.” A precise half-bow; a card offered with both hands; a calm, level voice.

“Hello. Dong-hwan hyung recommended you in the strongest terms — said you’d bring an angle nobody else has.”

He sat, openly curious. Hyun-woo’s gaze was focused in a way that skipped all the usual small talk.

“The sunbae is too generous. I’ve studied your current position carefully.” He turned a slim tablet around, charts already loaded — clean, unshowy. “First: congratulations on the year’s album crown. A remarkable result. But it also means the market’s expectations for your NEXT release have been raised to an altitude nobody’s oxygen works at.”

Which was precisely the pressure. Ji-ho nodded.

“And the company’s first-half schedule for you — film production, variety, endorsements, corporate events — is dense. Standard playbook for consolidating popularity and commercial value.” A turn of tone. “But there’s a core contradiction inside it. Call it dilution risk.”

He indicated the chart.

“Your heat and your credibility as the year’s champion MUSICIAN are the foundation of everything else you monetize. Heavy film schedules, variety exposure, and commercial stages will drain creative bandwidth — and, worse, will steadily overwrite ‘musician Ji-ho’ in the public’s mind. By the time you return to music in the second half, the market may need to be re-taught who you are. That reintroduction is never free.”

Ji-ho leaned in.

“Keep going.”

“So the planning core isn’t refusing exposure — it’s stamping every exposure with the musician’s mark, and quietly building the comeback narrative all year. Four workstreams.”

The man’s eyes changed when he crossed into his own territory — a blade coming out of its cloth.

Planning Memo · Lee Hyun-woo → Song Ji-ho

Holding the Throne While Crossing Over

First consultation · Gangnam · January 2013

One · The Film The project is itself the story: a musician narrating through script and performance instead of a track list. Build a deep-cut behind-the-scenes series — not standard set footage, but a record of how the OST, score, and songwriting weave into the role. During the film’s press cycle this reads as “creator,” never “rookie actor.”

Two · Variety & Endorsements Filter hard. Choose programs where musicality surfaces naturally — an unreleased melody at a guesthouse piano, a talk-show segment on how a hit was written. Prioritize brand categories adjacent to craft: audio, headphones, technology, design. Every appearance extends the musician; none dilute him.

Three · Small Rooms Alongside contracted commercial stages, design small music salons — galleries, bookshops, studios — for core fans and serious listeners. Low cost, high loyalty, real feedback, and raw material for the next record. Indie-circuit method, major-league application.

Four · The Countdown All year, a quiet drip: fragments of melody, reading lists, exhibitions visited, a film score dissected in an interview. Fragmentary but constant proof that the engine never stopped. It inoculates against overexposure and compounds anticipation for the return.

“My background taught me one thing above all,” Hyun-woo said, quieter now, and more earnest for it. “Planning isn’t drawing a glamorous route. It’s defending an artist’s core value inside a mess of competing interests — and engineering the occasional outsized win from a small position.”

“You’re standing on a summit. The next move isn’t expanding the map recklessly — it’s fortifying the music throne so every other territory becomes an extension of it. Actor, endorser, producer — all of it downstream of one durable brand: the creator-genius singer, Ji-ho.”

He closed the tablet and met Ji-ho’s eyes, steady.

“That requires your conviction and your participation. A planning manager isn’t a commander — he’s a strategic partner and a firewall. If you’re willing, we start by re-cutting the next three months of your schedule with this thinking built in, and measure what happens.”

Ji-ho sat with it a moment. Not everything the man said was right — but everything the man said had been PREPARED, and the difference between those two things was the whole industry. He needed exactly this: someone building the map while he walked it. First impressions: strong.

“Hyun-woo-ssi.” The first fully relaxed smile of the meeting. “I think we need to talk in detail about that schedule re-cut. Starting with next week’s variety interview — how about it?”

A nod; the faintest ghost of a smile crossed the composed face.

“The honor’s mine. I’ll have concrete entry points prepared by tomorrow.”

The meeting moved into its productive hours over cooling coffee.
