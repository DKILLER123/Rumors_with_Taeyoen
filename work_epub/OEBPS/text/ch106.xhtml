The Debut Stage

Friday Morning KBS Open Hall — Backstage hairspray, coffee, and adrenaline

The corridor ran like a hive at full production: idols in finished hair and makeup, managers moving at collision speed, assistants steering wardrobe racks through impossible gaps. The air itself smelled of hairspray, coffee, and nerves.

In the waiting room, Ji-ho’s styling wrapped — and today, the system wardrobe earned its keep.

A knock; IU’s head appeared around the door. She was already in her stage outfit — an ivory dress with an unusual cut, fresh with a whisper of something bolder — and her smile was easy.

“Ready, Ji-ho? Time to go pay respects to the sunbaes.”

“Just follow me. You know the greeting etiquette.”

He drew a breath and nodded.

“Ne. In your care.”

The greeting circuit turned out to be a miniature field course in industry sociology. IU — national little sister, digital juggernaut, and tonight not only a performer but one of the show’s MCs — opened doors for his brand-new face that would otherwise have stayed shut for a year.

Outside Super Junior’s enormous waiting room, the laughter was audible through the wall. IU pushed in with the ease of long acquaintance; inside, a small army was mid-final-prep. Leeteuk and Eunhyuk answered warmly as leader and delegate; Kim Heechul lounged against the makeup counter, gaze making one slow appraising loop around Ji-ho before he grinned at IU.

“Ji-eun-ah. You ‘killed’ the whole of Melon this time. Genuinely impressive.”

“Sunbae-nims — annyeonghaseyo.” She dipped a small bow. Ji-ho bowed with her, posture textbook-respectful.

Leeteuk’s expression carried something complicated; he clapped Ji-ho’s shoulder and pulled him upright.

“Ji-ho. Hwaiting! Show them something today.”

“Ne. Thank you, sunbae-nim.”

“Then — sunbae-nims, please prepare comfortably. We’ll be going.” IU waved.

“Hold on.” Heechul’s voice stopped them at the door. His finger found Ji-ho. “Yah. Kid. Come here.”

“…Me?” When did I offend Kim Heechul?

“Yes, you. Here.” Patience was not among Heechul’s listed talents; he simply crossed the room and towed Ji-ho off by the arm. “A sunbae calls and you DAWDLE.”

“Heechul oppa—” IU started forward. “What do you need with Ji-ho?”

“Private matter. PRIVATE.” All grin. He steered Ji-ho into a corner, dropped an arm over his shoulders, bent them both into a conspiratorial huddle, and lowered his voice. “Yah. Numbers. We’re trading numbers, and then you find a free evening and bring Taeyeon out for dinner.”

“That brat. Dating and she doesn’t even TELL me. She’s buying the meal. It’s decided.”

“…Eh?”

Heechul checked the perimeter with theatrical care and dropped lower still.

“That day. I SAW you two holding hands heading upstairs. You’re together. Obviously.”

Ji-ho went diplomatically silent — no confirmation, no denial. Heechul was close with all of Girls’ Generation, but this wasn’t his secret to hand out. That call belonged to Taeyeon.

Heechul read the silence and didn’t push. Out came the phone.

“Numbers. And from now on, just call me hyung.”

They traded contacts, and Ji-ho rejoined IU at the door.

“The duet with Ji-eun is TOO sweet — it made me want to date! Ji-ho-ssi, write us one next time, yeah?”

T-ara’s room ran at a different temperature. Mid-ascent and scheduled to the bone, the fatigue on them was visible from the doorway. Park So-yeon and Hahm Eun-jung greeted them kindly; Jeon Boram took one look at Ji-ho’s height, managed a hello, and relocated behind Qri; Areum and Dani offered polite bows.

Then Jiyeon — checking her makeup at the mirror — turned at the sound of them. Cat-eyes, lovely and faintly distant, met Ji-ho’s for exactly one unremarkable beat before she launched herself at IU with a full-warmth hug.

“Ji-eun-ah! The song’s SO good — congratulations.”

“And Ji-ho — wah, genuinely impressive. Writing something like that.”

Brisk. Clean. Absolutely nothing underneath it.

A completely different person from the drunk girl of that night — as if the memory had been surgically removed. Suspicion turned over in his chest; not one millimeter of it reached his face. He returned the courtesy in kind.

In the corner, one more figure — Hwayoung. Two strictly ceremonial nods were exchanged, and that was all.

The f(x) room was easier air entirely. IU and Sulli were practically family — and Sulli, at the sight of Ji-ho, lit up like a struck match and bounced over to seize his arm with both hands.

“Oppa! Congratulations on debuting! The song’s been on loop since day one! It’s AMAZING!”

The unconcealed closeness turned her members’ heads. Krystal folded her arms, leaned back, and raised exactly one eyebrow.

“I recommended it to all the Snowballs, you know.” Full credit-claiming mode. “AND to my friends on the drama set. Personally.”

He looked down at that face, transparently fishing for praise, and ruffled her hair with a laugh.

“Sulli-yah. Thank you.”

She narrowed her eyes in open contentment and settled in against his side, shamelessly breathing him in.

Victoria came over, sincere.

“Congratulations. You’re really something.”

“Thank you, Victoria nuna. Your new song’s charting well too — feeling confident about first place?”

She shrugged with a laugh.

“Not remotely. This week it’s Super Junior sunbae-nims, or T-ara, or SISTAR. We only made the candidate slot in June — this round we’re pacing runners again.”

The field, honestly assessed: Super Junior’s “Sexy, Free & Single” — the song itself middling, the fandom colossal, the album sales enormous. T-ara’s “Day by Day” — choreography and song both dangerous. SISTAR’s “Loving U” — the digital queens, always in the hunt. And “Some” — a digital monster with a real shot.

BEAST. 2PM. INFINITE. After School. Room after room: greetings given, encouragement or appraisal received. Ji-ho kept a rookie’s proper humility, but the straight spine and unhurried answers said something else was underneath it. IU filled the occasional silence with a deft half-sentence, defusing cold spots before they formed.

“How was that?” she asked quietly on the walk back.

“Like a speed-interview marathon.” He loosened his collar and told the truth. “But necessary. Thank you for walking me through all of it, nuna.”

“From here you walk it alone.” A smile. “But your first step was steady.”

08:00–12:00 · Pre-Recording Main Stage two stools, one warm spotlight

The “Some” staging was minimal by design: an LED backdrop of city night, two tall stools center stage, and around them a soft riot of balloons, flowers, and one enormous plush bear — everything built to funnel attention onto two voices and the space between them.

Pre-recording meant take after take for the edit, plus safety tracks: lighting passes, blocking checks, camera-eye rehearsal. IU, a veteran of a hundred of these mornings, towed him through the workflow at speed. And when the music ran, the director’s voice kept arriving through the talkback, shaving the performance finer:

“Ji-ho-ssi — on the what-are-we line, give Ji-eun a more lost look. Like the question costs you something.”

“Ji-eun — when you push back on how slow he is, lean a few degrees toward him. Scold him with your posture.”

Adjust, repeat, adjust — until the cameras caught exactly the pull-and-release the song was made of. It was his first music show, but the recording sessions had drilled the emotional map into him, and he was a fast study by unfair margins.

✦   ✦   ✦

Afternoon rehearsal, 1:00 to 4:00: full run-throughs with the house band and sound crew. Blocking, camera marks — and above all the two-shot grammar: when to meet eyes, when to miss them, when to let the song’s want-and-retreat show through. The lighting design wrapped them in a single warm cone — a private room the whole country was invited to look into.

Between passes, Ji-ho stood in a corridor corner running his marks — and walked into Sulli and Jiyeon, heads together with IU, laughter ringing off the concrete.

“Ji-eun, tonight’s number one is obviously Super Junior sunbae-nims, right? But ‘Some’ will DEFINITELY make the candidate slot!” Sulli had IU’s arm hostage.

“Mm — I wouldn’t be so sure.” IU’s smile curved as she spotted him and waved him over. “Who takes first tonight is very much an open question.”

Music Bank’s K-Chart: digital 65%, album 5%, broadcast 20%, viewer votes 10%. She had years of these Fridays behind her — and with “Some” posting those digital numbers, a missing 5% of album points was a rounding error. Besides. She had rather a lot of fans herself.

Jiyeon glanced over as he joined them — makeup immaculate, eyes clear, entirely natural with IU and Sulli, entirely calm toward him.

“Ji-ho. Rehearsal going smoothly?”

“Very. Thanks for asking, Ji-yeon.”

The wrongness of it deepened another notch. He’d braced for awkwardness at the reunion; instead: weather-report pleasantness. Did the blackout really take everything? Or is the acting simply that good?

No time to dig. Rehearsal resumed.

17:00–18:30 KST · LIVE Music Bank — Open Hall a sea of lightsticks

The hall was packed to the rafters, fandom colors pooling into a single electric sea. The broadcast opened on MCs Lee Jang-woo and IU at full energy, and then the stages came like waves:

SISTAR’s “Loving U,” a face-full of high summer. T-ara’s “Day by Day,” narrative choreography, gorgeous and sad. After School’s “Flashback,” heels striking with drilled precision. INFINITE’s “The Chaser,” knife-line formations pulling gasps out of the crowd. f(x)’s “Electric Shock,” electronic pulse and concept from five years in the future.

Backstage, Ji-ho watched the heat come off the monitors and closed his eyes, breathing the noise out of his body.

“Nervous?” IU looked infuriatingly at ease.

“A little. Mostly — eager.” His eyes opened. “All I have to do is sing ‘Some’ to everyone in the country. That’s all.”

“Exactly right.” She smiled. “Remember the pre-record. Look at my eyes — like the recording booth. Same room, more people.”

And then the MC call: next — the collaboration stage everyone’s been waiting for: IU, and rookie vocalist Ji-ho — “SOME!”

Performance

Blackout. Two warm spots ignite on two stools, center stage.

They sit facing each other, and the minimal set fills instantly with story. The intro spills out like water, and his low, magnetic line enters first — the restlessness that comes and goes while the feeling underneath refuses to move — the camera pushing in on a face wearing the city’s particular weary confusion.

Her voice joins, sweet with complicated weather, and the frame becomes a two-shot. They almost never look at each other — each gazing into private middle distance — but the glances that DO connect arc like static, carrying the whole is-it-mine-or-isn’t-it ache of the lyric.

On the line about a someone who is like a lover, and is not a lover, and is somehow a lover anyway, she tilts her head toward him — searching, exasperated, hoping. He holds her look, lips parting on something unsaid, and lowers his eyes instead: the entire undefinable thing, rendered in one dropped gaze.

In the audience, the sighs are audible — low, involuntary, mostly female.

No belted money-note. No choreography. Voice-grain, emotional layering, and eye-acting hold the entire hall still. The hook climbs, two voices braided, pushing that unnamed almost to its ceiling — and the last line, his exhausted plea for the words themselves, cuts to silence and falling light.

One suspended beat — then the hall detonates.

The camera panned across an audience still visibly inside the song. The stage had done the improbable: translated the audio’s ambiguity into something you could watch — high-grade emotional tension, live and unfiltered.

Backstage, staff and waiting artists alike turned approving looks on them. Ji-ho exhaled everything and traded a high-five with IU. Done. Done.

The hall stirred hard at the digital gap — at 65% weighting, the race had effectively ended there.

“Congratulations!! This week’s Music Bank first place — IU and Ji-ho, ‘SOME’!!”

The cheering came down like surf, threaded with open disbelief. First place — debut week — a collaboration single — over a top-tier boy group at full deployment. The kind of rookie miracle that happens once in a market cycle.

T-ara and f(x) stood frozen for a beat — then Sulli whooped, seized Jiyeon, and cheered for her oppa and her best friend at once.

On stage, Ji-ho visibly blanked — then the smile broke out of him, wide and utterly unguarded. Beside him IU’s startled beat dissolved into laughter. See? First place was always OURS.

She bowed, then touched his arm — together, forward. The award. The flowers. The trophy, cold metal and honest weight in his hands.

First place. I actually did it.

Ten thousand eyes, all cheering for him — so THIS was the feeling. Vanity, fed to bursting. Dangerously addictive.

She passed him the microphone. He looked out over the excited, astonished faces, and his voice — vibrating slightly — carried through the hall.

“Thank you. Thank you, Music Bank — thank you to everyone who loved ‘Some’ and voted for us. Thank you to the producer who sat with me through every take and every suggestion. And thank you to my partner, IU nuna — without her interpretation, this song would not be what it is.”

He turned and bowed to her; she returned it, laughing.

“‘Some’ is about all the unclear, undefined, unbearably precious flutterings we carry around. To share that feeling through music and receive this much love — as a rookie, there’s no greater honor. I’ll keep working, and bring you better music. Thank you!”

Measured words, humble posture, first summit — no wobble anywhere in it.

He sings with the staged tension stripped out — all release and unguarded warmth now, trophy in hand — and drops in a short, sharp improvised dance break that turns the front rows into a single scream.

And the encore’s texture — the formula-free ease between the two of them, the shared-trophy grins, the glances of plain professional delight — poured out through the live signal into a million living rooms.

The Same Evening Girls’ Generation Dorm — Living Room the price of a secret

Taeyeon sat cross-legged on the rug, eyes nailed to the screen.

At the announcement she’d punched the air with a strangled “Yes!” At his speech, her mouth had climbed without permission — the producer who sat with me through every take. That producer is ME.

Ji-ho. My nam-chin. You’re really, truly amazing. …I miss you so much.

And then the encore — and her smile, degree by degree, went out.

She was happy for him. Fiercely, honestly happy — happier than for any first place of her own. That was her boyfriend up there, her chosen person, proving his worth in the most public way that exists.

But the cameras kept finding it: IU and Ji-ho laughing into each other’s faces. IU’s easy tap on his arm. The wordless synchronization of two people sharing one triumph. And a cold, fine-needled ache began crawling up from somewhere deep in her chest.

IU. Ji-eun. The name the media had always lined up next to hers — digital scores, national reach, musical identity — the comparison that never quite stopped, solo debut or no solo debut. There was no bad blood; there was even something like friendliness. But the scale had always hung in the air between them. And Taeyeon had her pride.

And now her own boyfriend stood on the first-place podium beside the comparison herself, singing a duet about butterflies — her duet — while the whole internet cooed over their “chemistry” and their “heart-fluttering gazes.”

That’s MY song. He wrote that song for ME.

The better “Some” performed, the happier she was — his success. And the happier she got, the more it hurt — his success was fused to IU, and every viral clip of their staged almost-love scraped the same soft place raw.

The better it does, the happier I am. The happier I am, the sadder I get. Solve for X. There is no X.

“Wah — first place! Taeyeon eonni, you always said this song would hit, right? Ji-ho and IU are both SO impressive!” Seohyun emerged toweling her hair, glowing with uncomplicated goodwill at the encore on screen.

The words hit the raw spot dead center — and the reply was out before her brain could intercept it.

“Impressive? What’s impressive? The song is exceptional, sure — but plenty of people could have sung it.”

Hard-edged. Graceless. Seohyun’s towel stopped mid-motion, her eyes going uncertain. Doesn’t eonni get along fine with IU…?

Kim Hyo-yeon wandered out in a sheet mask, caught the tail of it, and peered at the TV.

“Omo, Ji-ho? Standing next to IU, honestly, easy on the eyes. And the song’s great — no WONDER the scores—”

A casual remark — landing with sniper precision on the single most inflamed nerve available.

“Easy on the EYES? Hyo-yeon, since when is your taste this shallow? It’s the FACE now?” Taeyeon was on her feet before she knew it, voice climbing, heat in it. “The scores are good because the SONG is good and the promotion is good — what does the face have to do with anything? Can either of you engage with the actual MUSIC?”

The living room air set solid.

Seohyun’s lips pressed thin, hurt rising in her eyes. Hyo-yeon peeled off the mask, pure bafflement underneath.

“Taeyeon — what’s WRONG with you? We didn’t say anything?”

And she knew it. Knew she’d misfired at the two most innocent people in the country — two dongsaengs reacting normally to a good stage by someone they knew. What could she say? That’s my boyfriend, so I don’t enjoy hearing you call him and his duet partner a matched set?

She couldn’t.

The trapped heat had nowhere to go — couldn’t be vented, couldn’t be swallowed. She spun on her heel.

“I’m tired. Going to sleep first.”

The door of her room shut hard behind her, leaving Hyo-yeon and Seohyun exchanging one long look.

“Seohyun-ah… is Taeyeon under too much pressure lately?”

“I don’t know.” Seohyun’s worried eyes stayed on the closed door. “But… she seems really unhappy.”

What they couldn’t see: on the far side of that door, Taeyeon sliding slowly down to the floor, face buried in her knees, while the encore’s cheerful music leaked faintly through the wall like a taunt.

She had cheered his triumph. She could not make peace with the too-bright scenery standing next to it. And the tangle — pride, love, vinegar, helplessness — had lashed out at the two most blameless people in her life.

I know it’s wrong. Unprofessional. Childish. I KNOW.

But feelings, tonight, were a bolted horse — and all she could do was sit on the floor of her room, holding a heavy, glorious, unshareable joy, and wait to grow big enough to carry it.
