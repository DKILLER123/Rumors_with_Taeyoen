Chapter Thirty-Two

A Den of Sirens, a Kingdom of Women

Ji-ho finished drawing the curtain and walked back to the bedside, polite smile in place.

“Yoon-a sunbae-nim. Ji-ho — that’s me.”

“Call me nuna.” She extended her hand, eyes warm with open gratitude. “Truly — thank you for saving Sulli.”

As a woman working this industry, Yoon-a understood with terrible precision what that night could have cost — and what stopping it was worth. Her goodwill required no performance at all.

“Ne, Yoon-a nuna.”

He took her hand — and registered, involuntarily, the sheer silk of it: long slender fingers, small fine knuckles, skin pale and smooth as white jade. The kind of hand you had to consciously remember to release.

“Yoon-a — where’s Taeyeon and the others?”

“Taeyeon eonni’s group should be right behind me — they’re coming from the dorm, I came straight from home, so I beat them here.” A beat. “Oh — where’s Hyo-yeon eonni?”

“Out. Don’t know where.”

The flatness in Tiffany’s tone said plenty. In truth, she and Kim Hyo-yeon had gone at each other first thing that morning — a real fight — and Hyo-yeon had walked out and not come back. Yoon-a, whose social radar missed nothing, read the weather instantly and steered elsewhere.

“Eonni — what actually HAPPENED last night?”

“Last night—”

“FANY!!”

The door burst open a second time. Taeyeon came through it at speed, the rest of Girls’ Generation streaming in behind her like a well-dressed cavalry charge.

Tiffany’s entire affect flipped to wounded kitten. Arms out, maximum pathos:

“Taeyeon-ah!”

“Are you okay?!” Taeyeon seized both her hands, eyes raking her top to bottom. “How does it feel? How bad?”

“Hairline fracture. Left leg. It hurts SO much.”

“That much?” Jessica was already bending over the bed, lifting the blanket, tugging the pant leg up to reveal a stretch of pale calf, frowning. “No cast?”

“Just a surface crack in the cortical bone, away from the joint. Doctor says rest and follow-ups will do it.”

“Oh thank god.”

Then Choi Soo-young spotted the tall figure squeezed politely into the corner.

“And who’s this handsome thing?”

“Soo-young sunbae-nim, hello — I’m Ji-ho.”

“Ji-ho?” Kwon Yu-ri closed in, examining him from shoes to hairline. “THE Ji-ho? The one who saved Sulli?”

And then — whoosh — the entirety of Girls’ Generation converged on him at once, curiosity weaponized, questions overlapping like gunfire:

“A new trainee? How have we never seen you?”

“The news explained NOTHING — what actually happened last night?”

“Yes yes — tell us everything, from the start!”

“He looks so young — how old are you?”

“WHO was the bastard? Tell me who it was.”

“Ji-ho. We meet again.”

Girls’ Generation, mid-twenties and mid-ascension: dresses over long pale legs, slip tops on jade-carved arms, a mingled cloud of high-end perfume closing in from every compass point. Pinned at the center of it, Ji-ho experienced the distinct sensation of a sailor hearing the first bars of siren song and noticing, too late, the rocks.

“Sunbae-nims, I—”

“YAH — one at a time! You’ll scare the poor kid!” Tiffany knew her chaos-goblin teammates far too well.

Soo-young’s eyes went instantly, gleefully dangerous.

“Ohhh-ho. Protective already, are we?”

“Well — hero saves the beauty, and the hero’s handsome,” Sunny piled on with a fire-stoking grin. “And our Fany IS a face fanatic. A fluttering heart is only natural~”

“YAH!”

Tiffany went red to the ears — and stole exactly one glance at Ji-ho while her pulse did something undignified. Because, fine, yes: she was a top-tier face fanatic, and last night’s version of him — decisive, unshakable, the kind of man who simply handled things — had landed. Hard. But she also knew the ledger: she was Girls’ Generation and he was a trainee; the gap in station was a canyon and the age gap wasn’t small either. Being teased about it in front of him was unbearable. She grabbed the wheel of the conversation and hauled:

“Taeyeon-ah! What did you mean, ‘we meet AGAIN’? You two know each other?!”

“I examined him.” Taeyeon nodded, remembering. “His entrance evaluation — I was on the panel. His singing is no joke. He left an impression.”

“Really?” Tiffany dug through her memory. “Wait — you DID say there was some incredible rookie… that was HIM?”

Taeyeon tipped her head back to look up at him — at 158 centimeters, conversing with Ji-ho involved a certain amount of neck — felt vaguely like she was addressing a lighthouse, and drifted half a step closer to Sunny on instinct.

“Mm. That was Ji-ho.”

“If Taeyeon says he’s good,” Jessica shrugged, “then he’s terrifying.”

“Okay, skill later, STORY now.” Yu-ri was practically vibrating. “Last night. Every detail. I am DYING.”

Ji-ho glanced at Tiffany. She sent back a reassuring nod.

“It’s fine. The members all know exactly what stays in this room.”

He collected his thoughts and began.

“I was working my part-time shift when…”

✦   ✦   ✦

An hour later, Ji-ho left Tiffany’s room carrying the second fruit basket, bound for Sulli’s ward.

The retelling had left Girls’ Generation collectively incandescent with outrage — and his stock with them climbing like a rocket off the pad. He’d walked out with every member’s phone number and KakaoTalk, and the honorifics had melted from courteous “sunbae-nim” to warm “nuna” across the board. His mood, understandably, was excellent.

He knocked at Sulli’s door.

“Come in!”

He pushed it open — and privately marveled. Straight out of the sirens’ den and into the kingdom of women.

The room was wall-to-wall with them: Victoria, Amber, Luna, Krystal arrayed around the bed — and beside them IU and Goo Hara, every one of them young and luminous and entirely different flavors of dazzling.

Sulli saw him and lit up like sunrise. She dropped IU’s hand, scrambled off the bed, and met him at the door.

“You— you came to see me.”

“Sulli sunbae-nim.” He raised the basket. “Get better soon, okay?”

“Don’t call me sunbae.” She took the basket, smiling up at him, eyes glittering bright. “What— what year were you born?”
