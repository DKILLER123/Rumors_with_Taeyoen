Chapter Thirty-One

Hospital Visits, and Drama Queen Yoon-a

Quest Settlement · Win the Fans — Stage Two

— Bound platforms —

MeTube23,331
VidTube7,129

Rewards: Professional-grade Piano  ·  Skill Upgrade Card ×1  ·  Opportunity Card ×1

[Skill Upgrade Card] — Raise any one skill by a full proficiency tier.

[Opportunity Card] — Redeem one opportunity in a chosen domain: Romance · Money · Connections · Career.

Ji-ho stared at the settlement screen and grinned like a man who’d found money in an old coat.

Professional-grade piano — instantly, endlessly useful. The Skill Upgrade Card he’d bank for later; his only upgradeable skill was the God of Learning, and burning the card at level one would be criminal waste. Better to let the levels climb first. As for the Opportunity Card — Romance and Money went straight off the table without ceremony. Connections and Career, though… both glittered. That decision deserved a clear head and a slow evening. Shelved, for now.

What genuinely puzzled him was the fan count. As a trainee under a confidentiality agreement, he’d posted exactly one workout video to VidTube since signing and then gone silent. Where had this flood come from? He’d half-assumed the fan quests would sit frozen until debut.

He opened MeTube and found the answer waiting: twenty-odd thousand new subscribers and an inbox drifted deep with gratitude and praise. The scandal had swept him onto the trending charts — and everyone who discovered the hero trainee kept an old channel had come flooding in. Ten thousand–plus fans from a single news cycle.

He remembered the game’s next milestone well: at 100,000 fans, the platform restriction lifted entirely. Seventy-seven thousand to go — and as a trainee forbidden from posting freely, only one road led there fast. Debut. Everything keeps pointing at debut.

Rewards banked, he hit the ab board with a convert’s zeal. That slogan — “Every rep you do on me is one more you’ll do on her” — struck him as funnier and more motivational every single time he read it. One more. One more. Fine — one more.

After a thorough session and a hot shower, he felt scoured clean, every pore breathing. And then, abruptly, came an unfamiliar sensation: free time. Weeks of lockstep training had left him with no idea what to do with an empty afternoon — until he remembered Tiffany and Sulli were still hospitalized. A visit was simple decency. It was also, he noted without shame, excellent relationship-building.

Visiting beautiful women demanded proper effort. He picked a deep navy fitted cardigan over a plain white tee — simple, quietly expensive-looking — with well-cut straight-leg jeans breaking just above the ankle, and clean loafers. Scholarly, but sharp.

He’d traded contacts with Tiffany and Sulli the night before; after a moment’s thought he called Tiffany, got the hospital address and room number, and rode the subway over.

Empty-handed visits were out of the question — and picking hospital fruit was its own small science. The rules: easy to peel, no pits. Jeju mandarins, strawberries, blueberries, bananas — yes. Apples needed a knife; grapes stained bedsheets; watermelon was huge, unshareable, and criminally priced. And Jeju mandarins carried the extra grace note of good fortune and health — local, beloved, affordable. He picked through the fruit stall outside the station with real care and carried the basket in.

Knock knock.

“Come in!”

Tiffany was propped up in bed scrolling the news. At the sight of him her eyes did a visible double-take — an eighteen-year-old with THIS kind of eye for clothes? The cardigan’s fine knit sat perfectly against that composed, quiet presence of his. Unfairly well put together.

“Tiffany sunbae-nim. Good morning.”

“Ji-ho! In, in, come IN.” She waved with both hands, glowing. “Have you seen the news? The whole internet is calling you brave AND brilliant.”

“Haven’t had the chance.” He took the bedside chair and set the basket gently on the table. “Sunbae — get well soon. I mean it.”

She put the phone down and looked at him properly. Last night replayed on its own: this boy planting himself between her and danger, dismantling two grown men in about six seconds. That — that was what a man taking responsibility looked like. Warmth rose up unbidden.

“‘Sunbae’ is so cold and distant. From now on — nuna.”

“Yes, nuna.” Instant, smooth adaptation. He plucked a mandarin from the basket. “Nuna, let me peel one for you.”

“Wait — Ji-ho, could I trouble you with the curtain first?” She pointed left, wincing faintly. “The sun’s right in my eyes.”

“On it, nuna.”

He rose and crossed toward the window—

BANG!

The door flew open and a tall, willow-slim woman burst through it — light gray crewneck, white straight trousers, little white sneakers, youth incarnate — currently scrubbing at her eyes with the back of one hand, face arranged into operatic tragedy.

“EONNI! EONNIII!”

She flung herself at the bedside, hands hovering over Tiffany’s leg — desperate to touch it, not daring to — voice thick with theatrical grief.

“Eonni. It hurts SO much, doesn’t it!”

“Don’t you worry. We would never abandon you. You are still our Tiffany, shinier than any gemstone — and from now on, on every stage, I will carry you. On my back. You’ll lie on my back and sing, eonni!”

“YAH! Yoon-a!” Tiffany didn’t know whether to laugh or file a complaint. “It’s a HAIRLINE fracture! A few days of rest! My leg is not FALLING OFF!”

Yoon-a was far too deep in the role to hear direction from the cheap seats.

“I’ll carry you to accept awards, eonni. I’ll carry you through entire concerts. Even when you date someday — I’ll carry you on the dates. We’re family forever, eonni — hukhukhuk—”

Tiffany gave up and turned her head — to find Ji-ho standing at the curtain, frozen mid-reach, staring in open awe. Her hand crept up to half-cover her face. I could curl my toes hard enough to dig a three-room apartment right now.

At last the performer registered her audience’s distress, tracked Tiffany’s sightline, turned — and locked eyes with a strange and startlingly handsome young man.

Time stopped. Yoon-a’s entire operating system blue-screened. There’s a STRANGER here. A handsome one. He saw. He saw ALL of it. What do I do what do I DO — the SHAME—

Then, with the composure of a true professional, she rebooted. Simple. If I’m not embarrassed, the embarrassment belongs to everyone else.

She rose in one fluid motion, transformed instantly into a portrait of elegance, swept a strand of hair behind her ear, smoothed her sweater, and produced a flawless social smile.

“Eonni. And this gentleman is…?”

“Yoon-a sunbae-nim — I’m Ji-ho.”

“YOU’RE Ji-ho?”
