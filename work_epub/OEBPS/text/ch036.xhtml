Chapter Thirty-Six

Filming “Twinkle”

There’s an old saying in the entertainment industry: the moment you get famous, everyone around you mysteriously becomes a good person.

For his first month on the Peninsula, Ji-ho — the foreign trainee, the outsider, the gyopo — had eaten his share of cold shoulders and could count his acquaintances on one hand. Then he saved Sulli, and overnight the entire company knew his name and had apparently always adored him.

With the fifty-million-won commendation landing after month-end evaluations, he’d quit the bar job outright and thrown every hour into training. Night after night, the last two bodies in the building were the same two bodies: Ji-ho and Irene — and all those shared midnights had quietly worn the formality off their acquaintance.

Tiffany’s injury proved mercifully minor; she was back at the company within three days. SM promptly issued a press release — Tiffany returns despite injuries, throwing herself into sub-unit album preparations — and the fandom, deeply moved, swore up and down to buy the album twice.

TaeTiSeo — TTS — the sub-unit history would crown among K-pop’s strongest, stood on the eve of their first mini-album, “Twinkle.” Recording was done. Only the music video remained.

And this MV was no small production in the scheme of things: TaeTiSeo’s debut visual, and simultaneously a keystone of SM’s EXO rollout. EXO-K’s Park Chanyeol, Byun Baekhyun, Oh Sehun, and Kim Jongin would all cameo — the classic SM “seniors carry the rookies” maneuver. And this time, the company had written Ji-ho into the call sheet too.

Having never touched a shoot in his life, he arrived at the soundstage almost indecently early. Rookie protocol was absolute: every staff member he passed got a bow and a bright greeting. The crew — SM’s in-house production team — turned out to be a friendly bunch. Courtesies complete, he found a seat with a wide view and studied the floor like a scholarship depended on it. MVs were the lifeblood of an idol’s image; free masterclasses didn’t come along every day.

A stir at the entrance: the TaeTiSeo van had arrived. He rose and headed for the door, where the EXO-K members — there a while already — were greeting the three women enthusiastically.

Tiffany had been scanning for him since before her feet hit the pavement. The moment she found him, her face lit up.

“Ji-ho! Over here, quick!”

“Fany nuna!”

He jogged over, hand raised — and was halfway into greeting Taeyeon and Seohyun when Tiffany commandeered his arm.

“Come, introductions. These are Park Chanyeol, Byun Baekhyun, Oh Sehun, Kim Jongin — EXO, debuting any minute now.”

“Sunbae-nims. Hello.”

“And THIS,” she announced to the EXO juniors, “is Ji-ho. You’ve heard the name lately, I assume. He’s my very dear little brother.”

“Of course we’ve heard.” Chanyeol’s grin was as big as the rest of him. “The hero trainee who saved Sulli sunbae. Yixing hyung and the others keep saying they want to meet you.”

“Ji-ho-ssi, hello, hello.”

Greetings all around. And — Taeyeon being Taeyeon-adjacent business — Ji-ho found his gaze lingering an extra beat on Byun Baekhyun.

“Yah, Ji-ho. Only your Fany nuna fits in those eyes of yours? Your First Disciple is standing RIGHT here and doesn’t rate a hello?”

He surfaced to find Taeyeon with her arm looped through Seohyun’s, radiating theatrical grievance. Explaining that he’d been interrupted mid-greeting would be tactical suicide; he chose the other road, eyes widening in staged wonder.

“Aigoo — Taeyeon nuna and Joo-hyun nuna! Forgive me. I genuinely mistook you both for a pair of fairies descending, and my mortal tongue seized up.”

Corny? Deeply. Effective? Devastatingly. The two of them dissolved on contact.

“Mwoya — hahahaha — FAIRIES, he says—”

“Wait.” Tiffany looked lost. “First Disciple? What First Disciple?”

“Eonni — The One seonsaeng-nim took Ji-ho as a formal disciple,” Seohyun supplied.

“REALLY?! The One seonsaeng-nim?!”

“Ne. You were still in the hospital, nuna — I hadn’t gotten the chance to tell you.”

“Wah. Daebak.” Tiffany rated her own talent and technique honestly — good, genuinely good, and still nowhere near The One’s bar for discipleship. Which meant she understood better than most exactly what this kid’s ceiling looked like. She thumped his shoulder. “Congratulations. Karaoke, soon. I need to witness this myself.”

A Tiffany-organized karaoke night meant Girls’ Generation attendance, which meant… his calendar had never wanted anything more.

“Just call me whenever you go, nuna.”

“All right, Fany, we still have to see the director.”

“I know, I know—” Tiffany paused, then simply grabbed Ji-ho again. “You come too. Meet the director.”

“Me?”

“Don’t dawdle! Tight schedule today. Move those long legs.”

Watching him get towed away, Baekhyun sighed with naked envy.

“He’s SO close with the sunbaes…”

“Not a thing you can copy,” Chanyeol said philosophically. “Come on — our turn to greet the director.”

TaeTiSeo, plus one, found director Park Seung-hyun by the monitors.

“Seung-hyun oppa~!”

“Fany, Taeyeon, our Hyun! You made it.”

“Seung-hyun oppa, we’re in your hands today.”

“Oppa, you’d better shoot us gorgeous today, understood?”

“Relax, relax — gorgeous is guaranteed. Have I ever failed you?”

“Then thank you in advance~” Tiffany pulled Ji-ho forward. “Oppa, this is Ji-ho — first MV ever. Take care of him for me?”

“Director-nim. Hello.”

“Ohh~ so YOU’RE Ji-ho.” The director looked him up and down with open approval. “Good-looking kid. Don’t worry — after what you did, shooting you handsome is a matter of personal honor.”

“Thank you, Director-nim.”

“Seung-hyun oppa, we’ll go get styled then — you finish your set.”

“Go, go. The backdrop needs another minute anyway.”

Riding Tiffany’s coattails, Ji-ho got waved into makeup alongside her — while EXO waited outside for the room to free up. How to describe the feeling… Being nuna’s kept man has a certain flavor to it. And the flavor is delicious.

On the Peninsula, makeup was social infrastructure. Ji-ho, as a former lounge singer, was no stranger to it — but light stage makeup was one thing; the full production was another animal. Full makeup meant a professional artist, forty to sixty minutes, a dozen-plus products, coordinated hair, accessories, and nails — a complete construction project.

The artist started with base: matte, long-wear, sweat-proof — high-coverage foundation layered with concealer, set hard through the T-zone, the whole face leveled to porcelain. Eyes were the centerpiece: shadow built in three graduated layers, a bold upswept line, dense false lashes, aegyo-sal brightened, outer corners deepened. Berry-toned lips. Sculpted shading — nose, jawline, cheekbones — then saturated blush to finish.

That pipeline could upgrade a average civilian into a head-turner. Run it on a face the system rated 8 out of 10, and the output walked out of the chair with sword-straight brows, starlit eyes, glossed lips, and an upswept flick at the corner of each eye that added a distinct note of menace to the handsome.

Tiffany’s build took longer, and she still had wardrobe after; Ji-ho excused himself to wait outside. Thirty-odd minutes later, all three of TaeTiSeo emerged more or less at once — and the difference detonated on contact.

Gone were the easygoing off-duty women. Full-dress TaeTiSeo radiated: skin lit matte-porcelain under clean bases; upswept liner and curled lashes over saturated true-red lips, glamorous and frankly predatory; matching double top-knots and slim black chokers; enormous fluffy fur coats — pink for Tiffany, blue for Taeyeon, purple for Seohyun — over camisoles and shorts, the sweet and the dangerous dialed to maximum simultaneously.

Ji-ho wasn’t the only one staring. The three of them were getting their own first look at full-makeup Ji-ho, and the appreciation ran mutual. But industry veterans recover fast: Tiffany folded her arms, sauntered over, and deployed.

“Excuse me — handsome stranger? I’m looking for a tall boy named Ji-ho. You wouldn’t happen to know where he went?”

With her approach came a wall of rich rose perfume — and inside the pink fur, a sweetheart neckline that made a compelling case for staring directly at the floor. He hauled his eyes up and away, strangling the awe before it reached his face.

“Nuna. Is it really that dramatic?”

“OBVIOUSLY.” She had absolutely clocked where his gaze had snagged, and straightened with unconcealed triumph. “You’ve gone from obedient golden retriever to full delinquent wolfhound in one makeup chair. Hasn’t he, Taeyeon?”

“Oh? …Right. Absolutely.” A full second’s lag before the answer arrived.

Because today’s Ji-ho happened to strike Taeyeon’s aesthetic dead center — that aggressive young-wolf styling had briefly disconnected her from the conversation entirely.

“All three nunas look staggering, for the record. I’m honestly nervous standing here.”

“Oh really?” Tiffany’s eyes did a full revolution of mischief. She dipped into a pose, all bare shoulder and wicked smile. “Then — which of the three of us makes you MORE nervous?”

Taeyeon and Seohyun fell in beside her, striking coordinated poses on pure groupmate instinct. Ji-ho made a genuine survey, twice, gaze pausing one professional beat at a certain sweetheart neckline — a man settles his debts; that view wasn’t free — and delivered the verdict.

“All three are flawless. But personally… I’ve always liked pink.”

“EXCELLENT taste!” Tiffany was delighted beyond disguise.

Taeyeon and Seohyun’s faces fell in perfect unison. Taeyeon shot him a glare with actual edges on it.

“Hyun-ah. We’re LEAVING.”

“Yes, eonni!”

“HAHAHA—” Tiffany smacked his arm, victorious. “Okay, I’m off to shoot. Watch and learn, wolfhound.”

“Yes, nuna.”

“Twinkle” was a lean production: one or two stage sets, straightforward camera moves, minimal effects. Dance blocking, lip-sync coverage, a few light story beats — core photography in a day, pickups the next. The real marathon belonged to TaeTiSeo’s stylists: eight full looks, each shot three-plus times, the floor a controlled whirlwind of wardrobe racks and hairspray.

Among the EXO cameos, the company’s favorite was obvious at a glance: Chanyeol’s reporter character ate up screen time while Baekhyun, Sehun, and Jongin flickered through in seconds. Ji-ho’s own setup was scheduled last — which suited him fine. He spent the hours studying everything: crew choreography, lighting resets, the grammar of the monitors. Tuition-free film school.

The shoot ran hot, so breaks ran twenty minutes. EXO wrapped their coverage and prepared to leave —

— and Byun Baekhyun stood watching Taeyeon across the floor, her head bent toward her assistant, a perfectly calibrated soft smile at her lips.

He’d rehearsed this. It didn’t help. His heart was hammering like someone had sat on the fast-forward button.

He was a fan — had been since before his own casting, drawn in by the voice first and the face right after. The same-company-senior thing had kept him orbiting at a respectful distance for years. But this shoot had given them actual scenes together, actual words exchanged, and her easy warmth had finally cranked his courage up to operational levels. Before leaving today — contact info. That was the mission.

“Tae—”

One breath, one step — and a figure cut across his path carrying a coffee cup.

“Nuna. Your iced americano.”

Taeyeon looked up, took the cup with a smile, and sipped. Satisfaction flickered visibly through her.

“Thank you, Soso.”

“Nuna. Please don’t call me Soso.”

“Why not? I think it’s adorable.”

He stared at her, caught in a perfect trap. Because he knew — every kid raised on Chinatown video-store kung-fu knew — exactly which character answered to that name: the sweet, doe-eyed little maidservant from an old wuxia classic, all soft voice and demure obedience. And explaining WHY he objected meant explaining that he knew, which meant losing twice.

“…I just don’t like it.”

“Okay~” Her mouth agreed. Behind her eyes, laughter was having a festival. Her good friend Kim Heechul was a fanatic for those same wuxia dramas and had made her watch entirely too many of them — she knew precisely which girl she’d just named him after. Teasing this kid might be my new favorite sport.

Tiffany and Seohyun drifted over; Ji-ho produced their coffees from the carrier on cue, and talk turned to the upcoming stage setup, easy and warm.

Baekhyun stood rooted where his first step had died, the rehearsed words jammed somewhere behind his sternum. He watched Ji-ho and Taeyeon-sunbae orbit each other with that unthinking ease — then reviewed his own sweat-slick palms — and the moment quietly declared itself over. The courage went out of him like air from a pricked balloon.

Next time. I’m debuting any day now. There’ll be better moments to ask for her number… later. When I’m someone.

He withdrew the step, turned, and left the soundstage without a sound, shoulders set at a lonely angle.
